## THE CLOSED FACT LIST

Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a number, date, or rule not listed
here or already stated on the page (attached).

1. The IRS standard mileage rate is a single flat per-mile rate that applies to the vehicle
   regardless of its fuel or drivetrain type — gasoline, diesel, hybrid-electric, and
   all-electric vehicles all use the same rate. The IRS's own annual mileage-rate notices state
   this explicitly (this is stated in the IRS's published standard-mileage-rate guidance,
   consistent across years).
2. The two 2026 business rates already stated on the page — 72.5 cents (Jan 1–Jun 30) and 76
   cents (Jul 1–Dec 31) — are the SAME rates that apply to an electric or hybrid vehicle used
   for business driving. Do not invent a separate EV-specific rate; none exists.
3. The standard mileage rate is a proxy for average operating cost across vehicle types (already
   stated on the page: it "covers fuel, insurance, maintenance, tyres, registration, and
   depreciation in one figure"). Do not claim it is calibrated specifically to EV charging costs
   or gasoline costs — it is one blended figure for all vehicle types.
4. Do NOT mention or imply anything about the federal EV purchase tax credit, EV charging
   infrastructure credits, or any other EV-specific tax incentive — those are separate programs
   unrelated to the mileage deduction and are out of scope for this answer.
5. Substantiation requirements (a dated log of miles, dates, destinations, business purpose —
   already stated on the page) apply the same way regardless of vehicle type; do not invent a
   different recordkeeping rule for EVs.
