CLOSED FACT LIST (anything not here, you do not know; never invent a number, date, or URL; say to verify with the IRA custodian or a tax professional):
1. When you take money out of an inherited traditional IRA, the custodian sends you Form 1099-R (or a similar statement). IRA distributions are shown in boxes 1 and 2a of Form 1099-R. (IRS Publication 590-B)
2. A number or letter code in box 7 of Form 1099-R tells you what type of distribution you received. Code 4 means death. (IRS Publication 590-B)
3. Federal income tax is withheld from traditional IRA distributions unless you choose not to have tax withheld. For nonperiodic payments (not regular installments over more than one year), you use Form W-4R to set the withholding, and tax is generally withheld at a 10% rate. Periodic payments use Form W-4P. (IRS Publication 590-B)
4. Withholding is only a prepayment toward your tax bill, not the bill itself. What you actually owe is set by your bracket when you file your return, because a traditional inherited IRA withdrawal is ordinary income stacked on your other income. In the page's worked example the beneficiary is in the 22% bracket for 2026, so the 10% default rate would leave her short.
5. On Form 1040, the total IRA distribution goes on line 4a and the taxable part on line 4b. (IRS Publication 590-B)
6. A beneficiary may be able to deduct federal estate tax paid on any part of a distribution that is income with respect to a decedent. The deduction is taken for the tax year the income is reported; the IRS points to Publication 559 for how to claim it. This only matters when the estate owed federal estate tax, which the page already says few estates do. (IRS Publication 590-B)
7. Reading the 1099-R before you file and checking box 1 against box 2a is how you confirm the custodian reported the right amounts. If a form looks wrong, ask the custodian for a corrected form before filing. (practical guidance, not a rule)
8. The tax rules here are federal. State income tax on withdrawals varies by state; check your state's rules or ask a tax professional.
