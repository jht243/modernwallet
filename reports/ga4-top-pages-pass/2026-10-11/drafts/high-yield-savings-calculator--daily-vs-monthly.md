Barely. Daily versus monthly compounding changes the result by cents in a year and a few dollars over five years on a $10,000 balance. A quoted annual percentage yield (APY) already includes the compounding, so compare APY to APY.

Take $10,000 at a 4.4% nominal annual rate with no deposits for one year. Daily compounding ends at about $10,449.80, and monthly compounding ends at about $10,448.98. The gap is about $0.81, and after five years on the same $10,000 it grows to about $4.85.

Under the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's (CFPB) [Regulation DD](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1030/), APY already reflects how often interest compounds. Compounding frequency changes the result only when a bank quotes a nominal rate without an APY. This calculator compounds monthly at the rate you enter.
