CLOSED FACT LIST. Anything not on this list, you do not know.
- The CFPB's Regulation DD (Truth in Savings) defines APY (annual percentage yield) as the yearly return that already reflects how often interest compounds. Because of that, two accounts with the same APY end a year with the same balance no matter how each one compounds. [consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1030/]
- The calculator on this page compounds monthly at the rate the reader enters, and its example uses a 4.5% APY.
- Worked comparison, plain arithmetic, same 4.4% nominal annual rate and $10,000 with no deposits for one year: daily compounding ends at about $10,449.80 (APY about 4.498%); monthly compounding ends at about $10,448.98 (APY about 4.490%). The gap is about $0.81. Over five years on the same $10,000 the gap is about $4.85.
- A bank that quotes a nominal rate without an APY is the case where compounding frequency changes the result; when a bank quotes an APY, compare APYs directly.
- Sources of confusion to name: "interest rate" (nominal) versus APY.
