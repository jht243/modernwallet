For a 12 to 17 year old, the case for a Trump Account rests on how many years remain before the money unlocks. The money is locked until January 1 of the year the child turns 18, so a 15 year old gets only a few years of tax-deferred growth. Withdrawals are then taxed as ordinary income.

An older child gets no $1,000 seed. The [$250 Dell deposit](/guides/dell-trump-account-250/) covers only children born 2016 through 2024 in eligible ZIP codes, so a teen born before 2016 is outside it. Compare the [alternatives](/guides/trump-account-alternatives/) first: a custodial Roth IRA grows tax-free if the teen has earned income, a [529](/compare/trump-account-vs-529/) is tax-free for school, and a custodial brokerage account has no lock-up. For a teen, the rule of thumb is to match larger savings to the alternative that fits the goal.
