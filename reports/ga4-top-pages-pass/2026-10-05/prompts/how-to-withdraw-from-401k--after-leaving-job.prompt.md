# CLOSED FACT LIST (anything not here, you do not know; never invent a number, limit, or URL)
- After leaving an employer you can usually leave the money in the old plan, move it to an IRA or a new employer's plan, or take a cash distribution. Some plans require small balances to move out, so check the plan documents.
- A direct rollover (plan to plan or plan to IRA, institution to institution) is not taxed and has no early-withdrawal penalty.
- A cash distribution paid to you is taxable ordinary income, and under 59½ it can also trigger the 10% early-withdrawal penalty.
- Eligible rollover distributions paid to you have 20% federal tax withheld by the plan. You then have 60 days to deposit the full amount, including the 20% withheld that you must replace from other money, into an IRA or plan to avoid tax on it.
- The Rule of 55 waives the 10% penalty if you leave your job in or after the year you turn 55, but only for the 401(k) of the employer you just left, and income tax still applies.
- Unvested employer match is forfeited when you leave.
