reader question: "Is personal loan interest tax deductible?"
answer: Usually no. Interest on a personal loan used for personal spending (a vacation, a car repair, paying off credit cards, a wedding) is personal interest and is not deductible. The answer changes by how the money is USED, not by the loan's label: proceeds used for a business, for taxable investments, or for qualified higher-education expenses can make part or all of the interest deductible under separate rules. An unsecured personal loan spent on home improvements does not qualify for the home mortgage-interest deduction because the debt is not secured by the home.

CLOSED FACT LIST — every claim of fact the page may state:
- Personal interest, meaning interest on money borrowed for personal, family or living expenses, is generally not deductible on a federal return (IRS Topic 505, Interest Expense).
- Interest on money borrowed for use in a trade or business is generally deductible as a business expense, subject to the rules in IRS Publication 535, Business Expenses. When one loan funds both personal and business spending, the interest is allocated by how the borrowed money was used, so a borrower should be able to show where each dollar went.
- Investment interest, meaning interest on money borrowed to buy property held for investment, is deductible only as an itemized deduction and only up to net investment income for the year; any excess carries forward to later years. It is figured on Form 4952 (IRS Publication 550, Investment Income and Expenses).
- The student loan interest deduction is worth up to $2,500 a year (IRS Topic 456). It applies to a qualified student loan, which the IRS defines as a loan taken out solely to pay qualified higher education expenses (IRS Publication 970). Do not state income limits, phase-outs or which forms to use; say the reader should check Publication 970 for eligibility.
- The mortgage-interest deduction applies to debt secured by a qualified home, and home equity interest is deductible only when the borrowed funds are used to buy, build or substantially improve the home that secures the loan (IRS Publication 936). An unsecured personal loan is not secured by the home, so its interest does not qualify even if the money pays for a renovation.
- Loan proceeds are not taxable income, because the borrower has an obligation to repay them.
- Canceled or forgiven debt is generally taxable income (IRS Topic 431), and a lender that cancels $600 or more of debt generally reports it to the borrower and the IRS on Form 1099-C.
- Personal loans are commonly unsecured installment loans with fixed rates and terms (general definition; state no rate, fee or term numbers).
