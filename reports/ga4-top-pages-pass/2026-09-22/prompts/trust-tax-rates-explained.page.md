# Trust Tax Rates Hit 37% at Just $16,000 in 2026

In 2026, a non-grantor trust that keeps its own income reaches the top 37% federal tax bracket once its taxable income exceeds just $16,000. By comparison, a single individual does not reach that rate until their taxable income is $640,600 or more.

The mistake we see most often is applying that compressed bracket to every trust. But a grantor trust does not pay income tax at the trust level at all. Instead, its income flows directly to the person who created it and is taxed at their individual rate.

## The 2026 Trust Income Tax Brackets
A non-grantor trust pays 10% on its first $3,300 of taxable income, 24% on the next portion up to $11,700, 35% up to $16,000, and 37% on anything above $16,000. A 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT) can stack on top of the 37% bracket once a trust's undistributed income passes a similarly low $16,000 threshold, producing a combined federal rate near 40.8% on ordinary trust income and 23.8% on long-term capital gains the trust doesn't distribute.

## A Grantor Trust Does Not Pay Trust-Level Tax
A grantor trust's income isn't taxed at the trust at all; it passes through to the person who created the trust. A non-grantor trust is its own separate taxpayer, files Form 1041, and pays tax on any income it keeps at the compressed brackets above.

## The Distribution Deduction Is the Real Planning Lever
A trust that distributes income to its beneficiaries deducts that amount from its own taxable income, shifting the tax bill onto the beneficiary's individual return instead, reported on a Schedule K-1.

## Deductions a Trust Can Claim
A trust can deduct trustee fees, tax preparation costs, and any charitable contributions the trust document authorizes.

## When Trust Tax Planning Matters
Trust-level tax planning matters most for a non-grantor trust holding income-producing assets that the trustee doesn't distribute every year.

## Retained Income Costs More Than Distributed Income
Worked example: a trust earning $30,000 and keeping all of it owes about $9,563 total (ordinary brackets plus NIIT), close to a 32% effective rate. Distributed to a beneficiary in the 22% bracket instead, the tax drops to roughly $6,600.

## Trust Income Tax vs. Estate and Gift Tax
Trust income tax taxes the income a trust generates every year. Estate and gift tax applies separately to the value of assets transferred into the trust, only above the federal exemption.

## FAQ

Q: What is the top trust tax rate in 2026?
A: 37%, which a non-grantor trust reaches once its taxable income passes $16,000 for the year. A 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax can stack on top of that for undistributed investment income, producing a combined federal rate near 40.8%.

Q: Do all trusts pay tax at the compressed trust brackets?
A: No. Only a non-grantor trust that keeps income pays tax at those compressed brackets. A grantor trust's income passes through to the person who created it and gets taxed at their individual rate instead.

Q: How do trusts avoid the 37% tax bracket?
A: By distributing income to beneficiaries each year instead of keeping it inside the trust. The trust deducts what it distributes, and the beneficiary reports that income on their own return, usually at a lower individual rate than the trust's compressed brackets.

Q: What is the capital gains tax rate for a trust in 2026?
A: A trust pays 0% on long-term capital gains up to $3,300, 15% between $3,300 and $16,250, and 20% above $16,250, before any Net Investment Income Tax that applies to undistributed gains.

Q: Can a trust deduct trustee and tax preparation fees?
A: Yes. A trust can deduct trustee fees and tax preparation costs paid from trust assets, along with any charitable contributions the trust document authorizes, reducing the trust's taxable income before the compressed brackets apply.

Q: Is trust income tax the same as estate tax?
A: No. Trust income tax applies every year to the income a trust generates, like interest or rental income, using the compressed brackets above. Estate and gift tax applies separately to the value of assets transferred into the trust, and only above a federal exemption in the tens of millions of dollars per person.

Q: Does distributing trust income always lower the total tax bill?
A: Usually, since most beneficiaries sit in individual tax brackets well below the trust's compressed 37% cliff. A $30,000 distribution taxed at a beneficiary's 22% bracket can owe roughly $3,000 less than the same $30,000 taxed inside the trust, though the exact savings depends on the beneficiary's own income and bracket.
