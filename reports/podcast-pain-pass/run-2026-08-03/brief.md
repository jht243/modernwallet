# Brief — week of 2026-08-03

## Interpreted brief

This week's 56 episodes keep circling the same shape of problem: listeners can do the
first step of a money decision but cannot do the arithmetic that tells them whether the
decision is worth it. The dividend-investing shows spend whole episodes on "how much can
you actually earn," "how do I get to a dividend yield number," and "how do I build a
portfolio out of that" — the math is described verbally and never turned into a number
the listener can plug their own balance into. The retirement shows frame it as a gap:
"how big of a portfolio do I need to fill that gap consistently," plus a recurring
tax-timing question ("should I be doing Roth conversions this year, or is this actually
gonna cost me?"). The women-and-money show surfaces two account-choice questions the site
does not answer head-on — 457(b) versus Roth 401(k) after taxes, and whether advisor fees
plus expense ratios are worth what they buy. The insurance segments circle a permanent-life
policy the caller already owns: what the cash value is, what has been paid in, and how much
can be borrowed against it. The real-estate show adds vacancy: gross rent is easy, but the
downtime between tenants is the number that decides whether the deal works.
