## CLOSED FACT LIST — the ONLY facts this section may state. Anything not on this list, you do not know.

1. The CFPB's own homebuying guidance states that even when a real estate agent or homebuilder has a preferred lender, a buyer should still shop around for a better deal — the agent or builder's familiarity with that lender does not mean it is the best price available to the buyer. [CFPB — Contact Multiple Lenders](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/process/explore/contact-multiple-lenders/)
2. The CFPB recommends contacting at least three lenders and comparing across different TYPES of lenders, not just one bank: a local bank or credit union, other lenders serving the area, and lenders found online, plus the option of working with a mortgage broker instead of a single lender directly. [CFPB — Contact Multiple Lenders](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/process/explore/contact-multiple-lenders/)
3. The CFPB notes that once a seller accepts a buyer's purchase offer, the buyer may have as little as a couple of days to line up financing, which is why comparing lenders BEFORE finding a house (not after an offer is accepted) matters. [CFPB — Contact Multiple Lenders](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/process/explore/contact-multiple-lenders/)

Do not state or imply that a referred lender is always worse, or that any specific lender type (bank, credit union, broker, online lender) is always cheaper — the fact list only supports "compare across types," not a ranking of which type wins.
