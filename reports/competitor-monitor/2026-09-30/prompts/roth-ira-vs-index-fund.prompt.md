FACTS (closed list; anything not here you do not know):
- A Roth IRA is an account type (a tax wrapper); an index fund is an investment. You can hold index funds inside a Roth IRA, so this is often a false either/or choice. Say this plainly in the opening.
- Roth IRA: contributions are after-tax; qualified withdrawals are tax-free; annual IRS contribution limit applies ($7,000 in 2025; the limit changes yearly, reader should verify the current figure); income limits apply to direct contributions; contributions (not earnings) can be withdrawn at any time without tax or penalty; earnings withdrawn early may face tax and a 10% additional tax; qualified distributions need age 59½ and a 5-year holding period.
- Index fund in a taxable brokerage account: no contribution limit; dividends taxed yearly; long-term capital gains taxed at 0/15/20% federal rates; money is accessible any time.
- Index funds track a market index, typically with low expense ratios; do not state specific expense ratio numbers or returns.
- Do not state any return figures.
