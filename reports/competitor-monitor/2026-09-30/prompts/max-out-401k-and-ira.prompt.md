FACTS (closed list; anything not here you do not know):
- Yes: the 401(k) and IRA limits are separate; contributing to one does not reduce the other's limit.
- 401(k) employee deferral limit is $24,500 in 2026 ($32,500 with age-50+ catch-up, per site convention).
- IRA limit is set annually by the IRS ($7,000 in 2025); reader should verify the current figure; it is capped at taxable compensation if lower.
- Deductibility of Traditional IRA contributions can be limited or phased out if you or your spouse are covered by a workplace plan and income is above IRS thresholds; Roth IRA direct contributions phase out at higher incomes. Do not state threshold numbers.
- Common order: (1) 401(k) up to the full employer match, (2) IRA (Roth or traditional), (3) return to the 401(k) toward the limit, (4) taxable account.
- Employer match does not count against your own employee deferral limit.
