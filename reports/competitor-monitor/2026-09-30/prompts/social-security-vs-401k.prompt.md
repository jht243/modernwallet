FACTS (closed list; anything not here you do not know):
- They are complements, not alternatives: Social Security is a government benefit earned through payroll taxes and work credits, paying a monthly benefit that adjusts for inflation and lasts for life; a 401(k) is an employer plan funded by your own (and possibly employer) contributions, invested by you, with a balance that can run out.
- 401(k) contribution limit is $24,500 in 2026 ($32,500 if 50 or older, per site convention).
- Social Security retirement benefits can start at 62 with a permanent reduction; delaying past full retirement age up to 70 increases the monthly amount.
- 401(k) withdrawals of pre-tax money are ordinary income; up to a portion of Social Security benefits can be taxable depending on income (see linked guide). 
- Employer match in a 401(k) is not guaranteed and varies by plan.
- Do not state any benefit dollar amounts or replacement-rate percentages.
