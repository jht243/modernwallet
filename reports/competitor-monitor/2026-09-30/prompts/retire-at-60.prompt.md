FACTS (closed list; anything not here you do not know):
- Medicare eligibility generally begins at 65, so retiring at 60 leaves roughly a five-year health coverage gap to plan for (employer COBRA, marketplace plan, or spouse's plan).
- Social Security retirement benefits can start as early as 62; claiming before full retirement age permanently reduces the monthly benefit; delaying past full retirement age up to 70 increases it. At 60 you cannot claim retirement benefits yet.
- 401(k)/IRA withdrawals before 59½ generally face a 10% additional tax; at 60 you are past 59½, so that penalty no longer applies to 401(k) and IRA withdrawals, though ordinary income tax still does. (Roth IRA earnings also need the 5-year rule.)
- Rule of thumb: the 4% rule is a starting point for a sustainable withdrawal rate, not a guarantee; a 60-year-old may need money to last 30+ years.
- Bridge years: the gap between 60 and Social Security/Medicare is usually funded from taxable accounts, cash, and tax-deferred withdrawals.
- Do not state any specific dollar savings target as a fact; show how to compute one: annual spending x 25 at a 4% withdrawal rate, minus expected guaranteed income capitalised.
