## CLOSED FACT LIST — the ONLY facts this page may state. Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, benchmark, or URL; say it is unpublished and tell the reader to verify at the lender's own page.

1. A secured personal loan is backed by an asset (collateral) the lender can seize if the borrower stops paying; an unsecured personal loan relies only on the borrower's creditworthiness. [CFPB — Personal Loans](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/personal-loans/)
2. Because the lender has an asset to recover, a secured loan generally carries a lower interest rate, a higher available loan amount, and an easier approval bar for a thin-credit borrower than a comparable unsecured loan from the same lender. [CFPB — Personal Loans](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/personal-loans/)
3. A "share-secured" or "savings-secured" loan uses money already sitting in the borrower's own savings account or share account at a credit union as collateral; a "certificate-secured" loan uses a certificate of deposit (CD) the same way. The institution places a hold on that account for the loan amount, and the pledged savings continues earning dividends or interest while the loan is outstanding. [MyCreditUnion.gov (NCUA) — Consumer Loans & Credit Cards](https://mycreditunion.gov/manage-your-money/consumer-loans-credit-cards)
4. Because a share- or certificate-secured loan is backed by cash the lender already holds, its rate is priced as a fixed margin above the rate the pledged savings or certificate itself earns, rather than being priced off the borrower's credit score the way an unsecured loan is. [MyCreditUnion.gov (NCUA) — Consumer Loans & Credit Cards](https://mycreditunion.gov/manage-your-money/consumer-loans-credit-cards)
5. Other common forms of collateral for a secured personal loan include a vehicle already owned outright, or (less commonly for a personal-loan product specifically) home equity. Using a car as collateral is different from a standard auto purchase loan; the same seizure risk (repossession) applies. [CFPB — Personal Loans](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/personal-loans/)
6. If a borrower stops paying a secured loan, the lender can repossess or foreclose on the pledged collateral without first suing the borrower in court, because the borrower already agreed to pledge the asset. [CFPB — Personal Loans](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/personal-loans/)
7. Average personal loan APR varies sharply by credit tier on the unsecured side: roughly 7% to 10% for excellent credit (760+), 10% to 14% for very good (720-759), 14% to 20% for good (680-719), 20% to 28% for fair (640-679), and 26% to 36% for poor credit (below 640). This is the unsecured benchmark a secured offer should be compared against. [Experian — Best Personal Loan Rates](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/best-personal-loan-rates/)
8. The Federal Reserve's G.19 Consumer Credit release tracks the average finance rate lenders charge on personal loans as a broad, lender-independent benchmark. [Federal Reserve — Consumer Credit (G.19)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/)
9. A secured credit card is a different product from a secured personal loan: a secured card is a revolving line backed by a cash deposit (commonly used to build or rebuild credit with ongoing purchases), while a secured personal loan is a lump-sum, fixed-term installment loan. [NCUA — Consumer Resources](https://ncua.gov/consumers)
10. High-interest "secured" loan products such as car title loans and pawnshop loans are a distinct, much higher-cost category from a bank or credit union share-secured loan; a title loan holds a paid-off vehicle's title as collateral and can carry annual rates far above a standard personal loan. [CFPB — Personal Loans](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/personal-loans/)
