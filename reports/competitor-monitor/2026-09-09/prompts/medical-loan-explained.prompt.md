## CLOSED FACT LIST — the ONLY facts this page may state. Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, benchmark, or URL; say it is unpublished and tell the reader to verify at the source.

1. A "medical loan" usually means one of three products: an unsecured personal loan used to pay a medical bill, a medical-specific installment loan offered through a provider's financing partner, or a medical credit card (e.g., CareCredit) used at the point of care. [CFPB — What should I know about medical credit cards and payment plans for medical bills?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-know-about-medical-credit-cards-and-payment-plans-for-medical-bills-en-1827/)
2. Medical credit cards commonly use a "deferred interest" promotion: no interest accrues during a promotional period (commonly 6 to 24 months), but if the full balance is not paid off by the end of that period, interest is charged retroactively on the ENTIRE original balance from the purchase date, not just the remainder. [CFPB — Medical Credit Cards and Financing Plans report, May 2023](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_medical-credit-cards-and-financing-plans_2023-05.pdf)
3. The CFPB's May 2023 report found deferred-interest medical financing terms carried APRs as high as 26.99% once the promotional period lapses, and consumers used these deferred-interest medical credit cards and loans for close to $23 billion in healthcare expenses across more than 17 million purchases from 2018 to 2020 — paying about $1 billion in deferred interest in that span. [CFPB — Medical Credit Cards and Financing Plans report, May 2023](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_medical-credit-cards-and-financing-plans_2023-05.pdf)
4. An unsecured personal loan (the general-purpose kind that can be used to pay a medical bill) charges a fixed rate for a fixed term, and average APRs vary sharply by credit tier: roughly 7% to 10% for excellent credit (760+), 10% to 14% for very good (720-759), 14% to 20% for good (680-719), 20% to 28% for fair (640-679), and 26% to 36% for poor credit (below 640), according to lender-rate surveys reported by Experian. [Experian — Best Personal Loan Rates](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/best-personal-loan-rates/)
5. The Federal Reserve's G.19 Consumer Credit release tracks the average finance rate lenders charge on personal loans over time, as a broad benchmark independent of any single lender. [Federal Reserve — Consumer Credit (G.19)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/)
6. Voluntary changes the three nationwide credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) put in place starting in 2022-2023: paid medical collection debt is no longer included on credit reports regardless of how long it took to pay; unpaid medical collection debt under $500 is not included on credit reports; and new medical debt has a 365-day waiting period before it can appear on a credit report at all, giving insurance and billing disputes time to resolve first. [CFPB — What should I know about medical credit cards and payment plans for medical bills?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-know-about-medical-credit-cards-and-payment-plans-for-medical-bills-en-1827/)
7. The CFPB finalized a separate federal rule in January 2025 that would have barred medical debt from credit reports and from lending decisions entirely. A federal court (U.S. District Court, Eastern District of Texas) vacated that rule in July 2025, in Cornerstone Credit Union League v. CFPB, finding it exceeded the CFPB's statutory authority and conflicted with the Fair Credit Reporting Act. As of this page's writing, that broader federal ban is NOT in effect — only the voluntary bureau policies in fact 6 apply nationwide. [CFPB — Prohibition on Creditors and Consumer Reporting Agencies Concerning Medical Information (Regulation V)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/final-rules/prohibition-on-creditors-and-consumer-reporting-agencies-concerning-medical-information-regulation-v/)
8. A hospital or provider's own payment plan is frequently interest-free or low-interest, since it is arranged directly with the billing office rather than through a third-party lender. [CFPB — What should I know about medical credit cards and payment plans for medical bills?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-know-about-medical-credit-cards-and-payment-plans-for-medical-bills-en-1827/)
9. Nonprofit and many hospital financial-assistance ("charity care") programs can reduce or eliminate a bill for patients under a certain income threshold; nonprofit hospitals are required by the IRS to have a written financial assistance policy under Internal Revenue Code Section 501(r). [IRS — Financial Assistance Policy and Emergency Medical Care Policy (Section 501(r)(4))](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/financial-assistance-policy-and-emergency-medical-care-policy-section-501r4)
10. A secured personal loan (backed by collateral such as a savings account or vehicle) typically carries a lower rate than an unsecured medical loan because the lender has an asset to recover if the borrower defaults — but the asset is then at risk. [CFPB — Personal Loans](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/personal-loans/)
