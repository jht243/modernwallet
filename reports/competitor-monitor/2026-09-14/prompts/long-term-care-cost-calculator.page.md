# Long-Term Care Cost Calculator: What It Costs and Who Pays

A long-term care cost calculator estimates what nursing home, assisted living, or home health aide care will cost when you actually need it, not what it costs today.

Using 2024 Genworth Cost of Care Survey medians projected forward at 4.5% annual inflation (LTC's historical rate), the 2026 US-median costs are $132,000/year for a private nursing home room, $68,000/year for assisted living, and $76,000/year for a home health aide (44 hr/wk). Alaska is the highest at 2.19x the national median, while Mississippi and Louisiana are the lowest at ~0.71x.

Because most people need care 10-20 years from now and LTC inflates faster than general CPI, the projected cost at the start age is often 50-100% higher than today's price.

Medicare covers exactly zero custodial care. Its Skilled Nursing Facility benefit maxes out at 100 days after a qualifying 3-night hospital stay. Everything else (assisted living, a home aide, and nursing home stays past day 100) comes from personal savings, LTC insurance, or Medicaid after spend-down.

In-Home Care vs. Assisted Living vs. Nursing Home

National care costs in 2026 range from $68,000 for assisted living to $132,000 for nursing home care. Based on Genworth data projected at 4.5% annual inflation, a private nursing home room averages about $361 a day. Assisted living runs about $5,668 a month, while a home health aide working 44 hours a week costs $76,000 a year at roughly $33 an hour.

Location and insurance rules alter what families pay out of pocket. State medians range from 0.71 times the national benchmark in Mississippi and Louisiana to 2.19 times in Alaska. Medicare excludes assisted living and long-term aides entirely, and covers skilled nursing facility care only up to 100 days after a qualifying hospital stay.

When we evaluate care options, daily physical need dictates the right setting. In-home aides work best when an elder wants to stay home and a family caregiver lives nearby to coordinate meals, logistics, and safety. Assisted living provides custodial help with bathing and dressing, whereas nursing homes deliver around-the-clock clinical care for severe medical conditions.

Default to assisted living for custodial care on a predictable budget. Choose home aides when family support keeps paid hours low, but pivot to a nursing home if skilled clinical needs emerge or if state Medicaid waitlists block home-care funding.
