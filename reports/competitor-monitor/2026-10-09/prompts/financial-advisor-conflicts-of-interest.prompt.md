- A conflict of interest exists when an advisor's compensation or affiliations could reward a recommendation that is not the best fit for the client.
- Common pay sources: a percentage of assets under management (often around 1% a year), a flat fee, an hourly rate, commissions on products sold (mutual funds, annuities, insurance), or a mix.
- Hypothetical worked example: a 1% annual fee on $500,000 is $5,000 a year. A 1% fee on $20,000 is $200 a year.
- Fee-only advisors are paid only by the client. Commission-based advisors earn from products they sell. Hybrid advisors can charge both. "Fee-based" is a different label from "fee-only": a fee-based advisor can still earn commissions, so ask directly how every dollar of pay arrives.
- A percentage-of-assets fee has its own conflict: the advisor earns more when the account grows, including when you add money, which can discourage advice such as paying off a mortgage or debt with those assets.
- Investment advisers registered with the SEC or a state regulator owe a fiduciary duty under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940. Broker-dealer representatives are held to Regulation Best Interest, which raises the older suitability standard but is not the same as fiduciary duty.
- A person can hold both roles, so ask whether the advisor is a fiduciary at all times and on all your accounts, and ask for the answer in writing.
- Other conflicts to ask about: payments from third parties tied to products recommended, affiliation with a broker-dealer or insurance company, sales quotas or production targets, and revenue minimums.
- Form CRS (Customer Relationship Summary) is a short plain-language document most advisers and brokers must give retail clients. It describes services, fees, conflicts, and disciplinary history.
- Form ADV Part 2 (the brochure) describes an investment adviser's business practices, fee structure, conflicts of interest, and disciplinary history.
- Investment Adviser Public Disclosure (IAPD) at adviserinfo.sec.gov is the free public database for advisers. FINRA BrokerCheck at brokercheck.finra.org is the free public database for brokers.
- If an advisor will not provide a Form CRS or Form ADV, that is a reason to keep looking.
- A second opinion from an unconnected advisor, paid by flat fee or hourly, is a way to test a recommendation, especially a product like an annuity or a switch of investments.
