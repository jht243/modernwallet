- Hypothetical worked example (engine-computed): carrying two mortgages. A $400,000 30-year fixed mortgage at 6.5% has a principal-and-interest payment of $2,528.27 a month. If the old home's payment is $2,528.27 and the new home's payment is $3,000, carrying both for 3 months costs $16,584.81 before taxes, insurance and utilities.
- Under IRC Section 121, a homeowner can exclude up to $250,000 of gain on the sale of a main home ($500,000 for most married couples filing jointly) if they owned and used it as their main home for at least 2 of the 5 years before the sale. Source: IRS Topic 701 and Publication 523.
- A home sale contingency makes an offer conditional on selling your current home; sellers in a competitive market often treat it as a weaker offer. A rent-back (post-closing occupancy) agreement lets a seller stay in the sold home for an agreed period after closing. Terms are negotiated and vary by state and contract; the page must say so and not state a standard length.
- A bridge loan is short-term financing secured by the current home, used to cover a down payment before the sale closes; rates, fees and terms vary by lender and are not published as a standard figure. The page must tell the reader to get a written quote. A HELOC on the current home is the common alternative; HELOCs generally must be opened before the home is listed because lenders can be reluctant to open one on a home that is for sale (reader to verify with the lender).
- Lenders count both housing payments in the debt-to-income ratio unless the old mortgage will be paid off at closing with documented sale proceeds; the page must say to ask the lender how it will treat the old mortgage. No specific DTI limit may be stated as a rule.
- Closing costs, moving costs, and overlapping insurance and utility bills are real costs of a double move; the page may not state a percentage of price.
