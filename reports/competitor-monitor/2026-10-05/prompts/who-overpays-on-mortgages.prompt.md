- Hypothetical worked example (engine-computed): $400,000, 30-year fixed. At 6.50% the principal-and-interest payment is $2,528.27; at 6.25% it is $2,462.87. The difference is $65.40 a month, which is about $23,545 over 360 payments if the loan is held to term.
- A lender must provide a Loan Estimate within 3 business days of receiving a mortgage application; it is a standard 3-page form that makes offers comparable. Source: CFPB.
- Credit scoring models generally treat multiple mortgage inquiries within a short window as a single inquiry for scoring, so shopping around does not stack hard-inquiry penalties. The window length depends on the scoring model (commonly cited as 14 to 45 days); the page must say to confirm with the lender and must not state one number as a rule. Source: CFPB.
- Compare the interest rate, the APR, points and lender credits, and the closing costs on page 2 of the Loan Estimate; a lower rate that comes with more points is not necessarily cheaper unless the borrower keeps the loan long enough to recoup them. The page may show the breakeven method: extra upfront cost divided by monthly savings.
- Hypothetical breakeven: paying $4,000 in points to lower the payment by $65.40 a month breaks even after about 61 months ($4,000 / $65.40 = 61.16).
- Mortgage rates and the credit score tier a borrower falls into affect pricing; the page may not state any score cutoffs or a current market rate, and must point to the existing current mortgage rates guide.
- Common ways to overpay (stated as patterns, with no statistics): accepting one lender's quote, ignoring lender fees and points, rolling closing costs into the loan without comparing, keeping private mortgage insurance after it can be removed, and never reviewing a refinance when rates drop meaningfully. Source: CFPB consumer guidance (reader to verify).
