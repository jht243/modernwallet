- Most large US online brokers now advertise $0 commissions for online stock and ETF trades; options contracts, mutual funds outside a no-transaction-fee list, broker-assisted trades, foreign stocks and OTC securities are often charged. The page must not state any specific broker's fee or any specific per-contract amount; it must tell the reader to read the broker's current fee schedule.
- The bid-ask spread is the gap between the best price a buyer will pay and the best a seller will accept; a market order buys at the ask and sells at the bid, so a wider spread costs the investor more even at $0 commission.
- Hypothetical worked example (illustrative numbers): a stock quoted $50.00 bid and $50.10 ask. Buying 100 shares at the ask costs $5,010 and selling immediately at the bid returns $5,000, a $10 round-trip cost, or 0.2% of the purchase.
- Payment for order flow is the practice of brokers receiving payments from market makers for routing customer orders to them; it is one way brokers earn money on commission-free trades, and it is disclosed in broker order-routing reports. The page must present this as a tradeoff to understand, not a claim about any broker.
- A limit order lets you set the maximum buy price or minimum sell price, which controls spread cost but may not fill. Source: general order-type definition.
- Regulatory fees on sales: the SEC Section 31 fee and the FINRA Trading Activity Fee are small fees charged on stock sales and are usually passed through by the broker; rates change, so the page must tell the reader to check sec.gov and finra.org for current rates and must not state a rate.
- Fund costs: an ETF's or mutual fund's expense ratio is deducted from fund assets every year and is separate from any trade commission. Hypothetical: a 0.50% expense ratio on $10,000 is $50 a year; a 0.05% ratio is $5 a year.
- Other possible charges: account transfer-out (ACATS) fees, inactivity or maintenance fees, paper statement fees, margin interest, wire fees. Policies vary by broker; the page must say to check the fee schedule.
