- A trust only controls assets that are titled in its name or that name it as beneficiary. An unfunded trust generally leaves assets subject to probate, which is the usual reason people create a revocable living trust. The page must present this as the general rule and say state rules differ.
- Real estate is moved into a trust by recording a new deed; recording requirements, transfer-tax exemptions and title-insurance effects vary by state and county, so the page must tell the reader to check with the county recorder and title insurer, with no specific fee stated.
- Bank and brokerage accounts are retitled in the trust's name with the institution's own forms; some institutions instead allow a payable-on-death (POD) or transfer-on-death (TOD) designation, which is simpler but is not the same as trust funding.
- Retirement accounts (401(k), IRA, 403(b)) are not retitled to a living trust during life because that would generally be treated as a distribution; the usual approach is to name beneficiaries on the account, sometimes including a trust for a specific reason. The page must tell the reader to get tax advice before naming a trust as an IRA beneficiary.
- Life insurance and annuities are funded by changing the beneficiary or owner on the policy; vehicles depend on state titling rules, and many people leave low-value vehicles outside the trust. Tangible personal property is typically moved with an assignment document. A pour-over will is a backstop that directs assets left outside the trust into it at death, but those assets still go through probate. Stated generally; states differ.
- Business interests: LLC membership or stock may need operating-agreement or shareholder consent to assign; the page must say to read the governing documents first.
- Hypothetical worked example: a household has a home, two bank accounts, a brokerage account, two IRAs and a car. Retitle the home, the two bank accounts and the brokerage account; keep the IRAs outside and name beneficiaries; leave the car out. No dollar figures may be used.
