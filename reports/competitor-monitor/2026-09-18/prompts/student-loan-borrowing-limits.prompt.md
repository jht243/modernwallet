## CLOSED FACT LIST

Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a specific school's cost of attendance,
a specific private lender's limit, or a specific current-year interest rate figure beyond what is
listed here.

1. Dependent undergraduate Direct Subsidized + Unsubsidized annual limits (unchanged by the One
   Big Beautiful Bill Act, per the U.S. Department of Education's Federal Student Aid Handbook):
   $5,500 for first-year students (of which up to $3,500 may be subsidized), $6,500 for
   second-year students (up to $4,500 subsidized), $7,500 for third-year and beyond (up to $5,500
   subsidized).
2. Independent undergraduates (and dependent undergraduates whose parent was denied a Direct PLUS
   Loan) have higher combined annual limits: $9,500 first year, $10,500 second year, $12,500
   third year and beyond — the subsidized portion caps are the same as the dependent limits above
   at each year.
3. Undergraduate aggregate (lifetime) limits: $31,000 total for dependent undergraduates (of
   which no more than $23,000 may be subsidized); $57,500 total for independent undergraduates
   (same $23,000 subsidized cap).
4. Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, effective July 1, 2026 for new borrowers: the graduate
   Direct Unsubsidized Loan annual limit stays $20,500 a year, but the graduate aggregate limit
   drops from $138,500 to $100,000; a professional-degree student (e.g. law, medical, dental) can
   borrow up to $50,000 a year with a $200,000 aggregate cap; and a new overall lifetime cap of
   $257,500 applies across all federal student loans combined (this lifetime cap excludes Parent
   PLUS loans, which are borrowed separately by a parent).
5. The Graduate PLUS Loan program is eliminated for new borrowers after June 30, 2026, under the
   same legislation; students already enrolled and borrowing before that date keep access to
   Grad PLUS through a legacy provision that runs through June 30, 2029.
6. A "legacy" or grandfather provision lets a borrower continue under the loan limits and program
   rules that applied before July 1, 2026, as long as they had a federal student loan disburse
   before that date while enrolled in the same qualified program of study; that legacy status
   lasts up to 3 years or until the borrower finishes that program, whichever comes first.
7. Private student loan limits are set by each individual lender rather than by federal law; many
   private lenders will lend up to the school's certified total cost of attendance minus any
   other financial aid the student has already received, but the exact ceiling always varies by
   lender and by the borrower's (or cosigner's) creditworthiness.
8. Federal Student Aid calculates a school's official cost of attendance (tuition, fees, room and
   board, books, and other certified costs), and federal loan amounts can never exceed that
   school-certified figure minus other aid already received, regardless of the annual dollar
   limits above.
