## CLOSED FACT LIST

Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a specific lender's name-brand approval
odds, a specific current advertised APR range, or a specific dollar income figure beyond what is
listed here as a general market figure.

1. Most private student loan lenders require the borrower (or a cosigner) to have a credit score
   in the good-to-excellent range, commonly cited around 670 or higher, though there is no single
   universal minimum score across all lenders, and approval below that range is possible with a
   qualified cosigner.
2. Lenders generally look for a minimum income (some as low as roughly $24,000 a year) and weigh
   a debt-to-income (DTI) ratio, with many private lenders preferring a DTI under roughly 36%; a
   student with little or no income history is the most common reason a cosigner is required.
3. A cosigner (commonly a parent) must generally show their own good-to-excellent credit
   (typically 670 or higher), reliable income, and an acceptable DTI, since the cosigner becomes
   equally responsible for repayment and any missed payment affects both people's credit.
4. Federal law (via accredited-institution eligibility rules Federal Student Aid enforces) and
   private lender underwriting both require the borrower to be enrolled at least half-time in a
   degree or certificate program at an eligible school before a student loan, federal or private,
   can be certified and disbursed.
5. Private lenders set their own age and citizenship rules; most require the primary borrower or
   cosigner to be a U.S. citizen or permanent resident and at or above the age of majority in
   their state (commonly 18), though some lenders extend private loans to international or DACA
   students specifically with a creditworthy U.S.-based cosigner.
6. The CFPB advises that private student loans generally carry none of the federal protections
   (income-driven repayment, deferment, standardized forbearance, and current loan-forgiveness
   programs) that federal Direct Loans carry, and recommends borrowers exhaust federal aid options
   first before applying for a private loan.
7. This site's own reporting on federal loan default (see the linked guide) notes that most
   private lenders define default after roughly 90 to 120 days of missed payments, a shorter and
   lender-set window compared to the federal 270-day standard, which is a downstream consequence
   of qualifying for and taking out a private loan rather than a qualification requirement itself.
