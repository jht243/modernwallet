## CLOSED FACT LIST

Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a specific real brokerage's default
accounting method — describe the general options generically.

1. Average (weighted-average) cost basis per share equals total dollars spent across every lot
   (including any commission paid) divided by total shares owned; it blends every purchase into
   one number regardless of when each lot was bought.
2. The IRS permits different cost-basis accounting methods for figuring gain or loss on a partial
   sale of shares, including First-In-First-Out (FIFO), specific-share identification, and, for
   mutual fund shares specifically, an average-cost method; a brokerage applies a default method
   unless the account holder elects a different one before a sale settles.
3. Average cost basis is most commonly used automatically for mutual fund and some brokerage
   dividend-reinvestment-plan shares; individual stock shares are more commonly tracked by
   specific-lot identification or FIFO by default, though many brokerages let the account holder
   choose.
4. Commission or transaction fees paid on a purchase are added to that lot's cost basis (increasing
   it), and commission paid on a sale is subtracted from sale proceeds (reducing the gain or
   increasing the loss), per general IRS cost-basis guidance.
5. Dollar-cost averaging (investing a fixed dollar amount on a regular schedule) is a common way
   investors end up with multiple lots at different prices in the first place; this site's own
   [dollar-cost averaging spoke](/investing/dollar-cost-averaging-calculator/) covers that
   strategy's mechanics and tradeoffs in depth.

## commonMistakes (4-6, concrete)

- Forgetting to include commissions/fees in the cost basis, which understates your true cost.
- Assuming average cost is the only method your broker uses — checking your actual 1099-B/account
  settings for the method that will apply.
- Mixing up "average price paid" with "average cost basis" when commissions differ per lot.
- Not updating your tracked average cost after a stock split (link to the split calculator).
