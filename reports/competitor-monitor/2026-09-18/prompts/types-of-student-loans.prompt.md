## CLOSED FACT LIST

Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a specific interest rate number, a
specific lender name, or a specific dollar limit — link to the site's own limits guide for those
instead of restating them here.

1. Federal student loans come from the U.S. Department of Education under the William D. Ford
   Federal Direct Loan Program; private student loans come from banks, credit unions, online
   lenders, or state-affiliated nonprofit lenders, each setting its own terms.
2. Direct Subsidized Loans are available only to undergraduate students with demonstrated
   financial need; the government pays the interest while the student is enrolled at least
   half-time, during a 6-month grace period after leaving school, and during any deferment period.
3. Direct Unsubsidized Loans are available to both undergraduate and graduate/professional
   students regardless of financial need; interest accrues the entire time the loan is
   outstanding, including while the student is still in school, and unpaid interest can be added
   to the loan's principal (capitalized) at certain points.
4. Direct PLUS Loans are unsubsidized federal loans requiring a credit check for adverse credit
   history (not income or a minimum score); the Parent PLUS variant is borrowed by a parent for a
   dependent undergraduate, and the Grad PLUS variant was borrowed directly by a graduate or
   professional student to cover costs beyond the standard unsubsidized limit.
5. Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, the Grad PLUS Loan program is eliminated for new
   borrowers after June 30, 2026 (existing borrowers keep access under a legacy provision through
   June 30, 2029); this site's dedicated limits guide covers the exact dollar figures.
6. Private student loans are underwritten on the applicant's (or a cosigner's) credit and income,
   not financial need, and never carry federal protections like income-driven repayment,
   standardized deferment/forbearance rules, or current federal forgiveness programs; the CFPB
   recommends exhausting federal loan and grant options before turning to a private loan.
7. Both federal Direct Loans and private student loans generally require the borrower to be
   enrolled at least half-time in a degree or certificate program at an eligible school before the
   loan can be certified and disbursed.
8. A general, widely cited rule of thumb (this site's own editorial framing, not a regulatory
   rule) for choosing among these types: take every dollar of subsidized federal loan you qualify
   for first (it's the only type that doesn't accrue interest in school), then unsubsidized
   federal loans, and treat PLUS or private borrowing as the last resort for any remaining gap,
   because that is the order that minimizes total interest paid before other, non-loan aid
   (grants, scholarships, work-study) is even considered.
