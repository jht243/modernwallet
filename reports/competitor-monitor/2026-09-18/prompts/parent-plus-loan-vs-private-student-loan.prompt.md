## CLOSED FACT LIST

Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a specific lender name, a specific
current interest rate number for either product, or a specific dollar origination-fee percentage
beyond what is listed here.

1. A federal Direct PLUS Loan for parents (Parent PLUS) is a federal loan the U.S. Department of
   Education makes directly to the parent of a dependent undergraduate, not to the student; the
   parent is the sole borrower and is solely responsible for repayment.
2. Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, effective July 1, 2026, new Parent PLUS borrowing is
   capped at $20,000 per year per dependent student (combined across all parents borrowing for
   that student), with a $65,000 aggregate lifetime cap per student. Before this cap took effect,
   Parent PLUS had no fixed annual dollar cap of its own; a parent could generally borrow up to
   the school's full cost of attendance minus any other financial aid the student received.
3. A Parent PLUS Loan requires a credit check for adverse credit history (not a minimum credit
   score or income test), and federal Direct PLUS Loans carry a loan origination fee that is
   deducted from the disbursed amount, unlike a typical private loan, which does not usually
   charge that mandatory federal origination fee.
4. Parent PLUS Loans carry federal borrower protections: access to Public Service Loan
   Forgiveness after loan consolidation into a Direct Consolidation Loan, deferment and
   forbearance options, and (per this site's own reporting) the new Repayment Assistance Plan
   (RAP) and Tiered Standard Repayment Plan that apply to federal loans first disbursed on or
   after July 1, 2026. A private parent loan or cosigned private student loan has none of these
   federal protections; its terms are whatever the private lender's contract states.
5. A private student loan for a parent (either a loan taken out directly by the parent, or a
   private loan the student takes out with the parent as cosigner) is priced on the applicant's
   (or cosigner's) credit profile, so a parent or cosigner with strong credit can sometimes secure
   a lower interest rate than the current Parent PLUS rate, while a parent with weaker credit may
   be offered a higher rate than Parent PLUS or be denied entirely.
6. Federal Direct PLUS Loans (including Parent PLUS) that are denied for adverse credit history
   can still be approved with an endorser (the federal equivalent of a cosigner) or by
   documenting extenuating circumstances, per Federal Student Aid's own published process.
7. Interest on a federal Direct PLUS Loan begins accruing as soon as the loan is disbursed, same
   as most private loans; neither product offers an in-school interest-free period the way a
   subsidized federal loan does, since PLUS loans (federal or private-equivalent) are never
   subsidized.

## Objectivity rule

Neither option is universally better. State plainly: a parent with excellent credit who wants a
potentially lower rate and doesn't need federal protections may prefer a private loan; a parent
who wants access to income-driven-style federal protections, or who has weaker credit and needs
the adverse-credit-only underwriting standard, is usually better served by Parent PLUS. Name who
should NOT take each option and what would change the recommendation (e.g., a much lower private
rate offer, or a need for federal forbearance flexibility).
