## CLOSED FACT LIST

Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a specific real fund's actual current
NAV, assets, or share count — use clearly-labeled hypothetical numbers for any worked example.

1. Net asset value (NAV) per share equals a fund's total assets minus its total liabilities,
   divided by its total shares outstanding, per the SEC's Investor.gov definition.
2. Mutual funds calculate and publish NAV once per business day, after the major U.S. exchanges
   close; a mutual fund purchase or sale order executes at that day's closing NAV, not a live
   intraday price.
3. An exchange-traded fund (ETF) also has a NAV calculated the same way, but unlike a mutual fund,
   an ETF trades throughout the day on an exchange at a market price that can run slightly above
   (a premium) or below (a discount) its NAV, because the market price is set by supply and demand
   between trades rather than by the once-daily NAV calculation alone.
4. This site's own [portfolio metrics formulas guide](/guides/portfolio-metrics-formulas-explained/)
   works through the NAV formula alongside portfolio turnover and beta with its own worked
   example; this calculator is the interactive version of that same formula for plugging in your
   own numbers.
5. A fund's "total liabilities" in the NAV formula typically includes accrued expenses such as
   management fees and other operating costs the fund owes but has not yet paid out of its assets.

## commonMistakes (4-6, concrete)

- Assuming an ETF always trades exactly at its NAV during the day (it can trade at a premium or
  discount).
- Forgetting to subtract liabilities (like accrued fees) before dividing by shares outstanding.
- Using stale asset figures instead of the fund's most recently published numbers.
- Confusing a fund's NAV per share with its total NAV (total net assets).
