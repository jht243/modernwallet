## CLOSED FACT LIST

Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a specific dollar figure for a prior
award year, a specific state's supplemental grant amount, or a specific school's disbursement
schedule beyond what is listed here.

1. A Federal Pell Grant is money the U.S. Department of Education gives undergraduate students
   with financial need to help pay for college; unlike a loan, a Pell Grant generally does not
   have to be repaid (except in specific cases, such as withdrawing early enough that the school
   must return a portion of the funds).
2. For the 2026-27 award year, the maximum Federal Pell Grant is $7,395 and the minimum award is
   $740, per the U.S. Department of Education's Federal Student Aid office.
3. Eligibility is based on the Student Aid Index (SAI), a number calculated from the FAFSA that
   replaced the old Expected Family Contribution (EFC). For 2026-27, a student is not eligible for
   any Pell Grant at all if their SAI is $14,790 or higher.
4. If a student's SAI qualifies them for less than the maximum award, the grant amount is
   calculated by subtracting the SAI from the annual maximum Pell Grant amount, then rounding to
   the nearest $5; a student whose SAI and family circumstances qualify them for the Maximum Pell
   Grant receives the full maximum regardless of the exact SAI number.
5. Maximum and Minimum Pell Grant eligibility (the two tiers that do not require the SAI
   subtraction math) are determined using tax filing status, family size, family composition
   (single-parent vs. not), and federal poverty guidelines for the family's state of residence.
6. Starting with the 2026-27 award year, a change tied to the One Big Beautiful Bill Act adds any
   foreign earned income exclusion amount reported on the FAFSA back into adjusted gross income
   when the Department of Education determines Maximum and Minimum Pell Grant eligibility.
7. You apply for a Pell Grant by filing the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA);
   there is no separate Pell Grant application. The school's financial aid office uses your FAFSA
   result to determine and disburse your award.
8. A Pell Grant can be used for tuition, fees, room and board, books, and other certified
   education costs, the same categories used to calculate a school's cost of attendance; it is
   generally not taxable income when used for tuition, fees, and required course materials, but
   any portion used for room, board, or other non-qualified expenses can be taxable.
9. A student can generally receive a Federal Pell Grant for up to 12 semesters (roughly 6 years)
   total across their lifetime, known as the Lifetime Eligibility Used (LEU) limit, and Pell
   eligibility is not limited to full-time students — a part-time student can receive a
   prorated award based on enrollment intensity.
