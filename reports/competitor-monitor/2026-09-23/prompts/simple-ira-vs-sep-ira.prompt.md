## CLOSED FACT LIST
Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a figure.

- SIMPLE IRA 2026 employee salary-deferral limit: $17,000, with a $4,000 catch-up for participants 50 and older, and an enhanced $5,250 catch-up for participants aged 60 to 63 under SECURE 2.0. Source: IRS COLA increases page (irs.gov/retirement-plans/cola-increases-for-dollar-limitations-on-benefits-and-contributions) — already used elsewhere on this site for the same figures.
- SIMPLE IRA employer contribution is MANDATORY every year the plan is active: either a dollar-for-dollar match up to 3% of compensation, or a flat 2% nonelective contribution to every eligible employee regardless of whether they defer. There is no year an employer can skip it.
- SIMPLE IRA eligibility (employer side): generally limited to businesses with 100 or fewer employees who earned $5,000 or more in the preceding year (a business that grows past 100 employees generally becomes ineligible to continue offering a SIMPLE IRA, subject to a brief grace period under IRS rules).
- SIMPLE IRA early-withdrawal penalty: a withdrawal before age 59 1/2 is normally subject to a 10% additional tax, but that penalty is INCREASED to 25% specifically for a SIMPLE IRA if the withdrawal happens within the first TWO YEARS of the employee's participation in the plan (measured from the date of the employee's first contribution). After the first two years, the standard 10% early-withdrawal penalty (subject to the usual exceptions) applies instead. Source: IRS (irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-simple-ira-contribution-limits and irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-exceptions-to-tax-on-early-distributions).
- SIMPLE IRA setup deadline: a new SIMPLE IRA plan generally must be established between January 1 and October 1 of the year contributions will be made for (a business established after October 1 gets a limited exception to set one up as soon as administratively feasible).
- SIMPLE IRA vesting: 100% immediate on all employer contributions. No plan loans are allowed from a SIMPLE IRA (it is IRA-based, not 401(k)-based).
- SIMPLE IRA Roth option: available at participating providers, added by SECURE 2.0 starting in 2023.
- SEP-IRA 2026 maximum contribution: the lesser of 25% of compensation or $72,000, with compensation itself capped at $360,000 for this calculation. Source: IRS COLA increases page.
- SEP-IRA is funded by the EMPLOYER ONLY — there is no employee salary-deferral option in a SEP-IRA (unlike a SIMPLE IRA, where the employee personally defers pay).
- SEP-IRA eligibility (employer side): no limit on the number of employees a business can have. But if the employer covers any employees, it generally must contribute the SAME PERCENTAGE of compensation for every eligible employee that year — an employer cannot contribute a higher percentage for itself/owners than for eligible staff.
- SEP-IRA employee eligibility rules the employer must generally follow (unless using less restrictive terms): age 21 or older, employed by the business during at least 3 of the immediately preceding 5 years, and earned at least $800 in compensation from the employer in 2026 (this minimum-compensation figure is itself adjusted periodically — $800 is specifically the 2026 figure). Source: IRS.
- SEP-IRA setup deadline: can generally be established and funded as late as the employer's tax filing deadline, INCLUDING extensions — considerably more flexible than the SIMPLE IRA's October 1 cutoff. This is one of the most practically useful, non-obvious differences for a reader deciding late in the year.
- SEP-IRA vesting: 100% immediate. No plan loans are allowed from a SEP-IRA (also IRA-based).
- SEP-IRA Roth option: also available at participating providers, added by SECURE 2.0 starting in 2023 (same law, same effective year, as the SIMPLE IRA Roth option above).
- Neither plan allows contributions to exceed its own respective cap regardless of age; catch-up contributions (age 50+/60-63) are a SIMPLE IRA feature tied to the EMPLOYEE deferral, which does not exist in a SEP-IRA, so a SEP-IRA has no separate catch-up contribution mechanism — the whole 25%-of-compensation/$72,000 figure is the entire cap, age aside.

## Comparison table dimensions to include (>=5 rows, real value both sides, no deferral)
1. Who contributes (employee + employer for SIMPLE IRA; employer only for SEP-IRA).
2. 2026 maximum (SIMPLE: $17,000 employee deferral, +$4,000/$5,250 catch-up, PLUS employer match/nonelective; SEP: lesser of 25% of comp or $72,000, employer-funded only).
3. Employer contribution requirement (SIMPLE: mandatory 3% match or 2% nonelective every year; SEP: employer decides the % each year, but must apply it equally to all eligible employees).
4. Early-withdrawal penalty (SIMPLE: 25% if within first 2 years of participation, then 10%; SEP: standard 10% like a traditional IRA, no 2-year rule).
5. Setup deadline (SIMPLE: generally January 1 to October 1; SEP: as late as the tax filing deadline including extensions).
6. Roth option (both: yes, at participating providers, added by SECURE 2.0 in 2023).
7. Plan loans (neither allows loans — both are IRA-based).
8. Employee eligibility rules (SIMPLE: must have earned $5,000+ in the prior year, from any employer; SEP: age 21+, 3-of-5-years employment, $800+ compensation in 2026, less restrictive terms optional).
