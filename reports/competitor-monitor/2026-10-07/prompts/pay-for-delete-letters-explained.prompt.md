- Pay for delete is a request that a collector remove a collection account from your credit reports in exchange for payment. It is not a right: neither the collector nor the credit bureaus are required to agree.
- A collector that reports to the bureaus is expected to report accurately. Deleting an accurate account in exchange for payment can conflict with the collector's agreement with the bureaus, which is why many collectors refuse. Whether a given collector agrees is its own policy; ask.
- Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, most negative items, including collection accounts, can stay on a credit report for seven years. Source: CFPB.
- Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act's seven-year limit, the clock for a collection runs from the date of first delinquency on the original account, not from the date the collector bought the debt. Source: CFPB.
- Paying a collection does not by itself remove it; the account can remain on the report and be marked paid. Source: CFPB.
- Newer FICO and VantageScore versions ignore paid collection accounts in their calculations, but many lenders still use older scoring models, and mortgage lenders commonly use older FICO versions. Which version a lender uses is the lender's choice; ask.
- Debt collectors are covered by the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA). You can send a written debt validation request, and a collector must provide validation information. Source: CFPB debt collection page.
- A partial payment or acknowledgement can restart the state statute of limitations on a debt in some states. Statutes of limitations vary by state; verify yours before sending money or signing anything.
- Hypothetical worked example: a $1,200 collection. A collector agrees in writing to delete on receipt of $700 (a 42% discount, since $500 / $1,200 is about 41.7%). Always get the agreement in writing and signed before paying, and pay with a traceable method, not a debit authorization over the phone.
- A goodwill letter is a request, with no payment bargain, that a creditor remove a late payment or paid item as a courtesy. The creditor may decline.
- Credit reports are available free from each bureau weekly at AnnualCreditReport.com. Source: CFPB.
