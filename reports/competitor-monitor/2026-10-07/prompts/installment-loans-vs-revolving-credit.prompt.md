## CLOSED FACT LIST

- An installment loan is a loan for a fixed amount that you repay in scheduled payments over a set term. Examples: mortgages, auto loans, student loans, personal loans.
- Revolving credit gives you a credit limit you can borrow against, repay and borrow again. The required payment varies with the balance. Examples: credit cards, home equity lines of credit (HELOCs), and lines of credit.
- Hypothetical worked example: a $12,000 installment loan at 8% APR over 36 months has a payment of about $376 a month (standard amortization). Total paid is about $13,540, so interest is about $1,540.
- Hypothetical worked example: a credit card with a $5,000 limit and a $2,000 balance has a utilization of 40% ($2,000 / $5,000). The minimum payment is set by the issuer and falls as the balance falls.
- Payment history and amounts owed are the two largest FICO categories (about 35% and about 30%). Credit mix is about 10%. New credit is about 10% and length of history about 15%. Source: myFICO.
- Utilization applies to revolving accounts. Scoring models weigh a paid-down installment loan's balance against its original amount differently from a revolving balance against its limit. Do not state a precise formula.
- Having both types can help the credit mix category slightly, but it is a small share (about 10%) and it is not a reason to take on a loan you do not need.
- A HELOC is revolving but secured by your home; missing payments can put the home at risk. A personal loan is usually unsecured installment debt.
- A credit card balance can be turned into an installment loan with a debt consolidation loan, which changes how the debt is reported and usually gives a fixed payoff date. Whether it lowers your cost depends on the APR and any origination fee; compare the APR in writing.
- Paying off an installment loan closes the account; the closed account can stay on a credit report. Paying off a card leaves the account open and the limit available. Source: CFPB general credit report guidance (verify details).
