# Best Retirement Calculators of 2026

The best retirement calculator for you depends less on the brand and more on what the tool actually models. Some use a simple fixed-rate projection, while others run thousands of randomized market scenarios (known as Monte Carlo simulation) to estimate your odds of not running out of money.

We compared nine widely used retirement calculators based on methodology, not made-up accuracy scores. We looked at whether each calculator models market volatility, Social Security, taxes, and inflation, as well as whether it's free. No provider paid to be included on this list.

This list is compiled from each provider's own published methodology pages, help-center documentation, and public calculator interfaces, not a paid placement or an aggregator's ranking. We did not assign a numeric "accuracy" score to any tool, because two calculators built on different methodologies aren't measuring the same thing.

## Monte Carlo Simulation vs. Fixed-Rate Projection: What the Difference Actually Means

A fixed-rate projection assumes one steady annual return every year between now and retirement. Monte Carlo simulation instead runs your plan through hundreds or thousands of randomized market paths built from historical volatility data, then reports the percentage of paths where your money lasted.

## Why Almost None of These Calculators Fully Model Taxes

Modeling taxes accurately requires knowing your filing status, state of residence, other income sources, and the specific account types you'll withdraw from. That's a lot of individual variation for a generic calculator to model correctly.

## How to Estimate the Social Security Piece These Calculators Handle Differently

Bankrate, SmartAsset, and Fidelity all estimate a Social Security benefit for you directly from your income. NerdWallet and Schwab's savings calculator instead ask you to enter your own Social Security estimate.

## A Five-Minute Checklist before You Trust Any Retirement Calculator's Number

Confirm which methodology you're looking at, check whether Social Security is auto-estimated, note whether taxes are included, check the inflation assumption, and run the same inputs through at least two calculators with different methodologies.

Verdict: if you want a two-minute sanity check with no signup, Bankrate, NerdWallet, SmartAsset, and our own retirement calculator all give you a fast fixed-rate projection. If you want to know your probability of not running out of money, you need a tool that actually runs Monte Carlo simulation. No calculator on this list fully models taxes.
