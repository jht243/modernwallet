### Section-by-section coverage (use these 8 steps, in this order, as your H2 headings)
1. Picture what retirement actually looks like for you (Step 1) — get concrete: where you'll
   live, whether you'll work part-time, what changes about your monthly bills (no mortgage? new
   travel budget?). This is a planning INPUT, not a number yet.
2. Pick a target retirement age (Step 2) — name the real tradeoff: retiring before 65 means
   self-funding health coverage until Medicare eligibility, and retiring before 59½ means most
   retirement-account withdrawals face the standard 10% early-withdrawal penalty unless you use
   a specific IRS exception.
3. Estimate what that lifestyle will cost per year (Step 3) — anchor to the DOL's 70–90% income-
   replacement guideline (fact list below) as a starting range, and flag that it is a rule of
   thumb, not a personal number — the reader's real number depends on their own planned spending.
4. Turn the annual cost into a savings target (Step 4) — use the 25x-annual-spending rule (fact
   list below; already established on this site) to convert the Step 3 number into a lump-sum
   target, and note this is the same math behind the 4% withdrawal guideline.
5. Build the actual savings plan: automate it, then raise it (Step 5) — automatic payroll
   deduction plus a scheduled annual increase (this site's own established convention, already
   used in how-to-become-a-401k-millionaire) beats relying on willpower or year-end lump sums.
6. Invest the savings for your actual timeline (Step 6) — a target-date fund (or a simple stock/
   bond mix that shifts toward bonds as retirement nears) removes the need to pick individual

7. Use every year of tax-advantaged room before it resets (Step 7) — the 2026 401(k)/IRA limits
