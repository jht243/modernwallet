## CLOSED FACT LIST

Anything not on this list, you do not know.

1. Refinancing generally makes the most sense when your credit score or debt-to-income ratio has
   improved since you took out the original loan, since that is what qualifies you for a
   meaningfully lower APR.
2. A borrower should confirm they have positive equity (the car is worth more than the remaining
   loan balance) before applying to refinance; owing more than the car is worth can make it hard
   to qualify for a new loan, per Experian.
3. Refinancing late in a loan's term — when only a small number of payments remain — rarely saves
   meaningful interest, since most of the interest on an amortizing loan is already front-loaded
   into the earlier payments; a new loan resets that front-loaded interest structure.
4. A borrower should confirm any new loan's fees (origination fee, title transfer fee) do not
   exceed the projected interest savings before refinancing.
5. The CFPB confirms some auto loans include a prepayment penalty on the original loan, which
   should be checked before refinancing since it can offset the expected savings.
