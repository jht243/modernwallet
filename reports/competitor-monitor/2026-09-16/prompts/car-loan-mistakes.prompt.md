## CLOSED FACT LIST

Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a specific lender name, a specific
current-year average APR figure, or a specific dollar penalty amount beyond what is listed here.

1. The CFPB recommends comparing the Annual Percentage Rate (APR), not just the monthly payment,
   when shopping for an auto loan, because APR includes the interest rate plus lender fees and is
   the only apples-to-apples number across offers.
2. The CFPB recommends getting preapproved by a bank or credit union before visiting a dealer, so
   the buyer has a firm rate and amount to negotiate against instead of relying on dealer-arranged
   financing as the only option.
3. Experian's credit-tier research shows a buyer's credit score is the single biggest driver of
   the APR a lender offers; a buyer who applies without first checking and, if needed, improving
   their credit score can lock in a materially higher rate than their true credit risk warrants.
4. A longer loan term lowers the monthly payment but increases total interest paid and raises the
   risk of negative equity (owing more than the car is worth) for longer, per CFPB guidance on
   loan term tradeoffs.
5. Focusing only on the monthly payment (instead of the total out-the-door price, APR, and term
   together) is a documented dealer negotiation tactic per the FTC — a dealer can hit a target
   monthly payment while quietly extending the term or raising the price.
6. The FTC states dealers cannot charge for add-ons (extended warranties, paint protection, gap
   insurance, etc.) a buyer did not agree to, and a buyer can decline any add-on; rolling
   unwanted add-ons into the loan increases both the principal financed and the total interest
   paid over the loan's life.
7. Rolling negative equity from a trade-in (still owing money on the old car) into a new loan
   increases the new loan's principal beyond the new car's value, which the CFPB flags as a way
   buyers end up financing a car they already owe more on than it is worth from day one.
8. Skipping a check for a prepayment penalty is a documented mistake: the CFPB confirms some auto
   loan contracts include a fee for paying the loan off early, which can reduce or erase interest
   savings from an accelerated payoff.
9. Not reading the full contract before signing — including the interest rate, fees, add-ons, and
   any prepayment penalty in writing — is a core FTC recommendation; verbal promises from a
   salesperson are not binding if they are not in the signed contract.
10. Applying to multiple lenders with hard-pull credit applications spread out over many days or
    weeks (instead of comparing prequalified, soft-pull offers first, or bunching hard-pull
    applications within a short focused shopping window) can cost more credit-score points than
    necessary; credit scoring models generally treat multiple auto-loan inquiries within a short
    window as a single inquiry for scoring purposes.
