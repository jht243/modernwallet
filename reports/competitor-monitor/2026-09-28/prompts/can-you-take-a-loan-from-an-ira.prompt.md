FACTS (closed list; anything not here you do not know):
- An IRA cannot make loans to its owner. Borrowing from an IRA or using it as collateral is a prohibited transaction; if the owner does it, the account stops being an IRA as of the first day of that year and its full fair market value is treated as distributed (taxable, and the 10% additional tax applies if under 59½).
- The 60-day rollover: you can take a distribution and redeposit it within 60 days to avoid tax; this works like a short-term loan but is a rollover. Only one IRA-to-IRA rollover is allowed in any 12-month period, across all your IRAs. Missing the 60 days makes the amount taxable and, before 59½, subject to the 10% additional tax.
- Workplace 401(k) plans may allow loans if the plan permits: generally up to the lesser of $50,000 or 50% of the vested balance, repaid within five years (longer for a home purchase). Not every plan offers loans.
- Alternatives: 401(k) loan where offered, penalty exceptions listed in IRS Topic 558, a HELOC or personal loan.
