FACTS (closed list; anything not here you do not know):
- 2026 Roth IRA contribution limit: $7,500, plus $1,100 catch-up at 50 or older ($8,600). Contributions need earned income.
- 2026 Roth IRA income phase-out: single filers $153,000 to $168,000; married filing jointly $242,000 to $252,000.
- Roth IRA qualified distributions: tax-free when made after a five-year period and after age 59½ (or disability, death, first-home up to $10,000 lifetime). The original owner has no required minimum distributions.
- Non-qualified annuity (bought with after-tax money): no IRS contribution limit; earnings are taxed as ordinary income when withdrawn, earnings come out first for withdrawals before annuitizing; earnings taken before 59½ are generally subject to the 10% additional tax. Annuity guarantees, fees, surrender charges and rates vary by insurer and contract; state none as numbers.
- Annuities can convert a balance into guaranteed lifetime income; a Roth IRA does not guarantee income.
