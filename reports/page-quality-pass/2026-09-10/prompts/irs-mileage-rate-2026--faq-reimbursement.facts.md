# Closed fact list — irs-mileage-rate-2026 FAQ addition (mileage reimbursement for W-2 employees)

Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, benchmark, or URL;
say it is unpublished and tell the reader to verify at the source.

- The 2026 IRS business standard mileage rate is 72.5 cents per mile from January 1 through
  June 30, 2026, and 76 cents per mile from July 1 through December 31, 2026. (Already stated
  elsewhere on this page — reuse, do not restate as new information.)
- Under an IRS "accountable plan," an employer's mileage reimbursement to an employee is NOT
  included in the employee's taxable wages (not reported on Form W-2, not subject to income
  or payroll tax) when it is reimbursed at or below the IRS standard mileage rate for the
  period the miles were driven.
- To qualify as an accountable plan, three conditions must be met: (1) the expense must have
  a business connection, (2) the employee must substantiate the expense (dates, miles,
  destination, business purpose) within a reasonable time, and (3) the employee must return
  any reimbursement in excess of substantiated expenses within a reasonable time.
- If an employer reimburses ABOVE the applicable IRS standard mileage rate, the excess amount
  is treated as taxable wages — it is added to Box 1 of the employee's Form W-2 and is subject
  to income and payroll tax — unless the employee separately substantiates a higher actual cost.
- If a reimbursement arrangement does NOT meet the accountable-plan conditions (a
  "non-accountable plan"), the ENTIRE reimbursement is taxable wages, not just the excess.
- Unreimbursed employee mileage (an employee who is not reimbursed at all) is generally not
  deductible under current federal law — this page's "Can employees deduct mileage in 2026?"
  FAQ already states this; do not contradict it.
- Source for the accountable-plan mechanics: IRS Publication 463, Travel, Gift, and Car
  Expenses (already cited on this page as a source — reuse it, do not add a new URL).
