Question (verbatim): "Why don't Chase, Bank of America, or Wells Fargo offer high-yield savings rates?"

Answer must give the genuinely useful, non-obvious reason: large branch-network banks carry
substantially higher fixed overhead (physical branches, staffing, ATM networks) than an
online-only bank, and they generally do not need to compete aggressively on deposit rates to
attract customers who already bank with them for convenience/relationship reasons — so their
standard savings account APY typically sits close to the low national average rather than a
high-yield rate. State plainly that a reader with an account at one of those banks can still open
a separate HYSA at an online bank without leaving their primary bank. 2-4 sentences.

## CLOSED FACT LIST

- Large traditional branch-network banks (the kind with thousands of physical locations) carry
  materially higher fixed operating costs than an online-only bank, which is the standard
  industry explanation for why their standard savings APY tends to track close to the low
  national average rather than compete like an online-only bank's HYSA.
- A saver can hold a primary checking/banking relationship at one bank while separately opening a
  HYSA at a different, online-only bank purely for the savings balance — the two are not mutually
  exclusive.
