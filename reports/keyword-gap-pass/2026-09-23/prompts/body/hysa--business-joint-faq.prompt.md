# Task
Add ONE new FAQ entry covering TWO related sub-questions.

Question (verbatim): "Can a business open a high-yield savings account, or open one jointly with someone else?"

Answer must cover both, 2-4 sentences:
1. Joint accounts: yes, banks generally allow a joint HYSA with a co-owner, each with equal
   withdrawal rights, and FDIC coverage on a joint account is separate from (and often higher
   than) individual-account coverage per depositor.
2. Business accounts: yes, but a business high-yield savings account is typically a DIFFERENT
   product from a personal HYSA at the same bank, usually with its own application (EIN/business
   documents) and sometimes different rate tiers, so a business owner should not assume the
   consumer HYSA application process applies.

## CLOSED FACT LIST
- Joint deposit accounts at an FDIC-insured bank are insured up to $250,000 PER CO-OWNER (i.e., a
  two-person joint account can be insured up to $500,000 total), separately from each co-owner's
  individual-account coverage — this is a standard FDIC ownership-category rule.
- Business deposit accounts are typically a separate product line from personal/consumer deposit
  accounts at most banks, usually requiring business formation documents (e.g., an EIN) rather
  than the consumer-account application flow.
