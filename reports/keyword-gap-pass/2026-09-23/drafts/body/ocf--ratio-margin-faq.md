The operating cash flow (OCF) ratio divides operating cash flow by current liabilities to measure liquidity, where a result above 1.0 indicates that cash from operations alone covers near-term obligations. In contrast, the operating cash flow margin divides operating cash flow by revenue to measure efficiency, showing what percentage of every revenue dollar converts into spendable cash. Because liquidity and revenue conversion answer completely different financial questions, a business should not treat the two metrics interchangeably. Using this page's $133,000 OCF alongside an illustrative $100,000 in current liabilities produces an OCF ratio of 1.33, whereas pairing that $133,000 with an illustrative $1,000,000 in revenue yields a 13.3% margin.
