## CLOSED FACT LIST
Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, benchmark, agency name, or
URL; say it is unpublished and tell the reader to check the relevant state body's own site.

1. Malaysia's zakat system is mandatory and state-administered, not voluntary the way this site's
   own live-price calculator is. Each of Malaysia's states (plus the Federal Territories) has its
   own official Islamic religious council (Majlis Agama Islam Negeri) or a dedicated zakat-collection
   body under that council, and Malaysian Muslims are expected to pay zakat to their own state's
   authorized body, not to a generic online tool. Source (official government list):
   https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/categories/aid-welfare-and-assistance/zakat/list-of-state-zakat-agencies
2. **Official state zakat body names/abbreviations** (per the official government listing above —
   use ONLY these, do not invent others for states not listed here):
   - Selangor: Lembaga Zakat Selangor (LZS)
   - Wilayah Persekutuan (Kuala Lumpur and the federal territories): Majlis Agama Islam Wilayah
     Persekutuan (MAIWP) — its zakat-collection arm is commonly known as PPZ-MAIWP (Pusat Pungutan
     Zakat)
   - Johor: Majlis Agama Islam Negeri Johor (MAIJ)
   - Kelantan: Majlis Agama Islam dan Adat Istiadat Melayu Kelantan
   - Kedah: Lembaga Zakat Negeri Kedah
   - Terengganu: Majlis Agama Islam dan Adat Melayu Terengganu (commonly abbreviated MAIDAM)
   - Negeri Sembilan: Zakat Negeri Sembilan (MAINS)
   - Melaka: Pusat Zakat Melaka
   - Penang (Pulau Pinang): Zakat Pulau Pinang
   - Perak: Baitulmal Majlis Agama Islam Perak (MAIAMP)
   - Sabah: Majlis Ugama Islam Sabah (MUIS)
   - Sarawak: Tabung Baitulmal Sarawak
   Source: https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/categories/aid-welfare-and-assistance/zakat/list-of-state-zakat-agencies
3. **JAIS is commonly confused with the zakat body, but is a different organization**: in Selangor
   specifically, JAIS (Jabatan Agama Islam Selangor — the Selangor Islamic Religious Department) is
   the enforcement and administrative department handling Islamic family law, religious education,
   mosque management, and Shariah enforcement in the state. It is NOT the zakat-collection body —
   that is LZS (Lembaga Zakat Selangor), a separate entity. A Selangor resident should pay zakat
   through LZS, not JAIS. Source: https://www.jais.gov.my/en/about-us-3/ (JAIS's own stated
   functions) and https://www.zakatselangor.com.my/en/about-us/ (LZS's own stated role as the
   entity "entrusted with the management of zakat" in Selangor).
4. **Payroll zakat deduction exists and is common**: many Malaysian employers offer a scheduled
   monthly salary deduction for zakat (potongan gaji berjadual, "PGB"), where the employer deducts
   the amount directly from pay and remits it to the employee's authorized state zakat body each
   month, rather than the employee paying a lump sum once a year. Source:
   https://www.imoney.my/articles/zakat-income-2026-tax-rebate
5. **Zakat is a tax REBATE, not a tax relief, under Malaysian law — and the two work differently**:
   under Section 6A(3) of Malaysia's Income Tax Act 1967, zakat, fitrah, or other obligatory Islamic
   religious dues paid to an authorized religious authority (evidenced by that authority's own
   receipt) are deducted directly from the amount of tax payable, ringgit for ringgit — not merely
   used to lower the income bracket the tax is calculated on the way an ordinary relief would. The
   rebate is capped at the actual tax payable; it cannot reduce tax below zero, and excess zakat
   paid beyond the tax bill is not refunded or carried forward. Source:
   https://www.imoney.my/articles/zakat-income-2026-tax-rebate
6. **Payroll-deducted zakat still qualifies for the rebate**: zakat taken out through the monthly
   payroll deduction described in fact 4 qualifies for the same Section 6A(3) rebate as a lump-sum
   payment, provided the reader keeps the receipt/statement from the state zakat body. Source:
   https://www.imoney.my/articles/zakat-income-2026-tax-rebate
7. **The receipt has to come from the authorized state body itself**: to actually claim the Section
   6A(3) rebate, a Malaysian taxpayer needs an official receipt issued by their state's authorized
   zakat-collection body (the bodies named in fact 2), not from an unofficial or third-party
   calculator. Source: https://www.imoney.my/articles/zakat-income-2026-tax-rebate
8. **This site's own zakat calculator is a general, currency-agnostic, live-price tool** — it is
   useful for a Malaysian reader who wants to sanity-check their own zakat math (net zakatable
   wealth times 2.5%, against a gold/silver nisab in whatever currency they enter), but it is not
   connected to any Malaysian state zakat body, does not produce an official receipt, and does not
   compute the income-tax rebate. Restate as established internal fact about /zakat/, no external
   citation needed.
