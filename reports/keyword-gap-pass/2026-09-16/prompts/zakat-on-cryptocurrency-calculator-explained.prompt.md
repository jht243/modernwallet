## CLOSED FACT LIST
Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, benchmark, or URL.

1. Zakat's underlying math (2.5% of net zakatable wealth once nisab and hawl are met) is unchanged
   for crypto — only the questions of "does crypto count as zakatable wealth" and "how do I value
   it" are specific to this asset class. Restate as established internal site content (/zakat/), no
   new external citation needed for this one line.
2. **Mainstream/majority contemporary position**: a growing number of modern scholars, including
   AAOIFI (the Accounting and Auditing Organization for Islamic Financial Institutions) and several
   national Shariah advisory boards in Malaysia, the Gulf, and South Asia, now treat cryptocurrency
   held for investment as a zakatable "tradeable asset" (mal mutaqawwim) — reasoned the same way
   gold and silver are: not because crypto existed in classical times, but because it functions the
   way a store of value accepted by a broad community for payment functions. Source:
   https://academy.musaffa.com/zakat-on-cryptocurrency/ and
   https://joebradford.substack.com/p/zakat-on-cryptocurrency (Bradford applies the classical
   principle that if a modern asset functions exactly as gold/silver functioned, the same zakat
   rules apply to it).
3. **Valuation date — the majority reasoning**: sources on this list that address the question use
   the spot market price of the crypto asset on the reader's own zakat/hawl anniversary date — NOT
   the price paid at purchase, and NOT an average of prices over the holding period. Source:
   https://academy.musaffa.com/zakat-on-cryptocurrency/ ("The price to use is the spot price of the
   asset on your zakat date... not the price you paid for it, or any average of prices") and
   https://joebradford.substack.com/p/zakat-on-cryptocurrency (2.5% "of the full market value on
   your zakat date").
4. **Hawl (one lunar year) applies the same way it does to any other zakatable asset**: the holding
   period is measured from when the crypto (or, if it pushed total wealth over nisab, the point it
   did so) has been held continuously. Do not state an exact day-count rule beyond "one lunar year"
   — no source on this list gives more precision than that for crypto specifically. Source:
   https://academy.musaffa.com/zakat-on-cryptocurrency/
5. **Staking rewards**: staking rewards are generally treated as new income/wealth added to the
   holding, valued at market price when assessed, rather than as a separate zakat category. The
   underlying staked principal itself stays zakatable at its current market value. Source:
   https://academy.musaffa.com/zakat-on-cryptocurrency/
6. **The genuinely contested edge case — locked or illiquid positions**: where crypto is locked (a
   staking lock-up period, funds frozen on an insolvent or restricted exchange, or otherwise not
   actually accessible to the holder), scholarly opinion is NOT settled. State both positions
   without picking a winner: the stricter view holds such positions are not zakatable until the
   holder can actually access them, following the same complete-ownership (al-milk al-tamm)
   reasoning used for restricted retirement accounts; the more cautious/conservative view still
   includes them in the zakat base as a precaution. Source: https://academy.musaffa.com/zakat-on-cryptocurrency/
   (illiquid-asset caution) and, for the underlying complete-ownership reasoning applied to a
   comparable restricted-asset case, https://joebradford.substack.com/p/zakat-on-retirement-accounts-a-complete
   (Bradford: "zakat is not owed on wealth you do not fully own" when access carries a real penalty
   or restriction — cite this as the reasoning pattern, not as a crypto-specific ruling).
7. **Complex crypto activity is out of scope for a simple calculator**: futures, leveraged
   positions, and derivatives built on crypto raise separate permissibility questions (potential
   riba or the sale of a non-existent asset) before a zakat number even applies — a reader engaged
   in those should consult a scholar rather than treat this page's guidance as covering them.
   Source: https://academy.musaffa.com/zakat-on-cryptocurrency/
8. **This site's calculator does not have a dedicated crypto input field**: readers should convert
   their crypto holdings to the calculator's cash-equivalent value at today's spot price and enter
   that total under cash/investments, alongside their other zakatable assets, then let the
   calculator apply the standard 2.5% math. Restate as established internal fact about /zakat/, no
   external citation needed.
