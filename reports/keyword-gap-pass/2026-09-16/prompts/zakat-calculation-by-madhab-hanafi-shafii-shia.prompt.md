## CLOSED FACT LIST

1. Zakat is 2.5% of net zakatable wealth once nisab and hawl (one full lunar year of holding) are
   met — this is common ground across all schools discussed on this page. Source: the site's own
   zakat hub (internal, already live at /zakat/ — you may restate this as established site content,
   no external citation needed for this one line).
2. **Hanafi school**: uses the silver nisab standard as its basis, and for cash/mixed wealth this
   silver-based threshold is applied because it sets a lower dollar/gram bar and brings more people
   into the obligation. Hanafi jurists generally combine gold and silver holdings (and cash) and
   assess the combined value against the silver nisab, rather than requiring gold and silver to
   independently clear separate thresholds. Source: https://joebradford.substack.com/p/how-to-calculate-your-zakat
   (Bradford: "the silver standard, which produces a lower threshold and thereby includes a broader
   base of Muslims in the obligation") and https://seekersguidance.org/answers/shafii-fiqh/should-the-minimum-zakatable-amount-be-measured-by-the-gold-or-silver-standards/
   (background on the gold/silver standard debate across schools).
3. **Hanafi jewelry rule**: gold and silver jewelry is zakatable regardless of whether it is
   personally worn, because the underlying material (not the use) carries zakat. This is already
   stated on the site's own zakat hub FAQ ("Should I pay zakat on jewelry I wear?") — restate
   consistently, no new external citation required for this specific line.
4. **Shafi'i, Maliki, and Hanbali schools (classical default)**: these three schools classically
   default to the GOLD nisab standard rather than silver, historically set at roughly 84-85 grams
   of pure gold (20 mithqals, using a mithqal conversion of about 4.25 grams). Source:
   https://seekersguidance.org/answers/shafii-fiqh/should-the-minimum-zakatable-amount-be-measured-by-the-gold-or-silver-standards/
5. **Contemporary split inside Shafi'i scholarship specifically**: even within the Shafi'i school,
   modern scholars disagree. The late Shafi'i mufti Shaykh Taha Karaan argued for keeping the gold
   standard, reasoning that the lower silver threshold would pull many low-income people into an
   obligation meant to redistribute the wealth of the rich. Contemporary Shafi'i scholar Shaykh Nuh
   Ha Mim Keller instead recommends the silver standard (he gives a figure of roughly 592.9 grams of
   silver) because, in his reasoning, it is "better for the poor" — i.e., it triggers the
   redistributive obligation sooner. Source (both positions):
   https://seekersguidance.org/answers/shafii-fiqh/should-the-minimum-zakatable-amount-be-measured-by-the-gold-or-silver-standards/
6. **Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali jewelry rule**: many scholars across these three schools exempt gold
   and silver jewelry that is genuinely worn for personal use, treating it as a personal item rather
   than a store of wealth — a different result from the Hanafi position in fact 3. This is already
   stated on the site's zakat hub FAQ; restate consistently.
7. **This site's zakat calculator's own numbers** (already live, restate as established internal
   fact, no new citation needed): the calculator defaults to 87.48 grams of gold and 612.36 grams of
   silver as its modern nisab conversion, sourced on the hub itself from
   https://www.zakat.org/what-is-ni-ab-in-islam (which instead cites a classical gold figure of
   roughly 85 grams) and https://www.islamicreliefcanada.org/our-work/zakat/zakat-on-gold. This is a
   real example of different reputable organizations rounding the same classical weight slightly
   differently — use it as the page's own illustration that "which gram figure" is itself a minor
   point of variation even within a single standard, not just between schools.
8. **Shia (Ja'fari/Twelver) practice — structurally different, not just a different number**: in
   Ja'fari fiqh, zakat is formally obligatory on a specific, enumerated list of categories rather
   than "wealth in general": wheat, barley, dates, raisins, gold, camels, cows, sheep/goats, and (by
   obligatory precaution) business goods, plus silver, at a 2.5% rate once nisab and the other
   conditions are met. Source (primary, official): https://www.sistani.org/english/book/48/2314/
9. **Shia gold/silver zakat only applies to coined/minted metal**: under Ja'fari fiqh, zakat on gold
   and silver becomes obligatory only when the metal is minted and prevalently used as currency
   (i.e., coins), and gold/silver jewelry is explicitly EXEMPT from zakat — a position closer to the
   Shafi'i/Maliki/Hanbali jewelry exemption in spirit, though for a different underlying reason
   (coined-currency status, not personal use). Source: https://www.sistani.org/english/book/48/2317/
10. **Shia gold nisab, exact figure from an official primary source**: the Ja'fari nisab for gold is
    twenty "legal" (shar'i) mithqals, and the Sistani office's own published conversion states one
    legal mithqal equals 3.456 grams — so twenty legal mithqals equals 69.12 grams. This is a
    meaningfully different gram figure from the roughly 85-87.48 grams used in the Sunni-school
    examples above; state the number and the reason (different mithqal-to-gram convention), do not
    speculate why beyond what the source says. Source:
    https://www.sistani.org/english/book/48/2317/
11. **Shia silver nisab is stated in mithqals only**: the Ja'fari nisab for silver is 105 "common"
    (sayrafi) mithqals. Do NOT convert this to a gram figure — the primary source does not publish a
    gram equivalent for the silver nisab, and no other source on this closed list supplies a
    reliable one. State it in mithqals and note that (unlike the gold figure) no official gram
    conversion is published, so a reader following Ja'fari fiqh should confirm the current gram/price
    equivalent with a Shia scholar or zakat calculator built specifically for that standard. Source:
    https://www.sistani.org/english/book/48/2318/
12. **Khums, not just zakat, is the primary lunar-wealth-purification mechanism for many Shia
    Muslims on modern income and savings**: Ja'fari fiqh has a separate, additional 20% obligation
    called khums, assessed on a household's net annual surplus (income left over after living
    expenses), that in practice does much of the redistributive work that a Sunni reader
    associates with "zakat on savings." A reader who is Shia and only runs this calculator's
    2.5%-of-net-wealth math may be missing their khums obligation entirely — this calculator does not
    compute khums. Source: https://www.sistani.org/english/book/48/2314/ (the difference between the
    two obligations is described on the official Sistani.org zakat/khums pages).
13. **Joe Bradford** (author cited by name in one of the search variants this page targets) is a
    scholar with a graduate degree in Islamic law from the University of Medina, associated with the
    Hanbali school, and the author of a published zakat guide/calculator. He recommends the silver
    nisab standard for the reason in fact 2. Do not attribute any other specific claim to him beyond
    what fact 2's source states. Source: https://joebradford.substack.com/p/how-to-calculate-your-zakat
14. **What this calculator does and does not decide**: this site's zakat calculator (at /zakat/)
    applies the standard 2.5% math to whatever nisab standard and asset figures the reader enters —
    it does not pick a school for the reader. Restate this consistently with the hub's own FAQ
    ("Does this calculator issue a religious ruling (fatwa)? No.").
