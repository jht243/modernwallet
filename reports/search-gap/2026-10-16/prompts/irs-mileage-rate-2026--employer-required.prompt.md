# CLOSED FACT LIST
Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, benchmark, or URL; say it is unpublished and tell the reader to verify.
1. No general federal law requires a private employer to pay the IRS standard mileage rate to W-2 employees. The IRS sets the rate mainly for self-employed taxpayers computing deductions and as a benchmark for tax-free reimbursement.
2. The 2026 business rate is 72.5 cents per mile from January 1 through June 30 and 76 cents per mile from July 1 through December 31.
3. A reimbursement paid under an accountable plan at or below the IRS rate is not taxable to the employee. If an employer pays more than the IRS rate, generally only the excess is taxed as wages on Form W-2.
4. The U.S. Department of Labor, in Wage and Hour Division Opinion Letter FLSA2020-12, said an employer may reimburse personal vehicle expenses through a reasonable approximation of costs; the FLSA does not mandate the IRS rate.
5. Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, vehicle costs an employer requires a worker to bear cannot push the worker's pay for the workweek below the federal minimum wage of $7.25 per hour (DOL Field Assistance Bulletin No. 2009-2).
6. California Labor Code Section 2802 requires employers to reimburse employees for necessary expenditures incurred in the discharge of their duties, which includes vehicle costs. It does not name the IRS rate as a floor or a ceiling; if actual costs exceed the per-mile payment the employer remains responsible for the difference.
7. Many employers adopt the IRS rate for convenience because payments up to it are non-taxable under an accountable plan.
8. Independent contractors and 1099 gig workers are not covered by these employee reimbursement rules; they recover vehicle costs through the mileage deduction on Schedule C.
