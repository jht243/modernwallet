County treasurers mail these notices every November to give property owners time to review proposed spending before city councils, county boards and school boards vote on final levies. Tools for this journey home affordability calculator → What a Truth-in-Taxation Notice Means for Your Tax Bill A Truth-in-Taxation notice is a parcel-specific estimate of your property taxes for the coming year based on preliminary levies adopted by your local taxing authorities. It is not a bill, and you do not make a payment from it. The county treasurer mails the notice by first class mail after November 10 and on or before November 24. It goes to each taxpayer at the address on the county assessment roll. You can ask in writing for electronic delivery. Under the Minnesota Department of Revenue press release , preliminary levies statewide show property taxes could increase by a maximum of $949 million over 2026, representing a 6.5% overall increase. The Department of Revenue says final levies can be set lower, but not higher, than preliminary levies. Minn. Stat. 275.065 subd. 6(a) lists limited exceptions. However, your individual parcel's bill could be more or less than the figure shown on the notice. The estimate does not account for certain items excluded by statute, such as special assessments or voter-approved referenda passed after the proposed levies were certified. You can take concrete steps right now: Compare the current-year tax column against the proposed tax column for each local taxing authority. Identify which specific entity, such as your county, city or school district, is proposing the largest dollar or percentage change. Note the meeting dates, times and locations listed on the notice for the authorities responsible for the increase. Attend the public budget meetings between late November and late December to ask questions and comment on proposed spending. Submit written comments directly to the taxing authority using the mailing address, phone number or official website printed on your notice if you cannot attend in person. Final levies must be set by local governments by December 28, 2026. The Department of Revenue then compiles the adopted levies and releases final property tax levy information in February. Last year, the final statewide levy increase was $74 million lower than preliminary levies, resulting in a final annual increase of 6.4%. How to Read the Notice, Column by Column The layout of your Truth-in-Taxation notice is governed by Minn. Stat. 275.065 subd. 3 . The document displays parcel data alongside an itemized comparison between current-year property taxes and proposed property taxes for each taxing jurisdiction serving your home. Notice element Statutory reference What is displayed on your notice Market value Subd. 3(d) County assessor market value on November 1 for the current tax year and next year; stated as final values. Property classification Subd. 3(d) Classifies the parcel as homestead or nonhomestead residential property. County tax Subd. 3(d) Actual current-year tax paid to the county alongside the proposed county tax amount. City or town tax Subd. 3(d) Actual current-year tax paid to the municipality alongside the proposed municipal tax. State general tax Subd. 3(d) Displays the state general tax; proposed equals final. School district levies Subd. 3(d) Separates voter-approved school operating levies from other local school board levies. Tax credits Subd. 3(d) Displays the agricultural homestead credit or school building bond credit where applicable. Special taxing districts Subd. 3(d) Shows the sum of special taxing districts. Total proposed tax Subd. 3(d) Sum of all taxing authorities showing dollar difference and overall percentage change. Hearing information Subd. 3(c) Meeting dates, times and physical addresses where budgets and levies will be discussed. Authority contact details Subd. 3(c) Official website URLs, telephone contact numbers and mailing addresses for comments. Where tax increment financing or the metropolitan fiscal disparities areawide tax applies to a property, those items are stated separately on the form. If a school referendum was pending at the time the notice was drafted, the notice includes a specific note warning that the final school tax amount may be higher than shown if voters approved the measure. Keep the statutory calendar in mind as you review the notice: November 10 to November 24: County treasurers print and deliver notices by first class mail. November 25 to December 28: Required public budget meetings take place across qualifying local jurisdictions. December 28, 2026: Deadline for local governments to set final 2027 property tax levies. February 2027: The Department of Revenue compiles local certifications and releases final statewide levy data. How to Use the Hearing Public budget meetings give taxpayers a direct forum to review spending decisions before local elected officials take a final binding vote. Under Minn. Stat. 275.065 subd. 3(c) , mandatory public hearings must be held by counties, cities with a population over 500, school districts, regional library authorities, metropolitan taxing districts and certain fire protection and emergency medical services special taxing districts. According to the Department of Revenue Truth-in-Taxation guidelines , these public meetings must take place between November 25 and December 28, 2026. The statute specifies that meetings must be scheduled after November 24 and must not begin before 6:00 p.m. To find the exact date, time and room location for your city council, county board or school district, check the meeting details printed directly on your parcel notice. At these meetings, the taxing authority discusses its budget and levy, and members of the public must be allowed to speak. If you plan to attend, prepare concise remarks focused on specific budget line items or tax percentage changes shown on your notice. Bring your mailed document to verify the proposed dollar increase for that jurisdiction. If you are unable to attend the scheduled public hearing, use the website, phone number or mailing address printed on the form to submit written comments to elected board members before the final levy certification on December 28. What the Hearing Can and Cannot Change Attending a Truth-in-Taxation meeting allows you to advocate for lower spending before final budgets are locked in, but the meeting has distinct legal limits. Local elected officials can choose to adopt a final levy that is lower than the preliminary levy based on public input. Under Minn. Stat. 275.065 subd. 6(a) , the final levy certified by a city, county, school district, regional library district or metropolitan special taxing district cannot exceed its proposed levy, with limited statutory exceptions: A school district levy where voters approved an operating or capital referendum after the preliminary levy was certified. A city or county levy approved by voters in a referendum after preliminary certification. A levy for principal and interest on voter-approved bonds. State-approved funding needs for natural disaster repairs, if the commissioner of revenue approves. Final tort judgments rendered against the municipality that became final after preliminary levies were set, if the commissioner of revenue approves. Changes to statutory levy limits certified after the initial proposed levy deadline. Subdivision 6(a) also allows amounts for emergency debt certificates and state unallotment. Subdivision 6(b) says the cap does not apply to towns or to special taxing districts other than regional library and metropolitan districts. Under subdivisions 3(c) and 3(d), a town or the state general tax is final at its proposed amount, and a town can change its levy at a special town meeting under section 365.52. The public hearing cannot change your property's assessed value or tax classification. Assessor records as of November 1 establish the market value listed on your notice. If you believe your property is incorrectly classified as nonhomestead when it is your primary residence, Minn. Stat. 275.065 subd. 3(g) provides relief: if you deliver satisfactory documentation to the county assessor by the applicable deadline. The statute does not give the date, so ask your county assessor what it is. The documentation must prove the home qualified for homestead status in that assessment year. If it does, the assessor must reclassify the property as homestead for taxes payable in 2027. Additionally, under subdivision 3(e), the law mandates that your notice explicitly state that proposed and final taxes do not include special assessments, natural disaster cleanup costs, final tort judgments, contamination taxes or voter-approved referenda passed after certified proposals. Because these exclusions exist, never treat the total proposed figure as a ceiling on your final tax bill. Your ultimate property tax bill could be more or less than the notice indicates. Our Takeaway For Minnesota homeowners with a proposed-tax notice, remember that this document is a preliminary estimate rather than a bill. Each taxing authority's final levy is generally capped at its proposed levy, which the Minnesota Department of Revenue describes as lower but not higher than preliminary levies. Your final bill can still differ. Subdivision 3(e) exclusions such as special assessments and post-certification referenda move it, and so do separate valuation and classification decisions. Compare the current and proposed numbers for each taxing district, identify which entity is driving the change and attend that authority's public hearing before final votes are cast. Then attend your local budget hearing before December 28 to give your input on spending. Frequently asked questions Is a Truth-in-Taxation notice a bill? No, a Truth-in-Taxation notice is not a bill, and you do not pay the amount listed. It is a parcel-specific proposal showing what your property taxes would be if local taxing authorities adopt their preliminary budgets. Can my property tax go up after the notice? Yes, your final tax bill could be more or less than the proposed amount. While local authorities generally cannot raise their final levies above preliminary numbers, final tax bills can rise due to items excluded from the notice, including special assessments, later voter-approved referenda, disaster cleanup levies and final tort judgments. When are final levies set? According to the Minnesota Department of Revenue, local governments must set final 2027 property tax levies by December 28, 2026. The Minnesota Department of Revenue compiles those local certifications and publishes final property tax levy information in February 2027. Which governments must hold a hearing? Counties, school districts, regional library authorities, metropolitan taxing districts, certain fire protection and emergency medical services special taxing districts and cities with populations over 500 must hold a public hearing between November 25 and December 28, 2026. What does the notice leave out? Under Minnesota law, the notice excludes special assessments, voter-approved referenda passed after proposed taxes are certified, post-certification disaster cleanup costs, post-certification tort judgments and contamination taxes on remediated property. What if my notice lists my home as nonhomestead? If your primary residence is listed as nonhomestead, contact your county assessor immediately. Under Minnesota Statutes 275.065 subdivision 3(g), providing satisfactory documentation to the county assessor by the applicable deadline (the statute gives no date, so ask your county assessor) requires the assessor to reclassify qualifying property as homestead for the upcoming tax year.
