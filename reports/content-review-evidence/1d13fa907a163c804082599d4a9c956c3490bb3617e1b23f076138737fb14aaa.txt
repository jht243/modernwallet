To withhold federal tax from your Social Security benefit, estimate your tax on the payments and choose the Form W-4V rate that covers it without falling short. Internal Revenue Service (IRS) Form W-4V allows only 7%, 10%, 12%, or 22% of each monthly payment, with no other percentage or dollar amount permitted. You submit the form directly to the Social Security Administration (SSA), not the IRS, and the choice stays in effect until you change or stop it. In our internal calculation, we ran a single retiree age 65 or older with a $2,400 monthly benefit ($28,800 a year) and $30,000 of other income to see which W-4V rate covers the tax. Provisional income equals $44,400, which makes $13,340 of the benefits taxable under IRS rules. After a standard deduction of $18,150, the estimated federal tax is $2,775. Withholding 7% takes $2,016 yearly, falling short by $759. Withholding 10% takes $2,880, covering the bill with a $105 buffer, making 10% the closest match. The senior deduction of up to $6,000 would lower that tax to about $2,055, and 10% would still be the closest rate that does not fall short. Under the Form W-4V instructions , you can only select 7%, 10%, 12%, or 22% of each payment, but no other percentage or amount. The signed form must be given directly to the payer, so you deliver it to Social Security rather than sending it to the IRS. You can also handle requests through the SSA online withholding request page or by calling 1-800-772-1213. The choice remains in place until you change the rate or stop it by filing a new form with line 7 checked. First figure how much of your Social Security is taxable to gauge your exposure. The calculator models federal tax using rules from IRS Publication 915 Worksheet 1. It adds other taxable income to half your benefits to determine provisional income. If provisional income tops $25,000 for single filers or $32,000 for married couples filing jointly, up to 50% becomes taxable. Above $34,000 single or $44,000 joint, up to 85% is taxable. Married people filing separately who lived with a spouse have a $0 base amount. The tool then applies the standard deduction and tax brackets from IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 . Calculations use 2026 tax rules, omitting state taxes, credits, and the senior deduction for 2025 through 2028. Comparing withholding rates across different income levels reveals whether a standard percentage fits your liability. A married couple filing jointly, both 65 or older, with a $3,800 monthly benefit and $50,000 of other income owes an estimated $4,902 in tax. At 10%, withholding takes $4,560, leaving them short by $342. Choosing 12% withholds $5,472, which covers the bill with $570 over. This example leaves out the senior deduction of up to $6,000 per person for 2025 through 2028, which phases out above $75,000 of modified adjusted gross income ($150,000 on a joint return) according to the IRS . Taking $12,000 of senior deductions would cut this couple's estimated tax to about $3,462, and 10% would then cover it with $1,098 over, so a lower rate may fit you. Frequently asked questions How is Social Security withholding calculated? The calculator first determines your taxable benefits using IRS Publication 915 rules. It combines taxable benefits with other income, subtracts the standard deduction, and calculates ordinary income tax using 2026 brackets. It then compares the 7%, 10%, 12%, and 22% rates against that tax to identify the closest match. How do I submit Form W-4V to Social Security? Give your completed, signed Form W-4V directly to the Social Security Administration, not the IRS. You can submit requests through the SSA online withholding request page or submit Form W-4V to the Social Security Administration. You can also call the Social Security Administration at 1-800-772-1213. Can I complete Form W-4V online for Social Security? The IRS Form W-4V instructions state that beneficiaries can request withholding changes online with Social Security. You can manage your request directly at the SSA online withholding request page. The choice stays active until you formally submit a change. Can I choose another percentage than 7%, 10%, 12% or 22%? No, IRS rules prohibit custom amounts or alternate percentages on Form W-4V. You must select exactly 7%, 10%, 12%, or 22% of each payment, but no other percentage or amount. How do I stop withholding from my Social Security benefits? To stop withholding, complete a new Form W-4V and check the box on line 7. Sign the form and give it to the Social Security Administration. Does withholding count as tax paid, or do I still owe at filing? Federal withholding from your benefits counts directly toward your total tax payments for the calendar year. When you file your annual return, your total tax liability is matched against all withholding. Any difference is either refunded to you or settled as an additional balance owed. Is my Social Security taxed if I have no other income? Benefits are not taxed when provisional income remains below the federal base threshold. For example, a single filer age 65 or older receiving $1,500 monthly with no other income has $0 in estimated tax. In this situation, none of the benefits are taxable and no Form W-4V request is needed. Do I need estimated tax payments instead of Form W-4V? Form W-4V instructions note that filers with other income not subject to withholding should consider making estimated tax payments using Form 1040-ES.
