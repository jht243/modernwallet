answer: "Form 8832, Entity Classification Election, is the form an eligible business entity — most commonly an LLC — files with the IRS to choose how it is taxed at the federal level: as a corporation, a partnership, or a disregarded entity. 1. Form 8832, "Entity Classification Election," is filed with the IRS by an "eligible entity" to elect how it is classified for federal tax purposes: as a corporation (an "association taxable as a corporation"), as a partnership, or — for a single-owner entity — as a disregarded entity.
2. These rules come from the "check-the-box" Treasury regulations (in effect since 1997), which let most unincorporated business entities choose their own federal tax classification instead of being locked into a classification based on their entity type.
3. An "eligible entity" is generally an unincorporated business entity — an LLC is the most common real-world example. A business entity that is formed as a corporation under a state's corporation statute is NOT an "eligible entity" under these rules and cannot use Form 8832 to elect out of corporate tax treatment; it is automatically classified as a corporation.
4. Default classification without ever filing Form 8832: a domestic eligible entity with a single owner defaults to disregarded-entity status (a single-member LLC's income flows to its owner's own return); a domestic eligible entity with two or more owners defaults to partnership status (a multi-member LLC). Filing Form 8832 to elect corporate/association status overrides this default.
5. Filing Form 8832 to elect C-corporation tax treatment means the entity starts filing its own corporate return, Form 1120, and paying the flat 21% federal corporate income tax rate set by the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act — instead of passing income through to the owners' personal returns.
6. Form 8832 is not the same as Form 2553. Form 2553 is the separate form used to elect S-corporation tax treatment, which carries its own eligibility rules (S corps restrict who can be a shareholder — generally individuals, certain trusts, and estates — and cap the number of shareholders). A business files Form 8832 for a straight C-corp (or partnership/disregarded-entity) election, and files Form 2553 instead if it wants the S-corp pass-through regime.
7. Effective-date window: an election made on Form 8832 can take effect no more than 75 days before the date the form is filed, and no more than 12 months after the date it is filed. An election cannot be backdated further than that 75-day window.
8. The 60-month rule: once an eligible entity's classification election takes effect, the entity is generally not permitted to make another election to change its classification again for 60 months (five years) from the effective date of the first election, subject to certain exceptions described in the form's own instructions (for example, a greater-than-50%-ownership-change exception).
9. Beyond the LLC-to-C-corp scenario, Form 8832 is also used by: a single-member LLC that wants to elect corporate tax treatment instead of its disregarded-entity default; certain foreign eligible entities choosing their U.S. federal tax classification; and any eligible entity reversing or changing a prior classification election (subject to the 60-month rule).
10. Filing Form 8832 does not, by itself, change an entity's legal form under state law and does not create shares of stock — an LLC that elects C-corp taxation is still legally an LLC, governed by its operating agreement and membership interests, not corporate bylaws and stock certificates. Actually becoming a corporation as a legal matter requires a separate state-law conversion process.
