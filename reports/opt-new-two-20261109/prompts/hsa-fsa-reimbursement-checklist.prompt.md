# Inspected Primary Facts and Closed Boundaries
Checked November 9, 2026. All three IRS primary sources fetched200 and retained in sources.json. Publications969/502 are the currently published2025 editions. Use qualitative account process, no prior-year limits. Notice2026-5 supplies2026 DPC guidance.

F01 IRS969: A health savings account (HSA) can pay or reimburse qualified medical expenses incurred after establishment. State law determines establishment. Rollover accounts may inherit earlier establishment. Ask custodian for actual establishment records when needed, without inventing a universal funding-date rule.
F02 IRS969: Qualified medical expenses generally mean medical care for beneficiary, spouse and qualifying dependents, uncompensated by insurance or otherwise. Avoid dependent eligibility detail and thresholds.
F03 IRS969: HSA distributions must be supported by records showing exclusive qualified medical expenses, no previous payment/reimbursement from another source, and no itemized deduction in any year.
F04 IRS969: Keep those records with tax records rather than send them with the tax return. No universal retention period or receipt-upload requirement established here.
F05 IRS969: HSA distributions are reported on Form8889. Reporting a distribution is different from mailing all receipts. No form-line or filing advice needed.
F06 IRS969: Health flexible spending arrangement/account (FSA) distributions generally reimburse qualified medical expenses incurred during coverage.
F07 IRS969: FSA requires written independent third-party statement showing expense incurred and amount, plus statement expense not paid/reimbursed by another health plan.
F08 IRS969: FSA cannot advance reimburse future/projected expenses. Keep this prohibition expressly FSA-only.
F09 IRS969: Some FSA debit-card transactions meeting substantiation methods do not need additional information. Paying by card does not universally establish all substantiation complete. Avoid claiming every swipe always requires a new receipt upload.
F10 IRS969: FSA qualified medical expenses are specified in plan and generally qualify for medical/dental deduction. Account-specific exceptions matter.
F11 IRS969: FSA does not reimburse expenses covered under another health plan. Tax-free reimbursed amounts also cannot be itemized on ScheduleA.
F12 IRS969: FSA grace/carryover availability depends on plan. Do not quote amounts or assume every employer offers either. Request actual service/coverage dates and submission deadline from plan administrator. Distinguish when service occurs from when paperwork is filed.
F13 IRS502: Medical expenses include diagnosis/cure/mitigation/treatment/prevention of disease or affecting body structure/function, including legal medical services and needed equipment/supplies/diagnosticdevices.
F14 IRS502: Merely general-health-beneficial spending is excluded. A medical-looking item, prescription or provider marketing does not by itself decide account eligibility. No clinical recommendation or individual treatment eligibility determination.
F15 IRS502 expressly says its paid-this-year deduction timing is not the FSA reimbursement rule. Do not transplant ScheduleA deduction thresholds/timing to either account.
F16 Recommendation, not a statutory universal receipt template: ask for itemized provider receipt/bill with provider name, patient name, service/item description, service/purchase date and amount. These fields connect the expense to qualified care and coverage. Ask actual administrator what documentation it accepts, rather than guarantee every plan requires identical fields.
F17 Recommendation: keep proof of payment and, when insurance is involved, an explanation of benefits (EOB). The EOB can show what insurance paid and patient responsibility. Do not label EOB alone as proof of payment or guarantee all administrators accept a card statement alone.
F18 Recommendation: compare eligible unreimbursed amount with actual supporting record. Mark which expense and amount has already been reimbursed so the same cost is not claimed again through HSA/FSA/insurance or deducted. No invented dollar example necessary.
F19 Recommendation: save claim confirmation, submitted documents, administrator response and actual reimbursement record. Acknowledgment of submission does not guarantee approval or tax-free status.
F20 Recommendation: for a missing or combined invoice, request itemization from actual provider. No promise that Opt or any provider supplies itemized invoices, receipts, clinical allocation, codes or letters on request.
F21 Recommendation: for a service membership containing different benefits, request the agreement, description of included services, coverage period and itemized charges. Documentation helps apply the actual rules but cannot convert a nonqualified fee into qualified care.
F22 A separately billed medical service still has to meet applicable qualification and timing rules. No automatic deductible clinical portion, all-prescribed-labs eligibility, or universal letter-of-medical-necessity process.
F23 IRSNotice2026-5Q20: For HSA reimbursement, qualifying direct primary care service arrangement (DPCSA) provides solely primary-care medical services by qualifying primary-care practitioners for sole compensation fixed periodic fee. Excludes procedures requiring general anesthesia, prescription drugs except vaccines, and labs not typically administered in ambulatory primary-care setting. Link the exact notice and keep conditions explicit. Do not determine that Opt or any membership qualifies.
F24 NoticeQ20: No specific fee cap for HSA reimbursement under this rule. Contribution eligibility is separate. Avoid capnumbers and broad contribution advice entirely.
F25 NoticeQ19: HSA may treat qualifying DPCSA expense incurred firstday eachmonth prorata, firstday coverageperiod, or fee paymentdate. Generally permits substantiated fee reimbursement before coverage begins, subject qualifyingarrangement. This defeats a universal HSA future-expense ban. Mention conditional exception in timing and membership answer with source; do not generalize to all memberships or FSA.
F26 NoticeQ18: Employer-paid DPCSA fees, including salary reduction through section125plan, are not HSA beneficiary expenses for HSA reimbursement. Prevent doubleclaim, no broad employer plan advice.
