# Closed fact list — merchant-cash-advance-vs-revenue-based-financing FAQ addition (worked cost-range example)

Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, benchmark, or URL;
say it is unpublished and tell the reader to verify with the provider.

- The page already states, for a $50,000 MCA at a 1.30 factor rate: total owed is $65,000,
  a $15,000 fee. (Already on the page — reuse this exact figure, do not restate as new.)
- MCA factor rates are commonly cited in a range of about 1.10 to 1.50. (Already on the page.)
- RBF repayment caps are commonly cited in a range of about 1.3x to 3x the amount funded.
  (Already on the page.)
- Worked low-end example (illustrative only, both use the same $50,000 principal so the two
  products are directly comparable):
  - $50,000 MCA at the low end of its range, a 1.10 factor rate: total owed $55,000, a $5,000 fee.
  - $50,000 RBF at the low end of its range, a 1.3x repayment cap: total owed $65,000, a $15,000 fee.
- Worked high-end example (same $50,000 principal):
  - $50,000 MCA at the high end of its range, a 1.50 factor rate: total owed $75,000, a $25,000 fee.
  - $50,000 RBF at the high end of its range, a 3x repayment cap: total owed $150,000, a $100,000 fee.
- The point these numbers illustrate: at the cheapest end of each product's typical range, MCA
  and RBF land close together on the same $50,000 principal ($55,000 vs $65,000). At the
  expensive end of each range, they diverge sharply — a 3x RBF cap totals roughly double a 1.50
  factor-rate MCA on the same principal ($150,000 vs $75,000). These are illustrative range
  endpoints, not a quote — the only way to know the real cost is the provider's exact factor
  rate or repayment cap for a specific deal.
- Do not state or imply that RBF is always more expensive than an MCA — the ranges overlap at
  the low end, per the existing FAQ "Is a merchant cash advance or revenue-based financing
  cheaper?" on this page. Do not contradict that FAQ.
