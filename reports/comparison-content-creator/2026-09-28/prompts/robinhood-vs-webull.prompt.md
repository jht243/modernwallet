## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- Robinhood and Webull are both commission-free U.S. brokerage apps offering trading in stocks, ETFs, and options; both also offer cryptocurrency trading and fractional shares.
- Robinhood was among the first mainstream apps to popularize commission-free stock trading with a simplified, mobile-first interface aimed at beginner and casual investors.
- Webull markets itself toward more active, technically oriented traders, with more built-in charting tools, technical indicators, and a paper-trading (simulated) mode for practicing strategies without real money.
- Robinhood offers a paid subscription tier called Robinhood Gold, which includes access to margin investing and a higher interest rate on uninvested brokerage cash than the standard account; do not state the current Robinhood Gold price or interest rate — direct the reader to Robinhood's own pricing page.
- Webull's cash management program pays a tiered interest rate on uninvested cash that depends on account balance and whether the customer subscribes to Webull's premium membership tier; do not state a specific rate — direct the reader to Webull's own page for current tiers.
- Both Robinhood and Webull sweep uninvested customer cash into FDIC-insured partner banks (a bank-sweep program), rather than a money-market mutual fund; this differs from brokers like Fidelity and Vanguard, which default to a money-market fund sweep covered by SIPC instead of FDIC.
- Like most commission-free brokerages, Robinhood and Webull generate a portion of their revenue from payment for order flow (PFOF) — routing customer stock and options orders to market makers who pay the broker for that order flow — a practice each company discloses in its own regulatory filings and order-routing disclosures; do not state a specific dollar amount either company earns from PFOF.
- Robinhood brokerage accounts, like Webull brokerage accounts, are members of the Securities Investor Protection Corporation (SIPC), which protects customer securities and cash up to SIPC's standard coverage limits if the brokerage fails — this is not the same as FDIC insurance and does not protect against a decline in investment value.
