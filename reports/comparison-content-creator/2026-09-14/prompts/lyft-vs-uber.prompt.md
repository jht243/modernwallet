## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- Uber launched in 2009 (originally UberCab, San Francisco); Lyft launched in 2012 (originally Zimride's rideshare spinoff).
- Uber operates in many countries across multiple continents; Lyft operates almost entirely in the United States and Canada.
- Uber's business spans ride-hailing plus Uber Eats (food delivery) and Uber Freight; Lyft is a ride-hailing and bikeshare/scooter company only — it does not operate a food-delivery arm.
- Both companies are publicly traded (Uber: NYSE, ticker UBER; Lyft: Nasdaq, ticker LYFT), both having IPO'd in 2019.
- Both classify their drivers as independent contractors, not employees, in most U.S. states — meaning both issue Form 1099 tax documents (1099-NEC and/or 1099-K depending on earnings) rather than a W-2, and neither withholds income or payroll tax from a driver's pay.
- Both apps use dynamic/surge-style pricing that raises fares during high demand — Uber calls its version "surge pricing," Lyft calls its version "Prime Time."
- Both platforms let riders tip in-app after a trip, and both let drivers see rider ratings before deciding whether to accept (subject to state law in a few jurisdictions).
