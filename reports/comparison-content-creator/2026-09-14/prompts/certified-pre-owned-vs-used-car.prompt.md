## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- A certified pre-owned (CPO) vehicle is a used car that has passed a multi-point inspection process defined by either the manufacturer (a "manufacturer-certified" program, backed by the automaker) or, in some cases, the dealership itself (a "dealer-certified" program, which typically carries less standardized requirements than a manufacturer program) and comes with an extended warranty beyond the vehicle's remaining factory coverage.
- A standard used car sold "as-is" or with only a limited dealer warranty has not gone through this formal inspection/certification process, and generally carries no extended warranty beyond whatever original factory coverage (if any) remains.
- CPO vehicles typically must meet age and mileage limits set by the manufacturer's program (varies by manufacturer) and typically must have a clean title with no history of major accident damage — a car with a branded/salvage title is generally not eligible for manufacturer CPO certification.
- CPO vehicles cost more than a comparable non-certified used vehicle of the same year, mileage, and condition — the certification premium pays for the inspection process and the extended warranty coverage, not for a materially different car.
- Manufacturer-backed CPO programs often also include additional perks such as a special CPO financing rate offer, a roadside assistance period, or a limited satisfaction/return period — the specific perks vary significantly by manufacturer and should not be assumed to apply universally.
- A standard used car (non-CPO) can still be a completely sound vehicle — the absence of certification doesn't mean the car has a problem, it just means the buyer (or an independent mechanic they hire) takes on the inspection work themselves instead of relying on the dealer's certification process.
- Do NOT state a specific dollar premium for CPO pricing, a specific mileage/age cutoff, or a specific warranty length — these vary by manufacturer and change over time.

## What to cover, section by section

1. **Intro** — direct answer: a certified pre-owned car costs more than an equivalent standard used car specifically because it has passed a formal inspection process and comes with an extended warranty, while a standard used car asks the buyer to do (or pay a mechanic to do) that inspection work themselves — the CPO premium is worth paying when the buyer values warranty certainty and inspection assurance more than saving the difference in price.
2. **What "certified" actually requires** — the manufacturer-backed multi-point inspection process, clean-title/no-major-accident requirement, and manufacturer-set age/mileage eligibility limits; note the meaningful difference between a manufacturer-certified program and a less-standardized dealer-only certification, and that a buyer should ask specifically which type is being offered.
3. **What the buyer actually gets for the premium** — the extended warranty beyond remaining factory coverage, and any additional perks like special CPO financing offers or roadside assistance, while being clear these perks vary by manufacturer and shouldn't be assumed.
4. **Comparison table** — at least 4-5 dimensions: inspection process (formal multi-point vs none required), warranty (extended vs none/remaining-factory-only), typical price (higher vs lower for equivalent car), eligibility requirements (age/mileage/clean-title limits vs none), typical extra perks (CPO financing/roadside assistance vs none).
5. **When the standard used car is the smarter buy instead** — a buyer who is comfortable paying for their own independent pre-purchase inspection, or who is buying a vehicle old enough that most manufacturer CPO programs wouldn't cover it anyway, may get equivalent peace of mind for less money by paying a trusted independent mechanic directly.
6. **Verdict** — recommend CPO for a buyer who wants warranty certainty and doesn't want to manage an independent inspection themselves, and who is comparing vehicles young/low-mileage enough to actually qualify for a manufacturer CPO program; recommend a standard used car (paired with the buyer's own independent inspection) for a buyer prioritizing the lowest total price who is willing to do that extra diligence step themselves. State who this doesn't serve well (a buyer shopping for an older vehicle outside any manufacturer's CPO age/mileage window, where CPO simply isn't available) and what would change the answer (a manufacturer CPO financing promotion that closes most of the price gap).
7. **Closing** — a concrete next action (before paying a CPO premium, get the specific program's inspection checklist and warranty terms in writing, and compare that price against a standard used listing plus the cost of an independent pre-purchase inspection).
