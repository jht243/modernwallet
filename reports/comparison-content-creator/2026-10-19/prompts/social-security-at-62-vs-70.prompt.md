## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- The earliest age you can start retirement benefits is 62 (SSA).
- For a person born in 1960 or later, full retirement age is 67 (SSA).
- Social Security benefits are reduced by 30% for a person who retires at 62 whose full retirement age is 67, i.e. born in 1960 or later (SSA). The reduction is smaller for people with an earlier full retirement age; for example it is 25% at 62 for a full retirement age of 66 (born 1943-1954).
- SSA adds 8% to your benefit for each full year you delay receiving benefits past full retirement age, up to age 70 (SSA). For people born after 1943 the credit for each month of delay is 2/3 of 1%.
- For someone born in 1960 or later, delaying to age 70 means 36 months of credits and a monthly benefit of 124% of the full-retirement-age amount (SSA).
- Once you reach age 70 the monthly benefit stops increasing (SSA), so there is no benefit to waiting past 70.
- Both the reduction for claiming early and the credit for waiting are permanent changes to the monthly amount.
