## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- Every extra dollar of principal you pay saves the interest that dollar would have accrued at your loan's rate for the rest of the loan, so the return from extra payments equals your mortgage rate and is not subject to market swings. Present as mechanism.
- Investment returns are not guaranteed and can be lower than your mortgage rate; do not state any expected stock return or historical average.
- You can deduct home mortgage interest only if you itemize deductions on Schedule A (IRS Publication 936). For debt secured after December 15, 2017, the deduction limit applies to up to $750,000 of acquisition debt ($375,000 if married filing separately); older mortgages fall under a higher $1 million limit (IRS).

- Money sent as extra payments is hard to get back out; a home is not a liquid asset, while investments in a taxable account can generally be sold. Present as mechanism.
