## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- A pension annuity pays a regular amount, usually monthly, from the plan for as long as the plan's terms say, often for life; a lump sum is a single payment of the benefit's value that you take instead of those payments.
- Mandatory income tax withholding of 20% applies to most taxable distributions paid to you directly in a lump sum, even if you plan to roll the money over within 60 days (IRS).
- You can defer tax by rolling over the taxable amount to an IRA within 60 days after you receive the distribution (IRS).
- You can ask the payer to directly roll over the taxable portion into an IRA or eligible plan, so the tax is deferred and the money does not pass through your hands (IRS).
- The IRS describes a 10% additional tax on certain early distributions from qualified retirement plans; it is 10% of the taxable part, and "early" generally means before age 59½ (IRS). Exceptions exist, including separation from service after age 55, total and permanent disability, and distributions after death; the reader should check the IRS page.
- Monthly pension payments are generally taxed as ordinary income when received (IRS Topic 410). Present this as general and tell the reader to confirm with the plan administrator or a tax professional.
- With a lump sum you take on the investment risk and the risk of outliving the money; with an annuity the plan or insurer carries those risks, subject to the plan's ability to pay. Present as mechanism, not guarantee.
