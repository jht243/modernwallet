## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- A credit union is a not-for-profit financial cooperative owned by its members; a bank is a for-profit company owned by shareholders.
- Credit union deposits are insured by the National Credit Union Administration (NCUA) through the National Credit Union Share Insurance Fund, up to $250,000 per member, per insured credit union, per ownership category.
- Bank deposits are insured by the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC), up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category.
- Both protections are backed by the full faith and credit of the US government. Check that a specific institution is actually insured, using the FDIC's BankFind or the NCUA's credit union locator.
- Credit unions require membership eligibility, commonly based on employer, place of residence, school, military or other association, or a small donation to a partner association; banks generally open accounts for anyone.
- Federal credit unions are subject to a statutory cap on loan interest rates (18% APR for most loans); banks have no federal rate cap but follow state law and regulation.
- On average credit unions tend to charge lower fees and lower loan rates and pay higher savings rates than large banks, but this varies a lot by institution and by product. Do not state specific rates or percentages; tell the reader to compare current rates directly.
- Large banks usually have more branches, ATMs, and more polished apps. Many credit unions offset small footprints through shared branching and the CO-OP ATM network. Say only that such networks exist and that coverage varies.
- Contested/unknown: do not state specific APYs, fees, branch counts, or number of credit unions or banks.
