## Donor-Advised Fund vs. QCD for Charitable Giving

A donor-advised fund (DAF) and a qualified charitable distribution (QCD) solve two separate financial planning goals. A DAF provides an upfront itemized deduction when you deposit cash or appreciated assets, letting you invest the balance and grant to charities later. In contrast, a QCD sends funds directly from a traditional individual retirement account (IRA) to an operating charity, which excludes the money from your taxable income entirely.

Because a QCD excludes up to $111,000 per donor in 2026 from gross income, it directly satisfies an IRA required minimum distribution (RMD) without requiring you to itemize deductions. However, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) strictly limits QCD recipients to operating public charities. You cannot direct a QCD into a DAF, private foundation, or supporting organization, and a DAF deposit will never satisfy an RMD.

| Feature | Donor-Advised Fund (DAF) | Qualified Charitable Distribution (QCD) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum donor age | Any age | Age 70½ or older |
| Eligible funding source | Cash, appreciated stock, and non-retirement assets | Traditional IRA balances only |
| Tax mechanism | Itemized tax deduction up to 60% of AGI for cash | Complete income exclusion up to $111,000 in 2026 |
| Satisfies required minimum distribution (RMD) | No | Yes |
| Eligible recipient organizations | 501(c)(3) public charities and qualifying institutions | Operating public charities only (excludes DAFs and private foundations) |

Donors under age 70½ or those donating appreciated stock rather than retirement funds should use a DAF to maximize their deductions and build a long-term giving reserve. IRA owners age 70½ or older who want to reduce taxable income and support an active charity immediately should choose a QCD instead.
