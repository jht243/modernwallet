## Target-Date Fund vs Index Fund: Which Should You Pick

A target-date fund (TDF) automatically shifts from stocks to bonds as you near retirement, whereas an individual index fund tracks a single market benchmark and leaves asset allocation to you. The two strategies are not mutually exclusive. Many target-date funds hold several underlying index funds, combining domestic equities, international equities, and bonds into a single balanced product.

Building an equivalent portfolio out of standalone index funds usually lowers your aggregate fund fees, but demands ongoing maintenance. You must choose your initial asset split, reduce stock exposure as you age, and manually rebalance your accounts. To weigh the tradeoff, test how fee differences compound over time with our [401(k) calculator](/retirement/401k-calculator/), or review fund structures in our guides to the [best index funds](/roundup/best-index-funds/) and [index fund vs ETF](/compare/index-fund-vs-etf/) investing.

| Dimension | Target-Date Fund | Index Fund Portfolio |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio structure | Single fund holding a diversified mix of stocks and bonds | Multiple standalone funds tracking distinct market benchmarks |
| Allocation management | Shifts automatically along a glide path toward retirement | Fixed to each benchmark, requiring you to set the overall split |
| Ongoing rebalancing | Handled automatically within the fund | Managed manually by the investor on a regular schedule |
| Fee structure | Single expense ratio covering the entire underlying mix | Individual expense ratios that typically cost less in aggregate |
| Best fit | Investors seeking an all-in-one account with zero maintenance | Investors seeking maximum fee savings who will rebalance on schedule |

Pick a target-date fund if you prefer a single holding that manages and rebalances your risk profile without ongoing oversight. Pick standalone index funds if your priority is securing the lowest aggregate fee and you are willing to reset your stock and bond split manually once every year.
