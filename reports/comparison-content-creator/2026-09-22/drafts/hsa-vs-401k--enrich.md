## HSA vs 401(k): Where Extra Money Should Go After the Match

Extra savings beyond an employer 401(k) match should go into a health savings account first if you carry a qualifying high-deductible health plan. The employer match still comes first. Once you claim those matching dollars, an HSA shields money going in and coming out for qualified care, while a traditional 401(k) taxes every future withdrawal.

| Feature | Health Savings Account (HSA) | Traditional 401(k) |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront contribution tax | Deducted from taxable income in the contribution year | Deducted from taxable income through pre-tax payroll deferrals |
| Withdrawal taxes | Untaxed for qualified medical expenses | Taxed as ordinary income on all contributions and investment earnings |
| 2026 contribution limit | $4,400 for self-only coverage or $8,750 for family coverage | $24,500 employee deferral |
| Employer match availability | Rare from most employers | Common at many workplaces |
| Required minimum distributions | None | Mandatory once you reach the IRS's required withdrawal age |

The HSA generally comes next after claiming the match. It pairs an upfront tax deduction with untaxed withdrawals for qualified medical expenses, whereas a traditional 401(k) taxes all distributions as ordinary income in retirement. Savers who lack an eligible high-deductible health plan, or who have already reached the annual HSA contribution limit, should keep directing extra money into their traditional 401(k).
