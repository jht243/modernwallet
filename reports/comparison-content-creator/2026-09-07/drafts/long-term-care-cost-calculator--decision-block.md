In-Home Care vs. Assisted Living vs. Nursing Home

National care costs in 2026 range from $68,000 for assisted living to $132,000 for nursing home care. Based on [Genworth](https://www.genworth.com) data projected at 4.5% annual inflation, a private nursing home room averages about $361 a day. Assisted living runs about $5,668 a month, while a home health aide working 44 hours a week costs $76,000 a year at roughly $33 an hour.

Location and insurance rules alter what families pay out of pocket. State medians range from 0.71 times the national benchmark in Mississippi and Louisiana to 2.19 times in Alaska. Medicare excludes assisted living and long-term aides entirely, and covers skilled nursing facility care only up to 100 days after a qualifying hospital stay.

When we evaluate care options, daily physical need dictates the right setting. In-home aides work best when an elder wants to stay home and a family caregiver lives nearby to coordinate meals, logistics, and safety. Assisted living provides custodial help with bathing and dressing, whereas nursing homes deliver around-the-clock clinical care for severe medical conditions.

Default to assisted living for custodial care on a predictable budget. Choose home aides when family support keeps paid hours low, but pivot to a nursing home if skilled clinical needs emerge or if state [Medicaid](https://www.medicaid.gov) waitlists block home-care funding.
