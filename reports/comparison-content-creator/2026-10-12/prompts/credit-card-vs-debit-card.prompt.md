## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- A credit card lets you borrow from the card issuer and pay later; a debit card pays from money already in your checking account.
- Credit card purchases and on-time payments are reported to the credit bureaus by the issuer, which can help build a credit history if you manage the card well. Debit card use is generally not a credit-building activity. State this as general, and tell the reader to confirm with their issuer.
- If you lose a debit card or it is stolen and you notify your bank or credit union within two business days of discovering it, you cannot be held responsible for more than the amount of the unauthorized transactions or $50, whichever is less (CFPB).
- If you notify the bank or credit union after two business days, you could be responsible for up to $500 in unauthorized transactions (CFPB).
- If your statement shows an unauthorized withdrawal you should notify the bank within 60 days; if you wait longer you could also have to pay the full amount of any transactions that occurred after the 60-day period and before you notify the bank (CFPB).
- Once you notify the bank of an unauthorized transaction it generally has ten business days to investigate; it must correct an error within one business day after determining one occurred, and then has three business days to report its findings (CFPB).
- Debit card losses come out of your own account first, so a dispute can leave you short on cash while it is investigated; a credit card dispute is a disagreement over money the lender fronted. Present this as a mechanism, not a guarantee.
- Credit cards carry interest if the balance is not paid in full, and many offer rewards; debit cards carry no interest but can trigger overdraft or other bank fees depending on the account. Do not state any APR, reward rate, or fee amount.
- Contested/unknown: do not state the federal credit card unauthorized-charge liability cap as a number; say credit cards have their own federal dispute protections and tell the reader to check their card agreement. Do not state any APR, fee, limit, or reward percentage.
