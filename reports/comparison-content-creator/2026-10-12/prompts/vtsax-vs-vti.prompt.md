## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- VTSAX is Vanguard Total Stock Market Index Fund Admiral Shares, a mutual fund; VTI is Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF, an exchange-traded fund. Both are issued by Vanguard and invest in the same US total stock market portfolio.
- Both track the CRSP US Total Market Index, which covers large-, mid-, and small-cap US stocks. Both are 100% US equity funds with no bond or international holdings.
- A mutual fund such as VTSAX is bought and sold at the fund's net asset value, calculated once at the end of the trading day. An ETF such as VTI trades on an exchange during market hours at a live market price.
- VTSAX has historically required a minimum initial investment set by Vanguard. Do not state the dollar amount; tell the reader to confirm the current minimum on Vanguard's fund page. VTI has no fund minimum, only the price of a share or fraction a broker allows.
- Expense ratios are very low on both and very close to each other, with VTI at 0.03% at the time of writing and VTSAX slightly different. State only that both are low and tell the reader to confirm current expense ratios on Vanguard's fund pages; do not state VTSAX's figure.
- VTSAX supports automatic investments and exact-dollar purchases at Vanguard; VTI is bought through any broker that offers it and supports commission-free ETF trading at most major US brokerages, with fractional-share support varying by broker.
- In a taxable account, ETFs are generally considered more tax-efficient than mutual funds because of how they handle share creation and redemption, although Vanguard's mutual funds are also tax-efficient index funds. Present this as general and tell the reader to ask a tax professional.
- Inside an IRA or 401(k), the tax-efficiency difference does not matter because gains are not taxed year to year there.
- Moving between a mutual fund and an ETF inside the same company may require selling one and buying the other, which can trigger capital gains in a taxable account. Tell the reader to check with Vanguard and a tax professional before switching.
