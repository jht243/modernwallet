## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- A credit score is a prediction of your credit behavior, such as how likely you are to pay a loan back on time, based on information from your credit reports (CFPB).
- Companies use a mathematical formula, called a scoring model, to create your credit score from the information in your credit report (CFPB).
- You do not have just one credit score. Each score depends on the data used to calculate it, and it may differ depending on the scoring model (which may depend on the type of loan product the score will be used for), the source of the data, and even the day it was calculated (CFPB).
- Most credit scores range from 300 to 850 (CFPB). FICO and VantageScore are the two best-known scoring brands; both use the 300 to 850 range in their widely used versions.
- Factors typically taken into account by scoring models include bill-paying history, current unpaid debt, the number and type of loan accounts, how long accounts have been open, how much available credit you are using, new applications for credit, and whether you have had a debt sent to collections, a foreclosure, or a bankruptcy, and how long ago (CFPB).
- A higher score usually makes it easier to qualify for a loan and may result in a better interest rate or loan terms (CFPB).
- Both models compute from the same kind of credit report data, which comes from the credit bureaus, so the scores usually move in the same direction when your habits change, though the numbers will not match.
- Lenders choose which model and which version they use; many mortgage, auto, and card lenders use a FICO version, and some free monitoring services show VantageScore.
