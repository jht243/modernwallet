## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- Deductions reduce the amount of your taxable income. In general, individuals may take a standard deduction or itemize their deductions (IRS Topic 501).
- The standard deduction is a flat amount based on filing status: single; married filing separately; married filing jointly; head of household; or qualifying surviving spouse.
- Taxpayers aged 65 and older and blind taxpayers may get an additional deduction.
- The IRS adjusts the standard deduction annually for inflation. Do not state any dollar amount of the standard deduction; tell the reader to check the current figure on the IRS standard deduction page.
- You should itemize on Schedule A (Form 1040) if your total allowable itemized deductions are greater than your standard deduction, or if you must itemize because you cannot use the standard deduction.
- You cannot take the standard deduction if you are married filing separately and your spouse itemizes; if you file a return for less than 12 months because of a change in accounting period; if you were a nonresident alien or dual-status alien (with a spouse-election exception); or if you file as an estate, trust, common trust fund, or partnership.
- You may also want to itemize if your standard deduction is limited because another taxpayer claims you as a dependent.
- Itemized deductions, subject to dollar limitations, may include state and local income or sales taxes, real property taxes, personal property taxes, mortgage interest, disaster losses, gifts to charity, and certain gambling losses and medical expenses.
- You choose one method per return, not both; the reader should add up the itemized list and compare it to the standard amount.
