## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- VFIAX is Vanguard 500 Index Fund Admiral Shares, a mutual fund; VOO is Vanguard S&P 500 ETF, an exchange-traded fund. Both are issued by Vanguard and track the S&P 500 index, roughly 500 of the largest US companies.
- A mutual fund such as VFIAX is bought and sold at net asset value, calculated once at the end of the trading day. An ETF such as VOO trades on an exchange during market hours at a live market price.
- VFIAX has historically required a minimum initial investment set by Vanguard. Do not state the dollar amount; tell the reader to confirm the current minimum on Vanguard's fund page. VOO has no fund minimum, only the price of a share or fraction a broker allows.
- VOO lists a 0.03% expense ratio at the time of writing; VFIAX is very close and slightly different. State only that both are low and tell the reader to confirm current expense ratios on Vanguard's fund pages; do not state VFIAX's figure.
- VFIAX supports automatic investments and exact-dollar purchases at Vanguard; VOO is bought through any broker that offers it, and commission-free ETF trading is available at most major US brokerages with fractional-share support varying by broker.
- In a taxable account, ETFs are generally considered more tax-efficient than mutual funds; Vanguard's index mutual funds are also tax-efficient. Present this as general and tell the reader to ask a tax professional.
- Inside an IRA or 401(k), the tax-efficiency difference does not matter because gains are not taxed year to year there. Some 401(k) plans offer only mutual funds, which can settle the choice.
- Switching from one to the other in a taxable account can trigger capital gains; check with Vanguard and a tax professional first.
- The SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY) and iShares Core S&P 500 ETF (IVV) are other S&P 500 ETFs covered on this site.
