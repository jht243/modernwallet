CLOSED FACT LIST:
- Washington is a community-property state (already stated elsewhere on this page).
- Property that is community property, or held jointly with a spouse, typically passes
  automatically to the surviving spouse without going through probate.
- A will is still needed to: direct where any SEPARATE (non-community, non-joint) property
  goes, name a guardian for minor children, and name an executor to handle the estate — without
  a will, a court appoints an administrator and the state's default intestacy rules decide who
  gets what, which may not match what the couple would have chosen.
