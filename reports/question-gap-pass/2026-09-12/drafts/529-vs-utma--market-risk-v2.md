Yes, both accounts can lose money if the market drops, and the real worry is watching the balance fall right before tuition is due. Neither account type is insured by the [Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC)](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/) or the [National Credit Union Administration (NCUA)](https://www.ncua.gov/consumers/share-insurance-coverage), so there is no guarantee on either side. A 529 plan's age-based portfolio option automatically shifts money from stocks toward conservative holdings (bonds, cash-like investments) as the child gets closer to college age. This reduces market risk right before the money is needed, but it does not eliminate it. A UTMA account has no automatic glide path like that, so it stays invested however the custodian originally chose, which can mean it is still heavily in stocks right up until the child takes control of the account.
