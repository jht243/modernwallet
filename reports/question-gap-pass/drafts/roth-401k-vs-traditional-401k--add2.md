Roth 401(k) money comes out fully tax-free only as a qualified distribution, which generally means a 5-year participation period has passed and the withdrawal happens on or after age 59½, after death, or due to disability. The 5-year period starts on the first day of the taxable year of your first designated Roth contribution and ends after five consecutive taxable years. In a nonqualified withdrawal, only the contribution share is tax-free: in the [IRS example](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plans-faqs-on-designated-roth-accounts), $5,000 taken from an account holding $9,400 of contributions and $600 of earnings splits into $4,700 tax-free and $300 of taxable earnings.
