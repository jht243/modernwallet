## The Employer Match Goes in Pre-Tax by Default

Employer matching contributions go into the pre-tax (Traditional) side of a 401(k) by default, even if all of your own deferrals go to the Roth 401(k) option. A 100% Roth 401(k) saver can therefore build a Traditional balance from the match. Plan for the mixed tax treatment.

Since the SECURE 2.0 Act (2022), a plan may let you take your match as Roth. Section 604 makes this optional, so employers are not required to offer it. Only vested match money qualifies, and a Roth match is not excludable from your gross income.

To see whether your plan offers a Roth match, check the plan document. Ask HR or read that document. The IRS sets out the rule in [Notice 2024-2, Part L](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-24-02.pdf).
