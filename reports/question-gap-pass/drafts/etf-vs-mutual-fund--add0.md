An actively managed ETF is not automatically cheaper than an actively managed mutual fund, because each fund charges its own expense ratio and the ETF wrapper does not set that fee. Both must publish a standardized fee table in the prospectus, and the expense ratio at the bottom of each table is the number to compare ([Investor.gov explains how to read the fee table](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/mutual-fund-and-etf-fees-and-expenses-investor-bulletin)). Active ETFs keep the in-kind redemption feature that lets ETFs avoid capital gains distributions, but buying and selling them adds a bid-ask spread cost. The same cost-first rule from [what is a good expense ratio](/guides/what-is-a-good-expense-ratio/) applies to active funds in either wrapper.
