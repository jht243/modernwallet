A Coverdell ESA a child doesn't use for college can move tax-free to a family member under age 30, either by a rollover within 60 days or by a beneficiary change, or it can fund a 529 plan for the same child. Cashing out makes the earnings taxable and generally adds a 10% additional tax, with exceptions if the beneficiary has died or is disabled, and up to the amount of a tax-free scholarship. [IRS Publication 970](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970) describes the SECURE 2.0 Roth IRA rollover for [529 plans](/guides/529-leftover-money-options/) only, and its Coverdell rollover rules name only another Coverdell, so a Coverdell has no stated route into a Roth IRA.
