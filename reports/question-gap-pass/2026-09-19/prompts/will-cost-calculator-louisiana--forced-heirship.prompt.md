CLOSED FACT LIST (verified 2026-09-19 against Louisiana Civil Code — use ONLY these
figures/citations, do not invent any other Louisiana forced-heirship detail):
- Louisiana is the only U.S. state with forced heirship. Under La. Civ. Code arts. 1493-1503, a
  "forced heir" is a descendant who is 23 years old or younger at the parent's death, OR a
  descendant of any age who is permanently incapable of caring for themselves or administering
  their own estate because of mental incapacity or physical infirmity.
- A forced heir cannot be completely cut out of a will. Regardless of what the will says, that
  child is legally entitled to a "forced portion" of the estate.
- A parent may only disinherit a forced heir for one of the specific just causes listed in La.
  Civ. Code art. 1621, such as striking the parent or persistent criminal conduct. Mere
  estrangement, disagreement, or disapproval of the child's lifestyle is not a valid ground.
- A child who is 24 or older and not permanently incapacitated is NOT a forced heir and can be
  disinherited or given an unequal share like in any other state.
