CLOSED FACT LIST (verified 2026-09-19 against Md. Code, Estates & Trusts §3-102 — use ONLY
these figures/citations, do not invent any other Maryland intestacy dollar threshold):
- Dying without a will in Maryland means the estate passes under Maryland's intestate
  succession statute, Md. Code, Estates & Trusts §3-102, not to whomever the decedent verbally
  intended.
- If there is a surviving spouse and no surviving minor child and no other descendants outside
  the spouse's own line, the surviving spouse inherits the ENTIRE intestate estate.
- If there is a surviving minor child, the surviving spouse's share drops to ONE-HALF of the
  estate (the other half passes to the decedent's descendants).
- If there is no surviving minor child, but there are surviving descendants who are NOT also
  descendants of the surviving spouse (e.g., children from a prior relationship), the surviving
  spouse's share is the first $100,000 plus one-half of whatever remains.
- This is a simplified summary of the main spousal-share rules; Maryland's statute has
  additional branches (e.g., when parents survive with no descendants) not covered here — do
  not state figures for scenarios not listed above.
