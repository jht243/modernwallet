CLOSED FACT LIST (verified against Oregon Revised Statutes — use ONLY these citations):
- No. Under ORS 112.235, a valid Oregon will only needs to be in writing, signed by a testator
  who is 18 or older (or lawfully married), and witnessed by two competent individuals who see
  the testator sign or hear the testator acknowledge the will. No notary is required for the
  will itself to be valid.
- Notarization becomes relevant only for the OPTIONAL self-proving affidavit authorized under
  ORS 113.055. That affidavit, signed before a notary at the same time as the will, lets an
  Oregon probate court admit the will without tracking down the witnesses to testify later —
  it speeds up probate but is not required for the will to be legally valid.
- Oregon does not recognize holographic (handwritten, unwitnessed) wills, so skipping the two
  witnesses is not an option the way skipping the notary is.
- Do not restate the $1 million Oregon estate tax threshold in this answer — that is already
  covered elsewhere on the page; keep this answer focused on the notarization/witness question.
