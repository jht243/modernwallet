CLOSED FACT LIST (verified 2026-09-19 against RSMo §474.010 — use ONLY these figures/citations,
do not invent any other Missouri intestacy dollar threshold):
- Dying without a will in Missouri means the estate passes under Missouri's intestate
  succession statute, RSMo §474.010, not to whomever the decedent verbally intended.
- If the decedent left no surviving descendants (children, grandchildren, etc.), the surviving
  spouse inherits the ENTIRE intestate estate.
- If all of the decedent's surviving descendants are also descendants of the surviving spouse
  (i.e., no children from a prior relationship), the surviving spouse inherits the first
  $20,000 of the estate PLUS one-half of whatever balance remains; the descendants split the
  rest.
- If some of the decedent's surviving descendants are NOT descendants of the surviving spouse
  (children from a prior relationship), the surviving spouse's share drops to ONE-HALF of the
  estate only.
- Intestate succession only reaches probate property. Assets with a named beneficiary,
  payable-on-death or transfer-on-death designations, or joint ownership with survivorship pass
  outside the estate automatically and are not affected by these rules.
