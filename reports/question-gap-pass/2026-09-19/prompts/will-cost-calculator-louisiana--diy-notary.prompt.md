CLOSED FACT LIST (verified against Louisiana Civil Code — use ONLY these figures/citations):
- Louisiana Civil Code article 1577 requires a notarial testament to be in writing, dated, and
  signed by the testator at the end and on each other separate page, in the presence of a
  notary AND two competent witnesses, followed by a specific attestation clause signed by the
  witnesses and the notary. This notary-plus-witnesses requirement is unique to Louisiana.
- Because most $0 online will services (FreeWill, LegalZoom's free tier, etc.) only prompt for
  witness signatures and do not arrange a notary appointment, a will produced entirely through
  those tools will NOT be valid in Louisiana unless the person separately takes it to a notary
  and two witnesses for execution.
- Louisiana Civil Code article 1575 is the one path that does not require a notary: an
  olographic will is valid if the ENTIRE document is written, dated, and signed in the
  testator's own handwriting, with no witnesses or notary needed. It must still be entirely
  handwritten (not typed) to qualify.
