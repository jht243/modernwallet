CLOSED FACT LIST (verified 2026-09-19 against Oregon Revised Statutes — use ONLY these
citations, do not invent any other Oregon divorce/will detail):
- No. Under ORS 112.315, once a divorce or annulment becomes final, Oregon law automatically
  revokes every provision in an existing will that favors the former spouse, and any provision
  naming the former spouse as personal representative. The will is then read as though the
  former spouse died before the testator.
- Exception: if the will itself states a different intent (explicitly says the ex-spouse's
  provisions should survive a divorce), that stated intent controls instead of the automatic
  revocation.
- This revocation happens automatically by operation of law the moment the divorce is final —
  it does not require refiling, amending, or rewriting the will.
- Important gap: this automatic state-law revocation does NOT reach ERISA-governed retirement
  plan beneficiary designations or life-insurance beneficiary designations. Those must be
  updated separately and directly with the plan administrator or insurer after a divorce, or
  the ex-spouse can still receive that money regardless of what the will or ORS 112.315 says.
