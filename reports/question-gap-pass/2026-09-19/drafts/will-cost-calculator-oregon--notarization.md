No, Oregon does not require a will to be notarized to be legally valid. Under ORS 112.235, a valid will only needs to be in writing, signed by a testator who is 18 or older (or lawfully married), and witnessed by two competent individuals. Both witnesses must see the testator sign or hear the will acknowledged, since Oregon does not recognize unwitnessed handwritten wills. Notarization only matters if you add an optional self-proving affidavit under ORS 113.055. That affidavit, signed before a notary at the same time as the will, speeds up probate by letting the court admit the document without tracking down witnesses to testify later.
