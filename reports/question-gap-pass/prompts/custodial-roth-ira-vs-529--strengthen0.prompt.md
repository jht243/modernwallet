Section to rewrite (current), heading "The earned-income gate decides eligibility":
The single biggest difference is who can open each account. A custodial Roth IRA can only be funded if the child has earned income — wages from a job or self-employment such as babysitting or lawn care. The IRS caps the contribution at the child's earnings or the annual IRA limit ($7,000 in 2025), whichever is lower.

A newborn with no income cannot have a funded custodial Roth. A 529 plan has no such rule. A parent, grandparent, or anyone else can open and fund it from the day the child is born. That is why the 529 is the default for early college savings and the custodial Roth becomes an option later, once your child starts working.
