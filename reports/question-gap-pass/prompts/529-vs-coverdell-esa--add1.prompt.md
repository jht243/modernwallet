READER QUESTION: What happens to a Coverdell ESA if my child does not go to college, and can I roll it to a Roth IRA?
TASK: New FAQ answering the three PAA questions (cash out / child skips college / Roth IRA): leftover can go to a family member under 30 via rollover or beneficiary change, or into a 529 for the same beneficiary; cashing out pays income tax on earnings plus 10% except for death, disability or scholarship; the 529-to-Roth rule does not apply to a Coverdell (rolling a Coverdell into a 529 first is not described by the IRS as a Roth route). No Roth promise. Practical tone, short.


CLOSED FACT LIST (anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, benchmark, or URL; if unsure leave it out):
- Distributions not used for qualified education expenses are taxable (earnings) and generally carry a 10% additional tax on the amount included in income (source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970 (Coverdell ESA, "Additional Tax on Taxable Distributions"))
- 10% additional tax exceptions: distribution after the beneficiary's death, because the beneficiary is disabled, or to the extent of a tax-free scholarship/educational assistance (source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970 (Coverdell ESA, "Exceptions"))
- Not taxable if rolled over (within 60 days) to another Coverdell ESA for the same beneficiary or a family member under age 30; the beneficiary can also be changed to a family member under 30 with no tax (source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970 (Coverdell ESA, "Rollovers" and "Changing the Designated Beneficiary"))
- A contribution to a 529 (QTP) on behalf of the Coverdell beneficiary (or, after a beneficiary change, a family member) is a qualified education expense (source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970 (Coverdell ESA, "Contributions to a qualified tuition program (QTP)"))
- The SECURE 2.0 rollover to a Roth IRA is described for long-term 529 plans (QTPs) only, in the same publication; the Coverdell rollover rules name only another Coverdell (source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970 (QTP "Rollover to Roth IRA" reminder vs Coverdell "Rollovers"))
- Page text: funds generally must be used by age 30 or are distributed with tax and 10% penalty on earnings (source: this page, FAQ "Does a Coverdell have to be used by a certain age?")
