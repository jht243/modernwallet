Bridge Loan Calculator

By Jonathan Velez, Personal Finance Writer & Editor · Reviewed against primary sources · Updated November 14, 2026

A bridge loan costs its fees plus a month of interest for every month until your old home sells. This bridge loan calculator returns that total cost and the monthly outlay for carrying both homes. It also returns the net proceeds left after the sale pays off the old mortgage, the bridge loan and selling costs. Start with the expected sale price and old mortgage payoff. Then enter the bridge loan terms and the number of months you expect to carry both homes. The sale price, loan and months already in the calculator are made-up example inputs. Replace them with your own sale details and quote.

In our internal modeling, we ran one example sale through the calculator and found a $7,000 bridge loan cost after 6 months. The example uses a $400,000 sale price, a $200,000 old mortgage payoff, and a $100,000 bridge loan. At 10% annual percentage rate (APR) with 2% origination, the loan accrues $5,000 in interest and $2,000 in fees. Selling costs of 6% leave $69,000 in net proceeds. Enter your own terms below to see how a different sale date changes the result.

What a Bridge Loan Costs Month by Month

Bridge interest is simple interest: multiply the loan by its APR, divide by 12, then multiply by the months until the old home sells. Fees equal the loan multiplied by the origination percentage, plus other fees charged once.

Bridge loan cost adds those fees to interest. The calculation assumes interest is repaid from the sale proceeds at payoff.

The example below holds the sale price, loan terms, selling costs, and monthly home payments fixed. Only the time until sale changes.

Each row shows the bridge cost alongside the separate outlay for carrying both homes.

Monthly overlap outlay adds the new mortgage payment to the old home's monthly carrying costs. Enter the old mortgage payment, taxes, insurance, and utilities in that carrying-cost field.

With a $2,500 new mortgage payment and $1,800 in old home carrying costs, the outlay is $4,300 each month.

The overlap total tells you how much cash you need for those home payments while waiting for a sale. It is separate from the bridge loan cost shown in the table.

Under the calculator's payoff assumption, bridge interest reduces sale proceeds rather than appearing in the monthly overlap outlay.

Selling costs equal the sale price multiplied by the selling-cost percentage. Net proceeds then subtract the old mortgage payoff, bridge loan, bridge cost, and selling costs from the sale price.

In the example, $24,000 of selling costs leaves $69,000 after those deductions.

The origination fee is charged once in this calculation, even if the sale takes longer than expected. If the old home sells after 0 months, the example bridge cost is the $2,000 fee alone.

With no origination fee, the 6-month bridge cost falls to $5,000 and net proceeds rise to $71,000. Enter any other one-time fees separately so they appear in the bridge cost.

How Much You Can Borrow

The calculator estimates a borrowing ceiling only when you enter your lender's maximum combined loan-to-value limit. It multiplies the expected sale price by that limit and subtracts the old mortgage payoff.

That gives you a figure to compare with your planned bridge loan.

For the example sale, an entered limit of 80% produces a maximum of $120,000. The calculation applies 80% to the $400,000 expected sale price, then subtracts the $200,000 old mortgage payoff.

The entered limit is an example. It is no standard lender limit and no loan offer.

Your lender's quote supplies the limit, rate, fees, and term. If you do not have a combined loan-to-value figure, leave that optional field empty rather than treating the example as an available borrowing amount.

You can still estimate bridge cost and net proceeds using the loan amount and terms you enter.

A higher bridge loan amount affects more than the amount repaid at sale. Because interest is calculated from the loan amount, changing the bridge amount also changes interest and the net proceeds calculation.

Origination fees change with the loan amount when you enter an origination percentage. Use the amount you intend to borrow when comparing the calculator's cost with the cash you expect from the old home.

When the Old Home Sells Late

A later sale raises bridge interest and extends the monthly outlay for carrying both homes. In the example, each extra month adds $833.33 of bridge interest and lowers net proceeds by $833.33.

The home payments continue separately at $4,300 a month under the example inputs.

Use the calculator's "If the old home sells in" table to compare sale dates. It shows the entered timeline alongside 3, 6, and 9 months, so you can see the cost of a delay without changing the inputs repeatedly.

The Copy results button lets you keep the figures from the scenario you entered.

The optional Alternative cost, per month field compares the bridge's fees and interest with a monthly cost you enter. Break-even months equal bridge fees divided by the alternative's monthly cost minus bridge monthly interest.

The calculation produces a break-even point only when the alternative costs more per month than bridge interest.

Against an example alternative costing $1,500 a month with no up-front fee, the bridge costs less once the old home takes more than 3.0 months to sell. If the home sells sooner, the bridge costs more than that alternative.

If the alternative costs no more than the bridge's $833.33 monthly interest, the bridge never costs less under this comparison.

A sale may also leave too little to repay everything. If the old mortgage payoff rises from $200,000 to $300,000, the example produces negative $31,000 in net proceeds.

The calculator warns when the sale does not cover the old mortgage, bridge loan, bridge cost, and selling costs. That result means you would bring cash to closing.

For another way to pay for the overlap, compare a bridge loan with a home equity line of credit (HELOC) in our bridge loan vs HELOC guide. For a related guide, see the guide to buying and selling a house at the same time.

Bridge cost rises with the months until sale, so price a timeline that fits your plan before committing. Enter your own months to sell and lender quote in the bridge loan calculator above, then read the Bridge loan cost line.

What the Calculator Does Not Include

  • The calculator leaves out closing costs on the new home. Budget for those separately when working out the cash needed for the purchase.
  • The calculator leaves out taxes on the sale. Keep any sale-related tax amount separate from the net proceeds shown here.
  • The calculator does not model prepayment terms or lender-specific rules. Use the terms in your lender quote when deciding how the calculated cost applies to a loan offer.
  • The calculator does not price an investment purchase financed with a different short-term loan. Use the hard money loan calculator for that loan instead.

Frequently asked questions

Does a bridge loan cost more?

It depends on the cost you compare it with. Enter an alternative monthly cost in the calculator to compare it with the bridge's fees and interest. Against the example alternative costing $1,500 a month, the break-even point is 3.0 months. For a comparison of financing choices, see bridge loan vs HELOC.

How much bridge loan can I get?

The amount depends on the combined loan-to-value limit in your lender's quote. The calculator uses an entered limit and expected sale price, then subtracts the old mortgage payoff to estimate the most you could borrow under that limit. An example 80% limit gives $120,000 for the example sale. No limit on this page is a standard lender limit or a loan offer.

How long does a bridge loan last?

The term comes from your lender's quote. This calculator assumes no standard term. Enter the months until the old home sells to calculate interest through that sale date. The calculator also shows how the cost and net proceeds change at other sale dates. If interest is paid monthly rather than at payoff, the displayed monthly outlay does not include those interest payments.
