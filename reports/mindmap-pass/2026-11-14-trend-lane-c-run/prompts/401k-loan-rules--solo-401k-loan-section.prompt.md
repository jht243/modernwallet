## CLOSED FACT LIST — anything not on this list, you do not know
- 26 U.S.C. 72(p)(2) sets the same limits for a loan from any qualified employer plan; the limits apply the same way to a loan from an owner-only 401(k) (this page's section 1 gives the formula and examples).
- 26 U.S.C. 4975(c)(1)(B), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4975: lending of money or other extension of credit between a plan and a disqualified person is a prohibited transaction.
- 26 U.S.C. 4975(d)(1): the prohibited-transaction rules do not apply to a loan made by the plan to a disqualified person who is a participant or beneficiary of the plan if the loan (A) is available to all such participants or beneficiaries on a reasonably equivalent basis, (B) is not made available to highly compensated employees in an amount greater than the amount made available to other employees, (C) is made in accordance with specific provisions regarding such loans set forth in the plan, (D) bears a reasonable rate of interest, and (E) is adequately secured.
- So a plan provision is one of five conditions, not the only one. The plan document and its administrator decide whether a given loan meets them.
- 26 U.S.C. 4975(f)(6): the exemptions in subsection (d) do not apply to a transaction in which a plan for owner-employees lends part of the plan's corpus or income to an owner-employee. For that lending rule, the term owner-employee covers only a participant or beneficiary of an individual retirement plan and an employer or association that establishes an individual retirement plan. So a 401(k) owner is not categorically barred by this rule, while an IRA-based plan (a SEP, SIMPLE or traditional IRA) cannot lend to its owner.
- 26 U.S.C. 416(i)(1)(A), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/416: a key employee includes a 5-percent owner of the employer. An owner is generally a key employee. Under 26 U.S.C. 72(p)(3), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/72, no deduction is allowed for interest on a plan loan during any period the borrower is a key employee, so interest on the owner's own plan loan is not deductible.
