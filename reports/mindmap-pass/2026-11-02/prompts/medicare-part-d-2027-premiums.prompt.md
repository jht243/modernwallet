## CLOSED FACT LIST (everything the page may state; anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, benchmark, date or URL; if something is not published, say it is not published)
F1. KFF news release published October 30, 2026: the average monthly premium for stand-alone prescription drug plans (PDPs) is projected to increase to $51 in 2027, up 50% from $34 in 2026, if current enrollees do not switch plans.
F2. KFF: Medicare Advantage plans with drug coverage (MA-PDs) are projected to average $9 a month in 2027, an increase of less than $2. The PDP premium is more than five times the MA-PD premium.
F3. KFF: more than 4 million enrollees in zero-premium PDPs will face higher premiums because there are no zero-premium PDPs available in 2027.
F4. KFF: eight national PDPs; projected 2027 average premiums range from about $6 to $204. Lowest: Centene's Wellcare Value Script Rx at $6.50 (up from $5.80). Highest: United's AARP Medicare Rx Preferred at $204.50 (up from $126.00).
F5. KFF: the 2027 maximum allowable deductible is $700, up from $615 in 2026; plans may charge less. Average deductible $643 for PDP enrollees (up from $540) and $489 for MA-PD enrollees (up from $360). 99.8% of PDP enrollees (up from 96%) and 85% of MA-PD enrollees (up from 80%) face a deductible if they keep their current plans.
F6. KFF: most enrollees will pay coinsurance rather than copayments for preferred and non-preferred drugs, which can mean less predictable out-of-pocket costs.
F7. KFF: "people facing large premium increases need to take action if they want to avoid paying significantly more." Open enrollment runs through December 7, and relatively low-premium PDP options exist. KFF notes the increases affect most PDP enrollees not receiving the extra subsidies provided to low-income enrollees.
F8. CMS fact sheet dated July 28, 2026: the 2027 national average monthly bid amount is $296.05 and the base beneficiary premium is $41.33. The annual increase in the base premium is capped by the Inflation Reduction Act's premium stabilization provision at 6% per year.
F9. CMS: it will discontinue the voluntary premium stabilization demonstration for stand-alone prescription drug plans, which began in 2025, at the end of 2026. CMS says sponsors had enough experience under the redesigned benefit, so the program returns to traditional market conditions in 2027. (KFF's page, as read, does not state the cause of the premium increase; do not claim CMS or KFF link the demonstration ending to the end of zero-premium plans.)
F10. Medicare.gov Part D costs page: no Medicare drug plan may have a deductible above $700 in 2027 ($615 in 2026); some plans have no deductible. After the deductible you pay 25% coinsurance until out-of-pocket spending reaches $2,100 in 2026 or $2,400 in 2027. After that you pay nothing for covered Part D drugs for the rest of the calendar year. Premiums vary by plan and may be higher depending on income. Copayment is a fixed dollar amount per prescription; coinsurance is a percentage of the drug cost.
F11. Medicare.gov Part D costs page: late enrollment penalty is 1% of the national base beneficiary premium ($38.99 in 2026) times the number of full uncovered months, rounded to the nearest $0.10 and added to the premium. Extra Help recipients do not pay the penalty.
F12. Medicare.gov Extra Help page: Extra Help is a Medicare program for people with limited income and resources. 2026 limits: individual income $23,940 and resources $18,090; married couple income $32,460 and resources $36,100. Limits may rise each year; Alaska and Hawaii have separate limits not listed. Some people qualify automatically: full-benefit Medicaid, state help with Part B premiums through a Medicare Savings Program, SSI. Not available in Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands or American Samoa.
F13. Medicare.gov Extra Help page, 2027 costs: plan premium $0; plan deductible $0; up to $5.80 per generic drug and up to $14.40 per brand-name drug at participating pharmacies; $0 per covered drug once total drug costs reach $2,400. Apply online at ssa.gov/medicare/part-d-extra-help; free help from your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP); can apply for Extra Help and a Medicare Savings Program together; if you do not qualify now you can reapply at any time. If approved and without a Part D plan, Medicare enrolls you in one.
F14. Arithmetic on F1 and F4 (label as arithmetic, not a KFF figure): $34 a month is $408 a year; $51 a month is $612 a year; the difference is $17 a month or $204 a year. $6.50 a month is $78 a year. $204.50 a month is $2,454 a year. The AARP Medicare Rx Preferred increase is $78.50 a month ($204.50 minus $126.00).
