## The Downside of Inflation and Assumption Drift

A major downside of Coast FIRE is assumption drift, where inflation and changing living costs erode a target calculated decades in advance. A Coast FIRE number is derived from a spending goal chosen today. If the cost of the life that goal was meant to buy rises over the intervening decades, the same invested balance funds less retirement spending than planned.

The underlying calculation also applies a single fixed rate of return every year for decades. Real markets do not deliver one steady rate year after year, so compounding projections over long windows magnifies any early error. Distance in time makes these calculations fragile.

The younger you are when calculating the number, the more years remain for personal circumstances and the economy to diverge from your expectations. Three variables move the target: the expected investment return, the planned retirement age, and the annual spending goal. Reviewing our [Coast FIRE number by age](/guides/coast-fire-number-by-age/) benchmarks shows how wide that margin of error grows when retirement sits thirty years away.
