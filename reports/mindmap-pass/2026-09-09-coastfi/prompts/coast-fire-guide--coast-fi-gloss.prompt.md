## THE CLOSED FACT LIST
Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, statistic, or URL.
- "Coast FI" and "Coast FIRE" are two names for the same milestone. FI stands for financial
  independence; FIRE stands for Financial Independence, Retire Early. Coast FI is the older,
  shorter form used inside the FIRE community; Coast FIRE is the form most calculators use.
  Neither name means anything different from the other.
- The formula, in the form it is usually quoted: Coast FI = retirement savings goal / (1 + r)^t,
  where r is the expected annual rate of return on investments and t is the number of years
  until retirement (that is, retirement age minus current age).
- That is the same calculation this page already describes in its section "How the Coast FIRE
  number is calculated": take the retirement number, then discount it back to today at the
  expected return over the years remaining.
- Worked example already published on this site: a 35-year-old who wants $60,000 a year and
  plans to retire at 65, at a 7% return, needs $197,051 invested today. ($60,000 / 0.04 =
  $1,500,000; 1.07^30 = 7.6123; $1,500,000 / 7.6123 = $197,051.)
