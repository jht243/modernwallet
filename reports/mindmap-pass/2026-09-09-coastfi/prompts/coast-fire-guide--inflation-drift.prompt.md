## THE CLOSED FACT LIST
Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, rate, statistic, or URL.
- A Coast FIRE number is derived from a spending goal chosen today. If the cost of the life that
  goal was meant to buy rises over the intervening decades, the same nominal balance funds less.
- The calculation is a single fixed rate of return applied every year for decades. Real markets
  do not deliver one rate every year.
- The distance in time is what makes it fragile: the younger you are when you calculate the
  number, the more years there are for both your circumstances and the economy to move away
  from what you assumed.
- Changes that move the number: a change in the target retirement age, a change in the expected
  return, and a change in the spending goal. This page already tells the reader to recheck the
  number at least once a year and after a big market move, a raise, or a change in the target
  retirement age or spending goal.
- The practical response is to stress-test with a lower expected return and a later retirement
  age before making an income decision. This page already says that in "Signs you're not
  actually ready to coast yet".
- Our /coast-fire/ calculator has a related FAQ on whether to enter a nominal or a real
  (inflation-adjusted) return, and why entering a nominal rate makes you look closer than you
  are. Point the reader at it rather than restating it.
- DO NOT state an inflation rate, a historical inflation figure, a historical market return, an
  average savings balance, a survey statistic, or the name or title of any person. You have none
  of them.
- DO NOT name a financial adviser, a firm, a newspaper, or a study.
