answer: A bank multiplies your balance by the annual rate divided by 365 for each day, adds up the daily amounts, and usually pays the total into the account once a month; with daily compounding each day's interest is added to the balance. For example, $10,000 at 4.00% earns about $33.97 in a 31-day month and $408.08 over a year, which is a 4.08% APY.

## THE CLOSED FACT LIST
Source: 12 CFR Part 1030 Appendix A (Regulation DD / Truth in Savings, annual percentage yield calculation), read 2026-10-21; the arithmetic below is computed.
- APY formula (12 CFR 1030 Appendix A): APY = 100 [(1 + Interest/Principal)^(365/Days in term) - 1], where Principal is the amount of funds assumed to have been deposited at the beginning of the account, Interest is the total dollar amount of interest earned on the Principal for the term, and Days in term is the actual number of days in the term of the account.
- Method (arithmetic, state as how the math works): daily interest = balance x annual rate / 365. Banks differ on the day-count basis (365 or 360) and on whether interest is compounded daily or monthly; the account's disclosure states the method, so check it.
- Worked examples, $10,000 balance at a 4.00% annual rate, no deposits or withdrawals (all computed):
  - One day: $10,000 x 0.04 / 365 = $1.0959 (about $1.10).
  - A 31-day month, no compounding within the month: $33.97. A 30-day month: $32.88. A 28-day month: $30.68.
  - Compounded daily over one year: $408.08 of interest, an APY of 4.08%.
  - Compounded monthly over one year: $407.42, an APY of 4.07%.
  - Simple interest, no compounding, one year: $400.00.
  - A 31-day month on a 360-day basis: $34.44, versus $33.97 on 365.
  - Compounded daily over five years: $2,213.89 of interest.
  - To earn a 4.00% APY with daily compounding, the annual rate (APR) must be about 3.92%.
- APR is the stated annual rate; APY is the yearly yield after compounding, so APY is higher than APR whenever interest compounds more than once a year.
- Interest on savings is taxable; the site has a guide on it (link it; do not add rules).
- FDIC insurance covers deposits up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category (fdic.gov, already used on the site).
