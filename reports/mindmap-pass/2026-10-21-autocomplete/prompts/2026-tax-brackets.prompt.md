## THE CLOSED FACT LIST
Source: IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 (2026 inflation adjustments), tables 1 to 4, and the IRS newsroom release for the 2026 adjustments.
- Rates: 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, 37%.
- Single (taxable income): 10% up to $12,400; 12% over $12,400 to $50,400; 22% over $50,400 to $105,700; 24% over $105,700 to $201,775; 32% over $201,775 to $256,225; 35% over $256,225 to $640,600; 37% over $640,600.
- Married filing jointly: 10% up to $24,800; 12% to $100,800; 22% to $211,400; 24% to $403,550; 32% to $512,450; 35% to $768,700; 37% over $768,700.
- Head of household: 10% up to $17,700; 12% to $67,450; 22% to $105,700; 24% to $201,750; 32% to $256,200; 35% to $640,600; 37% over $640,600.
- Married filing separately: same thresholds as single through the 35% band, which ends at $384,350; 37% over $384,350.
- 2026 standard deduction: $16,100 single; $16,100 married filing separately; $32,200 married filing jointly; $24,150 head of household.
- Brackets apply to taxable income (income after deductions), and each rate applies only to the dollars inside its band. This is how the IRS tables are built: for a single filer, tax on income over $50,400 and up to $105,700 is $5,800 plus 22% of the excess over $50,400.
- Worked example (computed): single filer with $85,000 of income, standard deduction $16,100, taxable income $68,900: 10% on $12,400 = $1,240; 12% on $38,000 = $4,560; 22% on $18,500 = $4,070; total $9,870, an effective rate of 11.6% of income and 14.3% of taxable income. Checked against the IRS table: $5,800 + 22% x ($68,900 - $50,400) = $9,870.
