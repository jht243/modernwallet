answer: A lender is the financial institution that makes the loan; a broker does not lend money and instead helps you find lenders and loan products, and is paid a loan-specific fee. Neither is automatically better, so the pick depends on whether you want one company's offer or someone to gather several, and you should shop around either way.

## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- A lender is a financial institution that makes direct loans (CFPB). A broker does not lend money (CFPB). You can use a broker to find different lenders or mortgage loans (CFPB).
- When you take out a loan with a lender, you pay them back based on the terms of your loan (CFPB). When you work with a broker, you pay them a loan-specific fee for their services (CFPB).
- Some financial institutions operate as both lenders and brokers, so you should ask whether a broker is involved in your loan transaction (CFPB).
- Regardless of whether you use a broker or a direct lender, you should always shop around for the best loan terms and the lowest interest rates and fees (CFPB).
- When you work with someone to get a mortgage, they are usually paid a loan-specific fee or commission. This money is paid by you or by the lender you use (CFPB).
- A mortgage loan officer or broker is the person who works with you to get a mortgage. Mortgage loan officers often work for one specific lender. Mortgage brokers typically work with multiple lenders (CFPB).
- Federal law prohibits commissions from varying based on the terms of the mortgage, but lenders can pay loan officers and brokers in many ways: a salary, a fixed amount per loan, a fixed percentage of the loan amount, or a combination. When paid per loan, lenders can set minimums or maximums per loan (CFPB).
- Before you work with a loan officer or broker, make sure you understand their fees and who pays them. You can shop around for a lender and a broker to find the best rate (CFPB).
