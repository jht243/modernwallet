WORKED EXAMPLES (computed, use exactly; all assume $500 extra per month, level payments, no taxes, no fees, monthly compounding):
Example A, locked-in owner: $300,000 balance, 3% rate, 25 years (300 months) remaining. Required payment $1,422.63/month. Total interest if paid on schedule $126,790. With an extra $500/month: paid off in 199 months (about 16.6 years), total interest $80,729, interest saved $46,061. Alternative: invest the $500/month for all 300 months. At an assumed 5% annual return the account reaches $297,755; at 7% it reaches $405,036 (contributions $150,000). If you prepay and then invest the freed-up $1,922.63 (payment plus extra) until month 300: $240,821 at 5% and $263,479 at 7%. So at 3% the invest-first path ends ahead by about $56,934 at 5% and $141,557 at 7% (differences of 297,755-240,821 and 405,036-263,479), but with market risk the prepay path does not carry.
Example B, new borrower: $300,000 loan, 7.5%, 30 years. Payment $2,097.64/month. Total interest on schedule $455,152. With an extra $500/month: paid off in 206 months (about 17.2 years), interest $233,428, interest saved $221,724. Investing the $500/month for 360 months: $416,129 at 5%, $609,986 at 7% (contributions $180,000). Prepay then invest the freed $2,597.64 for the remaining months: $559,292 at 5%, $645,302 at 7%. So at 7.5% the prepay path ends ahead at both 5% (by $143,162) and 7% (by $35,316), and it is guaranteed.
General principle: paying extra on a loan earns a guaranteed return equal to the loan rate (before taxes). Investment returns are not guaranteed. Existing guide /guides/pay-off-debt-or-invest/ states a priority order: employer 401(k) match first, starter emergency fund, high-rate debt, then invest.
CFPB has a page on paying extra on a mortgage (check prepayment penalty and make sure extra payments go to principal) — only claim: check your loan for a prepayment penalty and tell your servicer extra money should apply to principal.
