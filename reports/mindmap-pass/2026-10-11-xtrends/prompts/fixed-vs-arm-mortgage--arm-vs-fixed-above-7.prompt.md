reader question: "Is an ARM worth it when 30-year fixed rates are above 7%?"
answer: It can be, if you are confident you will sell or refinance before the first reset; the savings are real but the reset risk is the price.

## CLOSED FACT LIST (anything not on this list, you do not know)

WORKED EXAMPLE (computed; use exactly; assumes $400,000 loan, September averages above, 30-year amortization, no points or fees): 5/1 ARM at 6.2%: payment $2,449.88/month. 30-year fixed at 7.1%: $2,688.13/month. Difference $238.25/month, $14,295 over the 60 fixed months. Balance on the ARM after 60 payments: $373,126. If the rate resets by the typical 2-point first-adjustment cap (the page already describes 2/2/5 caps) to 8.2% for the remaining 300 months, the payment becomes $2,929.45, which is $241.32 above the fixed payment ($2,929.45 minus $2,688.13 = $241.32) and $479.57 above the old ARM payment. Breakeven: the five years of savings ($14,295) are roughly equal to 59 months of the post-reset gap vs fixed ($241.32 x 59 = $14,238), so if rates stay at the cap you would give the savings back in about five years after the reset. The ARM can also reset lower or stay flat; this example uses the cap as a stress case, not a forecast.
Another way to cut risk: refinancing before the first adjustment, with closing costs of 2% to 5% of the loan (stated on the page already).
