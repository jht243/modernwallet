How it works

Buyers use three checklists, in this order. Start with the Home Buying Checklist to manage 59 financing and purchase tasks, or estimate your price range with our home affordability calculator. During your inspection window, use the 62 checks on the Home Inspection Checklist to examine physical systems, then complete the 30 verification steps on the Final Walkthrough Checklist before signing closing papers.

Sellers follow an organized process to prepare a residence, market the listing, and coordinate required transfer documents. The Home Selling Checklist draws from a 44-item library to plan pre-listing repairs, staging and your seller disclosures.

Tenants and property owners use dedicated tools tailored to leasing decisions and daily management. The Rental Checklist for Renters contains 53 items for lease reviews, while first-time renters can use the 42 items on the First Apartment Checklist alongside our renting vs buying comparison. Document property turnover room by room with the Move-In / Move-Out Condition Checklist, which creates 36 items for a studio or 89 for a 3-bedroom, 2-bathroom house with laundry, a dishwasher, a garage and a yard, and apply the 45-item Landlord Checklist for lease compliance.

Your property details and regional hazard conditions directly determine which checklist items appear. For a buyer in Miami, Florida, the state preset deactivates freezing winter settings to remove frozen-pipe checks, while activating coastal hazard toggles for roof and wind-deductible tasks. Items on deposit limits, disclosure forms and notice deadlines tell you to check your state's rule instead of stating a number, so pick your situation above to produce your customized house checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Which checklist should I use?

Select the checklist that matches your immediate real estate transaction or leasing transition. Buyers can start with the home buying or inspection lists, sellers can pick the selling guide, and tenants can choose between rental, first apartment, or condition reports. Property owners managing tenants can select the dedicated landlord checklist to oversee leasing and maintenance.

Can I print these checklists or save them as a PDF?

Yes, every checklist includes a dedicated print button that formats your custom list as a clean worksheet. You can print physical copies or use your browser settings to save a Portable Document Format (PDF) file. The printed version prints only the checklist as a worksheet.

Why does the checklist ask for my state?

Your state selection adjusts environmental hazard toggles such as freezing winters or coastal wind zones to keep tasks relevant. State statutes also govern security deposit limits, return deadlines, disclosure forms, and notice rules. Items on these topics tell you to check your state's rule instead of stating a number.

Do the checklists save my progress?

Yes, your checkmarks and notes save automatically in your web browser. No account creation or email signup is required to preserve your progress.

Is there a checklist for landlords?

Yes, the landlord checklist provides 45 operational tasks designed specifically for property owners and managers. Select Landlord Checklist from the dropdown menu above to customize your worksheet.

Are these checklists legal advice?

No, these checklists serve as an educational planning aid and do not constitute legal advice. The items draw from guidance published by the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) and the American Society of Home Inspectors (ASHI).

Home Buying Checklist

Property address: ______________________________________________

Tick each item as you finish it. Items were chosen from your answers on the website.

1. Budget and Credit

☐Pull your credit reports from all three bureaus and dispute any errors
☐Set a monthly budget that includes principal, interest, property tax, insurance and any HOA dues
☐Decide your down payment and how much cash stays in savings after closing
☐Budget for closing costs on top of the down payment
☐Look up first-time buyer programs from your state housing finance agency and city

2. Mortgage Pre-Approval

☐Apply with two or three lenders within a few days of each other and compare their Loan Estimates line by line. A lender must send the Loan Estimate within three business days of your application.
☐Get a written pre-approval letter to send with offers
☐Avoid new credit cards, car loans or large purchases until closing

3. House Hunting

☐Write down your must-haves and deal-breakers before the first showing
☐Visit the neighborhood on a weekday evening and a weekend, and drive your commute at rush hour
☐Look up the address on the FEMA flood map. A high-risk flood zone means required flood insurance on most mortgages.
☐Check the property tax bill and whether a reassessment after sale will raise it
☐On each tour, look for water stains, musty smells, soft floors and fresh paint on one patch of ceiling
☐Ask the age of the roof, furnace, AC and water heater
☐Look for pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, attics or garages, and ask how they are insulated or heat-taped. Unheated spaces are where pipes freeze and burst.

4. Offer and Contract

☐Review recent comparable sales before choosing a price
☐Confirm the earnest money amount, who holds it, and when you can get it back
☐Choose your contingencies: inspection, financing and appraisal, and their deadlines
☐Read the seller's disclosure form for known defects
☐Built before 1978: get the federal lead-paint disclosure and pamphlet, and decide whether to use your 10-day window to test

5. Inspection and Appraisal

☐Book a licensed home inspector and attend the inspection
☐Add a radon test. EPA recommends testing all homes below the third floor.
☐Add a sewer-line camera scope
☐Ask whether your lender or area calls for a termite (wood-destroying insect) inspection
☐Decide which findings to ask the seller to repair or credit, in writing
☐Review the appraisal; if it comes in low, renegotiate, cover the gap, or use your contingency

6. Insurance and Closing

☐Buy homeowners insurance (or HO-6 for a condo) and send the binder to your lender
☐If the home is in a high-risk flood zone, buy flood insurance
☐Confirm title search and choose whether to buy an owner's title insurance policy
☐Compare the Closing Disclosure with your Loan Estimate. Your lender must give it to you at least three business days before closing.
☐Call your title or escrow company at a number you already know to confirm wiring instructions before sending money. Wire fraud by email is a common closing scam.
☐Do the final walkthrough
☐Bring photo ID and your funds as the closing agent instructs

7. First Week in the House

☐Change or rekey the locks and reset garage and alarm codes
☐Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, and add them where they are missing
☐Put utilities in your name and forward your mail
☐Find the main water shutoff and confirm it turns. You need it in seconds if a pipe bursts.
☐File your closing documents; you will need them for taxes and when you sell
