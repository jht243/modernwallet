reader question: "Are bank account sign-up bonuses taxable?" [autocomplete are-sweep; no PAA]
answer: Yes. The IRS treats gifts or services for opening an account as interest once their value passes $10 on deposits under $5,000, or $20 on deposits of $5,000 or more, and cash bonuses are taxed as interest income (definition + threshold).

WORKED EXAMPLE (ground truth, computed; use exactly, label hypothetical): a $300 cash bonus taxed at a 22% federal rate leaves $234 after federal tax ($66 tax). A $4,000 deposit with a $10 gift falls at the threshold: the IRS says gifts "more than $10" are reported, so a gift worth exactly $10 is not over it; a $12 gift on a $4,000 deposit is reported as interest. On a $5,000 deposit the threshold is $20. Do not add any other computed number.
CLOSED FACT LIST:
- IRS Pub 550: "For deposits of less than $5,000, gifts or services valued at more than $10 must be reported as interest." "For deposits of $5,000 or more, gifts or services valued at more than $20 must be reported as interest."
- IRS Topic 403: interest on bank accounts, money market accounts and certificates of deposit is taxable interest; interest is generally taxable as ordinary income in the year it is available; you must report all taxable and tax-exempt interest on your federal return even without a Form 1099-INT; payers should send Form 1099-INT for interest of $10 or more.
- IRS 1099-INT instructions: financial institutions include amounts of $10 or more paid or credited, "whether or not designated as interest."
- Taxable interest may require estimated tax payments.

- Bonus terms (minimum balance, direct deposit, time to hold funds) are set by each offer and change; state none as current.
