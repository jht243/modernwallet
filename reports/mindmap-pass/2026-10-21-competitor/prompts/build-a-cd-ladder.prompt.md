reader question: "How do I build a CD ladder?" [autocomplete how-sweep; no PAA]
answer: Split your money into equal parts, open CDs with staggered terms (for example 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 years), and each time the shortest one matures reinvest it into a new longest-term CD so one rung comes due every year (method).

CLOSED FACT LIST:
- CFPB: a CD is a savings product from a bank or credit union; you agree to leave the money in for a set term, and taking money out early means paying a penalty to the bank.
- CFPB advises comparing CD offers on three things: the term, the interest rate, and the early-withdrawal penalty.
- Federal rules require the bank to disclose whether a penalty applies, how it is calculated and when it is assessed; examples of penalty forms are a flat amount or a set number of days' interest. Penalties vary by bank and term, so there is no standard amount.
- CFPB: a callable CD can be ended by the bank before maturity, so it may not last its full term.
- FDIC: deposit insurance covers $250,000 per depositor, per FDIC-insured bank, for each account ownership category; deposits at different FDIC-insured banks are insured separately; deposits in the same ownership category at one bank are added together. CDs are covered. Credit union CDs are insured by the NCUA.
- IRS: interest on CDs is taxable interest; interest is generally taxable as ordinary income; payers send Form 1099-INT for interest of $10 or more; you report all interest even without a form.
- IRS Pub 550: if you withdraw from a deferred-interest account before maturity you may pay a penalty; you must report the total interest paid or credited without subtracting the penalty (the penalty is handled on the return separately; tell the reader to read Pub 550 and not state the deduction rules).
- Rates and terms are set by each bank and change; state no current market rate.
