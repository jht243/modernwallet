## CLOSED FACT LIST (everything the page may state; anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, benchmark, date or URL; if something is not published, say the IRS has not published it)
F1. The IRS announced on October 16, 2026 (news release IR-2026-123) that it has begun issuing automatic refunds to individuals who claimed the adoption tax credit carryforward from prior years. Refunds and adjustments began in late September 2026.
F2. More than 50,000 taxpayers had already received refunds as of that announcement. The IRS expects most eligible refunds to be complete by the end of 2026; the IRS adoption credit page says most of these refunds will be issued automatically by December 31, 2026.
F3. The U.S. Department of the Treasury clarified its interpretation of the Working Families Tax Cuts (the 2025 tax law) as it applies to the adoption tax credit.
F4. Under that interpretation, unused adoption credit carryforwards from 2020 to 2024 can increase the refundable amount of the credit for tax year 2025 only.
F5. For 2025, the refundable portion of the adoption credit is limited to $5,000 per eligible child. Actual refunds can be lower, depending on the allowable credit, income limitations, payments and offsets. The IRS publishes no income phaseout amounts for 2025 on these pages.
F6. Taxpayers do not need to file an amended return to get the adjustment. Taxpayers who filed their 2025 return before April 15, 2026 generally receive any adjustment automatically. Taxpayers who filed under an extension, or whose accounts have identity theft indicators, freezes, dependency conflicts or other issues, will be reviewed later in 2026, after processing or once the issue is resolved.
F7. The IRS generally sends a notice within 30 days of the adjustment. The notice states the amount and whether it produced a refund or was applied to another tax or non-tax debt.
F8. Generally no action is required. Review the notice, compare it with the 2025 return, and keep it with tax records. The IRS suggests creating an IRS Individual Online Account to view records, check refund status, view notices and make payments. More information: IRS.gov/AdoptionCredit.
F9. Eligibility per the IRS questions and answers: you filed a 2025 Form 1040, 1040-SR or 1040-NR; you claimed, or were eligible to claim, the adoption credit on Form 8839; you had prior-year carryforwards available for 2025 and your return and account information show the refundable credit should increase. If you did not claim the credit in 2025 but filed Form 8839 for 2020 to 2024 showing a carryforward, you may still qualify; if the IRS lacks enough information you may need to file Form 1040-X with Form 8839.
F10. Carryforwards from 2020 to 2024 can be used for 2025. Carryforwards expire after five years and the oldest is used first. Pre-2020 carryforwards have expired. The non-refundable portion of the credit can be carried forward up to five years; anything left after five years is forfeited.
F11. The adjustment does not change adjusted gross income (AGI), taxable income or qualified adoption expenses.
F12. The adjustment corrects the refundable portion, so future carryforwards may decrease. To recalculate your 2026 carryforward, use the Nonrefundable Adoption Credit Carryforward Worksheet (line 18) in the 2025 Form 8839 instructions; on line 8 enter your 2025 Form 8839 line 18 amount plus the refundable increase from the adjustment. Do not use the notice's total refund or balance due as the refundable increase; use the notice or your transcript. The 2025 carryforward treatment does not carry into 2026; follow the 2026 instructions. Complete the 2025 Form 8839 as issued and do not alter the form.
F13. Do not file an amended return just to request this adjustment. File Form 1040-X to correct other items such as filing status, income, dependents, qualified adoption expenses, employer adoption benefits, carryforward amounts or another credit. You may need to amend if you used a carryforward as a nonrefundable credit when another listed credit (such as on Form 5695 or Form 3800) could have been claimed instead. To dispute the adjustment you may need Form 1040-X with Form 8839. The IRS will not automatically remove an estimated tax penalty; to request abatement follow your penalty notice or file Form 843.
F14. Checking status: you will receive a notice; check your IRS Online Account, or request a tax account transcript or record of account transcript. "Where's My Refund?" will not show this adjustment. The IRS may not be able to reissue your original notice, but you can access a copy through your Online Account. Do not call the IRS unless a notice or letter tells you to.
F15. 2026 rule (IRS adoption credit page): qualified expenses are capped at $17,670 per child; up to $5,120 per child is refundable and Schedule 3-A (Form 1040) is required to claim it; the full credit applies at modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) of $265,080 or less, is reduced from $265,081 to $305,079 and is unavailable at $305,080 or more; the employer-provided benefit exclusion limit is also $17,670. Form 8839 is attached to the return; Part III (employer-benefit exclusion) is completed before Part II (the credit).
F16. The IRS notes that news items may not be updated after release, so readers should verify the date and details on IRS.gov before relying on them.
F17. Not published by the IRS on these pages: the 2025 maximum credit per child, 2025 income phaseout amounts, and a formula for how much of a given carryforward becomes refundable. Say so rather than estimating. Do not state or compute a refund amount for any hypothetical family.
