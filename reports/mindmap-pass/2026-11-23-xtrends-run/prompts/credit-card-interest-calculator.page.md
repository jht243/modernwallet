# Credit Card Interest Calculator

A credit card interest calculator finds one billing cycle's interest by dividing your annual percentage rate (APR) by the days in the year, then multiplying that daily rate by your average daily balance and by the number of days in the cycle. Enter your starting balance, APR, cycle length and 365- or 360-day basis in the calculator above. You can also add a payment made during the cycle and the day it posts. The calculator then shows the daily periodic rate, the average daily balance, the yearly cost if the balance stays flat, and the effective annual rate with daily compounding.

For example, take a $5,000 balance at 24% APR with no payment during the cycle. The cycle runs 30 days, and the daily rate uses a 365-day year. That gives a daily rate of 0.0658%, an average daily balance of $5,000, and interest of $98.63 for the cycle. If a $1,000 payment posts on day 11, interest for the same cycle drops to $85.48. That payment saves $13.15 compared with paying nothing during the cycle.

## How it works

The daily periodic rate is your APR divided by the number of days in the year your card issuer uses. At 24% APR on a 365-day year, the daily rate is 0.0658%. Some issuers divide by 360 instead, which raises the daily rate to 0.0667%. On the same $5,000 balance, that lifts one cycle's interest from $98.63 to $100.00. Set the day-count input to match the basis printed on your statement.

The average daily balance is the average of each day's balance across the billing cycle. The calculator uses the full balance until your payment posts and the reduced balance from that day on. Take the same $5,000 balance with a $1,000 payment that posts on day 11 of the cycle. The balance sits at $5,000 for the first 10 days. It sits at $4,000 for the remaining 20 days, which averages out to $4,333.33. Every day after the payment counts at the lower balance, so the same payment posted earlier in the cycle pulls the average down further.

Interest for the cycle equals the average daily balance times the daily rate times the days in the cycle. With no payment, the average daily balance stays at $5,000 and the daily rate is 0.0658%. Over 30 days, that comes to $98.63. With the day-11 payment, the same math on $4,333.33 gives $85.48. The calculator reports the $13.15 difference as the interest that payment saves.

The calculator also shows what your APR costs over a full year if the balance stays flat. Held flat for a year, the $5,000 balance costs $1,200 in simple interest. When interest compounds daily, each day's interest joins the balance and is charged interest itself. The effective annual rate then works out to 27.11%, above the 24% APR. The formula is one plus the daily rate, raised to the power of the days in the year, minus one.

The calculator covers purchase interest only. It leaves out any grace period, fees, cash advances and new purchases. Issuers also differ in how they compound within a cycle and how they round, so your statement can come in slightly off the figure above. Use the APR, cycle length and day-count basis shown on your statement, since the defaults in the calculator are placeholders.
