## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there a 10% credit card interest cap?
No, a 10% credit card interest cap is not federal law. Proposals [S. 381](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119s381is/html/BILLS-119s381is.htm) and H.R. 1944 in the 119th Congress would cap consumer credit card rates at 10% until January 2031, per a [Congressional Research Service](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF12861.html) (CRS) report updated March 10, 2026. S. 381 was introduced on February 4, 2025, and referred to Senate Banking, though current status after that is not confirmed here.

### How much would a 10% APR cap save on a $5,000 balance?
A 10% cap saves $57.53 in interest over one 30-day billing cycle on a $5,000 balance, dropping the charge from $98.63 at 24% to $41.10. Our calculation assumes a 365-day year, simple interest, no payments, no new fees, and the card staying available at 10%. Auxiliary fees or lost access to credit if banks limit products could erase those savings.
