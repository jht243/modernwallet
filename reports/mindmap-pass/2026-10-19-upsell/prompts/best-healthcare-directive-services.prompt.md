FreeWill (freewill.com):
- Offers an advance healthcare directive, also called a living will, which lets you specify your healthcare wishes and appoint someone to make decisions on your behalf if you're unable.
- Offers a Durable Financial Power of Attorney as a separate product for naming someone to make financial decisions if you can't.
- Its page says "100% free – no credit card required" and "absolutely no cost to you" for its estate planning tools.
- Only its Revocable Living Trust is labelled "California only"; the page lists no state restriction for the healthcare directive or the power of attorney.
LegalZoom (legalzoom.com):
- Basic Healthcare Directive: $39. Includes a living will, a healthcare power of attorney, online questions to guide you, unlimited document revisions for 30 days, and secure online document storage.
- Premium Healthcare Directive: $49. Everything in Basic plus unlimited 30-minute attorney consultations for 14 days. After the 14-day trial the attorney-consultation subscription renews at $25 per month unless canceled; the page says you can cancel online or by phone.
- Consultations are limited to one per new legal matter and exclude business-related matters.
- Documents are typically available for download within 2 to 3 business days.
- The page says the documents "have been accepted in all 50 states" and were created by attorneys, and that many states require notarization, so you should check your state's signing rules. The page does not list state-specific requirements.
- It does not clearly say whether Basic includes attorney review.
Trust & Will (trustandwill.com):
- Does not list standalone prices for a living will, healthcare directive or power of attorney; they come only in packages.
- Will Plan, "starting at" $199: includes a HIPAA Authorization, Living Will and Power of Attorney, along with a Last Will & Testament.
- Trust Plan, "starting at" $499: includes the same three documents plus a Revocable Living Trust, Pour Over Will, Schedule of Assets, Certification of Trust and Trust Funding Guide.
- Optional add-ons: Membership $49 per year; Attorney Support +$299.
- Individual or Couple option for each plan; the page does not list a separate couple price. Payment plans are available for both plans.
Mama Bear Legal Forms (mamabearlegalforms.com):
- Last Will & Testament: $159 individual or $249 for married couples; adding a spouse's will is $90. The package includes a last will and testament, a health care power of attorney, a finance power of attorney and a HIPAA release. The page does not list a living will by name.
- College Power of Attorney: $89. Includes health power of attorney documents, finance power of attorney documents and a HIPAA release and FERPA waiver; aimed at parents of college-age students ("help your college-age student in an emergency").
- Senior Adult Power of Attorney: $89 (the page gives only the title and price).
Rocket Lawyer (rocketlawyer.com):
- Its Colorado living will page describes a "free Colorado Living Will template" and says "Start your Colorado Living Will now and get Rocket Lawyer FREE for 7 days."
- The page does not state what membership costs after the 7-day trial.
- You can ask an attorney a question and get a response within one business day, or request a more in-depth document review; the page does not give prices for these. Membership benefits mention "Consult an Attorney Live"; the page does not say whether that is in the trial or a paid add-on.
- The living will is available for Colorado and the other states in the state selector, which includes the District of Columbia. The Colorado form can be used throughout Colorado.
Five Wishes (fivewishes.org):
- Five Wishes Paper: regular price $5.00 per copy; 10 or more copies $2.00 each; 25 or more copies $1.00 each.
- The page describes it as "a legal advance directive and living will written in simple, everyday language."
- It is a paper form you complete yourself; the page lists many language editions. The page excerpt gives no per-state validity list: tell the reader to check state requirements.
General (no number beyond these): a living will states your wishes about medical treatment; a healthcare power of attorney names a person to decide for you; signing rules (witnesses, notary) vary by state, so check your state's rules or ask a lawyer.
