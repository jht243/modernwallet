) FAFSA
- The U.S. Education Department had not issued guidance naming Trump Accounts as of early October 2026; FAFSA materials do not mention them. [search of fsapartners.ed.gov]
- Expert view 1 (Mark Kantrowitz, quoted by CNBC July 9, 2026): reported as a STUDENT asset, assessed at up to 20%; e.g. a $10,000 balance could reduce aid by about $2,000. A parent-owned 529 is assessed at up to 5.64%.
- Expert view 2 (Kalman Chany): the account could be treated like an IRA; retirement accounts are not reportable FAFSA assets.
- This is UNSETTLED. The page must present both views and not pick a definitive answer.
- After December 31 of the year the child turns 17, traditional-IRA rules apply. Withdrawals are taxable income and could count as student income on a later FAFSA. Whether the balance is then excluded is not confirmed.


) Program basics (already on the site)
- Contributions up to $5,000 a year combined from family and friends; employers can add up to $2,500, which counts inside the $5,000. The $1,000 seed does not count against the cap.
- Money is invested in a low-cost fund tracking the S&P 500 or a similar U.S. stock index; growth is tax-deferred; withdrawals generally blocked until January 1 of the year the child turns 18, then taxed like a traditional IRA.

- $1,000 seed eligibility: U.S.-citizen children with a valid SSN born 2025 through 2028.
