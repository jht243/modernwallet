## C1) Employer contributions (IRC §128)
- Employers can contribute up to $2,500 per year per EMPLOYEE (not per child), excluded from the employee's income; it counts toward the account's $5,000 annual cap. Two working spouses can each receive up to $2,500 (per Chase). [Mayer Brown, Aug 13 2026; chase.com]
- The $2,500 cap is inflation-indexed after 2027 (IRS instructions); do not give a later figure.
- Employers need a separate written "Trump Account Contribution Program" plan; Treasury guidance also requires employee self-certification of the child's age and dependent status, validation that the account is a Trump Account, notices to employees, annual statements and reporting to the trustee. [Treasury SB-0602, Aug 11 2026]
- Proposed regulations published August 11, 2026; comments were due September 25, 2026; they are PROPOSED, not final. [Mayer Brown]
- Contributions may not favor highly compensated employees; there is a safe harbor for matching the $1,000 pilot contribution. [Mayer Brown]
- Salary reduction through a cafeteria plan is allowed only for a dependent's account. [SB-0602]
- §128 amounts are free of federal income tax withholding but still subject to FICA (Social Security and Medicare); reported on Form W-2 Box 12, code "TA". [Mayer Brown]
- Over 50 companies had committed as of August 11, 2026 (Treasury); later counts range from 55 to about 72 depending on the tracker. [SB-0602; CNBC Oct 2 2026]
- Employees get nothing until the parent CLAIMS the child's account in the Trump Accounts app; employers cannot contribute to an unclaimed account. [sb0642]
- Mercer survey (April 2026, reported by Fortune July 27, 2026): about two-thirds of employers had no plans to contribute; 4% said they did.

## C2) Trustees / where accounts are held
- BNY was designated Treasury's financial agent on April 6, 2026. Robinhood Securities, LLC is the brokerage and INITIAL TRUSTEE for every Trump Account; Treasury controls the app. [Treasury bulletin; Robinhood customer agreement]
- Contributions opened July 4, 2026.
- Default fund: SPDR Portfolio S&P 500 ETF (ticker SPYM), per InvestmentNews (Jul 1 2026). Treasury named alternate funds IVV, VTI, SPTM and ITOT; until Treasury enables choosing, money stays in the default fund. Menu expense ratios run about 0.02% to 0.03%, versus the 0.10% legal cap. (Fund list is from news reporting; present it as "reported".)
- Robinhood's Trump Account customer agreement says it does not charge Trump Accounts other account fees and does not charge to transfer an account out. [Robinhood agreement, Sept 30 2026]
- Moving to another trustee is a full trustee-to-trustee "qualified rollover contribution", requested through the RECEIVING firm; only one funded Trump Account is allowed per child at a time; the old account must be closed; the new trustee must be a bank or IRS-approved nonbank trustee. [Robinhood agreement; Notice 2025-68]
- Fidelity (June 23 2026) PLANS rollover support once Treasury issues guidance — not live.
- Vanguard (July 1 2026) intends to support rollovers upon Treasury guidance; VTI is one of the alternate funds — not live.
- JPMorgan Chase: "Rollovers to J.P. Morgan are not yet available."
- Schwab and E*TRADE have said transfers should eventually be possible but have not committed (secondary report, early October 2026).
- As of early October 2026, no firm other than Robinhood accepts Trump Account transfers.
- No Treasury rollover guidance had been found as of October 8, 2026.
- Some websites list Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard and Bank of America as "approved trustees" — the firms' own pages say rollovers are not available yet; treat those lists as premature.
