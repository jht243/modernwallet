## What Would Settle the Question

Official guidance from the U.S. Department of Education would resolve how these funds affect federal financial aid. That clarification would appear on [studentaid.gov](https://studentaid.gov/) or within updated Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) instructions. 

If the agency classifies a Trump Account as a student asset, the balance would be assessed at up to 20 percent on the FAFSA. If the agency instead exempts the balance by treating it like an individual retirement account (IRA), the account value would not be reported as an asset at all.

A separate milestone occurs after December 31 of the year the child turns 17, when ordinary traditional-IRA rules begin to apply. Whether the underlying balance is officially excluded from aid formulas at that point remains unconfirmed. Any distributions taken after that transition become taxable income, which could count as student income on a subsequent FAFSA.

Families should monitor the specific FAFSA instructions published for the academic year their student will file. Some colleges also require institutional aid forms that ask for financial details beyond the federal application. Check each institution's specific aid policies to see how non-federal applications evaluate custodial assets or retirement balances held in a student's name.
