A mortgage is defined by three choices: loan type (conventional, or a government program such as FHA, VA or USDA), loan term (such as 15 or 30 years), and rate type (fixed or adjustable). Conventional is the most common type; the government programs exist for specific situations (lower down payment or credit score for FHA, veterans and service members for VA, low- to middle-income rural borrowers for USDA); and which one is cheapest depends on the borrower, so the reader should compare Loan Estimates from several lenders. - A loan has three elements: loan type, loan term and interest rate type. [CFPB https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/loan-options/ seen 2026-10-11]
- Mortgage loans are organized into categories based on the size of the loan and whether they are part of a government program: conventional, government, or special programs. The loan type affects how much you need for a down payment, the total cost of your loan including interest and mortgage insurance, and how much you can borrow and the house price range you can consider. [CFPB loan-options]
- Sometimes only one loan type fits a person's situation. If several fit, try scenarios and ask lenders for several quotes to see which type offers the best deal overall. [CFPB loan-options]
- Conventional: the majority of loans; typically cost less than FHA loans but can be harder to get. [CFPB loan-options]
- Government loans: FHA is a low down payment loan available to people with lower credit scores; VA is for veterans, servicemembers or surviving spouses; USDA is for low- to middle-income borrowers in rural areas. [CFPB loan-options]
- Special programs: state or local housing agencies can offer programs to low- to middle-income borrowers, first-time homebuyers or public service employees; Special Purpose Credit Programs are loans from private lenders to low- to middle-income borrowers generally in targeted communities. [CFPB loan-options]
- "Conventional" just means the loan is not part of a specific government program. Conventional loans typically cost less than FHA loans but can be more difficult to get. There are two main categories: conforming and non-conforming. [CFPB https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/conventional-loans/ seen 2026-10-11]
- Conforming loans have maximum loan amounts set by the government; other rules for conforming loans are set by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, which back conforming loans. Conforming is the most common loan type. If your down payment is less than 20 percent, you will typically need mortgage insurance. [CFPB conventional-loans]
- A conforming jumbo loan is a conforming loan for amounts above the standard conforming limit; it is only available in certain counties and the maximum loan amount varies by county. [CFPB conventional-loans] DO NOT state any dollar figure from the CFPB page: it is out of date. Say the limit is published each year by FHFA and varies by county.
- Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are restricted by law to purchasing single-family mortgages with origination balances below a specific amount known as the conforming loan limit. Loans above this amount are known as jumbo loans. The 2026 conforming loan limit values were set under the formula in the Housing and Economic Recovery Act of 2008. Limits are higher in some high-cost areas. [FHFA https://www.fhfa.gov/data/conforming-loan-limit seen 2026-10-11]
- Non-conforming loans are less standardized: eligibility, pricing and features can vary widely by lender, so it is particularly important to shop around and compare several offers. [CFPB conventional-loans]
- Jumbo (non-conforming) loans are for amounts greater than the conforming jumbo limit in the county. Rules vary by lender, but borrowers usually need good credit and a high down payment to qualify. [CFPB conventional-loans]
- A "non-conforming (other)" category covers loans of any size that do not fall into another category. Some are intended for borrowers with poor credit, tend to have high rates and may contain risky features such as minimal income documentation, interest-only payments or a balance that is allowed to increase. Many of the loans that got people in trouble during the financial crisis fell in this category. If considering a non-conforming loan, consult multiple lenders and ask whether you could qualify for a conforming or FHA loan instead. [CFPB conventional-loans]
- FHA loans are loans from private lenders that are regulated and insured by the Federal Housing Administration, a government agency. The FHA does not lend the money directly; private lenders do. [CFPB https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/fha-loans/ seen 2026-10-11]
- FHA loans allow down payments as low as 3.5 percent, allow lower credit scores than most conventional loans, and have a maximum loan amount that varies by county. Mortgage insurance is required for all FHA loans. [CFPB fha-loans]
- For borrowers with good credit and a medium (10 to 15 percent) down payment, FHA loans tend to be more expensive than conventional loans. For borrowers with lower credit scores or a smaller down payment, FHA loans can often be the cheapest option. There are no hard-and-fast rules and a lot depends on the current market; ask lenders for quotes for both and compare total costs. [CFPB fha-loans]
- VA direct and VA-backed home loans can help Veterans, service members and their survivors buy, build, improve or refinance a home. The borrower still needs the required credit and income for the amount borrowed. A VA loan may offer better terms than a traditional loan from a private bank, mortgage company or credit union. Nearly 90 percent of VA-backed loans are made with no down payment. VA offers a VA direct loan and 3 VA-backed loans. A Certificate of Eligibility (COE) shows a lender that the borrower qualifies, based on service history and duty status. [VA https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/ seen 2026-10-11, page last updated December 17, 2024]
- USDA: only the CFPB one-line description above is on the list (low- to middle-income borrowers in rural areas). Nothing else about USDA loans is known; do not state income limits, fees, down payments or map rules. Tell the reader to check eligibility with USDA.
- Loan term: 15-year vs 30-year. Shorter terms have higher monthly payments, typically lower interest rates and lower total cost; longer terms have lower monthly payments, typically higher interest rates and higher total cost. The interest rate on a shorter term is usually lower, by as much as a full percentage point, but how much depends on the terms and rates involved, and rates vary among lenders, especially for shorter terms. [CFPB loan-options]
- Fixed rate: the interest rate does not change and the monthly principal and interest payment stays the same; the total monthly payment can still change if property taxes, homeowner's insurance or mortgage insurance change. Most borrowers choose fixed-rate mortgages. [CFPB loan-options]
- Adjustable rate (ARM): lower interest rate to start; after the initial fixed period the rate can increase or decrease based on the market, so monthly principal and interest payments can go up or down, and could go up a lot, even double. Most ARMs have two periods: an introductory period where the rate is fixed and a second period where it adjusts regularly. Some ARMs include rate caps limiting how much rates can adjust. ARMs could be cheaper in the short term, for example if the borrower plans to move again within the initial fixed period; a borrower who stays longer than expected could pay a lot more. [CFPB loan-options]
- On an ARM, part of the interest rate is tied to an index; the actual rate is the index plus a set margin, subject to caps. Before taking an ARM, find out how high or low the rate and payments can go with each adjustment, how often the rate adjusts, how soon the payment could go up, whether there is a cap on how high the rate could go, and whether the borrower could still afford the loan at the maximums. Do not assume you will be able to sell or refinance before the rate changes: property value could decline or finances could change. [CFPB https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-a-fixed-rate-and-adjustable-rate-mortgage-arm-loan-en-100/ seen 2026-10-11]
- Be careful if a loan has a prepayment penalty, a balloon payment, negative amortization, or is interest-only. If it does, ask the loan officer why, and ask for another Loan Estimate for a loan without the feature to see the difference in cost. [CFPB loan-options]
- Generally a lender must document and verify income, employment, assets, debts and credit history to decide whether the borrower can repay. A loan that does so and follows certain criteria may be called a qualified mortgage. Nonqualified mortgages can be riskier or more expensive and are sometimes marketed to self-employed borrowers or borrowers without Social Security numbers. [CFPB loan-options]
- Always compare official Loan Estimates before deciding on a loan, and never make a final decision about which loan to take before getting them. [CFPB loan-options and conventional-loans]
