Medicare Part B premiums are deducted directly from Social Security checks for most beneficiaries. When the Part B premium increases, it absorbs a portion of your cost-of-living adjustment (COLA).

 The standard monthly premium rises from $202.90 to $209.40, an increase of $6.50.

For example, consider a retiree with a $2,000 monthly benefit who receives a $72.00 COLA raise:

- Gross COLA increase: plus $72.00 a month
- Part B increase from $202.90 to $209.40: minus $6.50 a month
- Net monthly gain: $65.50

The hold-harmless rule generally limits your Part B increase to the dollar increase in your Social Security benefit. In the $2,000 example, the $72 raise is much larger than the $6.50 premium increase, so the cap would not apply. The rule does not cover everyone, including new enrollees and some people who pay the income-related amounts.
