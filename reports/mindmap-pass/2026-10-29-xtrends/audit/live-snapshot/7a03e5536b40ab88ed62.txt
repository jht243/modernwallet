How it works The calculator adds three yearly costs and one monthly cost: maintenance and repairs, property taxes, homeowners insurance and homeowners association (HOA) dues. Its starting values are the national typical amounts from the Zillow and Thumbtack study: $11,171 for maintenance, $3,129 for property taxes and $2,266 for insurance. Those add to $16,566 a year, or $1,380.50 a month on top of the mortgage. These starting values are typical amounts reported for the whole country. They are not a quote for any house. In our internal cost modeling, we ran the calculator's arithmetic over seven homeowner scenarios, including a low-maintenance home, a condo with HOA dues and a high-insurance market. We chose those scenarios ourselves to see how far one household's cost can sit from the national typical. None of them was measured on a real house or drawn from a client case, and you can enter the same numbers in the calculator above to check them. With the national inputs, maintenance alone is $930.92 a month. Maintenance takes 67% of the total, property taxes 19% and insurance 14%. With nothing entered, the calculator returns $0 and asks for the three yearly figures. Maintenance, property taxes and insurance sit on separate lines because you find each number in a different place. Your property tax figure is the yearly amount on your tax bill. The calculator leaves out the income-tax effects of owning, and our tax deductions for homeowners guide covers that side of property taxes. Your insurance figure is the yearly premium on a homeowners insurance quote. Maintenance has no bill, so you enter a yearly reserve for upkeep and repairs. In the study, Thumbtack built the maintenance figure from home projects requested on Thumbtack, and Zillow estimated property taxes and insurance for a typical U.S. homeowner. If your home has HOA dues, enter the monthly amount and the calculator multiplies it by 12. The calculator projects your costs forward with a simple growth rule. Year one is your yearly total. Each later year is the previous year times one plus the growth rate, and the projection total is the sum of the years. The default growth rate is 3%. In the study, overall costs rose 2.8% over the last year, which rounds to about 3%. At the national inputs, five years at 3% growth total $87,951. The fifth year alone comes to $18,645. With no growth over ten years, the total is $165,660. Maintenance growth slowed to 1% in 2026. It had been 9% in 2022. The national typical can sit far from one household's real cost, and three of our modeled cases show how far. A low-maintenance home with $3,000 of yearly upkeep and the typical tax and insurance figures costs $8,395 a year. That is $699.58 a month, or $8,171 below the national typical. Our condo case uses $3,000 for maintenance, $3,129 for property taxes and $800 for insurance. Those three lines add to $6,929, which is $9,637 below the national typical. Add $400 a month of HOA dues, or $4,800 a year, and the household total is $11,729. That is $977.42 a month, and HOA dues make up 41% of it. The comparison line in the calculator leaves HOA out, so it shows the $9,637 gap. Compare the full $11,729 total against the national $16,566 as a separate step. A high-insurance market moves the cost the other way, and the release puts Miami insurance at nearly $5,000 a year. Put $5,000 of insurance into the national inputs and the total becomes $19,300 a year. That is $1,608.33 a month and $2,734 above the national typical. Its five-year total is $102,466. Over five years, the national total in the study is up 31.7%. Insurance rose fastest, up 54%. Maintenance is up 32% and property taxes are up 19% over the same period. Those changes describe what already happened. They do not predict the next five years, so the growth rate stays an input you can edit. If your own insurance quotes have climbed faster than your other costs, raise the growth rate to see a more cautious projection. The mortgage line is optional.
