## The calculator (describe only what it does)
Inputs (all with starting values): maintenance and repairs per year ($11,171), property taxes per year ($3,129), homeowners insurance per year ($2,266), HOA dues per month (0), mortgage principal and interest per month (optional, blank/0), home value (optional, 0), yearly growth in these costs (3%), years to project (5).
Outputs: cost of owning on top of the mortgage per month (the headline), per year, maintenance set-aside per month, total over the projection years and the final-year figure at the growth rate, whether the reader's maintenance, tax and insurance run above or below the national typical (HOA is left out of that comparison), share of each line, and, only when entered, mortgage plus these costs per month and the yearly cost as a percent of home value.
Method the page may state: add the three yearly lines and 12 times the monthly HOA; divide by 12 for the monthly figure; year 1 is that total and each later year is the previous year times one plus the growth rate; the projection total is the sum of the years.
Assumptions the page must state: the starting figures are national typical amounts reported from the 2026 Zillow and Thumbtack study, not a quote for any house; utilities, furnishings, remodeling the owner chooses to do, mortgage insurance and income-tax effects of owning are not included; 3% is the reported one-year change in the national figure and is used as a planning default, not a forecast.
Provenance (calculated, internal modeling): "In our internal cost modeling, we ran the calculator's arithmetic over seven homeowner scenarios, including a low-maintenance home, a condo with HOA dues and a high-insurance market, to see how far one household's cost can sit from the national typical." Say the scenarios are modeled, chosen by us to test the boundaries, not measured on real houses and not client cases, and that the reader can enter the same numbers in the calculator above to check them. Name the starting figures as the Zillow and Thumbtack release of October 22, 2026, and link that release at the first finding.
Takeaway (in howItWorks, near the end): treat $1,380 a month as the national typical starting point, then replace it with your own insurance quote, property tax bill and a maintenance reserve, and budget the sum. A newer home or a condo whose association pays for exteriors can run well below the typical (the $3,000-maintenance scenario is $8,395 a year) and a high-premium market can run well above (the $5,000-insurance scenario is $19,300). The condition that changes the answer is your own quotes. Who this is not for: someone who already has a full year of their own bills, who should use those numbers directly; or someone comparing renting with buying, who should use the rent vs buy calculator. Informational, not financial advice.

- R1. The typical annual hidden cost of owning a home is $16,566, about $1,380 a month, in addition to a mortgage payment. The mortgage payment is not included.
- R2. Maintenance is $11,171 a year, the largest category at about two-thirds of the total. Property taxes are $3,129 a year. Homeowners insurance is $2,266 a year. These sum to $16,566.
- R3. Over five years the total is up 31.7% (about 32%, nearly one-third). Insurance is up 54%, the largest increase. Maintenance is up 32%. Property taxes are up 19%.
- R4. Over the last year overall costs rose 2.8% (about 3%). Maintenance growth slowed from 9% in 2022 to 1% in 2026.
- R5. The study covers the typical U.S. homeowner and 30 metro areas, and costs vary by market. Zillow estimated property taxes and insurance for a typical U.S. homeowner in each metro, and Thumbtack supplied the maintenance figure, built from home projects requested on Thumbtack. The release does not define a typical home or state the home value behind the typical figures.
- R6. Across the 30 metros the total ranges from $14,187 in Phoenix to $25,111 in New York. Insurance is up at least 50% over five years in 19 of the 30 metros. Miami has the highest insurance cost, nearly $5,000 a year. Six metros top $3,000 a year for insurance.
