Cash Conversion Cycle Calculator

A cash conversion cycle calculator measures the number of days a business takes to convert inventory investments into cash collections from customers. It tracks your working capital timeline. For example, take a business with $600,000 in annual cost of goods sold and $1,000,000 in annual revenue. It maintains $90,000 in average inventory, $110,000 in average receivables, and $70,000 in average payables over a 365-day year. Its inventory sits for 54.8 days. Customer payments take 40.2 days to arrive. The company takes 42.6 days to pay its own suppliers. This produces a cash conversion cycle of 52.4 days. Every dollar invested in inventory remains tied up for over seven weeks before flowing back into the company. At ModernWallet, we see business owners use this metric to decide how much financing they need to bridge operating gaps. That 52.4-day span is a funding gap. You have paid your vendors, but customer cash has not yet arrived. Without cash reserves, you must finance those 52.4 days of operations. Many owners guess their financing needs and take on excess debt. This cash conversion cycle calculator provides an exact day count based on your financial statements. You can immediately see whether collections, inventory turnover, or vendor terms drain working capital.

Social Security Break-Even Calculator

A Social Security break even calculator finds your break-even age, which is the age past which claiming later instead of earlier has paid you more in total. For anyone born in 1960 or later, full retirement age is 67. Waiting from 62 to full retirement age breaks even at age 78 and 8 months. Waiting from full retirement age to 70 breaks even at age 82 and 6 months. Enter your own benefit, birth year and two claiming ages in the calculator above to find your break-even age. For example, take a $2,000 monthly benefit at full retirement age for someone born in 1962. Claiming at 62 pays $1,400 a month, and claiming at 67 pays $2,000. Claiming at 70 pays $2,480 a month. By age 80, the claim at 62 has paid $302,400 in total. The claim at 67 has paid $312,000 by then. By age 85, those totals reach $386,400 and $432,000.

Mileage Reimbursement Calculator

A mileage reimbursement calculator works out what you're owed by multiplying business miles by a rate per mile. Unless your employer sets its own rate, use the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) business rate: 72.5 cents a mile for January through June 2026, and 76 cents a mile from July 1. Enter your miles for each half of the year in the calculator above, plus your employer's rate if it has one. Under an accountable plan, a reimbursement at or below the IRS rate is not taxable wages. For example, say you drive 1,200 business miles from January to June and 1,000 miles from July to December. At the IRS rates, the first half pays $870 and the second half pays $760. That's a total reimbursement of $1,630, a blended rate of about 74.09 cents a mile. If your employer pays 80 cents a mile instead, you get $1,760. The $130 above the IRS-rate amount counts as taxable wages. At 60 cents a mile, you get $1,320, which is $310 less than the IRS-rate amount.

Charitable Donation Deduction Calculator

Charitable donations lower your 2026 taxes only if your itemized deductions exceed the standard deduction, or through the non-itemizer cash allowance. Most households take the standard deduction, which is $16,100 for single filers and $32,200 for married couples filing jointly under IRS 2026 inflation adjustments. Non-itemizers can deduct cash gifts up to $1,000, or $2,000 on a joint return. Itemizers face a floor of 0.5% of adjusted gross income (AGI). The charitable donation tax deduction calculator below compares both routes, shows what your giving adds, and tests two-year bunching. The standard deduction plus the $2,000 cash allowance gives them $34,200, so the standard route wins. Giving adds $2,000 of deductions, which saves $440 at a 22% marginal tax rate.

Stock Capital Gains Tax Calculator

Your tax depends on how long you held the stock and how much other income you have. Stock held more than one year gets the long-term rates. Those rates are 0%, 15% or 20%, depending on where the gain lands on top of your other taxable income. A gain on stock held one year or less is taxed as ordinary income. High earners also add the 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax. The stock capital gains tax calculator below shows your gain, federal tax, surtax and proceeds after tax for 2026. In our internal calculation, we took a single filer with $85,000 of other taxable income. They sell stock bought for $30,000 for $50,000, which is a $20,000 gain. If they held it more than one year, the gain is taxed at 15%, so the tax is $3,000. If they held it one year or less, the same gain is taxed at 22% as ordinary income. That tax is $4,400. Holding past one year saves $1,400.
