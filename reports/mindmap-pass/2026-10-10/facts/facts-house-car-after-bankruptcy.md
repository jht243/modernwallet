# Closed fact list — buying a house or car after bankruptcy

Compiled 2026-10-10. Every bullet is one fact, its source URL, and a date (seen = date read).

Legend: `[S]` = confirmed only from a search-engine snippet. `**secondary — unverified**` = not verified at the primary source. Quoted text is the source's wording. Where a primary page could not be loaded, this says so. The writer must not fill gaps from memory. "Waiting period" figures are the agencies' minimums. - TOTAL Scorecard (automated) path, section II.A.4.b.iii(F) "Bankruptcy (TOTAL)": "The Mortgagee must document the passage of two years since the discharge date of any bankruptcy. If the bankruptcy was discharged within two years from the date of case number assignment, the Mortgage must be downgraded to a Refer and manually underwritten." **secondary — unverified** [https://www.nrmlaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Handbook-4000.1-Redline-Version-8-13-2025.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Manual underwriting, section II.A.5.a.iii(H)(1) "Standard: Chapter 7": "A Chapter 7 bankruptcy (liquidation) does not disqualify a Borrower from obtaining an FHA-insured Mortgage if, at the time of case number assignment, at least two years have elapsed since the date of the bankruptcy discharge." **secondary — unverified** [https://www.nrmlaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Handbook-4000.1-Redline-Version-8-13-2025.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Same Chapter 7 paragraph: "During the most recent two years, the Borrower must have: reestablished good credit; or chosen not to incur new credit obligations." **secondary — unverified** [https://www.nrmlaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Handbook-4000.1-Redline-Version-8-13-2025.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 7 exception: "An elapsed period of less than two years, but not less than 12 months, may be acceptable, if the Borrower: can show that the bankruptcy was caused by extenuating circumstances beyond the Borrower's control; and has since exhibited a documented ability to manage their financial affairs in a responsible manner." **secondary — unverified** [https://www.nrmlaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Handbook-4000.1-Redline-Version-8-13-2025.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Manual underwriting, II.A.5.a.iii(H)(2) "Standard: Chapter 13": "A Chapter 13 bankruptcy does not disqualify a Borrower from obtaining an FHA-insured Mortgage, if at the time of case number assignment at least 12 months of the payout period under the bankruptcy has elapsed." **secondary — unverified** [https://www.nrmlaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Handbook-4000.1-Redline-Version-8-13-2025.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Same Chapter 13 paragraph: "The Mortgagee must determine that during the most recent 12 months, the Borrower's payment performance has been satisfactory and all required payments have been made on time; and the Borrower has received written permission from bankruptcy court to enter into the mortgage transaction." The forward-mortgage text says "bankruptcy court" and does not mention the trustee. **secondary — unverified** [https://www.nrmlaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Handbook-4000.1-Redline-Version-8-13-2025.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Manual-underwriting link (inference from the two sections above, not a single HUD sentence): any bankruptcy discharged less than two years before case number assignment must be downgraded to manual underwriting. That covers a Chapter 7 in the 12–24-month extenuating window and a Chapter 13 still in its payout period. **secondary — unverified** [https://www.nrmlaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Handbook-4000.1-Redline-Version-8-13-2025.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)

Also: "The Mortgagee must also document that the Borrower's current situation indicates that the events which led to the bankruptcy are not likely to recur." **secondary — unverified** [https://www.nrmlaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Handbook-4000.1-Redline-Version-8-13-2025.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The clock runs from "case number assignment", not from the application or closing date (FHA wording in both sections above). **secondary — unverified** [https://www.nrmlaonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Handbook-4000.1-Redline-Version-8-13-2025.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)

- Chapter 7, 1–2 years: "If the bankruptcy was discharged within the last 1 to 2 years, it is probably not possible to determine that the borrower or spouse is a satisfactory credit risk unless both of the following requirements are met". VA's Table 3 lists the two requirements: (1) "The borrower(s) had obtained consumer items on credit subsequent to the bankruptcy and has satisfactorily made the payments over a continued period." (2) "The bankruptcy was caused by circumstances beyond the control of the borrower or spouse such as unemployment, prolonged strikes, medical bills not covered by insurance, and so on, and the circumstances are verified." [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- VA on divorce: "Divorce is not generally viewed as beyond the control of the borrower and/or spouse." [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 7, under 12 months: "If the bankruptcy was discharged within the past 12 months, it will generally not be possible to determine that the borrower(s) is a satisfactory credit risk." [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Business-failure bankruptcy: if "caused by failure of the business of a self-employed borrower", VA may find a satisfactory credit risk if all four Table 4 conditions are met: a permanent position obtained after the business failed; no derogatory credit before the self-employment; no derogatory credit after the bankruptcy; and failure "was not due to the borrower's misconduct." [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 13, completed plan: "If the borrower(s) has finished making all payments satisfactorily, the lender may conclude that the borrower has re-established satisfactory credit." [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 13, during plan: "If the borrowers) has satisfactorily made at least 12 months' worth of the payments and the Trustee or the Bankruptcy Judge approves of the new credit, the lender may give favorable consideration." (The typo "borrowers)" is in VA's text.) [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- VA describes Chapter 13 payments as made "to a court- appointed trustee over a 2 to 3-year period or, in some cases, up to 5 years". [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "VA does not have a minimum credit score requirement." (Same Topic 7.) [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Lack of new credit after bankruptcy is not automatically negative. VA says absence of credit history "is not generally considered an adverse factor" and may result when the borrower "has not used since some disruptive credit event such as bankruptcy". [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- When a foreclosure, deed-in-lieu or short sale happens "in conjunction with a bankruptcy, use the latest date of either the discharge of the bankruptcy or transfer of title for the home to establish the beginning date of re-established credit." [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Unlike FHA (case number assignment), VA measures the Chapter 7 two years "from the date of closing". This comparison of the two quoted texts is ours.

- 7 CFR 3555.151(i)(4): "When evidence of significant derogatory credit is present, lenders may consider extenuating circumstances, including but not limited to, whether the problems were caused by factors temporary in nature, if the circumstances leading to the derogatory credit were beyond the control of the applicant, and if the loan would significantly reduce the applicant's housing expenses." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/7/3555.151] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 7 CFR 3555.151(i)(2): "A loan's acceptance by an Agency approved automated underwriting system eliminates the need for the lender to submit documentation of the credit qualification decision". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/7/3555.151] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Wording guardrail: the regulation calls a discharge within 36 months an "indicator of significant derogatory credit" that needs "further review". It does not use the term "waiting period". Write "USDA treats a bankruptcy discharged within 3 years (36 months) as significant derogatory credit", not "USDA bans borrowers for 3 years". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/7/3555.151] (seen 2026-10-10)

## 4. Fannie Mae (Selling Guide B3-5.3-07 and B3-5.3-08)

B3-5.3-07 shows topic date "(08/07/2019)". The Selling Guide PDF footer read "Published: October 07 2026".

- Chapter 7 or 11: "A four-year waiting period is required, measured from the discharge or dismissal date of the bankruptcy action." [https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.3-07/significant-derogatory-credit-events-waiting-periods-and-re-establishing-credit] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 7 or 11 exception: "A two-year waiting period is permitted if extenuating circumstances can be documented". [https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.3-07/significant-derogatory-credit-events-waiting-periods-and-re-establishing-credit] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 13: the waiting period is "two years from the discharge date," or "four years from the dismissal date." Also: "A borrower who was unable to complete the Chapter 13 plan and received a dismissal will be held to a four-year waiting period." [https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.3-07/significant-derogatory-credit-events-waiting-periods-and-re-establishing-credit] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 13 exceptions: "A two-year waiting period is permitted after a Chapter 13 dismissal, if extenuating circumstances can be documented." Also: "There are no exceptions permitted to the two-year waiting period after a Chapter 13 discharge." [https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.3-07/significant-derogatory-credit-events-waiting-periods-and-re-establishing-credit] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Multiple bankruptcies: "a five-year waiting period is required, measured from the most recent dismissal or discharge date." The summary table applies it "if more than one filing within the past 7 years". Exception: "A three-year waiting period is permitted if extenuating circumstances can be documented". [https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.3-07/significant-derogatory-credit-events-waiting-periods-and-re-establishing-credit] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Re-establishing credit: after a significant derogatory event, "the borrower's credit will be considered re-established if all of the following are met". The conditions are: "The waiting period and the related additional requirements are met."; "The loan receives a recommendation from DU that is acceptable for delivery to Fannie Mae"; "The borrower has traditional credit as outlined in Section B3–5.3, Traditional Credit History."; and "Nontraditional credit or "thin files" are not acceptable." [https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.3-07/significant-derogatory-credit-events-waiting-periods-and-re-establishing-credit] (seen 2026-10-10)
- DU note: B3-5.3-07 has no separate Desktop Underwriter rule beyond requiring a DU recommendation "acceptable for delivery" (above). It points to the DU documentation for "how the waiting period is determined." A separate DU-specific waiting-period rule was NOT FOUND — do not state one. [https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.3-07/significant-derogatory-credit-events-waiting-periods-and-re-establishing-credit] (seen 2026-10-10)
- For comparison, same table: foreclosure is "7 years" ("3 years" with extenuating circumstances). Deed-in-lieu, preforeclosure sale or charge-off of a mortgage account is "4 years" ("2 years"). [https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.3-07/significant-derogatory-credit-events-waiting-periods-and-re-establishing-credit] (seen 2026-10-10)
- B3-5.3-08 (dated 12/16/2014) defines extenuating circumstances as "nonrecurring events that are beyond the borrower's control that result in a sudden, significant, and prolonged reduction in income or a catastrophic increase in financial obligations." The second half of this sentence was first seen in a search snippet. The fetched page confirmed the opening words and described the rest in matching terms. [https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.3-08/extenuating-circumstances-derogatory-credit] (seen 2026-10-10)
- B3-5.3-08: the lender must obtain a written explanation (letter, email or other written documentation) showing "that the borrower had no reasonable options other than to default on his or her financial obligations". Example supporting documents include "a copy of a divorce decree, medical reports or bills, notice of job layoff" and job severance papers. [https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.3-08/extenuating-circumstances-derogatory-credit] (seen 2026-10-10)

Section 5202.1 "Credit assessment for Manually Underwritten Mortgages" is "Effective 02/04/2026". The task's "5202.5" is an older section number. The current text is in 5202.1. These are Freddie Mac's "recovery time periods" for manual underwriting. A Loan Product Advisor (automated) equivalent was NOT FOUND — do not state one.

- Financial-mismanagement table: "Chapter 7 or Chapter 11 bankruptcy — Recovery time period 48 months from the discharge or dismissal date". [https://guide.freddiemac.com/app/guide/section/5202.1] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Financial-mismanagement table: "Chapter 12 or Chapter 13 bankruptcy — Recovery time period 24 months after the discharge date or 48 months from the dismissal date". [https://guide.freddiemac.com/app/guide/section/5202.1] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Financial-mismanagement table: "Multiple bankruptcy filings in the past seven years — Recovery time period 60 months from the most recent discharge or dismissal date". [https://guide.freddiemac.com/app/guide/section/5202.1] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Extenuating-circumstances table: "Bankruptcy (all bankruptcy actions) — Recovery time period 24 months from the discharge or dismissal date". [https://guide.freddiemac.com/app/guide/section/5202.1] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Freddie Mac definition: "an extenuating circumstance to be a nonrecurring or isolated circumstance or set of circumstances that was beyond the Borrower's control and that significantly reduced income and/or increased expenses and rendered the Borrower unable to repay obligations as agreed". [https://guide.freddiemac.com/app/guide/section/5202.1] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "If the Borrower's credit history includes significant adverse or derogatory credit within the most recent two years, even if it was caused by extenuating circumstances, the Borrower's credit reputation cannot be considered acceptable." [https://guide.freddiemac.com/app/guide/section/5202.1] (seen 2026-10-10)
- For a bankruptcy within the last seven years, the file must also contain "Copies of the bankruptcy petition, schedule of debts and discharge or dismissal", "Evidence to indicate that all debts not satisfied by the bankruptcy have been paid or are being paid", and other evidence that the borrower "has reestablished and maintained an acceptable credit reputation". [https://guide.freddiemac.com/app/guide/section/5202.1] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Freddie Mac on mismanagement: "It will take a longer and more convincing reestablishment period to overcome derogatory information caused by financial mismanagement than would be needed if the Borrower had experienced financial difficulties due to extenuating circumstances." [https://guide.freddiemac.com/app/guide/section/5202.1] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Freddie Mac allows the Chapter 7 recovery period to be counted from the bankruptcy discharge date for a foreclosure on "a Mortgage that was extinguished in Chapter 7 bankruptcy". This requires that the foreclosure proceedings "did not begin before the bankruptcy filing" and that "The Mortgage was not reaffirmed through the bankruptcy". [https://guide.freddiemac.com/app/guide/section/5202.1] (seen 2026-10-10)

## 7. Car — Chapter 7: reaffirmation, redemption, surrender, deadlines

- Reaffirmation must come before discharge. 11 U.S.C. §524(c)(1): the agreement is enforceable only if it "was made before the granting of the discharge under section 727, 1141, 1192, 1228, or 1328 of this title". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §524(c)(2): "the debtor received the disclosures described in subsection (k) at or before the time at which the debtor signed the agreement". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §524(c)(3): the agreement must be "filed with the court" and, if the debtor had a lawyer, come with the attorney's declaration that it "represents a fully informed and voluntary agreement by the debtor" and "does not impose an undue hardship on the debtor or a dependent of the debtor". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Rescission, §524(c)(4): the debtor can rescind "at any time prior to discharge or within sixty days after such agreement is filed with the court, whichever occurs later, by giving notice of rescission to the holder of such claim". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §524(c)(6): for an unrepresented debtor, "the court approves such agreement as— (i) not imposing an undue hardship on the debtor or a dependent of the debtor; and (ii) in the best interest of the debtor." This does not apply to "a consumer debt secured by real property." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §524(k)(2) disclosures: "The terms "Amount Reaffirmed" and "Annual Percentage Rate" shall be disclosed more conspicuously than other terms". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §524(m)(1) presumption: for 60 days after filing, "it shall be presumed that such agreement is an undue hardship on the debtor if the debtor's monthly income less the debtor's monthly expenses ... is less than the scheduled payments on the reaffirmed debt." §524(m)(2): "This subsection does not apply to reaffirmation agreements where the creditor is a credit union". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Redemption, 11 U.S.C. §722: an individual debtor may "redeem tangible personal property intended primarily for personal, family, or household use, from a lien securing a dischargeable consumer debt, if such property is exempted under section 522 of this title or has been abandoned under section 554 of this title, by paying the holder of such lien the amount of the allowed secured claim of such holder that is secured by such lien in full at the time of redemption." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/722] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Lump-sum point: "in full at the time of redemption" was added by Pub. L. 109–8 (2005), per LII's amendment notes. The House report (95–595) says redemption "is accomplished by paying the holder of the lien the amount of the allowed claim secured by the lien". Treat "allowed secured claim" as roughly the collateral's value only by citing §722 plus §506. Do not state a valuation method. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/722] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Statement of intention, §521(a)(2)(A): file "within thirty days after the date of the filing of a petition under chapter 7 of this title or on or before the date of the meeting of creditors, whichever is earlier, or within such additional time as the court, for cause, within such period fixes". The statement must say whether the debtor will retain or surrender the property and whether the debtor "intends to redeem such property, or ... intends to reaffirm debts secured by such property". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/521] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §521(a)(2)(B): the debtor must perform the stated intention "within 30 days after the first date set for the meeting of creditors under section 341(a), or within such additional time as the court, for cause, within such 30-day period fixes". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/521] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §521(a)(6): an individual Chapter 7 debtor may "not retain possession of personal property as to which a creditor has an allowed claim for the purchase price secured in whole or in part by an interest in such personal property unless the debtor, not later than 45 days after the first meeting of creditors under section 341(a), either— (A) enters into an agreement with the creditor pursuant to section 524(c) ... or (B) redeems such property from the security interest pursuant to section 722". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/521] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Consequence under §521(a): "If the debtor fails to so act within the 45-day period referred to in paragraph (6), the stay under section 362(a) is terminated with respect to the personal property of the estate or of the debtor which is affected, such property shall no longer be property of the estate, and the creditor may take whatever action as to such property as is permitted by applicable nonbankruptcy law". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/521] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §362(h)(1): for an individual, the automatic stay "is terminated with respect to personal property of the estate or of the debtor securing in whole or in part a claim" if the debtor fails, within the §521(a)(2) time, to file the statement of intention or "to take timely the action specified in such statement". The exception is where the statement "specifies the debtor's intention to reaffirm such debt on the original contract terms and the creditor refuses to agree to the reaffirmation on such terms." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/362] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Surrender is one of the statutory options. §362(h)(1)(A) refers to a statement that indicates the debtor "will either surrender such personal property or retain it". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/362] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts: "If the debtor decides to reaffirm a debt, he or she must do so before the discharge is entered." Also: "Unless the debtor is represented by an attorney, the bankruptcy judge must approve the reaffirmation agreement." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts: a Chapter 7 discharge is entered "generally, 60 to 90 days after the date first set for the meeting of creditors." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

## 8. Car — Chapter 13: the 910-day rule and new car loans

- The "hanging paragraph" at the end of 11 U.S.C. §1325(a): "For purposes of paragraph (5), section 506 shall not apply to a claim described in that paragraph if the creditor has a purchase money security interest securing the debt that is the subject of the claim, the debt was incurred within the 910-day period preceding the date of the filing of the petition, and the collateral for that debt consists of a motor vehicle (as defined in section 30102 of title 49) acquired for the personal use of the debtor, or if collateral for that debt consists of any other thing of value, if the debt was incurred during the 1-year period preceding that filing." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1325] (seen 2026-10-10)
- LII notes this concluding text was added in 2005 by Pub. L. 109–8, §306(b). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1325] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts explains the effect: when "the obligation underlying the secured claim was used to buy the collateral (e.g., a car loan)" and was incurred within the look-back period, the plan must pay the full debt, not just the collateral's value. (Only the quoted fragment is verbatim; the rest paraphrases the page.) [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §1305(c): a post-petition consumer-debt claim "shall be disallowed if the holder of such claim knew or should have known that prior approval by the trustee of the debtor's incurring the obligation was practicable and was not obtained." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1305] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The House report (95–595) reproduced on LII gives "auto repairs in order that the debtor will be able to get to work" as an example of a post-petition claim for property or services "necessary for the debtor's performance under the plan". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1305] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts: during Chapter 13, "the debtor may not incur new debt without consulting the trustee". [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts on plan length: "the plan will be for three years unless the court approves a longer period" for below-median debtors, and "the plan generally must be for five years" for above-median debtors. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- One court's process, N.D. Ohio Administrative Order No. 21-1 (Canton and Cleveland): "Notwithstanding any restrictions in postpetition borrowing contained in confirmation orders, a debtor wishing to borrow money to finance the purchase of a vehicle may also obtain approval under the procedures set forth in this Administrative Order." [https://www.ohnb.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/administrative_orders/ao-21-1-governing-postpetition-vehicle-financing-chapter-13-cases-canton-and-cleveland-court.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Under that order, trustees post what they need, "such as: an explanation why the new or replacement vehicle is needed, the monthly loan/lease payment, and the interest rate". They also post evaluation parameters, "such as: fitting within the debtor's current or amended budget, a monthly payment of no more than $X, and an interest rate of no more than Y%." ("$X" and "Y%" are placeholders in the order, not figures.) [https://www.ohnb.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/administrative_orders/ao-21-1-governing-postpetition-vehicle-financing-chapter-13-cases-canton-and-cleveland-court.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- In that order's two-step process, the trustee first issues a "shopping letter" stating consent "with payments not to exceed a specified amount". After the debtor submits the deal (make/model/VIN, price, loan terms), the trustee issues a "final approval" letter if the terms "match (or are better than)" the shopping letter. The sample letter warns: "Trustee's final approval of the indebtedness must occur Prior to the purchase or lease signature - Not After". [https://www.ohnb.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/administrative_orders/ao-21-1-governing-postpetition-vehicle-financing-chapter-13-cases-canton-and-cleveland-court.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The order's sample letters say repayment "must be made directly by the Debtor(s) outside of the Chapter 13 Plan". [https://www.ohnb.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/administrative_orders/ao-21-1-governing-postpetition-vehicle-financing-chapter-13-cases-canton-and-cleveland-court.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- W.D. Washington Local Bankruptcy Rule 3015-2 (effective 12/01/2023): "a debtor may make a written request directly to the chapter 13 trustee for authority to incur post-confirmation debt". With trustee approval, no separate court order is needed. A debtor is "not precluded from submitting the request to the court by motion". [https://www.wawb.uscourts.gov/node/883] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Procedures differ by district (the two examples above differ). The writer must say "check your district's local rules and your trustee's website" and must not present one district's process as national.

## 9. Car financing protections (CFPB, FTC)

- CFPB on dealer financing: a lender's quote to the dealer "is known as a "buy rate." Interest rates through a dealer are generally higher because the rate they offer you is their "buy rate" plus additional interest that compensates them for handling your financing." Last reviewed Nov 08, 2022. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-dealer-arranged-and-bank-financing-en-759/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "Most dealers will generally reach out to roughly five lenders and then choose one loan to present to you." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-dealer-arranged-and-bank-financing-en-759/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB on preapproval for car loans: "The first step is to get preapproved by a bank or other lender. This will give you a loan quote, which will include an interest rate, loan length, and maximum loan amount". [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-dealer-arranged-and-bank-financing-en-759/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB on Buy Here, Pay Here: these dealers "finance loans to borrowers with no or poor credit histories. They may also advertise, "No Credit, No Problem."" Also: "The interest rate at these types of dealerships tends to be higher." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-dealer-arranged-and-bank-financing-en-759/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "Even without a strong credit score or history, it may be worth checking with a bank or credit union to see if you could get a loan with better terms." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-dealer-arranged-and-bank-financing-en-759/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB on rate shopping: "keep these credit inquiries within 14 to 45 days of each other so they're counted as only one inquiry." Last reviewed Aug 28, 2023. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-know-before-i-shop-for-auto-loan-at-a-bank-credit-union-dealership-or-other-lender-en-755/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "In general, the lower your credit score, the more likely you'll receive a higher interest rate, which would cost you more over the life of your loan." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-know-before-i-shop-for-auto-loan-at-a-bank-credit-union-dealership-or-other-lender-en-755/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB on add-ons: "Add-on products and services are optional. You're not required to purchase them, but if you choose to, the price is negotiable." The list includes "Extended warranties or service contracts", "GAP insurance", and "Credit insurance or debt cancellation or suspension products". [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-things-can-i-negotiate-when-shopping-for-a-car-or-auto-loan-en-2132/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB on loan length: "While a longer loan can reduce your monthly payment, you'll end up paying more interest over the life of your loan." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-things-can-i-negotiate-when-shopping-for-a-car-or-auto-loan-en-2132/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB on GAP: "GAP is an optional product that is intended to cover the difference between the amount you owe on your auto loan and the amount the insurance company pays if your car is stolen or totaled." Last reviewed Mar 08, 2024. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-guaranteed-asset-protection-gap-insurance-en-797/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: if told GAP is required for financing and that is true, "the cost of the GAP insurance must be included in the finance charge and reflected in the disclosed annual percentage rate (APR). If it's optional, you can decline it." Also: "You have the right to cancel these optional add-on products at any time and reduce your costs." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-guaranteed-asset-protection-gap-insurance-en-797/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "You have the right to cancel an extended warranty or vehicle service contract at any time and end the coverage." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-an-extended-warranty-or-vehicle-service-contract-en-729/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB on closing: the federal Truth in Lending Act "requires lenders to provide you with written disclosures with all of the important details of your loan terms fully filled out before you sign the paperwork". Also: "If the dealer asks you to sign a blank or partially filled in form, you should decline." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-know-before-i-finalize-a-car-or-auto-loan-en-2131/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB data (Data Point No. 2021-10, September 2021, "Subprime Auto Loan Outcomes by Lender Type"; subprime = credit score of 620 or less): "average interest rates at banks are approximately 10 percent, compared to 15 percent to 20 percent at finance companies and buy-here-pay-here dealerships." [https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_subprime-auto_data-point_2021-09.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Same Data Point: "the likelihood of a subprime auto loan becoming at least 60 days delinquent within three years is approximately 15 percent for bank borrowers and between 25 percent and 40 percent for finance company and buy-here-pay-here borrowers." [https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_subprime-auto_data-point_2021-09.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Same Data Point: a "shallow subprime" borrower with the same modeled default rate would pay an estimated "13 percent with a loan from a small buy-here-pay here lender, while it would be nine percent with a loan from a bank". That is a saving of "roughly $894 over the life of a loan" at the median term and amount. These are 2021 figures and must always be dated as such. [https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_subprime-auto_data-point_2021-09.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC Used Car Rule (16 CFR Part 455): "It requires car dealers to display a window sticker, known as a Buyers Guide, on the used cars they offer for sale." [https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/rules/rulemaking-regulatory-reform-proceedings/used-car-rule] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC consumer page: "Dealers have to display a Buyers Guide in every used car they offer for sale." For an as-is sale, the box "next to the "As Is - No Dealer Warranty" disclosure on the Buyers Guide must be checked". The Buyers Guide "will override anything in your sales contract." Page shows "April 2024". [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buying-used-car-dealer] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC: "Shop around, compare offers, and negotiate the best deal you can," and "make sure you understand the financing agreement before you sign any documents." [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buying-used-car-dealer] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC page on spot delivery / "yo-yo" financing: NOT FOUND (consumer.ftc.gov/articles/understanding-vehicle-financing returned 404) — do not state.
- A CFPB BHPH-specific research report: NOT FOUND. The only CFPB BHPH data found is the 2021 Data Point above — do not claim a dedicated CFPB BHPH report.
