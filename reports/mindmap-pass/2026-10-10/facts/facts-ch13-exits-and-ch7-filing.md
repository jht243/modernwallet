# TOPIC 1 — Getting out of a Chapter 13 plan early

## Plan length (§1322(d)) and applicable commitment period (§1325(b)(4))

- §1322(d)(1): if the combined current monthly income of the debtor and spouse, times 12, is **not less than** the applicable state median family income, "the plan may not provide for payments over a period that is longer than 5 years." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1322] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1322(d)(2): if that income is **less than** the applicable median, "the plan may not provide for payments over a period that is longer than 3 years, unless the court, for cause, approves a longer period, but the court may not approve a period that is longer than 5 years." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1322] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1325(b)(4)(A): the "applicable commitment period" is "3 years" or "not less than 5 years" if the combined current monthly income × 12 is not less than the applicable state median family income. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1325] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1325(b)(4)(B): the applicable commitment period "may be less than 3 or 5 years, whichever is applicable under subparagraph (A), but only if the plan provides for payment in full of all allowed unsecured claims over a shorter period." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1325] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The per-person add-on for households larger than 4 in §§1322(d) and 1325(b) was adjusted from $825 to **$925** ("each time it appears"), effective April 1, 2025 (Judicial Conference notice, 90 FR 8941). [https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-02-04/html/2025-02207.htm] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "If the debtor's current monthly income is less than the applicable state median, the plan will be for three years unless the court approves a longer period 'for cause.' … If the debtor's current monthly income is greater than the applicable state median, the plan generally must be for five years. In no case may a plan provide for payments over a period longer than five years." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "The plan may be less than the applicable commitment period (three or five years) only if unsecured debt is paid in full over a shorter period." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Chapter 13 basics (uscourts.gov)

- Chapter 13 "allows a debtor to keep property and pay debts over time, usually three to five years." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "The chapter 13 trustee both evaluates the case and serves as a disbursing agent, collecting payments from the debtor and making distributions to creditors." (citing 11 U.S.C. § 1302(b)) [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "Within 30 days after filing the bankruptcy case, even if the plan has not yet been approved by the court, the debtor must start making plan payments to the trustee." (citing § 1326(a)(1)) [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The plan "must provide for payments of fixed amounts to the trustee on a regular basis, typically biweekly or monthly." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "The debtor must make regular payments to the trustee either directly or through payroll deduction." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 13 fees per uscourts.gov: "a $235 case filing fee and a $75 miscellaneous administrative fee." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "A fee of $25 is charged for converting a case under chapter 13 to a case under chapter 7." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Discharge timing: a chapter 13 debtor "is entitled to a discharge upon completion of all payments under the chapter 13 plan" if the debtor certifies domestic support obligations are paid (if applicable), has no disqualifying prior discharge, and has completed an approved financial management course. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- If the debtor fails to make payments under the confirmed plan, "the court may dismiss the case or convert it to a liquidation case under chapter 7." (citing § 1307(c)) [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Full discharge after completing the plan (§1328(a))

- §1328(a): "as soon as practicable after completion by the debtor of all payments under the plan," and after any required domestic-support certification, "the court shall grant the debtor a discharge of all debts provided for by the plan or disallowed under section 502." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1328] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1328(a) exceptions (summary): long-term debts under §1322(b)(5); certain taxes and the debts listed in §523(a)(1)(B), (1)(C), (2), (3), (4), (5), (8), (9); "restitution, or a criminal fine, included in a sentence on the debtor's conviction of a crime"; and restitution or damages for willful or malicious injury causing personal injury or death. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1328] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1328(g)(1): "The court shall not grant a discharge under this section to a debtor unless after filing a petition the debtor has completed an instructional course concerning personal financial management described in section 111." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1328] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "The discharge in a chapter 13 case is somewhat broader than in a chapter 7 case," for example covering debts for willful and malicious injury to property, debts incurred to pay nondischargeable taxes, and divorce property-settlement debts. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Hardship discharge (§1328(b)–(c))

- §1328(b): "at any time after the confirmation of the plan and after notice and a hearing, the court may grant a discharge to a debtor that has not completed payments under the plan only if—" [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1328] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1328(b)(1): "the debtor's failure to complete such payments is due to circumstances for which the debtor should not justly be held accountable;" [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1328] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1328(b)(2): the value of property actually distributed under the plan on each allowed unsecured claim "is not less than the amount that would have been paid on such claim if the estate of the debtor had been liquidated under chapter 7 of this title on such date; and" [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1328] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1328(b)(3): "modification of the plan under section 1329 of this title is not practicable." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1328] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1328(c): a hardship discharge "discharges the debtor from all unsecured debts provided for by the plan or disallowed under section 502 of this title, except any debt— (1) provided for under section 1322(b)(5) of this title; or (2) of a kind specified in section 523(a) of this title." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1328] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "Injury or illness that precludes employment sufficient to fund even a modified plan may serve as the basis for a hardship discharge." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "The hardship discharge is more limited than the discharge described above and does not apply to any debts that are nondischargeable in a chapter 7 case." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Converting to Chapter 7 or dismissing (§1307)

- §1307(a): "The debtor may convert a case under this chapter to a case under chapter 7 of this title at any time. Any waiver of the right to convert under this subsection is unenforceable." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1307] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1307(b): "On request of the debtor at any time, if the case has not been converted under section 706, 1112, or 1208 of this title, the court shall dismiss a case under this chapter. Any waiver of the right to dismiss under this subsection is unenforceable." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1307] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1307(c): on request of a party in interest or the U.S. trustee, after notice and a hearing, the court may convert to chapter 7 or dismiss, "whichever is in the best interests of creditors and the estate, for cause," including "unreasonable delay by the debtor that is prejudicial to creditors" ((c)(1)), "failure to commence making timely payments under section 1326" ((c)(4)), and "material default by the debtor with respect to a term of a confirmed plan" ((c)(6)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1307] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Modifying a confirmed plan (§1329)

- §1329(a): "At any time after confirmation of the plan but before the completion of payments under such plan, the plan may be modified, upon request of the debtor, the trustee, or the holder of an allowed unsecured claim," to: (1) "increase or reduce the amount of payments on claims of a particular class provided for by the plan;" (2) "extend or reduce the time for such payments;" (3) adjust a creditor's distribution to account for payments made outside the plan; or (4) reduce plan payments by documented health-insurance costs meeting stated conditions. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1329] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1329(c): a modified plan may not run past the applicable commitment period measured from when the first payment under the original confirmed plan was due, "unless the court, for cause, approves a longer period, but the court may not approve a period that expires after five years after such time." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1329] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "Modification after confirmation is not limited to an initiative by the debtor, but may be at the request of the trustee or an unsecured creditor." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Paying off a Chapter 13 plan early — CONTESTED (courts are split)

- **CONTESTED.** Whether a debtor can pay off a confirmed plan early **without** paying allowed unsecured claims in full is decided differently by different courts. The bullets below are court opinions, which bind only their own jurisdiction. I did not locate a Supreme Court ruling on the question. Do not state any single rule as national law. [sources below] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Statutory anchor (undisputed): the applicable commitment period may be shorter than 3 or 5 years "only if the plan provides for payment in full of all allowed unsecured claims over a shorter period" (§1325(b)(4)(B)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1325] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Ninth Circuit BAP, *In re Fridley* (Dec. 18, 2007): "A debtor desiring to prepay a chapter 13 plan and obtain an early discharge without paying allowed unsecured claims in full must follow the § 1329 modification procedure." [https://www.caeb.uscourts.gov/documents/Judges/Opinions/published/fridley-07-1276.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- *Fridley* also held that the "applicable commitment period" in §1325(b) "is a temporal requirement of thirty-six months in this case, rather than a multiplicand of monthly payments," and affirmed the refusal of an early discharge. [https://www.caeb.uscourts.gov/documents/Judges/Opinions/published/fridley-07-1276.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Bankr. W.D. Va., *In re Niday* (Aug. 27, 2013): a below-median debtor has no "unqualified right" to pay off early the remaining payments under a 36-month plan when those payments would not pay all allowed unsecured claims in full. The debtor may instead seek modification of the confirmed plan under §1329. The modification must meet the good-faith requirement and needs an evidentiary hearing. [https://www.vawb.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/Niday%2008.27.13.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- *Niday* (describing *In re Murphy*, 474 F.3d 143 (4th Cir. 2007)): refinancing a home mortgage to fund an early payoff did not, by itself, "alter the financial condition of the debtor" enough to support modification under §1329(a)(1) or (a)(2). [https://www.vawb.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/Niday%2008.27.13.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Bankr. S.D. Fla., *In re Tibbs* (ordered Aug. 31, 2012): **granted** debtors' motion to pay off a five-year plan about four years early with a lump sum from a family gift, without paying unsecured claims in full. The court held "§ 1329 does not incorporate § 1325(b), and thus a chapter 13 plan may be modified so that it has a term shorter than the applicable commitment period," as long as the other §1329 requirements are met. [https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-flsb-9_11-bk-18943/pdf/USCOURTS-flsb-9_11-bk-18943-0.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- *Tibbs* records the split: some courts allow such modifications (e.g., *Sunahara*, 9th Cir. BAP 2005; *In re Smith*, Bankr. M.D. Fla. 2011; *In re Davis*, Bankr. N.D. Ill. 2010), and others prohibit them (e.g., *In re Heideker*, Bankr. M.D. Fla. 2011; *In re Stretcher*, Bankr. W.D. Tex. 2011; *In re Buck*, Bankr. N.D. Ga. 2010; *In re King*, Bankr. S.D. Ill. 2010). [https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-flsb-9_11-bk-18943/pdf/USCOURTS-flsb-9_11-bk-18943-0.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- In *Tibbs*, early payoff meant unsecured creditors would get about 6.8% of allowed claims immediately. In return they gave up "the possibility of greater recovery" if the debtors' income or non-exempt assets increased during the original plan term. [https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-flsb-9_11-bk-18943/pdf/USCOURTS-flsb-9_11-bk-18943-0.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Safe, uncontested writer framing: all three opinions treat an early payoff for less than 100% as something that goes through the court, either by §1329 modification or by a motion. None treats it as something a debtor can do just by sending the trustee a lump sum. [Fridley, Niday, Tibbs URLs above] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 13 trustee website statement on early-payoff procedure: **NOT FOUND — do not state** (no trustee site was loaded; procedures vary by district).

## Effect of dismissal (§349) and refiling limits (§362(c)(3), §109(g))

- §349(a): unless the court orders otherwise for cause, dismissal "does not bar the discharge, in a later case under this title, of debts that were dischargeable in the case dismissed; nor does the dismissal … prejudice the debtor with regard to the filing of a subsequent petition under this title, except as provided in section 109(g)." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/349] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §349(b)(3): dismissal "revests the property of the estate in the entity in which such property was vested immediately before the commencement of the case." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/349] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Plain-terms consequence for the writer: a dismissed case produces no discharge, so debts stay owed. §349(a) says those debts can still be discharged in a later case. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/349] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: if the court dismisses a case instead of confirming a plan, the trustee may keep some funds for costs, "but the trustee must return all remaining funds to the debtor (other than funds already disbursed or due to creditors)." (citing § 1326(a)(2)) [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §362(c)(3)(A): if an individual's chapter 7, 11, or 13 case is filed and a prior case of the debtor "was pending within the preceding 1-year period but was dismissed," the stay "shall terminate with respect to the debtor on the 30th day after the filing of the later case." There is an exception for a case refiled under a chapter other than 7 after a §707(b) dismissal. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/362] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §362(c)(3)(B): on a party in interest's motion, the court may extend the stay "after notice and a hearing completed before the expiration of the 30-day period only if the party in interest demonstrates that the filing of the later case is in good faith as to the creditors to be stayed." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/362] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §362(c)(3)(C): the later case is presumed not in good faith in listed situations. One is a prior case dismissed within the year after the debtor failed to "perform the terms of a plan confirmed by the court." The presumption is rebuttable by clear and convincing evidence. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/362] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §362(c)(4)(A): if 2 or more prior cases were pending within the previous year but were dismissed, "the stay under subsection (a) shall not go into effect upon the filing of the later case." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/362] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §109(g): no individual may be a debtor who was a debtor in a case pending "at any time in the preceding 180 days" if "(1) the case was dismissed by the court for willful failure of the debtor to abide by orders of the court, or to appear before the court in proper prosecution of the case; or (2) the debtor requested and obtained the voluntary dismissal of the case following the filing of a request for relief from the automatic stay provided by section 362." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/109] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Discharge time bars between cases (§1328(f), §727(a)(8)–(9))

- §1328(f)(1): no Chapter 13 discharge if the debtor received a discharge "in a case filed under chapter 7, 11, or 12 of this title during the 4-year period preceding the date of the order for relief under this chapter." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1328] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1328(f)(2): no Chapter 13 discharge if the debtor received a discharge "in a case filed under chapter 13 of this title during the 2-year period preceding the date of such order." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1328] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §727(a)(8): no Chapter 7 discharge if the debtor "has been granted a discharge under this section, under section 1141 of this title, or under section 14, 371, or 476 of the Bankruptcy Act, in a case commenced within 8 years before the date of the filing of the petition." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/727] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §727(a)(9): no Chapter 7 discharge if the debtor was granted a discharge under §1228 or §1328 "in a case commenced within six years before the date of the filing of the petition, unless payments under the plan in such case totaled at least— (A) 100 percent of the allowed unsecured claims in such case; or (B)(i) 70 percent of such claims; and (ii) the plan was proposed by the debtor in good faith, and was the debtor's best effort." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/727] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Converting a Chapter 13 case (§348)

- §348(a): conversion "constitutes an order for relief under the chapter to which the case is converted, but … does not effect a change in the date of the filing of the petition, the commencement of the case, or the order for relief" (subject to subsections (b) and (c)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/348] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §348(f)(1)(A): when a chapter 13 case is converted, property of the estate in the converted case "shall consist of property of the estate, as of the date of filing of the petition, that remains in the possession of or is under the control of the debtor on the date of conversion." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/348] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §348(f)(2): "If the debtor converts a case under chapter 13 of this title to a case under another chapter under this title in bad faith, the property of the estate in the converted case shall consist of the property of the estate as of the date of conversion." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/348] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §348(f)(1)(B): chapter 13 valuations of property and allowed secured claims carry over only into a case converted to chapter 11 or 12, "but not in a case converted to a case under chapter 7." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/348] (seen 2026-10-10)

# TOPIC 2 — How to file Chapter 7

## What Chapter 7 is, and fees (uscourts.gov)

- Chapter 7 "provides for 'liquidation' - the sale of a debtor's nonexempt property and the distribution of the proceeds to creditors." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Fees: "The courts must charge a $245 case filing fee, a $75 miscellaneous administrative fee, and a $15 trustee surcharge." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Installments: "The number of installments is limited to four, and the debtor must make the final installment no later than 120 days after filing the petition." The court may extend this for cause, but not past 180 days after filing. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Fee waiver: if income is less than 150% of the poverty level and the debtor cannot pay even in installments, "the court may waive the requirement that the fees be paid." (28 U.S.C. § 1930(f)) [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "If a joint petition is filed, only one filing fee, one administrative fee, and one trustee surcharge are charged." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "Debtors should be aware that failure to pay these fees may result in dismissal of the case." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Required documents and forms

- Along with the petition, the debtor must file "(1) schedules of assets and liabilities; (2) a schedule of current income and expenditures; (3) a statement of financial affairs; and (4) a schedule of executory contracts and unexpired leases." (Fed. R. Bankr. P. 1007(b)) [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Debtors must also give the case trustee "a copy of the tax return or transcripts for the most recent tax year as well as tax returns filed during the case." (11 U.S.C. § 521) [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Individuals with primarily consumer debts must also file a credit counseling certificate and any debt repayment plan, "evidence of payment from employers, if any, received 60 days before filing," a statement of monthly net income and anticipated changes, and a record of interests in federal or state qualified education or tuition accounts. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Information needed for the forms: a list of all creditors and their claims; the source, amount, and frequency of income; a list of all property; and a detailed list of monthly living expenses. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Married filers must gather the spouse's information "regardless of whether they are filing a joint petition, separate individual petitions, or even if only one spouse is filing." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Official Form B 101 is the "Voluntary Petition for Individuals Filing for Bankruptcy," category Individual Debtors, "Effective on June 22, 2024." [https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/forms/voluntary-petition-individuals-filing-bankruptcy] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The uscourts.gov Bankruptcy Forms list includes, for individuals: B 106A/B (Schedule A/B: Property), B 106C (Schedule C: The Property You Claim as Exempt), B 106D, B 106E/F, B 106G, B 106H, B 106I (Schedule I: Your Income), B 106J (Schedule J: Your Expenses), B 107 (Statement of Financial Affairs), B 108 (Statement of Intention for Individuals Filing Under Chapter 7), and B 121 (Your Statement About Your Social Security Numbers). [https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/forms/bankruptcy-forms] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The same list includes the fee forms B 103A ("Application for Individuals to Pay the Filing Fee in Installments") and B 103B ("Application to Have the Chapter 7 Filing Fee Waived"), and the means-test forms B 122A-1, B 122A-1Supp, and B 122A-2 ("Chapter 7 Means Test Calculation"). [https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/forms/bankruptcy-forms] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Credit counseling before filing (§109(h), §111)

- §109(h)(1): an individual may not be a debtor unless, "during the 180-day period ending on the date of filing of the petition," the individual received from an approved nonprofit budget and credit counseling agency "an individual or group briefing (including a briefing conducted by telephone or on the Internet)" outlining credit-counseling options and helping with a budget analysis. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/109] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "There are exceptions in emergency situations or where the U.S. trustee (or bankruptcy administrator) has determined that there are insufficient approved agencies to provide the required counseling." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §111(c)(2): an approved agency must, if it charges a fee, "charge a reasonable fee, and provide services without regard to ability to pay the fee." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/111] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Trustee Program list page: the agencies listed "are nonprofit budget and credit counseling agencies approved to provide counseling that individuals must take before they are eligible to file for bankruptcy protection, 11 U.S.C. § 109(h)." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/list-credit-counseling-agencies-approved-pursuant-11-usc-111] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Trustee Program list page: "In some states and judicial districts, credit counseling may be available only by internet and telephone, and not in person." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/list-credit-counseling-agencies-approved-pursuant-11-usc-111] (seen 2026-10-10)
- UST FAQ: "Every person who files for bankruptcy must obtain credit counseling, including people with primarily business debts." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-credit-counseling] (seen 2026-10-10)
- UST FAQ: "The USTP does not recommend any particular agency, or guarantee the quality of an approved agency's credit counseling." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-credit-counseling] (seen 2026-10-10)
- UST FAQ: "Credit counseling services are available for free or at a reduced rate, based on a client's ability to pay." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-credit-counseling] (seen 2026-10-10)
- UST FAQ: "a client whose household income is less than 150 percent of the poverty level is presumptively entitled to a fee waiver or fee reduction." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-credit-counseling] (seen 2026-10-10)
- UST FAQ: "an agency must always provide counseling services, including issuance of the certificate, without regard to a client's ability to pay." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-credit-counseling] (seen 2026-10-10)
- UST FAQ (agency standards): "A fee of less than or equal to $50 is presumed reasonable." Higher fees need advance USTP approval. [https://www.justice.gov/ust/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-credit-counseling] (seen 2026-10-10)
- UST FAQ: an agency "may not represent that its fees are federally mandated or required by law, or by the Department of Justice." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-credit-counseling] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Debtor education (personal financial management) course — required for discharge

- §727(a)(11): no Chapter 7 discharge if, "after filing the petition, the debtor failed to complete an instructional course concerning personal financial management described in section 111." Narrow exceptions apply (§109(h)(4) debtors, or districts where courses are determined inadequate). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/727] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Fed. R. Bankr. P. 1007(b)(7): unless an approved provider has notified the court of completion (or the debtor is exempt), an individual debtor in Chapter 7 or 13 "must file a certificate of course completion issued by the provider." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frbp/rule_1007] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Fed. R. Bankr. P. 1007(c)(4)(A): unless the court extends the time, in a Chapter 7 case the certificate must be filed "within 60 days after the first date set for the meeting of creditors under § 341." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frbp/rule_1007] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Fed. R. Bankr. P. 1007(c)(4)(B): in a Chapter 13 case, the certificate is due "no later than the date the last payment is made under the plan or the date a motion for a discharge is filed under … § 1328(b)." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frbp/rule_1007] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Fed. R. Bankr. P. 4004(c)(1)(H): the court does not grant a Chapter 7 discharge if "the debtor has not filed a certificate showing that a course on personal financial management has been completed—if such a certificate is required by Rule 1007(b)(7)." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frbp/rule_4004] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Rule 4004 Committee Note (2008): "If a debtor fails to file the required statement regarding a personal financial management course, the clerk will close the bankruptcy case without the entry of a discharge." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frbp/rule_4004] (seen 2026-10-10)
- UST FAQ: credit counseling must come before filing and "Debtor education is a separate course that must be taken after an individual files for bankruptcy, subject to very limited exceptions." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-debtor-education] (seen 2026-10-10)
- UST FAQ: "Debtor education is available for free or at a reduced rate, based on a debtor's ability to pay." Debtors under 150% of the poverty level are "presumptively entitled to a fee waiver or fee reduction." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-debtor-education] (seen 2026-10-10)
- UST FAQ: "a provider may file certificates of completion or notice of completion on behalf of a debtor." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-debtor-education] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The U.S. Trustee Program publishes a state- and district-searchable "List of Approved Providers of Personal Financial Management Instructional Courses (Debtor Education) Pursuant to 11 U.S.C. § 111." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/list-approved-providers-personal-financial-management-instructional-courses-debtor-education] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Means test (§707(b)) and the U.S. Trustee means-testing data

- §707(b)(1): the court may dismiss the Chapter 7 case of an individual whose debts are primarily consumer debts (or, with consent, convert it to 11 or 13) "if it finds that the granting of relief would be an abuse of the provisions of this chapter." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/707] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §707(b)(2)(A)(i): abuse is presumed if current monthly income, reduced by allowed expenses and multiplied by 60, is not less than the lesser of 25% of nonpriority unsecured claims (or a floor amount, whichever is greater) or a cap amount. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/707] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The floor and cap in §707(b)(2)(A)(i) were adjusted to **$10,275** (from $9,075) and **$17,150** (from $15,150), effective April 1, 2025. [https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-02-04/html/2025-02207.htm] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov states the same current figures: abuse is presumed if 5-year net income "is not less than the lesser of (i) 25% of the debtor's nonpriority unsecured debt, or $10,275, whichever is greater, or (ii)$17,150." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: the means test applies "If the debtor's 'current monthly income' is more than the state median," and the presumption can be rebutted "only by a showing of special circumstances." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §707(b)(7)(A): no one may file a §707(b)(2) means-test motion if the debtor's (and spouse's) current monthly income × 12 is equal to or less than the applicable state median family income for the household size. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/707] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "Current monthly income" is defined as average monthly income over "the six calendar months before commencement of the bankruptcy case," excluding social security income. (11 U.S.C. § 101(10A), as summarized by uscourts.gov) [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The U.S. Trustee Program's Means Testing page publishes the Census Bureau state median family income figures and IRS expense standards used on the 122A/122C forms. [https://www.justice.gov/ust/means-testing] (seen 2026-10-10)
- UST Means Testing page, latest updates seen: Census median income data updated 3/18/2026, applied "to cases filed on or after April 1, 2026." IRS data and administrative expense multipliers updated 6/30/2026, applying "to cases filed on or after July 15, 2026." Link to the page; **do not state any specific median-income dollar figure.** [https://www.justice.gov/ust/means-testing] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Automatic stay (§362)

- §362(a): filing a petition "operates as a stay, applicable to all entities." This covers, among others, starting or continuing lawsuits against the debtor ((a)(1)), enforcing prior judgments ((a)(2)), acts to create, perfect, or enforce liens ((a)(4)–(5)), and "any act to collect, assess, or recover a claim against the debtor that arose before the commencement of the case" ((a)(6)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/362] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov (plain terms): "As long as the stay is in effect, creditors generally may not initiate or continue lawsuits, wage garnishments, or even telephone calls demanding payments." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "The stay arises by operation of law and requires no judicial action." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §362(b) exceptions include criminal actions ((b)(1)) and domestic-support matters such as establishing paternity or establishing or modifying support orders ((b)(2)(A)), plus collection of domestic support from non-estate property ((b)(2)(B)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/362] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Meeting of creditors (§341; Rule 2003(a))

- §341(a): "Within a reasonable time after the order for relief in a case under this title, the United States trustee shall convene and preside at a meeting of creditors." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/341] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §341(c): "The court may not preside at, and may not attend, any meeting under this section." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/341] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §341(d): in a Chapter 7 case, the trustee must orally examine the debtor on the consequences of seeking a discharge "including the effects on credit history," the ability to file under a different chapter, the effect of a discharge, and the effect of reaffirming a debt. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/341] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Fed. R. Bankr. P. 2003(a)(1)(A) (restyled): the U.S. trustee must call the meeting "in a Chapter 7 or 11 case, no fewer than 21 days and no more than 40 days after the order for relief." Chapter 13 is "no fewer than 21 days and no more than 50 days" ((a)(1)(C)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frbp/rule_2003] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Fed. R. Bankr. P. 2003(a)(3): if the meeting place "is not regularly staffed by the United States trustee or an assistant who may preside, the meeting may be held no more than 60 days after the order for relief." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frbp/rule_2003] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "Between 21 and 40 days after the petition is filed, the case trustee … will hold a meeting of creditors." At the meeting "the trustee puts the debtor under oath, and both the trustee and creditors may ask questions." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "If a husband and wife have filed a joint petition, they both must attend the creditors' meeting and answer questions." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Chapter 7 trustee's role

- uscourts.gov: the U.S. trustee "appoints an impartial case trustee to administer the case and liquidate the debtor's nonexempt assets." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: if all assets are exempt or subject to valid liens, the trustee normally files a "no asset" report, and "Most chapter 7 cases involving individual debtors are no asset cases." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: the trustee's avoiding powers include the power to "set aside preferential transfers made to creditors within 90 days before the petition." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "Within 10 days of the creditors' meeting, the U.S. trustee will report to the court whether the case should be presumed to be an abuse under the means test." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Preferences (§547)

- §547(b)(4): the trustee may avoid a transfer to or for a creditor on account of an antecedent debt, made while insolvent, if it was made "(A) on or within 90 days before the date of the filing of the petition; or (B) between ninety days and one year before the date of the filing of the petition, if such creditor at the time of such transfer was an insider." It must also let the creditor receive more than it would in a chapter 7 liquidation ((b)(5)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/547] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Discharge timing (Chapter 7)

- uscourts.gov: unless a party files a complaint objecting to discharge or a motion to extend the time, the court "will issue a discharge order relatively early in the case – generally, 60 to 90 days after the date first set for the meeting of creditors." (Fed. R. Bankr. P. 4004(c)) [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "Generally, excluding cases that are dismissed or converted, individual debtors receive a discharge in more than 99 percent of chapter 7 cases." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Fed. R. Bankr. P. 4004(a)(1): in a Chapter 7 case, a complaint objecting to discharge "must be filed within 60 days after the first date set for the §341(a) meeting of creditors." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frbp/rule_4004] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Fed. R. Bankr. P. 4004(c)(1): when the times to object to discharge and to move to dismiss under Rule 1017(e) expire, "the court must promptly grant the discharge," subject to listed exceptions. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frbp/rule_4004] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The phrase "about four months after filing": **NOT FOUND — do not state** (uscourts.gov uses "60 to 90 days after the date first set for the meeting of creditors").

## Debts not discharged (§523(a)) — summary with statute link

- §523(a) lists debts a Chapter 7 (§727) discharge does not discharge. Main categories: certain taxes ((a)(1)); debts obtained by false pretenses, false representation, or actual fraud ((a)(2)); unlisted debts ((a)(3)); "fraud or defalcation while acting in a fiduciary capacity, embezzlement, or larceny" ((a)(4)); "a domestic support obligation" ((a)(5)); "willful and malicious injury" ((a)(6)); and certain government fines, penalties, or forfeitures ((a)(7)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/523] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §523(a)(8): education loans and benefit overpayments made, insured, or guaranteed by a governmental unit or nonprofit, and qualified private education loans, are not discharged "unless excepting such debt from discharge under this paragraph would impose an undue hardship on the debtor and the debtor's dependents." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/523] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §523(a) also excludes debts for death or personal injury from intoxicated operation of a motor vehicle, vessel, or aircraft ((a)(9)); "any payment of an order of restitution issued under title 18" ((a)(13)); debts incurred to pay nondischargeable taxes ((a)(14), (14A)); non-support divorce or separation obligations to a spouse, former spouse, or child ((a)(15)); and certain retirement-plan loans ((a)(18)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/523] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §523(c)(1): debts of the kind in (a)(2), (4), and (6) are discharged unless, on the creditor's request and after notice and a hearing, the court determines them excepted from discharge. In plain terms, the creditor must act. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/523] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: those fraud/fiduciary/willful-injury debts "will be discharged unless a creditor timely files and prevails in an action to have such debts declared nondischargeable." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "a bankruptcy discharge does not extinguish a lien on property." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Luxury goods and cash advances presumption (§523(a)(2)(C)) — current amounts

- §523(a)(2)(C)(i)(I): consumer debts "owed to a single creditor and aggregating more than [$900] for luxury goods or services incurred by an individual debtor on or within 90 days before the order for relief … are presumed to be nondischargeable." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/523] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §523(a)(2)(C)(i)(II): "cash advances aggregating more than [$1,250] that are extensions of consumer credit under an open end credit plan obtained by an individual debtor on or within 70 days before the order for relief … are presumed to be nondischargeable." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/523] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Current amounts: the Judicial Conference notice (dated Jan. 30, 2025; published Feb. 4, 2025 at 90 FR 8941) adjusted §523(a)(2)(C)(i)(I) from **$800 to $900** and (i)(II) from **$1,100 to $1,250**, effective **April 1, 2025**. [https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-02-04/html/2025-02207.htm] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The same adjustment is recorded in LII's note to §523: "By notice dated Jan. 30, 2025, 90 F.R. 8941, effective Apr. 1, 2025, … '800' was adjusted to '900' and … '1,100' was adjusted to '1,250'." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/523] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The 2025 notice says the adjustment "does not apply with respect to cases commenced before" April 1, 2025. This matches §104(c). [https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-02-04/html/2025-02207.htm] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §523(a)(2)(C)(ii)(II): "luxury goods or services" "does not include goods or services reasonably necessary for the support or maintenance of the debtor or a dependent of the debtor." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/523] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §104(a): these dollar amounts are adjusted "On April 1, 1998, and at each 3-year interval ending on April 1 thereafter" to reflect CPI-U changes, rounded "to the nearest $25." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/104] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Filing without a lawyer (uscourts.gov)

- "Individuals can file bankruptcy without an attorney, which is called filing pro se. However, seeking the advice of a qualified attorney is strongly recommended because bankruptcy has long-term financial and legal outcomes." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/filing-without-attorney] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "Misunderstandings of the law or making mistakes in the process can affect your rights." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/filing-without-attorney] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "Court employees and bankruptcy judges are prohibited by law from offering legal advice." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/filing-without-attorney] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Pro se filers "are expected to follow the rules and procedures in federal courts" and should be familiar with the Bankruptcy Code, the Federal Rules of Bankruptcy Procedure, and the local rules of their court. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/filing-without-attorney] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "Bankruptcy Forms are available to the public free of charge." Individuals and married couples use the 100-series forms. "Many courts require local forms." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/filing-without-attorney] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Non-attorney petition preparers "can only enter information into forms. They are prohibited from providing legal advice, explaining answers to legal questions, or assisting you in bankruptcy court." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/filing-without-attorney] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov eligibility note: an individual cannot file under any chapter if a prior petition was dismissed within the preceding 180 days in the §109(g) circumstances. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
