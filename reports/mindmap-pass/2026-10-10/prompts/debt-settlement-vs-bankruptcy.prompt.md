- CFPB: "If you stop paying your bills, you will usually incur late fees, penalty interest and other charges". [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-debt-relief-program-and-how-do-i-know-if-i-should-use-one-en-1457/] (seen 2026-10-10)

## 2. FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule — debt-relief provisions

- 16 CFR 310.4(a)(5)(i)(A): no fee may be requested or received until "The seller or telemarketer has renegotiated, settled, reduced, or otherwise altered the terms of at least one debt" under a settlement agreement, debt management plan, or other valid contract the customer executed. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/310.4] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 16 CFR 310.4(a)(5)(i)(B): the customer must also have made at least one payment under that agreement before any fee is collected. *paraphrase* [https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/310.4] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 16 CFR 310.4(a)(5)(i)(C): where debts are handled individually, the fee must either be proportional to that debt's share of the total enrolled debt, or be a percentage of the amount saved that is the same for every debt. *paraphrase* [https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/310.4] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 16 CFR 310.4(a)(5)(ii): a provider may require a dedicated account only if (A) funds are at an insured financial institution, (B) the customer owns the funds and any interest, (C) the account administrator is not owned, controlled or affiliated with the debt relief provider, (D) the administrator gives or accepts no referral payments, and (E) the customer can withdraw at any time without penalty and receive all funds (less earned fees) within seven business days. *paraphrase* [https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/310.4] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 16 CFR 310.3(a)(1)(viii) begins "In the sale of any debt relief service:" and requires disclosure of (A) the time to achieve results and, for settlements, when offers will be made; (B) the amount or percentage of each debt the customer must save before an offer is made; (C) if the service relies on missed payments, the likely harm to creditworthiness, possible collection or lawsuits, and added fees and interest; (D) dedicated-account ownership and withdrawal rights. It has four subparagraphs, (A)–(D). *paraphrase* [https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/310.3] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC business guide: "You can't collect any fees from a customer before you have settled or otherwise resolved the consumer's debts." [https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/debt-relief-services-telemarketing-sales-rule-guide-business] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC press release: the advance-fee ban protects consumers who "enroll in a debt relief service after October 27, 2010." [https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2010/10/debt-relief-companies-prohibited-collecting-advance-fees-under-ftc-rule-takes-effect-october-27-2010] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC press release: "The rule does not cover nonprofit firms, but does cover companies that falsely claim nonprofit status." The rule covers telemarketed for-profit credit counseling, debt settlement and debt negotiation. [https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2010/10/debt-relief-companies-prohibited-collecting-advance-fees-under-ftc-rule-takes-effect-october-27-2010] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Federal Register final rule: the amendments took effect September 27, 2010, except §310.4(a)(5), which took effect October 27, 2010. [S] [https://www.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/federal_register_notices/telemarketing-sales-rule-final-rule-amendments/100810tsr.pdf] (snippet seen 2026-10-10; PDF not loaded, so this URL is not in the allowed list)
- FTC "Choosing a Credit Counselor": for credit counseling that promises a DMP, "the company cannot collect a fee until you have entered the DMP and made at least one payment to your creditors using the DMP." [https://consumer.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/pdf-0040-choosing-a-credit-counselor.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)

## 4. Tax on forgiven or settled debt (IRS)

- IRS Topic 431: "In general, if your debt is canceled, forgiven, or discharged for less than the amount owed", the canceled amount is taxable. [https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc431] (seen 2026-10-10)
- IRS Pub 4681: if a debt is canceled for less than its full amount, "you are treated for income tax purposes as having income." [https://www.irs.gov/publications/p4681] (seen 2026-10-10)
- IRS Pub 4681 example: you owed $1,000 and paid $400 "in satisfaction of the entire debt", so "You have canceled debt of $600." [https://www.irs.gov/publications/p4681] (seen 2026-10-10)
- IRS Pub 4681: even without a Form 1099-C, "you must report canceled debt as gross income on your tax return" unless an exception or exclusion applies. [https://www.irs.gov/publications/p4681] (seen 2026-10-10)
- IRS Instructions for Forms 1099-A and 1099-C: "File Form 1099-C, Cancellation of Debt, for each debtor for whom you canceled a debt owed to you of $600 or more if:" [https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099ac] (seen 2026-10-10)
- IRS Topic 431 exclusions include "Debt canceled in a Title 11 bankruptcy case" and "Debt canceled to the extent insolvent". [https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc431] (seen 2026-10-10)
- IRS Pub 4681: "Debt canceled in a title 11 bankruptcy case isn't included in your income." [https://www.irs.gov/publications/p4681] (seen 2026-10-10)
- IRS Pub 4681: "Don't include a canceled debt in income to the extent that you were insolvent immediately before the cancellation." You are insolvent "to the extent that the total of all of your liabilities was more than the FMV of all of your assets". [https://www.irs.gov/publications/p4681] (seen 2026-10-10)
- IRS: excluded amounts are reported on "Form 982, Reduction of Tax Attributes Due to Discharge of Indebtedness". For the insolvency exclusion you "attach Form 982 to your federal income tax return" and check line 1b. [https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc431] [https://www.irs.gov/publications/p4681] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 26 U.S.C. §108(a)(1)(A): gross income excludes a discharge where "the discharge occurs in a title 11 case," and §108(a)(1)(B) where "the discharge occurs when the taxpayer is insolvent," [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/108] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 26 U.S.C. §108(a)(3): "the amount excluded under paragraph (1)(B) shall not exceed the amount by which the taxpayer is insolvent." §108(d)(3): "insolvent" means "the excess of liabilities over the fair market value of assets." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/108] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC: "Any savings you get from debt relief services could be considered income and taxable." [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-get-out-debt] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: settlement "Tries to get some of your debts forgiven, which could mean you owe taxes on the amount forgiven". [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-credit-counseling-and-debt-settlement-debt-consolidation-or-credit-repair-en-1449/] (seen 2026-10-10)

## 5. Credit counseling and debt management plans (DMPs)

- CFPB: "Credit counseling organizations can advise you on your money and debts." They are usually nonprofits. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-credit-counseling-en-1451/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB, on a DMP: "you make a single payment to the credit counseling organization each month or pay period," and the "credit counseling organization makes monthly payments to each of your creditors." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-credit-counseling-en-1451/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: a DMP "typically lowers your monthly payments to creditors as well as lowers interest charges and fees." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-credit-counseling-en-1451/] (seen 2026-10-10)

- CFPB: "The Federal Trade Commission has found that some organizations that offer debt management plans have defrauded people." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-credit-counseling-en-1451/] (seen 2026-10-10)

- CFPB (archived blog, 2020): a DMP "generally involves closing most of your accounts," and "there may be initial impacts to your credit worthiness due to closing accounts." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/blog/coronavirus-and-dealing-debt-tips-help-ease-impact/] (seen 2026-10-10)

- FTC: "A successful DMP requires you to make regular, timely payments; it could take 48 months or more to complete your DMP." [https://consumer.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/pdf-0040-choosing-a-credit-counselor.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC: "You may have to agree not to apply for — or use — any additional credit while you're" in the plan. [https://consumer.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/pdf-0040-choosing-a-credit-counselor.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC: "Contact your creditors and confirm that they have accepted the proposed plan before you send any payments". [https://consumer.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/pdf-0040-choosing-a-credit-counselor.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)

- CFPB: "Keep in mind that a low interest rate could be a "teaser rate" that applies only for a limited time." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-credit-counseling-and-debt-settlement-debt-consolidation-or-credit-repair-en-1449/] (seen 2026-10-10)

## 7. Negotiating directly and hardship plans

## 8. Protected income and "judgment-proof" (garnishment limits)

- 15 U.S.C. §1673(a): weekly garnishment may not exceed the lesser of "25 per centum of his disposable earnings for that week, or" "the amount by which his disposable earnings for that week exceed thirty times the Federal minimum hourly wage". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1673] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 15 U.S.C. §1673(b): these limits do not apply to support orders, Chapter 13 court orders, or debts for state or federal taxes. *paraphrase* [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1673] (seen 2026-10-10)
- DOL Fact Sheet #30: if weekly "disposable earnings are $217.50 ($7.25 × 30) or less, there can be no garnishment." [https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/30-cppa] (seen 2026-10-10)
- DOL Fact Sheet #30: disposable earnings are "the amount of earnings left after legally required deductions are made." [https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/30-cppa] (seen 2026-10-10)
- DOL Fact Sheet #30: "The CCPA prohibits an employer from firing an employee whose earnings are subject to garnishment for any one debt." [https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/30-cppa] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 31 CFR 212.6(a): "The financial institution shall ensure that the account holder has full and customary access to the protected amount, which the financial institution shall not freeze in response to the garnishment order." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/212.6] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 31 CFR 212.3: the "Lookback period means the two month period that begins on the date preceding the date of account review". Benefit agencies are SSA, VA, OPM and RRB. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/212.3] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: before taking Social Security or VA benefits, a collector must "sue you and win a judgment against you for the amount you owe." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/can-a-debt-collector-take-my-social-security-or-va-benefits-en-1157/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "Two months' worth of benefits are protected and remain in your account for you to use." If benefits come by paper check, "the bank does not have to protect two months' worth of benefits in the account". [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/can-a-debt-collector-take-my-social-security-or-va-benefits-en-1157/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: Social Security and SSDI can sometimes be garnished for government debts (such as taxes and student loans) and for child or spousal support, but SSI is generally protected even from those. *paraphrase* [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/can-a-debt-collector-take-my-social-security-or-va-benefits-en-1157/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- A federal source using the term "judgment-proof": NOT FOUND — do not attribute that term to a government source.

## 9. Statute of limitations and time-barred debt

- 12 CFR 1006.26(a): "Time-barred debt" means "a debt for which the applicable statute of limitations has expired." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1006/26/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 12 CFR 1006.26(b): "A debt collector must not bring or threaten to bring a legal action against a consumer to collect a time-barred debt." This "does not apply to proofs of claim filed in connection with a bankruptcy proceeding." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1006/26/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "They can't sue or threaten to sue you if the statute of limitations has passed", but they may still contact you by letter or phone if they follow the law. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/can-debt-collectors-collect-a-debt-thats-several-years-old-en-1423/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: limitation periods vary. Most states set three to six years, some are longer, and the period can depend on the debt type, the state, and the law named in the credit agreement. *paraphrase* [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/can-debt-collectors-collect-a-debt-thats-several-years-old-en-1423/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: a partial payment or acknowledging an old debt may restart the time period, even after the limitations period has expired. *paraphrase* [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/can-debt-collectors-collect-a-debt-thats-several-years-old-en-1423/] (seen 2026-10-10)

## 10. Credit-report effect: settlement vs bankruptcy

- 15 U.S.C. §1681c(a)(4): consumer reports may not include "Accounts placed for collection or charged to profit and loss which antedate the report by more than seven years." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 15 U.S.C. §1681c(c)(1): for delinquent accounts, the 7-year period begins "upon the expiration of the 180-day period beginning on the date of the commencement of the delinquency." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 15 U.S.C. §1681c(a)(1): title 11 bankruptcy cases may not be reported once they are more than 10 years old, measured from the order for relief or adjudication date. *paraphrase* [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 15 U.S.C. §1681c(b): these time limits do not apply to reports for credit of "$150,000 or more" or for jobs paying "$75,000, or more." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: credit bureaus "generally can report most negative information for seven years". Bankruptcies "can stay on your report for up to ten years." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-long-does-information-stay-on-my-credit-report-en-323/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB (July 2020) data: credit records "identify accounts where the creditor reports the account settled for less than the full balance". [https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_quarterly-consumer-credit-trends_debt-settlement-credit-counseling_2020-07.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Exact consumer-facing CFPB/FTC wording that settled accounts show as "settled" or "paid less than full balance": NOT FOUND. The FCRA does not single out settlements. Settled delinquent accounts fall under the 7-year rule in §1681c(a)(4)/(a)(5) and (c)(1).

## 11. Bankruptcy-side facts for the comparison

- 11 U.S.C. §362(a): a bankruptcy petition "operates as a stay, applicable to all entities," of, among other things, "the commencement or continuation" of actions against the debtor, "the enforcement, against the debtor or against property of the estate, of a judgment," and "any act to collect, assess, or recover a claim against the debtor that arose before the commencement of the case". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/362] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §727(a): "The court shall grant the debtor a discharge, unless—". Under §727(a)(8), a debtor discharged in a case "commenced within 8 years before the date of the filing of the petition" is barred. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/727] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §727(b): subject to §523, a Chapter 7 discharge covers "all debts that arose before the date of the order for relief under this chapter". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/727] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §1328(a): Chapter 13 discharge comes "as soon as practicable after completion by the debtor of all payments under the plan," subject to exceptions. §1328(b) allows a hardship discharge in limited cases. *partly paraphrase* [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1328] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §523(a) nondischargeable debts include certain taxes; debts from "false pretenses, a false representation, or actual fraud"; "a domestic support obligation"; "willful and malicious injury"; certain government fines; student loans unless excepting them "would impose an undue hardship"; and drunk-driving death or injury debts. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/523] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(2)(C) (as adjusted April 1, 2025): luxury-goods debts over $900 to one creditor within 90 days, and cash advances over $1,250 within 70 days, before the order for relief are presumed nondischargeable. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/523] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Debt discharged in bankruptcy is excluded from income under 26 U.S.C. §108(a)(1)(A) (see section 4). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/108] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts: "chapter 13 offers individuals an opportunity to save their homes from foreclosure," and debtors "may cure delinquent mortgage payments over time". [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts: Chapter 13 plans run "usually three to five years", and "In no case may a plan provide for payments over a period longer than five years." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts: Chapter 13 eligibility requires unsecured debts "less than $526,700" and secured debts "less than $1,580,125". [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts: "The discharge in a chapter 13 case is somewhat broader than in a chapter 7 case." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC: you "must get credit counseling from a government-approved organization within six months before you file for any bankruptcy relief." [https://consumer.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/articles/pdf/pdf-0040-choosing-a-credit-counselor.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)

## 12. Debt collection rights (Regulation F)

- 12 CFR 1006.14(b)(2)(i): a collector is presumed to comply only if it calls a person about a particular debt neither "More than seven times within seven consecutive days; nor" "Within a period of seven consecutive days after having had a telephone conversation" with the person about that debt. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1006/14/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: collectors are "generally prohibited from contacting you before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/when-and-how-often-can-a-debt-collector-call-me-on-the-phone-en-2110/] (seen 2026-10-10)
