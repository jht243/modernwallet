## 2. VA (VA Pamphlet 26-7, Lenders Handbook, Chapter 4, Topic 7)

- Chapter 7: "If the bankruptcy was discharged more than 2 years ago from the date of closing for purchases and refinances, it may be disregarded." [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 7, 1–2 years: "If the bankruptcy was discharged within the last 1 to 2 years, it is probably not possible to determine that the borrower or spouse is a satisfactory credit risk unless both of the following requirements are met". VA's Table 3 lists the two requirements: (1) "The borrower(s) had obtained consumer items on credit subsequent to the bankruptcy and has satisfactorily made the payments over a continued period." (2) "The bankruptcy was caused by circumstances beyond the control of the borrower or spouse such as unemployment, prolonged strikes, medical bills not covered by insurance, and so on, and the circumstances are verified." [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- VA on divorce: "Divorce is not generally viewed as beyond the control of the borrower and/or spouse." [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 7, under 12 months: "If the bankruptcy was discharged within the past 12 months, it will generally not be possible to determine that the borrower(s) is a satisfactory credit risk." [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Business-failure bankruptcy: if "caused by failure of the business of a self-employed borrower", VA may find a satisfactory credit risk if all four Table 4 conditions are met: a permanent position obtained after the business failed; no derogatory credit before the self-employment; no derogatory credit after the bankruptcy; and failure "was not due to the borrower's misconduct." [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 13, completed plan: "If the borrower(s) has finished making all payments satisfactorily, the lender may conclude that the borrower has re-established satisfactory credit." [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 13, during plan: "If the borrowers) has satisfactorily made at least 12 months' worth of the payments and the Trustee or the Bankruptcy Judge approves of the new credit, the lender may give favorable consideration." (The typo "borrowers)" is in VA's text.) [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- VA describes Chapter 13 payments as made "to a court- appointed trustee over a 2 to 3-year period or, in some cases, up to 5 years". [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "VA does not have a minimum credit score requirement." (Same Topic 7.) [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Lack of new credit after bankruptcy is not automatically negative. VA says absence of credit history "is not generally considered an adverse factor" and may result when the borrower "has not used since some disruptive credit event such as bankruptcy". [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)
- When a foreclosure, deed-in-lieu or short sale happens "in conjunction with a bankruptcy, use the latest date of either the discharge of the bankruptcy or transfer of title for the home to establish the beginning date of re-established credit." [https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting] (seen 2026-10-10)

## 7. Car — Chapter 7: reaffirmation, redemption, surrender, deadlines

- Reaffirmation must come before discharge. 11 U.S.C. §524(c)(1): the agreement is enforceable only if it "was made before the granting of the discharge under section 727, 1141, 1192, 1228, or 1328 of this title". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §524(c)(2): "the debtor received the disclosures described in subsection (k) at or before the time at which the debtor signed the agreement". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §524(c)(3): the agreement must be "filed with the court" and, if the debtor had a lawyer, come with the attorney's declaration that it "represents a fully informed and voluntary agreement by the debtor" and "does not impose an undue hardship on the debtor or a dependent of the debtor". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Rescission, §524(c)(4): the debtor can rescind "at any time prior to discharge or within sixty days after such agreement is filed with the court, whichever occurs later, by giving notice of rescission to the holder of such claim". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §524(c)(6): for an unrepresented debtor, "the court approves such agreement as— (i) not imposing an undue hardship on the debtor or a dependent of the debtor; and (ii) in the best interest of the debtor." This does not apply to "a consumer debt secured by real property." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §524(k)(2) disclosures: "The terms "Amount Reaffirmed" and "Annual Percentage Rate" shall be disclosed more conspicuously than other terms". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §524(m)(1) presumption: for 60 days after filing, "it shall be presumed that such agreement is an undue hardship on the debtor if the debtor's monthly income less the debtor's monthly expenses ... is less than the scheduled payments on the reaffirmed debt." §524(m)(2): "This subsection does not apply to reaffirmation agreements where the creditor is a credit union". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Redemption, 11 U.S.C. §722: an individual debtor may "redeem tangible personal property intended primarily for personal, family, or household use, from a lien securing a dischargeable consumer debt, if such property is exempted under section 522 of this title or has been abandoned under section 554 of this title, by paying the holder of such lien the amount of the allowed secured claim of such holder that is secured by such lien in full at the time of redemption." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/722] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Lump-sum point: "in full at the time of redemption" was added by Pub. L. 109–8 (2005), per LII's amendment notes. The House report (95–595) says redemption "is accomplished by paying the holder of the lien the amount of the allowed claim secured by the lien". Treat "allowed secured claim" as roughly the collateral's value only by citing §722 plus §506. Do not state a valuation method. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/722] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Statement of intention, §521(a)(2)(A): file "within thirty days after the date of the filing of a petition under chapter 7 of this title or on or before the date of the meeting of creditors, whichever is earlier, or within such additional time as the court, for cause, within such period fixes". The statement must say whether the debtor will retain or surrender the property and whether the debtor "intends to redeem such property, or ... intends to reaffirm debts secured by such property". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/521] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §521(a)(2)(B): the debtor must perform the stated intention "within 30 days after the first date set for the meeting of creditors under section 341(a), or within such additional time as the court, for cause, within such 30-day period fixes". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/521] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §521(a)(6): an individual Chapter 7 debtor may "not retain possession of personal property as to which a creditor has an allowed claim for the purchase price secured in whole or in part by an interest in such personal property unless the debtor, not later than 45 days after the first meeting of creditors under section 341(a), either— (A) enters into an agreement with the creditor pursuant to section 524(c) ... or (B) redeems such property from the security interest pursuant to section 722". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/521] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Consequence under §521(a): "If the debtor fails to so act within the 45-day period referred to in paragraph (6), the stay under section 362(a) is terminated with respect to the personal property of the estate or of the debtor which is affected, such property shall no longer be property of the estate, and the creditor may take whatever action as to such property as is permitted by applicable nonbankruptcy law". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/521] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §362(h)(1): for an individual, the automatic stay "is terminated with respect to personal property of the estate or of the debtor securing in whole or in part a claim" if the debtor fails, within the §521(a)(2) time, to file the statement of intention or "to take timely the action specified in such statement". The exception is where the statement "specifies the debtor's intention to reaffirm such debt on the original contract terms and the creditor refuses to agree to the reaffirmation on such terms." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/362] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Surrender is one of the statutory options. §362(h)(1)(A) refers to a statement that indicates the debtor "will either surrender such personal property or retain it". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/362] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts: "If the debtor decides to reaffirm a debt, he or she must do so before the discharge is entered." Also: "Unless the debtor is represented by an attorney, the bankruptcy judge must approve the reaffirmation agreement." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts: a Chapter 7 discharge is entered "generally, 60 to 90 days after the date first set for the meeting of creditors." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

## 8. Car — Chapter 13: the 910-day rule and new car loans

- The "hanging paragraph" at the end of 11 U.S.C. §1325(a): "For purposes of paragraph (5), section 506 shall not apply to a claim described in that paragraph if the creditor has a purchase money security interest securing the debt that is the subject of the claim, the debt was incurred within the 910-day period preceding the date of the filing of the petition, and the collateral for that debt consists of a motor vehicle (as defined in section 30102 of title 49) acquired for the personal use of the debtor, or if collateral for that debt consists of any other thing of value, if the debt was incurred during the 1-year period preceding that filing." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1325] (seen 2026-10-10)
- LII notes this concluding text was added in 2005 by Pub. L. 109–8, §306(b). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1325] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts explains the effect: when "the obligation underlying the secured claim was used to buy the collateral (e.g., a car loan)" and was incurred within the look-back period, the plan must pay the full debt, not just the collateral's value. (Only the quoted fragment is verbatim; the rest paraphrases the page.) [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

Ohio Administrative Order No. 21-1 (Canton and Cleveland): "Notwithstanding any restrictions in postpetition borrowing contained in confirmation orders, a debtor wishing to borrow money to finance the purchase of a vehicle may also obtain approval under the procedures set forth in this Administrative Order." [https://www.ohnb.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/administrative_orders/ao-21-1-governing-postpetition-vehicle-financing-chapter-13-cases-canton-and-cleveland-court.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)

With trustee approval, no separate court order is needed. A debtor is "not precluded from submitting the request to the court by motion". [https://www.wawb.uscourts.gov/node/883] (seen 2026-10-10)

# Closed fact list — credit reports and rebuilding credit after bankruptcy

Compiled 2026-10-10. Every bullet is one fact, its source URL, and a date (seen = date read).

Legend: `[S]` = confirmed only from a search-engine snippet (the page itself could not be loaded — HTTP 403); `**secondary — unverified**` = not verified at a primary source. Quoted text is the source's wording. Where sources conflict, the conflict is stated; the writer must not resolve it by picking one silently.

## 1. How long a bankruptcy stays on a credit report

### Statute (FCRA, 15 U.S.C. §1681c)

- The FCRA bars consumer reports from including bankruptcy cases that, "from the date of entry of the order for relief or the date of adjudication," "antedate the report by more than 10 years" (§1681c(a)(1)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "Accounts placed for collection or charged to profit and loss which antedate the report by more than seven years" may not be reported (§1681c(a)(4)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c] (seen 2026-10-10)
- "Any other adverse item of information, other than records of convictions of crimes," that "antedates the report by more than seven years" may not be reported (§1681c(a)(5)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c] (seen 2026-10-10)
- For collection and charged-off accounts, the 7-year period begins "upon the expiration of the 180-day period beginning on the date of the commencement of the delinquency" (§1681c(c)(1)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The time limits in §1681c(a)(1)–(5) do not apply to reports used for "a credit transaction involving, or which may reasonably be expected to involve, a principal amount of $150,000 or more" (§1681c(b)(1)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The same exemption covers the underwriting of life insurance involving "a face amount of $150,000 or more" (§1681c(b)(2)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The same exemption covers employment of any individual at an annual salary that equals or may reasonably be expected to equal "$75,000, or more" (§1681c(b)(3)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c] (seen 2026-10-10)
- When a bankruptcy is reported, the report must "include in the report an identification of the chapter of such title 11 under which such case arises," if the source provides it (§1681c(d)(1)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c] (seen 2026-10-10)
- If a bankruptcy case is withdrawn by the consumer before a final judgment, the reporting agency must note that "such case or filing was withdrawn upon receipt of documentation certifying such withdrawal" (§1681c(d)(1)). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681c] (seen 2026-10-10)

### CFPB / FTC

- CFPB: "If you filed for bankruptcy protection, that information will remain in your credit report up to 10 years" from "the date of entry of the order or the date of adjudication" — it lists Chapters 7, 11, 12 and 13, and adds "In certain instances, bankruptcy can be reported beyond 10 years." Page last reviewed Dec 12, 2024. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-long-does-a-bankruptcy-appear-on-credit-reports-en-325/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "A credit reporting company generally can report most negative information for seven years"; bankruptcies can stay up to ten years; the limits do not apply for a job paying over $75,000 a year or an application for more than $150,000 of credit or life insurance. Page last reviewed Sep 2, 2026. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-long-does-information-stay-on-my-credit-report-en-323/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC: negative information generally stays seven years, and "bankruptcy information will stay on for 10 years." [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/fixing-your-credit-faqs] (seen 2026-10-10)
- NOTE — the 10-year figure is the legal maximum for every chapter (CFPB lists Chapter 13 under 10 years). The shorter 7-year Chapter 13 period below is the bureaus' stated practice, not a statutory limit. Do not call the 7-year Chapter 13 period "the law." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-long-does-a-bankruptcy-appear-on-credit-reports-en-325/] (seen 2026-10-10)

### Credit bureaus' stated practice

- Experian: a bankruptcy "drops off your credit report after 10 years if you file for Chapter 7 bankruptcy" and "after seven years if you file Chapter 13 bankruptcy"; "the expiration timeline is dated from the month you first filed for protection with the court." Article dated Feb 26, 2024. [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/when-does-bankruptcy-fall-off-my-credit-report/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Experian (data-furnisher FAQ): "Bankruptcy (Chapters 7, 11 and 12) = 10 years from the filing date (petition and discharged)"; "Bankruptcy (Chapters 7, 11 and 12) = seven (7) years from the filing date (dismissed)"; "Bankruptcy (Chapter 13) = seven (7) years from the filing date." [https://www.experian.com/business/solutions/data-furnisher-reporting/frequently-asked-questions] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Equifax: "Chapter 7 or 11 filed and discharged status bankruptcies remain for 10 years from the date filed"; "Chapter 12 and 13 bankruptcies remain for 7 years from the date filed"; "Dismissed bankruptcies (all chapters) remain for 7 years from the date filed." [https://www.equifax.com/personal/help/article-list/-/h/a/information-stays-equifax-credit-report/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- TransUnion: Chapter 7, 11 and 12 bankruptcies can stay up to 10 years from the filing date; Chapter 13 generally stays 7 years from the filing date. [S] [https://www.transunion.com/blog/credit-advice/how-long-does-bankruptcy-stay-on-credit-report] (seen 2026-10-10)
- TransUnion: if a Chapter 13 repayment plan is not completed, the bankruptcy will remain on the report for 10 years. [S] [https://www.transunion.com/blog/credit-advice/how-long-does-bankruptcy-stay-on-credit-report] (seen 2026-10-10)
- myFICO: Chapter 7 and Chapter 11 public records "typically remain" up to 10 years from the filing date; Chapter 13 up to 7 years from the filing date. myFICO elsewhere says "completed Chapter 13 bankruptcies remain for 7 years." [https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/faq/negative-reasons/bankruptcy-types] [https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/faq/improving-a-score/considering-bankruptcy] (seen 2026-10-10)
- No action is needed to remove an expired bankruptcy: Experian says it is deleted automatically seven or 10 years after filing, depending on the chapter. [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/removing-bankruptcy-from-your-credit-report/] (seen 2026-10-10)

### Accounts "included in bankruptcy" — when they drop off

- Experian: "Accounts included in bankruptcy remain on the report for seven years from the original delinquency date"; an account never late before the bankruptcy runs seven years from the filing date instead. Article dated Jun 6, 2018. [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/can-accounts-included-bankruptcy-deleted/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Experian: "Any late payment history will be deleted seven years from the original delinquency date"; accounts current before the bankruptcy "will remain on your report for seven years from the date the bankruptcy was filed." Article dated Sep 12, 2013. [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/late-payments-continue-to-appear-after-declaring-bankruptcy/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- myFICO: the accounts included in a bankruptcy should be removed from the report after 7 years. [https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/faq/negative-reasons/bankruptcy-types] (seen 2026-10-10)
- TransUnion: accounts included in a bankruptcy remain up to seven years from the date of closing or last activity, regardless of chapter. [S] [https://www.transunion.com/blog/credit-advice/how-long-does-bankruptcy-stay-on-credit-report] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CONFLICT — Equifax's help page says items included in a bankruptcy remain "for up to 10 years from the date of last activity," depending on the bankruptcy type. This conflicts with Experian, myFICO and the 7-year limit in §1681c(a)(4)–(5). Do not state the Equifax 10-year figure for included accounts as fact. [https://www.equifax.com/personal/help/article-list/-/h/a/account-bankruptcy-credit-report/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Equifax: accounts included in a bankruptcy are "not automatically removed from your Equifax credit report." [https://www.equifax.com/personal/help/article-list/-/h/a/account-bankruptcy-credit-report/] (seen 2026-10-10)

## 2. Removing a bankruptcy early, disputes, and credit repair

- Experian: "No, there is no way to remove an accurate record of bankruptcy from a credit report." [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/when-does-bankruptcy-fall-off-my-credit-report/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Experian: if a bankruptcy entry stays past its expiration date, or is wrong (for example, an incorrect filing date), you have the right to dispute it with the credit reporting agencies. [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/when-does-bankruptcy-fall-off-my-credit-report/] [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/removing-bankruptcy-from-your-credit-report/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FCRA §1681i(a)(1)(A): after receiving a dispute, the bureau must reinvestigate free of charge, or delete the item, "before the end of the 30-day period beginning on the date on which the agency receives the notice of the dispute." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1681i(a)(1)(B): the 30-day period "may be extended for not more than 15 additional days" if the consumer sends relevant information during the 30 days. Under (a)(1)(C) the extension does not apply if the item is found inaccurate or unverifiable within the 30 days. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1681i(a)(5)(A): if an item is found "inaccurate or incomplete or cannot be verified," the bureau must "promptly delete that item of information from the file of the consumer, or modify that item." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1681i(a)(6)(A): the bureau must give written notice of the results "not later than 5 business days after the completion of the reinvestigation." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC: "However you filed your dispute, the credit bureau has 30 days to investigate it," and "they have to do it for free." Page dated April 2024. [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/disputing-errors-your-credit-reports] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC: if the business that supplied the information finds it inaccurate or incomplete, it "must tell the credit bureau to update or delete that information from your report." [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/disputing-errors-your-credit-reports] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "Furnishers generally must investigate and respond to your dispute within 30 days of when they receive the dispute"; if the furnisher finds its information accurate, you can ask it to include a statement explaining the dispute. Page last reviewed Sep 2, 2026. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-do-i-dispute-an-error-on-my-credit-report-en-314/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "The only time you should dispute information is when it's inaccurate." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-credit-counseling-and-debt-settlement-debt-consolidation-or-credit-repair-en-1449/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "credit repair companies cannot legally get information removed if it is accurate and timely." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-credit-counseling-and-debt-settlement-debt-consolidation-or-credit-repair-en-1449/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: when credit repair companies dispute accurate items, "accurate items disappear temporarily while the credit reporting company verifies the information," and "After the item is verified, it reappears on your credit report." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-credit-counseling-and-debt-settlement-debt-consolidation-or-credit-repair-en-1449/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC (consumer alert, Jan 7, 2026): "Credit repair companies also can't legally remove negative information from your credit report that's correct and up to date." [https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/01/spot-scams-when-fixing-your-credit] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC: "No one promising to repair your credit can legally remove information if it's both accurate and current." [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/fixing-your-credit-faqs] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC: "Disputing mistakes or outdated things on your credit report is free." [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/fixing-your-credit-faqs] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC scam warning signs: "Scammers insist you pay them before they help you"; "Scammers tell you not to contact the credit bureaus directly"; scammers may tell you to dispute accurate information or lie on credit applications. [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/fixing-your-credit-faqs] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC: credit repair companies must give a written contract explaining your rights, including "your three-day right to cancel without any charge." [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/fixing-your-credit-faqs] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Credit Repair Organizations Act, 15 U.S.C. §1679b(b): "No credit repair organization may charge or receive any money or other valuable consideration" for a service it agreed to perform "before such service is fully performed." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1679b] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1679b(a)(1)–(3): a credit repair organization may not make any statement "which is untrue or misleading," may not advise altering a consumer's identification to hide accurate, non-obsolete adverse information, and may not "make or use any untrue or misleading representation of the services" it provides. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1679b] (seen 2026-10-10)

### Public records other than bankruptcy (NCAP)

- CFPB (Feb 22, 2018): under a settlement "called the National Consumer Assistance Plan (NCAP)," starting July 1, 2017 all civil public records needed minimum identifiers, and "When the NCAP was implemented, all civil judgments and about half of the tax liens on consumer credit records were removed"; "the number of reported bankruptcies remained virtually unchanged." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/blog/removal-public-records-has-little-effect-consumers-credit-scores/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB (Dec 10, 2019): "Almost half of tax liens survived the July 2017 removals, but by April 2018, none remained," and "Bankruptcies are now the only type of public record on NCRA credit reports." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/blog/new-retrospective-on-removing-public-records/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Experian: "Bankruptcy is the only public record that appears on your credit report." [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/public-records-that-appear-on-your-report/] (seen 2026-10-10)

### Goodwill letters

- Experian (Apr 25, 2026): "A goodwill letter is a request to a creditor asking them to remove a legitimate negative mark from your credit report." [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/what-is-goodwill-letter/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Experian: goodwill letters are not meant for "more serious negative marks like charge-offs, accounts in collections or bankruptcy," and "Creditors don't have to agree to make credit adjustments as a courtesy to you." [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/what-is-goodwill-letter/] (seen 2026-10-10)

## 3. How discharged debts should be reported

- Experian: accounts included in bankruptcy, "upon discharge, they should be recorded as "discharged," with the balance at $0." Article dated Apr 20, 2026. [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/removing-included-in-bankruptcy-status-from-closed-accounts/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Experian (data-furnisher FAQ): "Chapters 7, 11, and 12 bankruptcies display a zero balance on all accounts included in bankruptcy," and "Effective Feb. 16, 2018, Chapter 13 bankruptcy petition trade balances have also been set to blank." [https://www.experian.com/business/solutions/data-furnisher-reporting/frequently-asked-questions] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Experian (data-furnisher FAQ): for an account included in bankruptcy, the balance date, payment status and payment history are frozen as of the month the bankruptcy indicator was first reported; Experian "won't display any status or history changes that are reported after the bankruptcy petition date." [https://www.experian.com/business/solutions/data-furnisher-reporting/frequently-asked-questions] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Experian (data-furnisher FAQ): if only one borrower on a joint account files, the non-filing borrower's account "will display normally with the balance amount and all other updates." [https://www.experian.com/business/solutions/data-furnisher-reporting/frequently-asked-questions] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Experian: after a bankruptcy, included accounts "will no longer show a balance owed," but the account history, including pre-bankruptcy late payments, stays on the report. [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/late-payments-continue-to-appear-after-declaring-bankruptcy/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Experian: if an account is missing its "included in bankruptcy" status, "provide the Schedule A document from your bankruptcy filing, showing that account" so Experian can update the report; accounts change to "discharged in bankruptcy" once the bankruptcy is discharged (for Chapter 13, when the plan is complete). [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/can-accounts-included-bankruptcy-deleted/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- myFICO: after filing, check that "only the debts that were actually included in the bankruptcy filing are being reported as discharged through bankruptcy," and "that all of the accounts that ARE included in the bankruptcy show a balance of zero." [https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/faq/improving-a-score/considering-bankruptcy] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB credit report review checklist, item 12: "Are zero balances recorded for debts discharged in bankruptcy?" [https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201701_cfpb_credit-report-review-checklist.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Equifax: discharged accounts should be reported as closed, and outdated information can lower scores more than necessary; contact the creditor first, then dispute errors with the bureaus. [https://www.equifax.com/personal/education/personal-finance/articles/-/learn/rebuilding-credit-after-bankruptcy/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB Supervisory Highlights Issue 37 (Dec 2024): examiners found that furnishers' lack of reasonable written accuracy policies contributed to systemic accuracy problems, "including, for example, continuing to report accounts that had been discharged in bankruptcy." [https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_Supervisory-Highlights-Issue-37_Winter-2024.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB Supervisory Highlights, Consumer Reporting Special Edition (Dec 2019): furnishers failed to update information sent to consumer reporting companies "when consumers' charged-off balances had been discharged in bankruptcy," although their own records showed the status change. [https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_supervisory-highlights_issue-20_122019.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §524(a)(2): a discharge "operates as an injunction against the commencement or continuation of an action, the employment of process," or an act to collect a discharged debt as a personal liability of the debtor. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts: the discharge is a "permanent order prohibiting the creditors of the debtor from taking any form of collection action on discharged debts"; if a creditor tries to collect, the debtor "can file a motion with the court, reporting the action." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/discharge-bankruptcy-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "Debt collectors cannot try to collect on debts that were discharged in bankruptcy"; a lender with a repossession right (such as an auto lender after default) may still repossess after discharge if the debt is unpaid. Last reviewed Aug 24, 2022. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/can-a-debt-collector-try-to-collect-on-a-debt-that-was-discharged-in-bankruptcy-en-1425/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Regulation F, 12 CFR 1006.30(b)(1): "a debt collector must not sell, transfer for consideration, or place for collection a debt" if it "knows or should know that the debt has been paid or settled or discharged in bankruptcy" (exception in (b)(2)(ii) for debts secured by an enforceable lien, with notice that personal liability was discharged). [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1006/30/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB Bulletin 2023-01 (Mar 16, 2023): student loan servicers that collect on student loans discharged by a bankruptcy court are likely engaging in an unfair act or practice under the Consumer Financial Protection Act. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/supervisory-guidance/cfpb-bulletin-2023-01-unfair-billing-and-collection-practices-after-bankruptcy-discharges-of-certain-student-loan-debts/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- White v. Experian (C.D. Cal.): a 2008 injunctive settlement requires the three bureaus to presumptively report pre-bankruptcy debts as discharged after a Chapter 7 discharge, and a 2011 monetary settlement was $45 million. **secondary — unverified** (search snippets of law-firm and news pages only; court order not loaded). Do not state without a primary source.

## 4. Rebuilding credit after bankruptcy

### FICO score factors and bankruptcy impact

- myFICO: FICO Score categories are "payment history (35%), amounts owed (30%), length of credit history (15%), new credit (10%) and credit mix (10%)"; the weights "may be different for different credit profiles." [https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/whats-in-your-credit-score] (seen 2026-10-10)
- myFICO: "A bankruptcy will always be considered a very negative event by your FICO Score"; the impact depends on the whole credit profile, and filings that include more accounts tend to hurt more. [https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/faq/negative-reasons/bankruptcy-types] (seen 2026-10-10)
- myFICO: "Typically, someone with a score in the mid 700s might see their score fall by 100 points or more." [https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/faq/improving-a-score/considering-bankruptcy] (seen 2026-10-10)
- myFICO: "A bankruptcy is going to be factored into your FICO® score until it falls off of your credit report," but its impact "will lessen over time." [https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/faq/negative-reasons/minimizing-bankruptcy-effects] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Experian: a bankruptcy's "negative impact will lessen over time until the bankruptcy eventually expires." [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/when-does-bankruptcy-fall-off-my-credit-report/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FICO presentation (Tom Quinn, FICO VP Global Scoring, March 2010, © 2010 Fair Isaac; hosted by the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco): in a hypothetical, "Bankruptcy posted" moved a 680-score consumer to 530–550 and a 780-score consumer to 540–560. The 680 profile had six accounts, an eight-year history, 40–50% utilization and two past delinquencies; the 780 profile had ten accounts, a fifteen-year history, 15–25% utilization and no missed payments. These are 2010-era illustrations and must be labelled as such. [https://www.frbsf.org/community-development/files/Foreclosure_Lives_Quinn.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The same FICO presentation states that as derogatory information ages, "less points are lost for "recency related" characteristics (assumes no new late payments hit the file)." [https://www.frbsf.org/community-development/files/Foreclosure_Lives_Quinn.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FICO's March 24, 2011 mortgage-delinquency research post (680/720/780 profiles) does NOT include bankruptcy, and FICO notes "research was done only on select consumer credit profiles." Do not attribute bankruptcy figures to it. [https://marketplace.fico.com/blogs/research-looks-how-mortgage-delinquencies-affect-scores] (seen 2026-10-10)
- myFICO's current "How Credit Actions Impact FICO Scores" simulation table does not include bankruptcy. A current FICO-published bankruptcy point-drop table: NOT FOUND — do not state. [https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/faq/affects-of-credit-actions] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Score-recovery timelines after bankruptcy (for example, "X years to reach 700"): NOT FOUND at any primary source — do not state. Only myFICO forum anecdotes exist.

### Rebuilding steps (CFPB, myFICO, Equifax)

- CFPB "How to rebuild your credit": "Rebuilding it takes time. There are no shortcuts or secrets." [https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201702_cfpb_rebuild_credit_english_OaH.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "Pay your bills on time, every time"; "On time" means the payment reached the company by the due date. [https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201702_cfpb_rebuild_credit_english_OaH.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "Experts advise using no more than 30 percent of your total credit limit – some even say you should use less than 10 percent." [https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201702_cfpb_rebuild_credit_english_OaH.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: "Your credit score may go down if you apply for or open a lot of new accounts in a short time." [https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201702_cfpb_rebuild_credit_english_OaH.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB on secured cards: "You put an amount equal to your credit limit in an account as a deposit"; "Fees and interest rates can be high for secured cards, but using one can help you to establish a credit record." [https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201702_cfpb_rebuild_credit_english_OaH.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB lists four things that don't help rebuild credit: using a debit card or cash, using a prepaid card, taking out a payday loan ("Even making on-time repayments might not help your credit"), and a "buy here, pay here" auto loan unless the lender promises in writing to report on-time payments. [https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201702_cfpb_rebuild_credit_english_OaH.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB: credit builder loans let "You build credit and savings at the same time, through a loan from your bank or credit union"; the money is held and released after you finish paying. Last reviewed Sep 13, 2024. [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-are-some-ways-to-start-or-rebuild-a-good-credit-history-en-2155/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB research (Jul 13, 2020): "the CBL proved more effective for participants who entered the study without existing debt"; "Taking out a CBL appeared to reduce borrowers' abilities to keep up on other existing loan payments." [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/targeting-credit-builder-loans/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- myFICO: rebuild soon after filing. "A good practice is to obtain a secured credit card and continually make all of your payments on time." [https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/faq/negative-reasons/minimizing-bankruptcy-effects] (seen 2026-10-10)
- myFICO: "for most people, rebuilding credit can take months or years," and "more severe issues, like bankruptcy or accounts in collections, may require a longer timeline"; choose a secured card "that reports to all three major bureaus." [https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/credit-scores/how-to-rebuild-credit] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Equifax: scoring models put more weight on the past 24 months, so keeping open accounts in good standing can help scores improve within about two years. [https://www.equifax.com/personal/education/personal-finance/articles/-/learn/rebuilding-credit-after-bankruptcy/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Equifax: becoming an authorized user on someone else's card may give a small score boost if the primary owner pays on time; a cosigner's credit is hurt by late payments too. [https://www.equifax.com/personal/education/personal-finance/articles/-/learn/rebuilding-credit-after-bankruptcy/] (seen 2026-10-10)

### Rent / utility reporting (product claims — label as such)

- PRODUCT CLAIM (Experian Boost): qualifying bills include phone, rent, utilities, telecom, monthly insurance, internet and video streaming; "most people get an instant increase in their FICO® Score by an average of 13 points"; based on the FICO Score 8 model; "Results will vary... Some users may not receive an improved score." [https://www.experian.com/credit/score-boost/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Experian Boost's effect is limited to the Experian credit file. Experian says utility bills continue to have no influence on Equifax or TransUnion reports or scores based on them. [S] [https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/what-is-experian-boost/] (seen 2026-10-10)
- VantageScore 4.0 can factor in rental payment history if it is reported to the credit bureaus. [S] (vantagescore.com page returned 403) [https://www.vantagescore.com/consumers/blog/4-ways-to-establish-credit-if-you-are-credit-invisible] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FICO/myFICO statement that rent or utility reporting helps a post-bankruptcy score: NOT FOUND — do not state.

## 6. Getting credit during a Chapter 13 plan

- U.S. Courts: a Chapter 13 debtor "may not incur new debt without consulting the trustee," because added debt may compromise completing the plan. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts: plans run three years if income is below the state median (unless the court approves longer), generally five years if above, and "In no case may a plan provide for payments over a period longer than five years"; the debtor is entitled to a discharge on completing all plan payments, subject to conditions. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §1305(a)(2): a proof of claim may be filed for a postpetition consumer debt "for property or services necessary for the debtor's performance under the plan." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1305] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §1305(c): such a claim "shall be disallowed if the holder of such claim knew or should have known that prior approval by the trustee of the debtor's incurring the obligation was practicable and was not obtained." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1305] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Example local rule (Bankr. D.N.M. LBR 3015-6, one district only; rules vary by district): a debtor "may incur non-mortgage consumer debt of less than $1,000 in the aggregate in any calendar year" without approval; above that, the debtor submits a written application to the trustee with amended Schedules I and J, and must file notice of approval within ten days. [https://www.nmb.uscourts.gov/node/2207] (seen 2026-10-10)
- A single national dollar threshold or procedure for new debt in Chapter 13: NOT FOUND — it varies by district, plan and trustee. Do not state a universal rule.

## 7. Predatory offers and reaffirmation

- FTC: it has brought cases "typically against scammers who falsely promise that, in return for an up-front fee, you'll get a credit card or a loan," "especially if you have bad credit, no credit, or a bankruptcy." [https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/consumer-finance/credit-cards] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC: the Telemarketing Sales Rule "prohibits telemarketers from collecting fees before a consumer is offered credit" when they claim a high success rate. [https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/consumer-finance/credit-cards] (seen 2026-10-10)
- FTC (advance-fee loans, July 2022): "Any up-front fee that the lender wants to collect before granting the loan is a cue to walk away"; some scammers "promise you a loan or credit card regardless of your credit history." [https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-advance-fee-loans] (seen 2026-10-10)
- CFPB/FTC warning specifically about high-cost (but legal) credit marketed to people right after bankruptcy: NOT FOUND — do not state.
- U.S. Courts: "A reaffirmation is an agreement between the debtor and the creditor that the debtor will remain liable" on a debt that would otherwise be discharged; it must be signed, filed with the court, and made "before the discharge is entered." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts: "Unless the debtor is represented by an attorney, the bankruptcy judge must approve the reaffirmation agreement." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §524(c): a reaffirmation agreement is enforceable only if it was made before discharge, the §524(k) disclosures were given, it was filed with the court, and it was not rescinded "at any time prior to discharge or within sixty days after such agreement is filed with the court, whichever occurs later." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §524(k) requires disclosures in which "Amount Reaffirmed" and "Annual Percentage Rate" are shown more conspicuously than other terms. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] (seen 2026-10-10)
- §524(f): nothing in subsections (c) or (d) "prevents a debtor from voluntarily repaying any debt"; U.S. Courts: "A debtor who has received a discharge may voluntarily repay any discharged debt." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/524] [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/discharge-bankruptcy-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- U.S. Courts: in Chapter 7 the discharge is generally granted "60 to 90 days after the date first set for the meeting of creditors," and the meeting is held 21 to 40 days after the petition. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
