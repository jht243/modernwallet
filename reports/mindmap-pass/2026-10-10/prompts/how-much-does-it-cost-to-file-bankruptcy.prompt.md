## Court filing fees: current schedule and totals

- The Bankruptcy Court Miscellaneous Fee Schedule on uscourts.gov is marked effective December 1, 2023; the page lists no other effective date. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/fees/bankruptcy-court-miscellaneous-fee-schedule] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The old uscourts URL (/services-forms/fees/bankruptcy-court-miscellaneous-fee-schedule) now 301-redirects to /court-programs/fees/bankruptcy-court-miscellaneous-fee-schedule. Link to the new URL. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/fees/bankruptcy-court-miscellaneous-fee-schedule] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Statutory filing fee, 28 U.S.C. §1930(a)(1): "For a case commenced under— (A) chapter 7 of title 11, $245, and (B) chapter 13 of title 11, $235." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1930] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Statutory filing fee for a Chapter 11 case that does not concern a railroad, 28 U.S.C. §1930(a)(3): $1,167. (Reference only.) [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1930] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Item 8 of the fee schedule (administrative fee): $78 for filing a petition under Chapter 7, 12, or 13; $571 for a petition under Chapter 9, 11, or 15. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/fees/bankruptcy-court-miscellaneous-fee-schedule] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Item 9 of the fee schedule (trustee surcharge): a $15 fee, paid to trustees under 11 U.S.C. §330(b)(2), applies to Chapter 7 petitions, to notices or motions to convert a case to Chapter 7, and to motions to divide or reopen a Chapter 7 case. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/fees/bankruptcy-court-miscellaneous-fee-schedule] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 7 total = $245 filing fee + $78 administrative fee + $15 trustee surcharge = $338 (sum of the three primary-source components above). A U.S. Bankruptcy Court fee page lists "Chapter 7 $338.00", marked "Current as of December 1, 2023". [https://www.akb.uscourts.gov/node/81] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 13 total = $235 filing fee + $78 administrative fee = $313. The same court page lists "Chapter 13 $313.00". [https://www.akb.uscourts.gov/node/81] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Chapter 11 (non-railroad) total on the same court page: $1,738.00 ($1,167 statutory fee + $571 administrative fee). Reference only. [https://www.akb.uscourts.gov/node/81] (seen 2026-10-10)
- On a joint petition, "only one filing fee and one administrative fee are charged." (uscourts Chapter 13 basics page) [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

The $75 figure is older than the current $78 on the official fee schedule. Do not quote $75. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Conversion, reopening and other case fees

- Item 10: in addition to the Item 9 fee, converting Chapter 13 to Chapter 7 costs $10, and converting Chapter 12 to Chapter 7 costs $45. There is no refund if the new chapter's filing fee is lower. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/fees/bankruptcy-court-miscellaneous-fee-schedule] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Converting a Chapter 13 case to Chapter 7 is listed at $25.00 in total on a bankruptcy court fee page ($15 Item 9 + $10 Item 10). Converting Chapter 7 to Chapter 13 is listed as "None". [https://www.akb.uscourts.gov/node/81] (seen 2026-10-10)
- A debtor's conversion from Chapter 7 to Chapter 11 (non-railroad) is listed at $922.00, and from Chapter 13 to Chapter 11 (non-railroad) at $932.00 (reference only). [https://www.akb.uscourts.gov/node/81] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Item 11 (reopening): $245 to reopen a Chapter 7 case; $235 to reopen a Chapter 13 case. The fee does not apply to reopening to correct an administrative error, to redact a record when that is the only reason, to file a Rule 4007(b) complaint, or to address an alleged discharge violation under §524. The court may waive or defer it, and should waive it if no additional assets are found. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/fees/bankruptcy-court-miscellaneous-fee-schedule] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Item 4: amending schedules, lists of creditors, or the mailing list costs $34 (rising to $38 on Dec. 1, 2026). There is no fee for changing only a creditor's or attorney's address. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/fees/bankruptcy-court-miscellaneous-fee-schedule] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Item 19: a motion to terminate, annul, modify, or condition the automatic stay costs $199 (rising to $221 on Dec. 1, 2026). The fee does not apply to a motion for relief from the co-debtor stay. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/fees/bankruptcy-court-miscellaneous-fee-schedule] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Fee waiver (Chapter 7 only)

- 28 U.S.C. §1930(f)(1): the court "may waive the filing fee in a case under chapter 7 of title 11 for an individual if the court determines that such individual has income less than 150 percent of the income official poverty line ... applicable to a family of the size involved and is unable to pay that fee in installments." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1930] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 28 U.S.C. §1930(f)(2): the court "may waive for such debtors other fees prescribed under subsections (b) and (c)." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1930] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The waiver application is Official Form 103B, "Application to Have the Chapter 7 Filing Fee Waived" (form version 12/15). [https://www.uscourts.gov/file/18714/download] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Form 103B is signed under penalty of perjury: "I declare that I cannot afford to pay the filing fee either in full or in installments." [https://www.uscourts.gov/file/18714/download] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Form 103B asks whether the debtor has paid, or expects to pay, an attorney or "A bankruptcy petition preparer, paralegal, or typing service" for the case, and how much. [https://www.uscourts.gov/file/18714/download] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Fed. R. Bankr. P. 1006(c): "The clerk must accept for filing an individual's voluntary Chapter 7 petition accompanied by a completed and signed application to waive the filing fee (Form 103B)." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frbp/rule_1006] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The uscourts Chapter 7 basics page describes the waiver as available "If the debtor's income is less than 150% of the poverty level (as defined in the Bankruptcy Code)". [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- There is no statutory fee waiver for Chapter 13 filing fees. §1930(f)(1) covers only "a case under chapter 7". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1930] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Paying in installments

- Fed. R. Bankr. P. 1006(b)(2) (restyled rules, eff. Dec. 1, 2024): the court may order installments "(not to exceed 4)"; "All payments must be made within 120 days after the petition is filed. The court may, for cause, extend the time to pay an installment, but the last one must be paid within 180 days after the petition is filed." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frbp/rule_1006] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Fed. R. Bankr. P. 1006(b)(3): "Until the filing fee has been paid in full, the debtor or Chapter 13 trustee must not make any further payment to an attorney or any other person who provides services to the debtor in connection with the case." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frbp/rule_1006] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The installment application is Official Form 103A, "Application for Individuals to Pay the Filing Fee in Installments" (12/15). It says: "You may apply to pay the filing fee in up to four installments" and "You must propose to pay the entire fee no later than 120 days after you file this bankruptcy case." [https://www.uscourts.gov/file/18700/download] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Form 103A warns: "Your debts will not be discharged until your entire fee is paid," and "If you do not make any payment when it is due, your bankruptcy case may be dismissed". [https://www.uscourts.gov/file/18700/download] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Some districts set stricter local terms. For example, a District of Hawaii page says installment requests in Chapters 12 and 13 "are disfavored" and the court "generally will order you to pay the filing fee in full within 14 days". [S] [https://www.hib.uscourts.gov/node/513] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Credit counseling and debtor education costs

- The U.S. Trustee Program describes two separate requirements: "Credit counseling (CC) must be obtained before an individual files for bankruptcy" and "Debtor education (DE) is a separate course that must be taken after an individual files for bankruptcy." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/credit-counseling-debtor-education-information] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Credit counseling must have been received "within 180 days before filing" (uscourts Chapter 13 basics page). [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §111(c)(2)(B): approved credit counseling agencies must, "if a fee is charged for counseling services, charge a reasonable fee, and provide services without regard to ability to pay the fee". §111(d)(1)(E) says the same for instructional (debtor education) courses. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/111] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 28 CFR 58.21(a)(1): "A fee of $50 or less for credit counseling services is presumed to be reasonable"; under (a)(2), a fee above $50 needs prior U.S. Trustee approval. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/58.21] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 28 CFR 58.21(b): "An agency shall waive the fee in whole or in part whenever a client demonstrates a lack of ability to pay the fee"; a client presumptively lacks the ability to pay if household current income is "less than 150 percent of the poverty guidelines". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/58.21] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 28 CFR 58.34(a)(1): "A fee of $50 or less for an instructional course is presumed to be reasonable." Section 58.34(b) applies the same waiver duty and the same 150-percent-of-poverty presumption to debtor education providers. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/58.34] (seen 2026-10-10)
- USTP credit counseling FAQ: "Credit counseling services are available for free or at a reduced rate, based on a client's ability to pay." It also says "A fee of less than or equal to $50 is presumed reasonable and does not require justification." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-credit-counseling] (seen 2026-10-10)
- USTP debtor education FAQ: "Debtor education is available for free or at a reduced rate, based on a debtor's ability to pay." [https://www.justice.gov/ust/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-debtor-education] (seen 2026-10-10)
- The USTP lists of approved agencies and providers are at /ust/list-credit-counseling-agencies-approved-pursuant-11-usc-111 and /ust/list-approved-providers-personal-financial-management-instructional-courses-debtor-education (link targets taken from the USTP page; the list pages themselves were not loaded). [https://www.justice.gov/ust/credit-counseling-debtor-education-information] (seen 2026-10-10)
- GAO (2008) found that "Fees related to the new credit counseling and debtor education requirements typically total about $100." This is a 2008 figure; label it as historical. [https://www.gao.gov/assets/a277577.html] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Attorney fees: Chapter 13 presumptively reasonable ("no-look") fees by district

- These fees vary by district. Each bankruptcy court sets its own presumptive Chapter 13 fee by local rule or general order. The four orders below set four different amounts with different conditions. (Inference from the four orders below; no single national figure exists in any source loaded.)
- S.D. Indiana, General Order 25-0001 (Nov. 14, 2025): "For all cases filed on or after December 1, 2025, the maximum fee allowable under the Local Rule's 'Presumed Reasonable Fee' process is $5,000." It adds $500 per adversary proceeding to avoid an unsecured mortgage. [https://www.insb.uscourts.gov/sites/insb/files/General%20Order%2025-0001.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- N.D. Indiana, order amending Local Bankruptcy Rule B-2016-1 (dated July 27, 2026): "Four thousand five hundred dollars ($4,500.00) is presumed to be a reasonable attorney's fee for counsel's services representing a debtor in a routine, consumer case under Chapter 13." [https://www.innb.uscourts.gov/sites/innb/files/Order%20Amending%20Local%20Bankruptcy%20Rule%20B-2016-1.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- E.D. Wisconsin: "For Chapter 13 cases filed on or after May 1, 2024, the presumptively reasonable fee ... through at least plan confirmation, is $5,500 (including costs, but not including the Chapter 13 filing fee)"; it is $6,000 if the debtor joins the court's mortgage modification mediation program. [https://www.wieb.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/forms/Presumptively%20Reasonable%20Fees%205-1-24v2.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- M.D. North Carolina, Standing Order 25-02 (revised May 16, 2025): the presumptive base fee for cases filed on or after July 7, 2023 is $5,200 for below-median-income debtors and $5,500 for above-median debtors. These rise to $5,700 and $6,000 if counsel has 7 hours of bankruptcy continuing legal education. The fee is capped at $2,500 if the total plan payments are under $7,000. [https://www.ncmb.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/general-ordes/25-02%20Ch%2013%20Presumptive%20Fee%20Standing%20Order%20Revised%205.16.25.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- N.D. Alabama (Northern Division), Administrative Order 22-03: for cases filed on or after October 1, 2022, a Chapter 13 attorney "may charge total compensation that does not exceed $4,500 without filing a detailed application for compensation". [https://www.alnb.uscourts.gov/sites/alnb/files/general-ordes/Admin%20Order%2022-03.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Chapter 13 attorney fees paid through the plan

- 11 U.S.C. §1326(b): "Before or at the time of each payment to creditors under the plan, there shall be paid— (1) any unpaid claim of the kind specified in section 507(a)(2) of this title" (administrative expenses). [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1326] (seen 2026-10-10)
- N.D. Alabama Administrative Order 22-03: "the balance of the fee owed to Debtor(s)' attorney shall be payable in accordance with this Administrative Order". The Chapter 13 trustee disburses up to $3,500 of the awarded attorney's fee, less any amount paid before filing, from post-confirmation plan payments, then pays the remainder in monthly fixed payments. [https://www.alnb.uscourts.gov/sites/alnb/files/general-ordes/Admin%20Order%2022-03.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- E.D. Wisconsin: if a Chapter 13 case is dismissed before confirmation, a $1,500 presumed fee may be allowed, and "The Chapter 13 trustee may deduct up to this amount ... payable to debtor's counsel before returning any funds to the debtor". [https://www.wieb.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/forms/Presumptively%20Reasonable%20Fees%205-1-24v2.pdf] (seen 2026-10-10)
- Upsolve (organization's own explainer): "The remaining attorney fees then will get paid through the Chapter 13 repayment plan." **secondary** [https://upsolve.org/learn/how-much-does-bankruptcy-cost/] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Chapter 13 trustee fee and other costs

- 28 U.S.C. §586(e)(1)(B)(i): for a debtor who is not a family farmer, the standing trustee's percentage fee may not exceed "ten percent" of payments made under the plan. Family-farmer cases use a tiered cap: 10% up to $450,000, then 3%. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/586] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 28 U.S.C. §586(e)(2): the standing trustee collects the percentage fee "from all payments received by such individual under plans in the cases under subchapter V of chapter 11 or chapter 12 or 13". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/586] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §1326(b)(2): the standing trustee's percentage fee under 28 U.S.C. §586(e)(1)(B) is paid before or at the time of each payment to creditors under the plan. [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/1326] (seen 2026-10-10)

## Free and low-cost help

- uscourts.gov: "Individuals can file bankruptcy without an attorney, which is called filing pro se." It adds that "seeking the advice of a qualified attorney is strongly recommended." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/filing-without-attorney] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "Filing personal bankruptcy under Chapter 7 or Chapter 13 takes careful preparation and understanding of legal issues." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/filing-without-attorney] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov: "If you are unable to afford an attorney, you may qualify for free legal services." The page links to the ABA's Legal Help and to the Legal Services Corporation. [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/filing-without-attorney] (seen 2026-10-10)
- uscourts.gov on petition preparers: "By law, preparers can only enter information into forms." They "are prohibited from providing legal advice" and cannot "receive payment for court fees." [https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/filing-without-attorney] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §110(a)(1): a bankruptcy petition preparer is "a person, other than an attorney for the debtor ..., who prepares for compensation a document for filing". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/110] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §110(e)(2)(A): a petition preparer "may not offer a potential bankruptcy debtor any legal advice". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/110] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §110(h)(1)–(3): the Supreme Court or Judicial Conference may set "a maximum allowable fee chargeable by a bankruptcy petition preparer". The preparer must disclose any fee received in the 12 months before filing, and the court "shall disallow and order the immediate turnover to the bankruptcy trustee" any fee "in excess of the value of any services rendered". [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/110] (seen 2026-10-10)
- 11 U.S.C. §110(l)(1): a preparer who violates the section's requirements "may be fined not more than $500 for each such failure." [https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/11/110] (seen 2026-10-10)
