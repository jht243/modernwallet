Days after Microsoft and the FAA went dark, Chase and Wells Fargo customers can’t get to their money

If you had trouble logging into your bank’s website on Monday, you’re not alone. JPMorgan Chase customers spent much of the day unable to log in or move money, with the bank saying the outage spread from it’s website and app to some branches and to outside apps that connect to Chase accounts. Wells Fargo, Capital One, and Zelle also had problems with their online banking platforms as customers reported trouble.

Reports started Monday morning and climbed quickly. Downdetector logged nearly 4,500 Chase reports shortly after 1:30 p.m., most of them about website logins. By 2:22 p.m., the count had passed 20,000.
“Due to a technical issue, some transactions are taking longer than usual to complete but customers still have access to their accounts and funds,” a Chase spokesperson told Fortune. “We are working to resolve this as quickly as possible.”
Customers described a wider disruption than that statement suggests. Comments on Downdetector mentioned problems with ATMs and in-person systems as well as online banking and Zelle. Some customers opening the Chase app got a message reading, “Some services aren’t available.”
Ashley Lutz
The problems also reached apps outside the bank. Plaid, which links bank accounts to payment, investing and budgeting apps, said its own network was working normally but that it was seeing connection problems with Chase accounts. Customers trying to link or refresh a Chase account through another app may have gotten errors as a result.
Downdetector listed Chase, Wells Fargo, Capital One and Bank of America among the banks with spikes in outage reports, along with U.S. Bank, Truist and Zelle. By 2 p.m. Eastern, Bank of America saw about 2,000 with U.S. Bank and Wells Fargo seeing about 1,000 each. Wells Fargo did not confirm the outage.
It’s also affecting local branches. On the subreddit r/TalesFromYourBank, one person wrote: “I’m at work and haven’t been able to take a single client.” Three others corroborated to their bank phone systems being down.
Bank of America and U.S. Bank had not explained their customers’ problems as of Monday afternoon. Downdetector counts user complaints, not confirmed outages, and it was not clear whether the Chase and Wells Fargo failures shared a cause.
The outages follow several other technology failures in recent days. Microsoft restored service on Saturday after a global outage took down Outlook and Teams, and said a technical problem, not a cyberattack or AI, was the cause. No bank has tied Monday’s problems to the Microsoft outage.
Meanwhile, a Federal Aviation Administration comminications problem grounded flights across the country on Friday, with service not fully restored until Monday. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed an upgrade from copper wire to fiber-optic cables at one location that caused routers across the air traffic control system to be flooded with more than 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds. Because it disrupted some radar feeds, the FAA ordered the ground stop to ensure safety. It was lifted about four hours later.
Chase has had large online outages before. In 2010, its online banking was down for more than a day, and CEO Jamie Dimon apologized publicly. The bank said then that it would refund late fees to customers who missed payments during the outage, as long as they called to ask.
