Chase says transactions back to normal after outage online and at some branches
The bank said that it “experienced a temporary issue related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems.”

By Rob Wile and Kayla Steinberg
Chase Bank said transactions were posting normally Monday evening after a nationwide outage that had some customers reporting trouble accessing their accounts and withdrawing funds.
“Transactions are now posting normally,” a Chase spokesperson said around 6:30 p.m.
The outage began Monday morning, according to a source familiar with the matter who was not authorized to speak publicly.
Reports began to surface online Monday afternoon from customers across a range of banks saying they were receiving error messages when attempting to perform bank transactions.
Chase said at around 4 p.m. on X that “JPMorganChase has experienced a temporary issue related to a software bug in on-premise hardware system” and that it was working to resolve it.
The issues had been affecting both online and in-person branch transactions.
The issue was not “cyber-related,” the bank said, and did not  impact the entire banking system. “Other banks may have been impacted by slowness in transactions only to the extent that they were a party to a transaction with us.”
The bank said that customers still had access to their accounts and funds, but some transactions were taking longer than normal.
Regulators were in touch with the big banks affected “immediately,” a person familiar with the situation said earlier Monday. They “quickly determined this was contained and service was already in process of restoration,” the person said then.
The issues at Chase were the latest in a series of nationwide tech glitches over the past week that have shaken public confidence in systems that tens of millions of Americans use every day. However, there is no evidence that these incidents are connected to one another.
Microsoft Outlook went offline for approximately 48 hours last week due to what it called a capacity upgrade.
On Friday, the FAA issued a nationwide ground stop for several hours over what U.S. officials said was a botched facilities upgrade.
It was not immediately clear how many Chase accounts were affected by the glitch. The bank counts approximately 94 million customer relationships in the United States, with 5,000 physical branches and 15,000 ATMs.
By late afternoon Monday, several Chase bank branches in Manhattan appeared to be operating normally. Customers leaving a branch at 6th Avenue and W. 51st Street told NBC News they didn’t know there had been a problem until they arrived at the branch, where they were told by employees that they’d gotten there at just the right time.
“This is really retail banking,” and not tied to the rest of JPMorgan Chase, said a source familiar with the matter who was not authorized to speak publicly.
The issues with Chase accounts also appeared to be preventing customers from transferring money via Zelle. A representative for the company said that the technical issues on its platform originated with Chase.
“Zelle is not down. Zelle is operating as it should,” the company told NBC News. “An issue at the financial institutions may be delaying Zelle payments for some of their customers. Please contact them directly.”
JPMorgan Chase operates around 15,000 ATMs in the United States alone. Michael Kappeler / dpa / picture alliance via Getty Images fileA representative for U.S. Bank confirmed that Zelle customers were experiencing issues.
“U.S. Bank systems are not directly impacted,” the spokesman said. “However, clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience transaction delays.”

A spokesperson for PNC Bank said it was “not experiencing any direct impact. We are aware of reports of transaction delays outside of the bank, but those appear to be resolving.”
Truist bank said its services “are operating normally,We continue to closely monitor our systems and are not aware of any broad service disruptions at this time.”
Customers at Wells Fargo also experienced delays, but the company did not provide a statement.
Citibank likewise declined to comment.
A spokesperson for Capital One said the bank “has not been impacted and continues to operate normally at this time.”
