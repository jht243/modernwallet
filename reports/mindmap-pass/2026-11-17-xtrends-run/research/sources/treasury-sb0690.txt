Treasury, IRS Issue Final Rules to Help Hardworking Americans Build a Financially Secure Retirement
November 17, 2026
SECURE 2.0 Final Regulations Expand Automatic Enrollment in Workplace Retirement Plans
WASHINGTON — The U.S. Department of the Treasury and the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) today issued final regulations to help more Americans save for retirement through their workplaces. The regulations implement a key provision of the SECURE 2.0 Act, providing employers with clear guidance on automatic enrollment for certain 401(k) and 403(b) retirement plans. The rules help workers build retirement savings while preserving their control over how much they contribute.
Treasury estimates that SECURE 2.0’s automatic enrollment expansion provision will increase retirement savings by an average of $4.2 billion annually over the 2023-2032 period.
“A financially secure retirement is a cornerstone of the American Dream, and every worker deserves the opportunity to achieve it,” said Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. “Today’s rules help more American families and workers build retirement savings while keeping them in control of their contributions, and give employers the clarity and flexibility to invest in their employees’ futures.”
“With the SECURE 2.0 regulations in place, American families will be able to boost their savings and keep more of their hard-earned money through their automatic enrollment in a retirement account,” said IRS Chief Executive Officer Frank J. Bisignano. “These regulations reduce the administrative burden placed on taxpayers when making long-term financial decisions. More broadly, they further the IRS goal of improving taxpayer service by making interactions with the Service simpler and easier.”
STRENGTHENING AMERICANS’ RETIREMENT SAVINGS
The final regulations implement a key provision of the SECURE 2.0 Act by clarifying requirements for plan sponsors and simplifying certain participant notices.
Covered retirement plans generally must automatically enroll eligible employees at a contribution rate of three percent. After an initial participation period, the default contribution rate must generally increase by at least one percentage point each year until it reaches at least ten percent, subject to a fifteen percent maximum. Employees may opt out or choose a different contribution rate.
The final regulations generally apply to retirement plan years beginning after December 31, 2027, giving employers time to make necessary administrative changes.
The final regulations:
Clarify statutory exceptions for qualifying small and new businesses, governmental plans, church plans, and SIMPLE 401(k) plans;
Give employers flexibility in the timing and amount of automatic contribution increases, within statutory limits;
Simplify plan communications by allowing certain required notices to be combined and reducing annual notice requirements for qualifying employees who are not participating, provided they receive annual reminders and any plan documents they are entitled to request; and
Explain how automatic enrollment requirements apply following plan mergers.
The final regulations are available here.
