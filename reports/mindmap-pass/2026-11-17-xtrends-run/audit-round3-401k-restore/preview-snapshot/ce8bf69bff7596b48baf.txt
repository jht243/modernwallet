The U.S. Department of the Treasury and the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) issued final regulations implementing this SECURE 2.0 Act (Setting Every Community Up for Retirement Enhancement) provision on November 17, 2026. Treasury estimates the provision will increase retirement savings by an average of $4.2 billion annually over the 2023 to 2032 period. The statutory mandate under 26 U.S. Code 414A sets an initial uniform contribution rate between 3% and 10% of pay, followed by annual automatic increases of 1 percentage point up to at least 10% and at most 15%. However, plans established before December 29, 2022, businesses under three years old, small employers that have not normally employed more than 10 employees (the exemption runs until one year after the close of the first taxable year in which they did), church plans, governmental plans, and SIMPLE (Savings Incentive Match Plan for Employees) 401(k) plans are exempt from the federal requirement. Employees always retain the legal right to opt out entirely or choose their own savings rate. Tools for this journey 401(k) calculator → What the Final Auto-Enrollment Rules Require Covered 401(k) and 403(b) plans must automatically enroll eligible employees at a default rate of at least 3% of compensation. The statutory framework established by Congress in 26 U.S. Code 414A requires the initial uniform percentage to be not less than 3% and not more than 10% during the employee's first year of participation. After this initial participation period, the plan must automatically increase the employee's contribution rate by 1 percentage point at the start of each subsequent plan year. These annual 1-point increases must continue until the default contribution rate reaches at least 10%, subject to a statutory maximum of 15%. Two distinct federal effective dates apply to these automatic enrollment rules. Under Public Law 117-328 (Division T, Title I, Section 101(c)), the statutory amendments apply to plan years beginning after December 31, 2024. In press release SB-0690, the U.S. Department of the Treasury stated that its final regulations generally apply to plan years beginning after December 31, 2027, giving employers time to make necessary administrative changes. The checked primary sources do not state which date governs a specific workplace plan, and workers should not assume an existing plan is exempt or has until 2028 without reviewing formal plan notices. Employees who are automatically enrolled keep the right to opt out or adjust their contribution amount. Under 26 U.S.C. 414A(b)(3), an employee can elect not to have any salary-reduction contributions made or elect to contribute at a different percentage entirely. When an employee does not select specific investment options for their automatic contributions, the statute requires the plan to invest those amounts in accordance with federal default-investment regulations under 29 CFR 2550.404c-5. Which Plans Are Exempt Several categories of retirement plans are completely exempt from the mandatory automatic enrollment rules under 26 U.S. Code 414A(c) . The federal statute specifically excludes plans established before December 29, 2022, which was the enactment date of Public Law 117-328. This grandfathering provision applies to any cash or deferred arrangement under section 401(k) or annuity contract under section 403(b) in existence prior to that date. The law also exempts distinct employer and plan categories: Small employers that have not normally employed more than 10 employees, remaining exempt until one year after the close of the first taxable year in which the business normally employed more than 10 workers. New businesses that have been in existence, together with any predecessor company, for less than 3 years. Governmental plans. Church plans. SIMPLE 401(k) plans established under section 401(k)(11) of the Internal Revenue Code. The U.S. Department of the Treasury stated that the final regulations clarify these statutory exceptions for qualifying small and new businesses, governmental plans, church plans, and SIMPLE 401(k) plans. If an employer fits into an exempt category, it is not required by federal law to implement automatic enrollment. However, an exempt employer may still choose to offer voluntary automatic enrollment under its own plan terms. How the Default Rate Rises Year by Year The statute sets a minimum path for automatic increases. The minimum path under the rule, shown as our illustrative arithmetic, starts at a 3% contribution rate in year 1 and increases by 1 percentage point for each completed year of participation until reaching 10% after seven step increases. To see how this baseline schedule affects gross compensation, consider a worker earning a constant $60,000 annual salary. This is pre-tax, salary-only illustrative arithmetic and not a prediction of investment returns, employer contributions, or take-home pay. At a $60,000 salary, a 3% default contribution in Year 1 equals $1,800 per year, or $150 per month. By Year 8, when the automatic escalation reaches the 10% minimum target, an employee earning the same $60,000 salary would contribute $6,000 per year, or $500 per month. The statutory escalator increases savings gradually, unless you opt out or choose another rate. How to Opt Out or Change Your Rate Automatic enrollment is never mandatory for the individual employee. Under 26 U.S. Code 414A(b)(3) , every participant has the affirmative right to opt out of contributions entirely or select an alternative contribution percentage that differs from the plan default. You can elect not to have contributions made or elect to contribute at a different percentage. When you receive an automatic enrollment notice from your employer, examine the details carefully. Check the specified default contribution percentage, the date automatic payroll deductions begin, the annual escalation schedule, and the exact steps required to submit an opt-out election. If you do not want deductions taken from your paycheck, submit your election before the plan's stated deadline. What the Final Regulations Changed for Employers The final Treasury regulations addressed operational considerations for employers establishing or operating automatic contribution arrangements. The U.S. Department of the Treasury stated that the final rules give employers administrative flexibility in the timing and amount of automatic contribution increases within statutory boundaries. The final regulations also simplify communication burdens for plan administrators. Treasury stated the rules simplify plan communications by allowing certain required notices to be combined and reducing annual notice requirements for qualifying non-participating employees who receive annual reminders. Treasury also stated the regulations explain how automatic enrollment requirements apply following plan mergers. Frequently asked questions Is 401(k) auto-enrollment mandatory? No, automatic enrollment is not mandatory for employees. Federal law requires covered employers to enroll eligible workers by default, but you always have the statutory right under 26 U.S.C. 414A to opt out of contributions entirely or select a different savings rate. Can I opt out of automatic enrollment? Yes, you can opt out of automatic enrollment. Under 26 U.S.C. 414A, employees may elect not to have contributions made or choose a different contribution rate. Does my current plan have to change? The sources checked do not state whether a specific plan must change today. Plans established before December 29, 2022, are grandfathered under 26 U.S.C. 414A(c), though an exempt employer may still choose to offer automatic enrollment voluntarily. What rate will I start at under automatic enrollment? You will start at a default rate of at least 3% of your pay. The statute allows plans to select an initial default between 3% and 10% in your first year, which then increases by 1 percentage point each year until reaching at least 10%. When do the final regulations apply? Treasury states the final regulations generally apply to plan years beginning after December 31, 2027. However, the underlying statutory provisions of Public Law 117-328 apply to plan years beginning after December 31, 2024, and primary sources do not state which date governs a particular workplace plan. Which plans are exempt from the auto-enrollment rules? Exempt plans include those established before December 29, 2022, plans of businesses in existence under 3 years, small employers that have not normally employed more than 10 employees, until one year after the close of the first taxable year in which they did, church plans, governmental plans, and SIMPLE 401(k) plans.
