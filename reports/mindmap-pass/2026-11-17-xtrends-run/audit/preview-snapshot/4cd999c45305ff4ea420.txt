Gather your transaction timestamps and account statements before calling, record the representative's name and case details, and escalate to a Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) complaint if Chase declines or fails to resolve the issue. A bank fee reversal is a customer request decided at the discretion of Chase, not an automatic legal right. A fee from your own regular spending or an ordinary late payment has no link to the outage, so the outage gives you nothing to cite. Tools for this journey How to Ask Chase to Reverse Outage Fees Asking Chase directly to review and refund charges tied to downtime requires clear documentation and prompt communication. Follow these five steps to submit your request: Identify and list every fee that posted around the November 16 window, noting the exact date, transaction description, and dollar figure. Gather supporting proof, including screenshots of app error messages, connection failure notices from linked tools, and account statements showing attempted payment dates. Contact Chase by phone or in writing through your Chase account. Record the date and time of every conversation, the name or identification number of each representative you speak with, and any reference number assigned to your case. Follow up in writing through your Chase account if you spoke by phone, confirming the details discussed and providing attached copies of your documentation. Submitting this request does not guarantee that Chase will reverse the charge. Each fee reversal remains subject to individual review by Chase. What Chase Said Happened on November 16 According to reporting by NBC News , Chase customers reported trouble logging into accounts and withdrawing funds on Monday, November 16, 2026. Around 4:00 p.m. on X, JPMorganChase said it experienced a temporary issue related to a software bug in an on-premise hardware system. Chase confirmed that the problem was not cyber-related. While Chase stated that customers still maintained access to accounts and funds, the bank acknowledged that some transactions were taking longer than normal. The disruption affected online services as well as in-person branch transactions across an institution serving approximately 94 million customer relationships, 5,000 branches, and 15,000 automated teller machines (ATMs). A Chase spokesperson told NBC News around 6:30 p.m. that transactions were posting normally. The report did not state that service was fully restored. Fortune reported that the system problem spread across the Chase website and mobile application to certain branches and third-party financial applications. Plaid noted that its own network operated normally but observed connection problems communicating with Chase accounts, which caused errors when users attempted to link or refresh accounts. A Chase spokesperson told Fortune that technical problems slowed completion times for transactions. Fortune noted that when Chase online banking experienced an outage lasting more than a day in 2010, the bank agreed to refund late fees for customers who missed payment deadlines, provided those customers called Chase to ask. That past decision serves as historical precedent rather than a binding commitment for 2026. What to Document and a Request Script Before speaking to a representative, organize your evidence so you can state the facts clearly. Keep copies of the following items: The dates and exact times you attempted to access your account, transfer funds, or make payments. Account statement lines displaying the posted fees alongside the underlying transactions. Screenshots of mobile application errors, website timeout pages, or third-party transfer alerts. Records showing sufficient funds were present or intended for deposit before the disruption began. A contact log listing the date, time, representative name or ID, and ticket reference number for every interaction. Use this request script as a guide when calling customer service or writing a message through your Chase account: "I am contacting you to request a fee reversal for charges caused by the technical outage on November 16, 2026. As a direct result, my account was assessed a fee on [Date] in the amount of [Dollar Amount]. I attempted to complete this transaction during the downtime, and the delay was outside my control. Please review my account history, waive this charge, and confirm the adjustment in writing." State the facts without asserting that Chase is legally mandated to grant the waiver. Focus on showing that your account remained in good standing prior to the system failure. Delayed Zelle Payments and Electronic Transfer Errors Account holders who attempted person-to-person transfers during the disruption faced delays. In a statement reported by NBC News, Zelle stated: "Zelle is not down. Zelle is operating as it should. An issue at the financial institutions may be delaying Zelle payments for some of their customers. Please contact them directly." Because the transfer delays stemmed from processing at the financial institution rather than the central payment network, resolution requires working directly through Chase. If you believe an electronic fund transfer was handled incorrectly, federal rules provide a formal procedure. Under 12 CFR 1005.11 , known as Regulation E, an error includes an unauthorized electronic fund transfer, an incorrect electronic fund transfer to or from your account, or the omission of an electronic fund transfer from your periodic statement. The financial institution must investigate if you submit an oral or written notice of error no later than 60 days after the institution sends the periodic statement displaying the disputed item. Your error notice must provide your name and account number, along with the type, date, and dollar amount of the suspected error. Chase may require written confirmation within 10 business days of receiving an oral notice. The bank must investigate and determine whether an error occurred within 10 business days, or take up to 45 days if it grants a provisional credit for the disputed amount within the initial 10-business-day window. The sources checked do not state whether outage-related processing delays or incidental bank fees qualify as errors under Regulation E. Treat your request for a fee refund as a direct appeal to Chase rather than a statutory error claim unless a specific transfer posted for the wrong amount. When to Escalate to the CFPB If Chase refuses to refund fees resulting from the system downtime or fails to provide an answer, you can submit a formal complaint to the CFPB. The CFPB routes submitted consumer complaints directly to financial companies for a response. According to published CFPB complaint procedures, most companies provide an initial response within 15 days, and in some cases give a final response within 60 days. Before submitting a complaint, verify that you attempted to resolve the dispute with Chase first. The CFPB intake questionnaire asks whether you reached out to Chase before filing. When compiling your complaint, include only the most important dates, fee figures, and communication logs. You may upload supporting attachments such as monthly statements, transaction receipts, and customer service correspondence, up to a maximum file limit of 50 pages. Consumers generally cannot file a second complaint regarding the exact same problem, so ensure your initial submission contains all supporting documentation. The CFPB enters complaint details, excluding direct personal identifiers, into its public Consumer Complaint Database. Once Chase submits its official response through the system, you have 60 days to provide consumer feedback on the outcome. You can file online through the complaint portal or call the agency directly at (855) 411-2372. Our Takeaway Ask Chase during your initial outreach to reverse each fee tied to the November 16 outage, documenting transaction times and communication reference numbers, and submit a CFPB complaint if the bank leaves the charges in place. Reversal decisions remain at the discretion of Chase, while a Regulation E error notice serves as a separate legal avenue only if an electronic fund transfer was unauthorized, incorrect, or omitted. These steps apply directly to charges triggered by system downtime, though any future announcement from Chase regarding automatic refunds or blanket waivers would supersede individual requests.
