How to Get Chase Outage Fees Reversed After the November 16 Disruption

By Jonathan Velez, Personal Finance Writer & Editor · Reviewed against primary sources · Updated November 17, 2026

Ask Chase directly, by phone or in writing through your Chase account, to reverse each fee and cite the November 16, 2026 outage date. Gather your transaction timestamps and account statements before calling, record the representative's name and case details, and escalate to a Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) complaint if Chase declines or fails to resolve the issue.

The checked sources do not show a Chase policy that reverses fees for this outage, so treat each reversal as a request to Chase. A fee from your own regular spending or an ordinary late payment has no link to the outage, so the outage gives you nothing to cite.

How to Ask Chase to Reverse Outage Fees

Asking Chase directly to review and refund charges tied to downtime requires clear documentation and prompt communication. Follow these five steps to submit your request:

  1. Identify and list every fee that posted around the November 16 window, noting the exact date, transaction description, and dollar figure.
  2. Gather supporting proof, including screenshots of app error messages, connection failure notices from linked tools, and account statements showing attempted payment dates.
  3. Contact Chase by phone or in writing through your Chase account.
  4. Record the date and time of every conversation, the name or identification number of each representative you speak with, and any reference number assigned to your case.
  5. Follow up in writing through your Chase account if you spoke by phone, confirming the details discussed and providing attached copies of your documentation.

Submitting this request does not guarantee that Chase will reverse the charge. Each fee reversal remains subject to individual review by Chase.

What Chase Said Happened on November 16

According to reporting by NBC News, Chase customers reported trouble logging into accounts and withdrawing funds on Monday, November 16, 2026. Around 4:00 p.m. on X, JPMorganChase said it experienced a temporary issue related to a software bug in an on-premise hardware system. Chase confirmed that the problem was not cyber-related. While Chase stated that customers still maintained access to accounts and funds, Chase acknowledged that some transactions were taking longer than normal. The disruption affected online services as well as in-person branch transactions across an institution serving approximately 94 million customer relationships, 5,000 branches, and 15,000 automated teller machines (ATMs).

A Chase spokesperson told NBC News around 6:30 p.m. that transactions were posting normally. The report did not state that service was fully restored. Fortune reported that the system problem spread across the Chase website and mobile application to certain branches and third-party financial applications. Plaid noted that its own network operated normally but observed connection problems communicating with Chase accounts, which caused errors when users attempted to link or refresh accounts. A Chase spokesperson told Fortune that technical problems slowed completion times for transactions.

In 2010, Chase online banking was down for more than a day. Fortune reports that Chase then agreed to refund late fees to customers who missed payments, if they called to ask. That past decision serves as historical precedent rather than a binding commitment for 2026.

What to Document and a Request Script

Before speaking to a representative, organize your evidence so you can state the facts clearly. Keep copies of the following items:

  • The dates and exact times you attempted to access your account, transfer funds, or make payments.
  • Account statement lines displaying the posted fees alongside the underlying transactions.
  • Screenshots of mobile application errors, website timeout pages, or third-party transfer alerts.
  • Records showing sufficient funds were present or intended for deposit before the disruption began.
  • A contact log listing the date, time, representative name or ID, and ticket reference number for every interaction.

Use this request script as a guide when calling customer service or writing a message through your Chase account:

"I am contacting you to request a fee reversal for charges caused by the technical outage on November 16, 2026. As a direct result, my account was assessed a fee on [Date] in the amount of [Dollar Amount]. I attempted to complete this transaction during the downtime, and the delay was outside my control. Please review my account history, waive this charge, and confirm the adjustment in writing."

State the facts without asserting that Chase is legally mandated to grant the waiver. Focus on showing that your account remained in good standing prior to the system failure.

Delayed Zelle Payments and Electronic Transfer Errors

Account holders who attempted person-to-person transfers during the disruption faced delays. In a statement reported by NBC News, Zelle stated: "Zelle is not down. Zelle is operating as it should. An issue at the financial institutions may be delaying Zelle payments for some of their customers. Please contact them directly." Because the transfer delays stemmed from processing at the financial institution rather than the central payment network, resolution requires working directly through Chase.

If you believe an electronic fund transfer was handled incorrectly, federal rules provide a formal procedure. Regulation E (12 CFR 1005.11) lists the errors it covers. Three are an unauthorized electronic fund transfer, an incorrect transfer to or from your account, and a transfer missing from your periodic statement. The financial institution must investigate if you submit an oral or written notice of error no later than 60 days after the institution sends the periodic statement displaying the disputed item.

Your error notice must provide your name and account number, along with the type, date, and dollar amount of the suspected error. Chase may require written confirmation within 10 business days of receiving an oral notice. Chase must decide within 10 business days whether an error occurred, or take up to 45 days if it grants a provisional credit for the disputed amount within the initial 10 business days. Longer periods of 20 business days and 90 days apply to some new accounts and to certain point-of-sale and foreign transfers (12 CFR 1005.11(c)(3)). The sources checked do not state whether outage-related processing delays or incidental bank fees qualify as errors under Regulation E. Treat your request for a fee refund as a direct appeal to Chase rather than a statutory error claim unless a specific transfer posted for the wrong amount.

When to Escalate to the CFPB

If Chase refuses to refund fees resulting from the system downtime or fails to provide an answer, you can submit a formal complaint to the CFPB. The CFPB routes submitted consumer complaints directly to financial companies for a response. According to published CFPB complaint procedures, most companies provide an initial response within 15 days, and in some cases give a final response within 60 days.

Before submitting a complaint, contact Chase first. The CFPB complaint guide suggests trying the company first. When compiling your complaint, include only the most important dates, fee figures, and communication logs. You may upload supporting attachments such as monthly statements, transaction receipts, and customer service correspondence, up to a maximum file limit of 50 pages.

You generally cannot file a second CFPB complaint about the same problem. Put every date, fee and document in the first one. The CFPB enters complaint details, excluding direct personal identifiers, into its public Consumer Complaint Database. Once Chase submits its official response through the system, you have 60 days to provide consumer feedback on the outcome. You can file online through the complaint portal or call the agency directly at (855) 411-2372.

Our Takeaway

Ask Chase during your initial outreach to reverse each fee tied to the November 16 outage, documenting transaction times and communication reference numbers, and submit a CFPB complaint if Chase leaves the charges in place. The checked sources do not show that Chase must reverse a fee. A Regulation E error notice is a separate route that applies only if an electronic fund transfer was unauthorized, incorrect, or omitted. These steps apply directly to charges triggered by system downtime, though any future announcement from Chase regarding automatic refunds or blanket waivers would supersede individual requests. Frequently asked questions

Does Chase refund fees after an outage?

The checked sources do not show a Chase policy that guarantees reversals. Checked sources do not show an announced blanket waiver or automatic refund program for the November 16, 2026 disruption. In a 2010 outage that lasted more than a day, Chase refunded late fees to customers who called to ask.

What should I do if Chase says no?

Submit a complaint to the CFPB if direct customer service appeals fail. Provide your itemized fee amounts, transaction dates, and account records. Most companies respond to CFPB inquiries within 15 days, with up to 60 days in certain cases.

How long do I have to dispute a transaction error?

Submit your notice within 60 days of the periodic statement date if you are disputing an electronic transfer error under Regulation E (12 CFR 1005.11). Chase must decide within 10 business days, or within 45 days if it issues provisional credit. Longer periods of 20 business days and 90 days apply to some new accounts and to certain point-of-sale and foreign transfers. The checked sources do not state whether outage delays or incidental fees qualify as statutory errors.

What if a Zelle payment was delayed?

Contact Chase directly to check the processing status of the transaction. Zelle told NBC News it was operating as it should and that an issue at the financial institutions may be delaying Zelle payments for some of their customers.

Which Chase overdraft fees can I avoid in the future?

Avoid fees by keeping ending daily overdraft balances at $50 or less, or by depositing funds before 11:00 p.m. ET on the next business day under Chase Overdraft Assist. You can also turn Debit Card Coverage off so everyday card transactions decline with no fee, or switch to a Chase Secure Banking account.
