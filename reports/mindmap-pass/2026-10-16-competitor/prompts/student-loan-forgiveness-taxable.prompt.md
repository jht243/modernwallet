answer: It depends on the kind of forgiveness and the year. Debt canceled is generally taxable income, and the temporary federal exclusion for student loan discharges covered discharges after December 31, 2020 and before January 1, 2026. Forgiveness that comes from working in certain professions, and discharges because of the student's death or total and permanent disability, are excluded under section 108(f). Forgiveness that is none of these, such as a balance canceled at the end of an income-driven plan, is generally taxable in the year it is canceled unless another exclusion such as insolvency applies.

## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- Income from discharge of indebtedness is listed as an item of gross income (26 U.S.C. § 61(a)(11)).
- IRS Topic 431 (Canceled debt): "the amount of the canceled debt is taxable" and must be reported on the tax return for the year in which the cancellation occurred; nonbusiness canceled debt is reported on Form 1040 or 1040-SR with Schedule 1. A creditor may send Form 1099-C showing the amount and date canceled. You must report the correct taxable amount regardless of the accuracy of the Form 1099-C, and should contact the creditor if it is wrong.
- IRS Topic 431 lists as an exception: certain student loan discharges after December 31, 2020, and before January 1, 2026; and cancellation due to the death or total and permanent disability of the student.
- Section 108(f)(1): gross income does not include a student loan discharge made under a loan provision that forgives all or part of the debt if the individual worked for a certain period of time in certain professions for any of a broad class of employers. (Do not name specific programs: the sources here do not list them. Say the reader should check whether their program is one that qualifies.)
- Section 108(f)(5) as amended in 2025 applies to discharges after December 31, 2025: it excludes loans discharged on account of the death or total and permanent disability of the student; the exclusion covers federal student loans and private education loans and is conditioned on including the taxpayer's Social Security number on the return for that year.
- Insolvency exclusion: section 108(a)(1)(B) excludes canceled debt when the discharge occurs when the taxpayer is insolvent, limited under § 108(a)(3) to the amount by which the taxpayer is insolvent. "Insolvent" means the excess of liabilities over the fair market value of assets (§ 108(d)(3)). Debt canceled in a Title 11 bankruptcy case is also excluded (§ 108(a)(1)(A); IRS Topic 431).
- Excluded amounts generally require reducing tax attributes and are reported on Form 982, "Reduction of Tax Attributes Due to Discharge of Indebtedness" (IRS Topic 431).
