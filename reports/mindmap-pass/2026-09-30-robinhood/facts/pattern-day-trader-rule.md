# CLOSED FACT LIST — The pattern day trader rule and its 2026 replacement, verified 2026-09-30

## The old rule (FINRA Rule 4210 day trading margin requirements)
- Under the former Rule 4210, "day trading" was defined, subject to exceptions, as "the purchasing and selling or the selling and purchasing of the same security on the same day in a margin account." [source: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-104572.pdf]
- FINRA rules defined a "pattern day trader" as any customer who executes four or more day trades within five business days, provided that the number of day trades is more than six percent of the customer's total trades in the margin account for that same five-business-day period. [source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/pattern-day-trader]
- Under the former rule, a customer whose day trades were six percent or less of total trades for a five-business-day period was not considered a pattern day trader. [source: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-104572.pdf]
- Investor.gov: the definition is a minimum requirement, and "some broker-dealers use a slightly broader definition" (e.g., a firm that provided day trading training before opening the account could designate that customer a pattern day trader). [source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/pattern-day-trader]
- The former rule required minimum equity of $25,000 in a pattern day trader's account. That equity had to be deposited before the customer could continue day trading and maintained at all times. [source: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-104572.pdf]
- "Day-trading buying power" under the former rule was the equity at the previous day's close, less the maintenance margin requirement, "multiplied by four for equity securities." [source: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-104572.pdf]
- Exceeding day-trading buying power created a special maintenance margin deficiency. Among the required actions, buying power was then limited to two times (not four) for equity securities. [source: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-104572.pdf]
- Pattern day traders who failed to meet a special maintenance margin call within five business days could trade only on a cash-available basis for 90 days or until the call was met. [source: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-104572.pdf]
- Funds deposited to meet the pattern day trader minimum equity or maintenance requirements could not be withdrawn for a minimum of two business days following the close of business on the deposit day. [source: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-104572.pdf]
- The day trading margin requirements came from rule changes by the NYSE and NASD (FINRA's predecessor) that the SEC approved in 2001 (Release No. 44009, Feb. 27, 2001). [source: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-104572.pdf]

## The 2026 change
- FINRA filed the proposed rule change, File No. SR-FINRA-2025-017, with the SEC on December 29, 2025. The SEC's notice of filing is Release No. 34-104572, dated January 9, 2026 (91 FR 1580, January 14, 2026). [source: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-104572.pdf]
- The SEC approved it on April 14, 2026 (Exchange Act Release No. 105226, 91 FR 20731, April 17, 2026), granting accelerated approval of the rule as modified by Amendment No. 1. [source: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/Regulatory-Notice-26-10.pdf]
- FINRA's April 15, 2026 update: "The SEC approved SR-FINRA-2025-017, which establishes new intraday margin standards to replace the longstanding day trading margin requirements under FINRA Rule 4210." [source: https://www.finra.org/compliance-tools/weekly-archive/04152026]
- FINRA published Regulatory Notice 26-10 on April 20, 2026. [source: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/Regulatory-Notice-26-10.pdf]
- Regulatory Notice 26-10: the new standards replace "in their entirety" the day trading margin requirements, "including the day trade count requirements for designating a customer as a 'pattern day trader' and the $25,000 pattern day trader minimum equity requirement." [source: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/Regulatory-Notice-26-10.pdf]
- Effective date: June 4, 2026 (45 days from publication of the Notice). Firms that need more time may phase in implementation over 18 months, until October 20, 2027. [source: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/Regulatory-Notice-26-10.pdf]
- Investor.gov: during the transition, "Your brokerage firm might continue operating under the old day trading margin requirements during the transition, or they might choose to migrate to the new intraday margin standards sooner." [source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/pattern-day-trader]
- Under the new rule (Rule 4210(d)(2)), each firm must determine the "intraday margin deficit" for each customer margin account (other than a good faith account or portfolio margin account) on each day with an "IML-reducing transaction." [source: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/Regulatory-Notice-26-10.pdf]
- Real-time monitoring is not required. Firms may instead make a single calculation of an account's intraday margin deficit, as they do now for maintenance margin. [source: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/Regulatory-Notice-26-10.pdf]
- The new rule "does not change the regular maintenance margin requirements as they exist today, but rather supplements them." [source: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/Regulatory-Notice-26-10.pdf]
- An intraday margin deficit must be satisfied "as promptly as possible." It stays outstanding until satisfied, or until immediately after the close of business on the 15th business day after the deficit date. [source: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/Regulatory-Notice-26-10.pdf]
- New 90-day freeze: a customer who "makes a practice" of failing to satisfy intraday margin deficits promptly, and who fails to satisfy one by the close of business on the fifth business day, is blocked from creating or increasing a short position or debit balance for 90 calendar days, or until the deficit is satisfied. [source: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/Regulatory-Notice-26-10.pdf]
- Deficits that do not exceed the lesser of 5 percent of account equity or $1,000 do not count toward "making a practice." [source: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/Regulatory-Notice-26-10.pdf]
- Portfolio margin accounts with less than $5 million in equity must maintain margin for intraday risk that is substantially similar to end-of-day margin. [source: https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/Regulatory-Notice-26-10.pdf]
- The $2,000 minimum is the pre-existing general margin equity requirement in Rule 4210(b) ("equity of at least $2,000"). The new intraday rule did not create it. [source: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/4210]
- FINRA investor page (updated June 4, 2026): "To trade using margin ... you must maintain a minimum of $2,000 in equity in your margin account," and firms may set higher "house" requirements. [source: https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/frequent-intraday-trading]

## Cash accounts (Regulation T)
- A cash account requires full payment for securities purchased; the investor cannot borrow from the broker-dealer to pay for trades. [source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/updated-9]
- Buying and selling a security before paying for it in a cash account is "freeriding." It is not permitted under Regulation T and may require the broker to freeze the account for 90 days. During the freeze the investor may still buy, but must pay in full on the trade date. [source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/updated-9]
- FINRA: a "good faith violation" can occur if you buy a security with cash from an unsettled transaction and sell it before the funding proceeds have settled. [source: https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/frequent-intraday-trading]

## Broker implementation
- Robinhood: "On June 4, 2026, FINRA's new intraday margin standards will replace Pattern Day Trading (PDT)." Starting June 4: "No more day trade restrictions or day trade calls with your Robinhood margin account," and existing PDT flags or restrictions will be removed. [source: https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/pattern-day-trading/]
- Robinhood: "You will no longer need to maintain a $25,000 minimum portfolio value to day trade in your margin account. Keep in mind that the $2,000 margin minimum equity requirement will still apply." [source: https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/pattern-day-trading/]
- Robinhood says it monitors accounts in real time to prevent account activity from creating or increasing intraday margin deficits (IMD), and that repeated failures to meet requirements can lead to further restrictions. [source: https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/pattern-day-trading/]
- E*TRADE says it implemented the changes on June 9, 2026. Intraday buying power for its margin clients is now based on real-time intraday margin excess. An IMD is due five days after issuance, and three violations in a rolling 12-month period may bring 90-day restrictions. [secondary: E*TRADE] [source: https://us.etrade.com/knowledge/library/margin/pattern-day-trading-rule-change]

## Contested / unverified
- "Three day trades allowed in a rolling five-business-day window" is inferred from the four-or-more definition. No fetched primary source states "three" directly. Phrase it as "a fourth day trade within five business days triggered the designation (if over 6% of trades)."
- The Schwab International article (starting point) returned HTTP 403 and was not used.
- The Regulatory Notice cites the SEC approval order as Release No. 105226. The sec.gov PDF (34-105226.pdf) did not download cleanly, so the approval-order text was not read directly.
- Robinhood's page gives no "last updated" date. Its cash-account rules were not covered on that page.

## DO NOT STATE
- Do not say the $2,000 minimum is new, or that it is an "eligibility threshold" of the intraday rule. It is the long-standing Rule 4210(b) margin minimum.
- Do not say every broker has dropped PDT. Firms have until October 20, 2027, and may keep the old rules during the transition.
- Do not say the SEC "eliminated" the rule on its own. FINRA amended Rule 4210 and the SEC approved it.
- Do not say margin is no longer required or that day trading is unlimited. Intraday margin deficits and the 90-day freeze still apply.
- Do not say cash accounts were subject to the PDT rule, or that freeriding/good-faith rules changed. They did not change.
- Do not use a filing number other than SR-FINRA-2025-017, or approval, effective or deadline dates other than April 14, 2026 / June 4, 2026 / October 20, 2027.
