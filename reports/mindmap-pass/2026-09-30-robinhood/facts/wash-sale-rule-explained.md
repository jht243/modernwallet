# CLOSED FACT LIST — The wash sale rule (26 U.S.C. §1091), verified 2026-09-30

## Definition and window
- Pub 550 says you cannot deduct losses from sales or trades of stock or securities in a wash sale "unless the loss was incurred in the ordinary course of your business as a dealer in stock or securities." [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Per Pub 550, a wash sale occurs when you sell or trade stock or securities at a loss and, within 30 days before or after the sale, you: (1) buy substantially identical stock or securities, (2) acquire substantially identical stock or securities in a fully taxable trade, (3) acquire a contract or option to buy substantially identical stock or securities, or (4) acquire substantially identical stock for your IRA or Roth IRA. [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- The statute's window is "a period beginning 30 days before the date of such sale or disposition and ending 30 days after such date." (30 days before + sale day + 30 days after = 61 days; this total is arithmetic, not a statutory phrase.) [source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1091]
- §1091(a): "the term 'stock or securities' shall, except as provided in regulations, include contracts or options to acquire or sell stock or securities." [source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1091]
- Pub 550: "If you sell stock and your spouse or a corporation you control buys substantially identical stock, you also have a wash sale." [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Pub 550: the wash sale rules apply to losses from sales or trades of contracts and options to acquire or sell stock or securities; they do not apply to losses from sales or trades of commodity futures contracts and foreign currencies. [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Pub 550: the wash sale rules apply if you sell common stock at a loss and, at the same time, buy warrants for common stock of the same corporation. [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- §1091(e) applies similar rules to a loss on closing a short sale if substantially identical stock or securities were sold, or another short sale of them entered into, within 30 days before or after the closing. [source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1091]
- An employee who sells bonus-award shares at a loss and receives another bonus award of substantially identical stock within 30 days of the sale cannot deduct the loss (Pub 550 Example 2). [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]

## What happens to the disallowed loss
- Pub 550: "If your loss was disallowed because of the wash sale rules, add the disallowed loss to the cost of the new stock or securities (except in (4) above)" [(4) = IRA/Roth IRA purchase]; "The result is your basis in the new stock or securities." [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Pub 550: "This adjustment postpones the loss deduction until the disposition of the new stock or securities." [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Pub 550: "Your holding period for the new stock or securities includes the holding period of the stock or securities sold." [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Pub 550 Example 1: buy 100 shares of X for $1,000; sell for $750; within 30 days buy 100 shares for $800. The $250 loss is not deductible; basis in the new shares is $1,050 ($800 + $250). [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Pub 550: loss from a wash sale of one block cannot be used to reduce gains on identical blocks sold the same day. [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]

## IRA purchases (Rev. Rul. 2008-5)
- Rev. Rul. 2008-5 appears in Internal Revenue Bulletin 2008-3, dated January 22, 2008. [source: https://www.irs.gov/irb/2008-03_IRB]
- Rev. Rul. 2008-5 holding: if an individual sells stock at a loss and causes their IRA or Roth IRA to buy substantially identical stock within 30 days before or after the sale, the loss is disallowed under §1091, and the individual's basis in the IRA or Roth IRA is not increased by virtue of §1091(d). [source: https://www.irs.gov/irb/2008-03_IRB]

## Partial replacements (more or fewer shares)
- Pub 550: if you buy more or fewer shares than you sold within the window, match the shares bought with an equal number of the shares sold, "in the same order that you bought them, beginning with the first shares bought." [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Pub 550 dated example: 100 shares of M bought Sept 20, 2024 for $5,000; 50 shares bought Dec 13, 2024 for $2,750; 25 shares bought Dec 20, 2024 for $1,125; the 100 original shares sold Jan 3, 2025 for $4,000 ($1,000 loss). Loss on 75 shares ($750) is disallowed; loss on 25 shares ($250) is deductible. The Dec 13 lot's basis becomes $3,250 ($2,750 + $500); the Dec 20 lot's basis becomes $1,375 ($1,125 + $250). [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Pub 550 second dated example: 100 shares bought Sept 16, 2024, sold Jan 29, 2025 at a $1,000 loss; 50 shares bought on each of Feb 3–6, 2025. The full $1,000 loss is disallowed; $500 is added to the basis of the Feb 3 shares and $500 to the Feb 4 shares. [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]

## "Substantially identical"
- Pub 550: "In determining whether stock or securities are substantially identical, you must consider all the facts and circumstances in your particular case." [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Pub 550: "Ordinarily, stocks or securities of one corporation are not considered substantially identical to stocks or securities of another corporation. However, they may be substantially identical in some cases," e.g., predecessor and successor corporations in a reorganization. [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Pub 550: bonds or preferred stock are not ordinarily substantially identical to the same corporation's common stock, but convertible bonds/preferred may be, depending on relative values, price changes and other circumstances. [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- The wash sale section of Pub 550 (2025) gives no test for mutual funds or ETFs; the only guidance is the facts-and-circumstances language above. [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]

## Broker reporting and Form 8949
- Form 1099-B instructions (2026), box 1g "Wash Sale Loss Disallowed": brokers "must report any loss disallowed under section 1091 if both the sale and purchase transactions occur in the same account with respect to covered securities with the same CUSIP number." [source: https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099b]
- Form 1099-B instructions: brokers are "permitted but are not required to report in box 1g all loss disallowed under section 1091," e.g., a sale in one account and repurchase in a different account. [source: https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099b]
- Pub 550: box 1g shows the wash sale loss disallowed only if the securities sold were covered securities and the replacement had the same CUSIP and was bought in the same account; "However, you cannot deduct a loss from a wash sale even if it is not reported on Form 1099-B." [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Pub 550 how to report: report the wash sale in Part I or Part II of Form 8949, enter "W" in column (f), and enter the disallowed loss as a positive number in column (g). [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Form 8949 instructions (2025), code W: if the nondeductible wash sale loss in box 1g of Form 1099-B (or box 1i of Form 1099-DA) is incorrect, enter the correct amount as a positive number in column (g); if it is less than the amount shown, attach a statement explaining the difference; if no part of the loss is a wash sale loss, enter -0- in column (g). [source: https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8949]

## Traders and dealers
- IRS Topic 429: for traders without a mark-to-market election, "both the limitations on capital losses and the wash sales rules continue to apply." [source: https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc429]
- IRS Topic 429: "The limitations on capital losses, the wash sale rules, and certain other rules do not apply to traders using the mark-to-market method" (the §475(f) election). [source: https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc429]
- Pub 550: the wash sale rules do not apply to a dealer in stock or securities if the loss is from a transaction made in the ordinary course of business. [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]

## Crypto / digital assets (status as of 2026-09-30)
- The text of §1091 applies to "stock or securities" and does not mention digital assets; the most recent amendment listed is from 2002 (Pub. L. 107–147). [source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1091]
- IRS: "For U.S. tax purposes, digital assets are considered property, not currency." [source: https://www.irs.gov/filing/digital-assets]
- Pub 550: "A digital asset is treated as property, and general tax principles that apply to property transactions apply to transactions using digital assets," citing Notice 2014-21. [source: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550]
- Form 1099-DA instructions (2026), box 1i: "Losses from wash sales of tokenized securities must be reported," and the same-account, same-CUSIP requirement applies to tokenized securities "treated as stock or securities under section 1091." [source: https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099da]
- On Sept 16, 2026, the House Ways and Means Committee approved H.R. 10357, the Digital Asset Tax Certainty Act, 38–5. It would apply wash sale rules to traded digital assets (other than qualified U.S. dollar stablecoins) sold after Sept 14, 2026. It has not passed the full House or the Senate. [secondary: Unchained] [source: https://unchainedcrypto.com/house-panel-advances-crypto-tax-bill-that-would-limit-certain-loss-write-offs/]
- Forvis Mazars (Sept 17, 2026): "broader House and Senate action remain uncertain, especially considering the impending mid-term elections." [secondary: Forvis Mazars] [source: https://www.forvismazars.us/forsights/2026/09/ways-means-approves-digital-asset-tax-certainty-act]

## Contested / unverified
- Whether H.R. 10357 will be enacted, and whether its Sept 14, 2026 effective date would survive: this is unknown. Only secondary sources were fetched; congress.gov was not checked. Describe it only as "a bill approved by a House committee."
- The "61-day window" label is widely used, but the statute says only "30 days before ... 30 days after." Present 61 days as the arithmetic total.
- Whether two different index funds or ETFs tracking the same index are "substantially identical": the IRS has not addressed this in Pub 550. Do not give an answer either way.
- The acquisition by a spouse or controlled corporation comes from Pub 550. The statute text fetched does not state it explicitly.

## DO NOT STATE
- Do not say crypto is currently subject to the wash sale rule. Also do not say it is permanently exempt; a pending bill would change it retroactively to Sept 14, 2026, if enacted.
- Do not say the loss in an IRA wash sale is "deferred" or "added to IRA basis." Rev. Rul. 2008-5 says basis is not increased.
- Do not say brokers track wash sales across accounts, across brokers, or into IRAs. They are required to report only same-account, same-CUSIP covered securities.
- Do not say that if a wash sale isn't on the 1099-B you can deduct the loss.
- Do not give an IRS "bright-line" test for when funds or ETFs are substantially identical.
- Do not say day traders are exempt. Only traders with a valid §475(f) mark-to-market election (and dealers acting in the ordinary course of business) are outside the rule.
- Do not cite a Pub 550 edition other than 2025 (for 2025 returns), 1099-B/1099-DA instructions other than 2026, or 8949 instructions other than 2025.
