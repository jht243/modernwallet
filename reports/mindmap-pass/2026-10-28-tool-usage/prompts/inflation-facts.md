## CLOSED FACT LIST — every number the addition may state. Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent an index value, a year, a statistic or a URL.
- The calculator now has a second mode, "From a past year". Inputs: an amount in that year's dollars and a year from 1913 to 2025. Outputs: the amount in September 2026 dollars, the percent prices rose since that year, the average inflation per year, and the two index values used.
- Formula: value today = amount × CPI-U for September 2026 ÷ CPI-U annual average for the chosen year. The average per year treats an annual average as mid-year.
- Data: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U), U.S. city average, all items, not seasonally adjusted, series CUUR0000SA0, retrieved from the BLS Public Data API on October 28, 2026. Series page: https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/CUUR0000SA0 . BLS CPI page: https://www.bls.gov/cpi/
- CPI-U values: September 2026 = 336.94 (latest month in the data); 2010 annual average = 218.056.
- Worked example (calculated, checked in two separate programs): $10,000 in 2010 = $15,452 in September 2026 dollars; prices up 54.5% since 2010; about 2.7% a year on average over about 16.2 years.
- The figures move when BLS publishes a new month.
- The national index is an average; the reader's own prices may have risen faster or slower.
- The future-cost mode (the default) is unchanged: it still uses the rate the reader enters and has no forecast built in.
