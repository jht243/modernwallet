How it works The mortgage recast calculator sets a payment with payment = balance × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where r is the annual rate divided by 12 and n is the months left. A recast reruns the formula on the lower balance with the same r and n. On the example loan, the new $208,000 balance gives a payment of $1,432.69, down from $1,639.33. Enter the recast fee your servicer charges, and the calculator shows how many months of the lower payment it takes to earn the fee back. On the example, a $250 fee is earned back in 2 months, and the recast still saves $34,221.26 in interest after the fee. Prepay $30,000 without a recast and keep paying $1,639.33, and the lower balance clears the example loan in 228 months. That is 84 months, or 7 years, sooner, with $109,002.12 saved in interest. The recast saves $34,471.26, so it gives up interest savings in exchange for a lower required payment. A recast fits a borrower who wants a lower required payment, for example after a home sale or a drop in income. A recast saves less interest than prepaying the same lump sum without one. On the example loan, the recast saves $34,471.26, while prepaying saves $109,002.12. Paying extra principal without a recast saves more interest, and a recast gives a lower required payment.
