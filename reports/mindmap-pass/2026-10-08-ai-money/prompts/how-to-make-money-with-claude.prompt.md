## 2. FTC enforcement and rules

### Operation AI Comply (Sept 25, 2024)
- The FTC announced five actions on Sept 25, 2024: DoNotPay, Ascend Ecom, Ecommerce Empire Builders, Rytr and FBA Machine/Passive Scaling. [P] https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes (2024-09-25)
- **Ascend Ecom** (William Basta, Kenneth Leung). The FTC alleged false claims that "cutting edge" AI-powered tools would let consumers quickly earn thousands of dollars a month in passive income from online stores, and that consumers were promised "five-figure monthly income by the second year." It alleged at least $25 million in consumer losses. Case filed in C.D. Cal. [P] same URL (2024-09-25)
- **Ecommerce Empire Builders** (Peter Prusinowski). The FTC alleged that it marketed an "AI-powered Ecommerce Empire" with training costing almost $2,000 and storefronts costing up to $35,000, and that it advertised $10,000-a-month earnings with no evidence. Case filed in E.D. Pa. [P] same URL (2024-09-25)
- **FBA Machine / Passive Scaling** (Bratislav Rozenfeld). The FTC filed in June 2024, alleging more than $15.9 million in losses from "AI-powered" storefront promises, "7-figure business" claims, and false "risk-free" refund guarantees. Case filed in D.N.J. [P] same URL (2024-09-25)
- **DoNotPay** paid a $193,000 settlement over "robot lawyer" claims. **Rytr** faced an order banning its review-generation feature, approved by a 3–2 vote. [P] same URL (2024-09-25)

### Case outcomes

[P] https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/06/ftc-case-leads-order-banning-ascend-ecom-its-owners-business-opportunity-marketing (2025-06-23)
- Ecommerce Empire Builders: the court entered an order on May 8, 2025, banning the defendants from business opportunities, with a $9,786,124 judgment, partially suspended. [S] https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/05/ftc-action-ends-ecommerce-empire-builders-online-business-opportunity-scam (2025-05)
- FBA Machine: proposed order banning the defendants from business opportunities, with a $15.7 million judgment, partially suspended. [P] https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/07/ftc-obtains-permanent-ban-e-commerce-business-opportunity-scheme-operator (2025-07-30)
- Automators AI (Cresto, Chapman): settled in February 2024 after an August 2023 filing. The FTC alleged "passive investment income" from AI-powered Amazon and Walmart stores. The order bans the defendants from e-commerce business opportunities and coaching, with a $21,765,902.65 judgment, partially suspended. [P] https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/02/ftc-action-leads-ban-owners-automators-ai-e-commerce-money-making-scheme (2024-02-27)

### Click Profit (2025)
- The court issued a TRO on March 5, 2025. The FTC alleged promises of "passive income" from a proprietary AI-powered system for Amazon, Walmart and TikTok stores, ads calling the scheme "safe, secure, and proven to generate wealth," and false ties to Nike and Disney. It also alleged a management fee of at least $45,000 plus inventory costs, losses of at least $14 million, and that about 95% of the stores were blocked, suspended or terminated by Amazon. [P] https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/ftc-acts-stop-click-profit-online-business-opportunity-has-cost-consumers-least-14-million (2025-03-18)
- Settlement: permanent ban from the industry, with cash, real estate and personal property turned over for redress. [S] https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-case-against-e-commerce-business-opportunity-scheme-its-operators-results-permanent-ban-industry (2025-08). Secondary reports put the two judgments at $13,625,783 and $7,304,885. I did not confirm those amounts on ftc.gov.

### Air AI (2025–2026)
- The FTC sued in D. Ariz. on Aug 25, 2025, naming Air AI Technologies (d/b/a Air AI, Scale 13), related companies, and owners Caleb Maddix, Ryan O'Donnell and Thomas Lancer. It alleged claims that buyers would earn back "tens of thousands of dollars in a matter of days or months," refund guarantees that were rarely honored, losses as high as $250,000 per consumer, and violations of the Telemarketing Sales Rule and the Business Opportunity Rule. [P] https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-sues-stop-air-ai-using-deceptive-claims-about-business-growth-earnings-potential-refund (2025-08-25)
- Proposed settlement: a ban on selling business opportunities and an $18 million judgment, mostly suspended, with the operators paying $50,000 for consumer relief. It also bars unsubstantiated earnings claims. [P] https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/air-ai-its-owners-will-be-banned-marketing-business-opportunities-settle-ftc-charges-company-misled (2026-03-24)

### Rules and consumer guidance
- Under the Business Opportunity Rule (16 CFR Part 437), a seller may make an earnings claim only with a reasonable basis and written substantiation, and must give buyers an "EARNINGS CLAIM STATEMENT REQUIRED BY LAW." The statement must give the number and percentage of buyers who reached the stated earnings. Earnings claims in ads also need substantiation, plus the dates and the number and percentage of buyers who reached the result. [S] https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-437 (current)
- In January 2025 the FTC proposed amending the rule and renaming it the "Business and Money-Making Opportunity Rule." [S] https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/r511993bizoppnprm01132025.pdf (2025-01-13). I did not find whether it has been finalized.
- FTC consumer alert on side hustle scams. Warning signs: unrealistic pay for minimal effort, pressure to accept immediately, requests for upfront fees. [S] https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/02/how-avoid-side-hustle-scam (2026-02)
- FTC consumer page on money-making opportunity scams: https://consumer.ftc.gov/all-scams/money-making-opportunity-scams [S]. FTC hub page on AI enforcement: https://www.ftc.gov/industry/technology/artificial-intelligence [S]
- Report fraud at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. [S]

## 7. Platform AI policies

- Amazon KDP: publishers must disclose AI-generated text, images or translations when publishing or republishing a book. "AI-generated" means created by an AI tool, even if substantially edited afterward. AI-assisted content (your own writing refined, or brainstormed with AI) does not need to be disclosed. [P] https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390 (read 2026-10-09)
- KDP daily title limit: in September 2023 Amazon cut the number of new titles a publisher can create per day, citing protection against abuse. Press reports put the limit at 3 per day, and publishers can request an exception. [S] https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/digital/content-and-e-books/article/93207-kdp-will-limit-daily-number-of-new-titles.html (2023-09). The current KDP Content Guidelines page does not mention the limit. [P] Whether it is still in force as of 2026 is unverified.
- Etsy Creativity Standards: AI-generated items made from a seller's original prompts belong in the "designed by" category. Sellers must disclose AI use in listings, and selling AI prompt bundles is prohibited. [S] https://www.etsy.com/legal/creativity/ and https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1275449912004. The page blocked the fetcher, and the effective date was not seen.
- Suno: for songs made on Pro or Premier, "you are considered the owner of the song," and commercial-use rights continue after you cancel. On the free (Basic) plan, Suno "retain[s] ownership," and songs are for non-commercial use only. [P] https://help.suno.com/en/articles/2416769 (read 2026-10-09)
- Suno: upgrading does not retroactively grant commercial rights to songs made on the free plan. [S] https://help.suno.com/en/articles/2425729
- U.S. Copyright Office, Registration Guidance (88 FR 16190, March 16, 2023): material generated purely by AI is not registrable. Applicants must disclose more than de minimis AI content and claim only the human-authored parts. [S] https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/03/16/2023-05321/copyright-registration-guidance-works-containing-material-generated-by-artificial-intelligence (2023-03-16)
- Copyright Office, *Copyright and AI Part 2: Copyrightability* (Jan 29, 2025): copyright does not cover purely AI-generated material. With current generally available technology, prompts alone do not give enough human control. Using AI to assist, or adding human expression or arrangement, can be protected, decided case by case. [S] https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf (2025-01-29)

## 6. US taxes for YouTube creators

- Google must collect tax info from YPP creators. US creators: "Google will not withhold taxes on earnings if you've provided valid tax info" (W-9). US creators must resubmit the W-9 if their legal address changes. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362?hl=en] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- Non-US creators who submit tax info (W-8 form) face withholding "between 0-30% on earnings you get from U.S. viewers," depending on tax-treaty status. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362?hl=en]
- If no tax info is provided: non-US business accounts default to 30% of US earnings; US business accounts and individual accounts default to 24% of total worldwide earnings (backup withholding). [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362?hl=en]
- Google's deadline: submit tax info by Dec. 10 each year. Forms expire after the third full calendar year following the year signed. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362?hl=en]
- Google's YouTube help lists year-end forms as "1099-MISC, 1099-K, 1042-S." The page does not say which form or box applies to which US creator. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10390801?hl=en] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- SE tax rate: 12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare (= 15.3%). You must pay SE tax and file Schedule SE with net SE earnings of $400 or more. [https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- "Generally, the amount subject to self-employment tax is 92.35% of your net earnings from self-employment." You can deduct one-half of SE tax when figuring AGI. [https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc554] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- 2025 Social Security wage base for SE income: $176,100. [https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sse] (2025 instructions)
- Additional Medicare Tax: 0.9% above $200,000 (single/HOH), $250,000 (MFJ), $125,000 (MFS). [https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes]
- Form 1040-ES is used "to figure and pay your estimated tax." [https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1040-es]
- Estimated tax is generally required if you expect to owe at least $1,000 after withholding and refundable credits. Safe harbor: pay the smaller of 90% of current-year tax or 100% of prior-year tax (110% if prior-year AGI > $150,000, or $75,000 MFS). [https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- Quarterly due dates: April 15 (Jan 1–Mar 31), June 15 (Apr 1–May 31), Sept 15 (Jun 1–Aug 31), Jan 15 of the next year (Sep 1–Dec 31). These shift to the next business day if they fall on a weekend or holiday. [https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax]
- Business expenses must be "ordinary" (common and accepted in your industry) and "necessary" (helpful and appropriate). Personal, living, or family expenses are generally not deductible. [https://www.irs.gov/faqs/small-business-self-employed-other-business/income-expenses] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- Equipment depreciation: IRS points to Publication 946 (How to Depreciate Property) and Form 4562. Pub 334 is the small-business guide for Schedule C filers. Publication 535 is discontinued (last revision 2022). [https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/deducting-business-expenses] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- Home office requires regular and exclusive use plus principal place of business. Self-employed people use Form 8829 for the regular method; full rules are in Publication 587. [https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/home-office-deduction]
- Simplified home-office method: $5 per square foot, up to 300 sq ft (max $1,500). [https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/simplified-option-for-home-office-deduction]
- Hobby vs business: IRS lists factors, including businesslike operation and records, time and effort, dependence on the income, losses beyond control, changes to improve profitability, expertise, past success, and profit in some years. "No one factor is more important than another." Hobby income is reported on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8. [https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/heres-how-to-tell-the-difference-between-a-hobby-and-a-business-for-tax-purposes] (Tax Tip 2022-57, 2022-04-13, archival)
- Schedule C instructions: an activity is a business if its primary purpose is income or profit and it is carried on with continuity and regularity. A sporadic activity or a hobby does not qualify. [https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sc] (2025 instructions)
