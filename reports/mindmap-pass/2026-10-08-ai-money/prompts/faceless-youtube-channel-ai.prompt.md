## 1. YouTube Partner Program (YPP): eligibility, revenue share, RPM/CPM

- Expanded YPP (fan-funding / Shopping tier), route A: 500 subscribers + 3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days + 3,000 qualified (public long-form) watch hours in the last 12 months. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- Expanded YPP route B: 500 subscribers + 3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days + 3 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- Expanded-tier features: channel memberships, Super Chat & Super Stickers, Super Thanks, Jewels and gifts, Shopping. It is available only in eligible countries. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- Ad-revenue tier: 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 qualified public long-form watch hours in the last 12 months, OR 1,000 subscribers + 10 million qualified public Shorts views in the last 90 days. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- Watch hours from Shorts in the Shorts Feed do not count toward the 4,000-hour threshold. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- YouTube's own words: "The share is 55% for long-form videos and 45% for shorts." [https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-partner-program-explained/] (2025-02-03)
- Shorts: creators "will keep 45% of their allocated revenue, regardless if music was used or not." Shorts Feed ad revenue is pooled monthly. Part of the pool covers music licensing, and the rest (the Creator Pool) is allocated by each creator's share of engaged views in each country. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- RPM, YouTube's definition: "Revenue Per Mille (RPM) is a metric that represents how much money you've earned per 1,000 video views." [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314357] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- CPM, YouTube's definition: "Cost per 1,000 impressions (CPM) is a metric that represents how much money advertisers are spending to show ads on YouTube." [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314357] (accessed 2026-10-09)


## 2. Inauthentic content, reused content, AI disclosure

- On July 15, 2025, YouTube renamed the "repetitious content" monetization policy to "inauthentic content." The reused content policy was not changed. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392] (2025-07-15 update; accessed 2026-10-09)
- The inauthentic-content policy targets content that "looks like it's made with a template" or "may feel repetitive to viewers after watching several videos in a row from the same channel." [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392]
- Not monetizable, per the page: "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production." [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392]
- Not monetizable: "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value." Also not monetizable: videos that repeat the same scenario with the same outcome. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392]
- Allowed: "Content that expresses your unique creative voice, like using AI to visualize a unique character and narrative you invented." With automated tools or templates, "the final product must still demonstrate your creative vision." [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392]
- The page also bars AI personas presenting as experts on sensitive topics, such as health, legal, or financial advice. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392]
- Allowed: using the same intro and outro when "the bulk of your content is different." [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392]
- Reused content: "channels that repurpose content that's already on YouTube or another online source". The policy "applies even if you have permission from the original creator," and it is separate from copyright. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392]
- Reused content that is not monetizable: "Clips of moments from your favorite show edited together with little or no narrative"; "Content downloaded or copied from another online source without any substantive modifications." Allowed: reaction videos with commentary, and reused footage with original commentary, storyline, or substantive editing. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392]
- AI disclosure: YouTube requires creators "to disclose when they use AI to meaningfully alter or generate photorealistic content." Examples: a real person shown saying or doing something they didn't; altered footage of real events or places; realistic scenes that never happened; AI music that is the main focus. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- No disclosure needed for: unrealistic or fantasy content or animation; minor aesthetic edits such as filters, blur, captions, or upscaling; cloning your own voice for voiceovers; AI help with scripts, titles, thumbnails, or ideas. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491]
- Disclosure does not limit audience or monetization eligibility. Creators who repeatedly fail to disclose may face labels applied by YouTube or penalties, including removal or suspension from YPP. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491]

## 4. AI slop / faceless channel enforcement (news)

- On December 18, 2025, YouTube confirmed it had terminated Screen Culture and KH Studio, channels that made AI-generated fake movie trailers and had "well over a billion views." Spokesperson Jack Malon said that after the channels regained monetization they "reverted to clear violations of our spam and misleading metadata policies." [https://www.thewrap.com/?p=7915970] (2025-12-18)
- Neal Mohan's annual letter (January 2026) said YouTube is "building on our established systems that have been very successful in combatting spam and clickbait" to reduce low-quality, repetitive content, and that it requires disclosure of "realistic altered or synthetic content." [https://au.variety.com/2026/digital/global/youtube-channels-using-ai-tools-reduce-slop-neal-mohan-letter-32190/] (2026-01-22)
- Same letter, per Variety: more than 1 million channels used YouTube's AI creation tools daily, on average, in December 2025. [https://au.variety.com/2026/digital/global/youtube-channels-using-ai-tools-reduce-slop-neal-mohan-letter-32190/] (2026-01-22)

## 6. US taxes for YouTube creators

- Google must collect tax info from YPP creators. US creators: "Google will not withhold taxes on earnings if you've provided valid tax info" (W-9). US creators must resubmit the W-9 if their legal address changes. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362?hl=en] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- Non-US creators who submit tax info (W-8 form) face withholding "between 0-30% on earnings you get from U.S. viewers," depending on tax-treaty status. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362?hl=en]
- If no tax info is provided: non-US business accounts default to 30% of US earnings; US business accounts and individual accounts default to 24% of total worldwide earnings (backup withholding). [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362?hl=en]
- Google's deadline: submit tax info by Dec. 10 each year. Forms expire after the third full calendar year following the year signed. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362?hl=en]
- Google's YouTube help lists year-end forms as "1099-MISC, 1099-K, 1042-S." The page does not say which form or box applies to which US creator. [https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10390801?hl=en] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- SE tax rate: 12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare (= 15.3%). You must pay SE tax and file Schedule SE with net SE earnings of $400 or more. [https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- "Generally, the amount subject to self-employment tax is 92.35% of your net earnings from self-employment." You can deduct one-half of SE tax when figuring AGI. [https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc554] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- 2025 Social Security wage base for SE income: $176,100. [https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sse] (2025 instructions)
- Additional Medicare Tax: 0.9% above $200,000 (single/HOH), $250,000 (MFJ), $125,000 (MFS). [https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes]
- Form 1040-ES is used "to figure and pay your estimated tax." [https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1040-es]
- Estimated tax is generally required if you expect to owe at least $1,000 after withholding and refundable credits. Safe harbor: pay the smaller of 90% of current-year tax or 100% of prior-year tax (110% if prior-year AGI > $150,000, or $75,000 MFS). [https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- Quarterly due dates: April 15 (Jan 1–Mar 31), June 15 (Apr 1–May 31), Sept 15 (Jun 1–Aug 31), Jan 15 of the next year (Sep 1–Dec 31). These shift to the next business day if they fall on a weekend or holiday. [https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax]
- Business expenses must be "ordinary" (common and accepted in your industry) and "necessary" (helpful and appropriate). Personal, living, or family expenses are generally not deductible. [https://www.irs.gov/faqs/small-business-self-employed-other-business/income-expenses] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- Equipment depreciation: IRS points to Publication 946 (How to Depreciate Property) and Form 4562. Pub 334 is the small-business guide for Schedule C filers. Publication 535 is discontinued (last revision 2022). [https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/deducting-business-expenses] (accessed 2026-10-09)
- Home office requires regular and exclusive use plus principal place of business. Self-employed people use Form 8829 for the regular method; full rules are in Publication 587. [https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/home-office-deduction]
- Simplified home-office method: $5 per square foot, up to 300 sq ft (max $1,500). [https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/simplified-option-for-home-office-deduction]
- Hobby vs business: IRS lists factors, including businesslike operation and records, time and effort, dependence on the income, losses beyond control, changes to improve profitability, expertise, past success, and profit in some years. "No one factor is more important than another." Hobby income is reported on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8. [https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/heres-how-to-tell-the-difference-between-a-hobby-and-a-business-for-tax-purposes] (Tax Tip 2022-57, 2022-04-13, archival)
- Schedule C instructions: an activity is a business if its primary purpose is income or profit and it is carried on with continuity and regularity. A sporadic activity or a hobby does not qualify. [https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sc] (2025 instructions)
