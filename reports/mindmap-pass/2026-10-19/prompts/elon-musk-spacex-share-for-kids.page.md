## Can You Claim Musk's SpaceX Share Yet?
No parent or child can claim the promised SpaceX share today. Under the Trump Account rules published September 30, 2026 (T.D. 10056), a person can give stock to children only through a charity. The charity's gift is split across a whole group, and a donor cannot pick an individual child. Musk posted the statement on his personal social media account, but neither SpaceX nor any government agency has released an application form, registry, or enrollment portal.

Instead of searching for claim forms that do not exist, parents should take three practical steps right now:

1. Claim your child's Trump Account in the "Trump Accounts: Official App" or at [TrumpAccounts.gov](https://trumpaccounts.gov). Treasury completed automatic enrollment of eligible children under 18 with a valid Social Security number on October 1, 2026 ([SB-0642](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0642)). Our guide on [how to claim a Trump Account](/guides/how-to-claim-a-trump-account/) walks through the claim.
2. Keep your contact information current in the Trump Accounts: Official App.
3. Disregard any message, email, or third-party website asking for payment or personal data to secure free SpaceX stock.

Our advice would change if Musk, SpaceX, or Treasury publishes official terms or opens a sign-up. This wait-and-claim plan is not for you if you want your child to own SpaceX stock now; our guide on [how to buy SpaceX stock](/guides/how-to-buy-spacex-stock/) covers buying it in a custodial account.

## How a SpaceX Share Could Reach a Trump Account
If Musk routes the gift through Trump Accounts, it must follow T.D. 10056, the temporary regulations published in the [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/30/2026-20026/trump-accounts) on September 30, 2026. Those rules set who can give stock to Trump Accounts and how.

Under T.D. 10056, general contributions can only originate from government entities or section 501(c)(3) charities, including donor-advised funds. An individual person or private corporation cannot transfer corporate stock directly into a child's account. To reach every child, Musk or SpaceX would first give the shares to an eligible charity. That charity would then make a general contribution split equally across a whole qualified class, such as all beneficiaries under 18. Private donors cannot pick individual recipients, and families cannot decline a general contribution.

The regulations also dictate how individual stock holdings operate inside these accounts. Donated stock must be issued by a domestic company, trade on a registered national exchange, and carry zero transfer restrictions. The trustee may hold donated shares for up to five years before selling them into the account's index fund. Parents cannot buy SPCX or any single stock inside a Trump Account, as explained in our guide on [what Trump Accounts are invested in](/guides/what-are-trump-accounts-invested-in/).

Because the trustee may sell donated shares into the index fund, your child may never hold SPCX in a Trump Account long term, even if a SpaceX share arrives there. If Musk gives the shares outside Trump Accounts, a child would usually hold them in a custodial (UTMA/UGMA) account opened by an adult. Our guide on [how to open a custodial brokerage account for kids](/guides/how-to-open-a-custodial-brokerage-account-for-kids/) covers the setup.

## What You Get by Claiming Your Child's Trump Account
Claiming your child's Trump Account matters even without a Musk claim process, because some money reaches only claimed accounts. Treasury press release [SB-0642](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0642) says automatic enrollment of eligible children under 18 with a valid Social Security number was completed October 1, 2026. Unclaimed accounts currently sit in a Treasury master trust.

Unclaimed accounts can receive broad general contributions, but family and employer contributions remain blocked until a parent or guardian claims the account. To learn which minors qualify for automatic enrollment, see our guide on [Trump Account eligibility](/guides/trump-account-eligibility/). U.S.-citizen children born January 1, 2025 through December 31, 2028 can get a one-time $1,000 federal seed, but only if the account is claimed.

Claimed accounts also allow parents, relatives, and family friends to contribute up to $5,000 annually per child, with participating employers contributing up to $2,500 within that total limit. Money is locked until January 1 of the calendar year the child turns 18. You can model how regular deposits accumulate using our free [Trump Account calculator](/trump-account/).

Other private gifts already go through Trump Accounts. Michael and Susan Dell pledged $6.25 billion for $250 deposits to 25 million children born 2016 through 2024 in ZIP codes with median family income about $118,000 or less. No application is needed. Our [Dell Trump Account $250](/guides/dell-trump-account-250/) guide explains who qualifies. ## Watch for Free SpaceX Share Scams
Because no official claim process exists, every message instructing you to claim a share right now is fraudulent.

Official Trump Account email comes only from no-reply@TrumpAccounts.Treasury.gov. You should never pay an administrative processing fee, send cryptocurrency, or provide your child's Social Security number to an unverified third-party website.

If you have questions about the validity of a notice, you can call the official Treasury support line at 1-866-USA-4547 or access the official web portal at [TrumpAccounts.gov](https://trumpaccounts.gov). For additional methods to identify fraudulent portals and protect your family's records, review our detailed guide on [Trump Account scams](/guides/trump-account-scams/).
