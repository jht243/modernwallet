## Two Ways the Shares Could Reach Kids, Neither Confirmed

Musk has named no route, and the two realistic ones work very differently.

**Route 1: Through Trump Accounts.** Under temporary regulations under Treasury Decision (T.D.) 10056 published in the [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/30/2026-20026/trump-accounts), an individual can only donate corporate stock by routing it through an eligible 501(c)(3) charity as a general contribution split equally across an entire qualified class. Donated shares must be issued by a domestic corporation, trade on a registered national exchange, and carry no transfer restrictions. The donor cannot select individual children, and recipient families cannot select the stock or refuse the gift.

Under these same Treasury rules, the trustee may hold donated shares for up to five years before selling them into the account's index fund, so your child may never keep the share there. Because unclaimed auto-enrolled accounts can still receive general contributions under current rules, you would not need to claim an account first for a transfer to land. Families cannot buy single stocks inside these accounts either, as explained in our guide on [what Trump Accounts are invested in](/guides/what-are-trump-accounts-invested-in/).

**Route 2: Outside Trump Accounts.** Outside government savings programs, a child usually holds stock in a custodial Uniform Transfers to Minors Act (UTMA) or Uniform Gifts to Minors Act (UGMA) account opened by an adult. Musk has described no transfer process, did not specify tax handling, and did not state whether non-citizens qualify. Musk has described no process for delivering shares this way, so whether parents would need one is unknown, though you can review [how to open a custodial brokerage account for kids](/guides/how-to-open-a-custodial-brokerage-account-for-kids/).

Until Musk picks one, neither route asks anything of parents.
