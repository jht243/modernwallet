Facts:
- Charitable rate (14 cents) is set by statute; business rate is based on an annual study of the fixed and variable costs of operating an automobile; medical and moving rate is based on only the variable costs. (IR-2025-128)
- The July 1, 2026 change (Announcement 2026-11) applies to expenses on or after July 1, 2026 and "results from recent increases in the price of fuel." Expenses before July 1, 2026 use the Notice 2026-10 rates.
- Mid-year changes in this 2010–2026 window: July 1, 2011; July 1, 2022; July 1, 2026. (Only state "in this period"; do not claim these are the only mid-year changes ever.)
- Since 2018, moving expenses are deductible only for active-duty Armed Forces members moving under military orders for a permanent change of station; from 2026 the IRS also names certain members of the intelligence community. (IR-2018-127; IR-2025-128)
- Most employees cannot deduct unreimbursed employee mileage (miscellaneous itemized deduction eliminated for tax years after 2017); self-employed people still deduct business mileage. IR-2025-128: "taxpayers cannot claim a miscellaneous itemized deduction for unreimbursed employee travel expenses, except for certain educator expenses." Do NOT say "permanent".
- The rates apply to electric, hybrid, gasoline and diesel vehicles (IR-2025-128).
- The rate that applies is the one in effect when the mile was driven (use the rate for the period the expense was incurred).
