The IRS usually sets one set of rates for the full calendar year. In most tax seasons, that single rate applies from January 1 through December 31. Drivers and payroll teams use that number without mid-year adjustments.

In three tax years between 2010 and 2026, the IRS split the calendar year into two separate periods. Those mid-year splits took effect on July 1, 2011, July 1, 2022, and July 1, 2026. For the 2011 tax year, [IR-2011-69](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-increases-mileage-rate-to-55-5-cents-per-mile) raised the business rate from 51 cents to 55.5 cents starting July 1. For 2022, [IR-2022-124](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-increases-mileage-rate-for-remainder-of-2022) raised the business rate from 58.5 cents to 62.5 cents on July 1.

For 2026, the IRS announced a mid-year increase in [Announcement 2026-11 in Internal Revenue Bulletin 2026-29](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-29_irb). The agency stated that this July 1 increase results from recent increases in the price of fuel. Expenses incurred before July 1, 2026, remain subject to the rates established in Notice 2026-10. Expenses on or after July 1 take the new higher rate.
