CURRENT TEXT TO REWRITE:
Treasury bills offer flexible automated rolling for cash management, whereas notes and bonds deliver long-term certainty by locking your interest rate for years. Savers using short bills face reinvestment risk, which occurs when a bill matures and newly issued replacement bills offer lower yields than the original purchase.

TreasuryDirect provides built-in tools to automate continuous bill purchases. You can schedule automatic reinvestment at the time of purchase or up to four business days before maturity. A bill automatically reinvests into the same term length, such as a 52-week bill rolling once or a 4-week bill reinvesting up to 25 consecutive times.

Notes and bonds eliminate the need to constantly monitor auction schedules. Once you purchase a 7-year note or a 20-year bond, your interest rate remains locked for the entire multi-year holding period. You avoid the operational overhead of rolling positions, though you surrender the chance to capture higher short-term rates if market yields rise.

## A) Buying
- Bill terms: 4, 6, 8, 13, 17, 26, and 52 weeks. Minimum $100, increments of $100. Largest non-competitive bid: $10 million per auction. [TD bills page]
- Cash Management Bills are only available through a bank, broker, or dealer, not TreasuryDirect. [TD bills page]
- TreasuryDirect account: U.S. citizen, resident, or individual at least 18 with a valid SSN; a U.S. address of record; an account at a U.S. financial institution that accepts ACH; routing and account number; email address. [TD user guide]
- TreasuryDirect allows non-competitive bids only; a bank, broker, or dealer allows competitive and non-competitive bids. A non-competitive bidder is guaranteed the security in the amount wanted and accepts the auction rate. [TD buying page]
- TreasuryDirect steps: log in; BuyDirect tab; choose security and amount; after 5 PM Eastern on auction day check Pending Purchases and Reinvestments; have funds in the bank account by the issue date. [TD buying page]

## B) Taxes
- Interest on Treasury bills, notes and bonds is subject to federal income tax but exempt from all state and local income taxes; reported on Form 1099-INT box 3. [IRS Pub 550]
- Bills are issued at a discount; the difference between the price paid and face value at maturity is interest income, generally reported when the bill is paid at maturity. [Pub 550]
- Reinvested bills: report the full interest on each bill by the time it reaches maturity. [Pub 550]
- Short-term obligations (a year or less): you generally can choose to include discount in income currently (accrue); if not, a gain on sale is ordinary income up to the ratable share of the discount. [Pub 550]
- Form 1099-INT box 3 = "Interest on U.S. Savings Bonds and Treasury Obligations". [1099-INT instructions]
- TreasuryDirect issues Form 1099-INT (ManageDirect > Manage My Taxes). A bill maturing Dec 31 on a weekend/holiday is paid the next business day but shown on the 1099-INT on the maturity date. [TD tax forms page]
- Practical consequence allowed: a bill bought in one year that matures the next year is taxed in the maturity year (derived from "reported when paid at maturity").
## C) Bills vs notes vs bonds
- Bills: 4 to 52 weeks; sold at a discount or at par; paid face value at maturity; min $100. [TD bills]
- Notes: 2, 3, 5, 7, or 10 years; interest every six months; min $100. [TD notes]
- Bonds: 20 or 30 years; interest every six months; min $100. [TD bonds]
- TIPS: 5, 10, or 30 years; fixed rate paid every six months on principal that adjusts with inflation/deflation; at maturity the greater of adjusted or original principal. [TD TIPS]
- FRNs: mature in two years; interest paid four times a year, tied to the 13-week bill rate plus a spread. [TD FRN]
- All backed by the full faith and credit of the U.S. government. [TD marketable securities]
- Held to maturity you get face value plus interest; sold before maturity it may be worth more or less than face value; when rates rise you might have to sell at a discount. [investor.gov]
