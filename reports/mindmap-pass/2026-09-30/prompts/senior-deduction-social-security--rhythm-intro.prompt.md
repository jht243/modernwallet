Social Security benefits remain taxable in 2026 under the same calculation rules as before. The new tax law created a social security tax deduction for seniors, officially known as the enhanced deduction for seniors, but it did not exempt retirement benefits from gross income. Instead, eligible individuals age 65 and older receive an extra deduction of up to $6,000 ($12,000 for married couples filing jointly if both qualify) for tax years 2025 through 2028. This deduction phases out once your modified adjusted gross income climbs above $75,000, or $150,000 on a joint return.

At ModernWallet, we review tax changes by tracking the actual dollar calculations on federal schedules. The new deduction helps many retirees lower their bottom-line taxable income, sometimes eliminating their tax balance completely. Even so, the calculation determining whether your monthly benefit is taxable remains on the books. Understanding how these separate rules interact prevents surprise tax bills when you file your return.
