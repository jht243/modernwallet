## Maturity Horizons and Secondary Market Price Risk

Holding any Treasury security to maturity guarantees return of your full face value plus all stated interest, but selling early exposes you to market price risk. As detailed by [Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/bonds-or-fixed-income-products/bonds), secondary market bond prices drop when interest rates rise. That dynamic means you might have to sell at a discount if you cannot wait for the maturity date.

Maturity length dictates the severity of price swings. A 4-week or 13-week Treasury bill experiences minimal market price fluctuation because its scheduled maturity date sits right around the corner. A bill sold early through a broker usually fetches close to what you paid. Little time remains before it pays face value.

A 10-year note or 30-year bond has years of fixed coupons left, so its market price falls further than a bill's when interest rates rise.
