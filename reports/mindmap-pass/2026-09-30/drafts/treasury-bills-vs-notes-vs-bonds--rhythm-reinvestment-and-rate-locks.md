## Reinvestment and Rate Locks

Treasury bills offer flexible automated rolling for cash management, whereas notes and bonds deliver long-term certainty by locking your interest rate for years. Savers using short bills face reinvestment risk. That occurs when a bill matures and newly issued replacement bills offer lower yields than the original purchase.

TreasuryDirect provides tools for automated bill purchases. You can schedule automatic reinvestment at purchase or up to four business days before maturity. A bill automatically reinvests into the same term length, such as a 52-week bill rolling once or a 4-week bill reinvesting up to 25 consecutive times.

Notes and bonds eliminate the need to constantly monitor auction schedules. Once you purchase a 7-year note or a 20-year bond, your interest rate remains locked for the entire multi-year holding period. You avoid rolling overhead, though you surrender higher short-term rates if market yields rise.
