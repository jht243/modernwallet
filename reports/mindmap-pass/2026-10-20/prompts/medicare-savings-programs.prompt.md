## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit/name this page may state
Source for all of it: medicare.gov "Medicare Savings Programs" and "Medicare costs" pages, read 2026-10-20.

- A Medicare Savings Program helps pay your Medicare Part A (Hospital Insurance) and Part B (Medical Insurance) premiums. If you qualify, it might also pay your Part A and Part B deductibles, coinsurance and copayments.
- You apply through your state. When you apply, your state determines which program(s) you qualify for. Even if you do not think you qualify, you should still apply.
- There are 4 programs: QMB, SLMB, QI, QDWI.
- In most cases income and resources must be below a limit. The limits go up each year. You may still qualify in your state even if your income or resources are higher than the federal limits listed, because some states do not count certain types or amounts of income or resources. Contact your state.
- Income limits are slightly higher in Alaska and Hawaii.
- QMB (Qualified Medicare Beneficiary): helps pay Part A premiums (if you do not have premium-free Part A); Part B premiums, deductibles, coinsurance and copayments for services and items Medicare covers. 2026 monthly income limit: individual $1,350, married couple $1,824. Resource limit: individual $9,950, married couple $14,910. If you qualify, Medicare providers are not allowed to bill you for services and items Medicare covers, including deductibles, coinsurance and copayments. You may get a bill for a small Medicaid copayment, if one applies. Show both your Medicare card and Medicaid card (or QMB card) each time you get care. You also get Extra Help paying for prescription drugs: no more than $12.65 in 2026 for each drug covered by your Medicare drug plan.
- SLMB (Specified Low-Income Medicare Beneficiary): helps pay Part B premiums only; you must have both Part A and Part B. 2026 monthly income limit: individual $1,616, married couple $2,184. Resource limit: individual $9,950, married couple $14,910. You also get Extra Help (no more than $12.65 in 2026 per covered drug).
- QI (Qualifying Individual): helps pay Part B premiums; you must have both Part A and Part B. 2026 monthly income limit: individual $1,816, married couple $2,455. Resource limit: individual $9,950, married couple $14,910. You must apply every year to stay in the program. States approve applications first-come, first-served, with priority to people who got QI benefits the previous year. QI is only available to people who do not qualify for any other Medicaid coverage or benefits, but you may qualify for help from another Medicare Savings Program. You also get Extra Help (no more than $12.65 in 2026 per covered drug).
- QDWI (Qualified Disabled & Working Individual): for people who have a disability, are working, and lost their Social Security disability benefits and premium-free Part A because they returned to work. Helps pay Part A premiums only. 2026 monthly income limit: individual $5,405, married couple $7,299. Resource limit: individual $4,000, married couple $6,000.
- 2026 Medicare costs: Part B premium $202.90 a month (or higher depending on income), which is $2,434.80 over 12 months (computed: 202.90 x 12). Part B yearly deductible $283. Part B coinsurance usually 20% of the cost for each covered service after the deductible. Part A premium is $0 for most people; if you do not qualify for premium-free Part A you might buy it for $311 or $565 a month depending on how long you or your spouse worked and paid Medicare taxes. Part A deductible $1,736 per inpatient hospital benefit period.
- Page-specific framing allowed: Medicaid is the state-run program; the Medicare Savings Programs are run by states, which is why the application goes to your state. Link the Medicare-vs-Medicaid comparison for the difference between the two programs.
