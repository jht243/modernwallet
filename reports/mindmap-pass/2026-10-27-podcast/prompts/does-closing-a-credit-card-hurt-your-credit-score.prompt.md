REPORTED.
- It depends on your situation. In some cases closing a credit card may be a good financial step, but do not assume it will improve your credit score.
- Some people keep a card account open, especially an old account with a positive payment history, because this may help maintain a higher credit score.
- Closing the account might be a good decision if: the card has annual fees or poor terms that outweigh the benefits; it helps you avoid accumulating debt you cannot pay off; or you are not planning to apply for credit in the near future.
- Otherwise, closing an existing card could decrease your credit score. The change to your score may be temporary or minor, but the full impact varies based on other factors about your credit profile.
- Part of your score is based on the amount of credit you are using divided by the total amount you have available, known as the credit utilization ratio. Closing an existing card can increase your utilization ratio and lower your score.
- Tip: if you keep an unused account open, watch your statements to protect against identity theft and to check for unexpected fees or charges.

CALCULATED. Method: utilization = total balances divided by total credit limits. Scenario: four cards with limits totalling $20,000 and $4,000 owed in total (20.0% utilization). No scoring model is applied and no score change in points is claimed. Assumes the closed card has a $0 balance.
- Close an unused $2,500-limit card: limits fall to $17,500; utilization 22.9%.
- Close an unused $5,000-limit card: limits fall to $15,000; utilization 26.7%.
- Close an unused $10,000-limit card: limits fall to $10,000; utilization 40.0%.
- If the balance owed is first paid down from $4,000 to $1,000 and then the $10,000 card is closed: utilization is 10.0% (1,000 / 10,000).
