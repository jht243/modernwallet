How it works The biweekly mortgage calculator sets the monthly payment with payment = balance × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where r is the annual rate divided by 12 and n is the months left. A biweekly plan pays half that amount every two weeks. That is 26 half-payments a year, or the value of 13 monthly payments. Each two-week period is charged the annual rate divided by 26 in interest, and each half-payment is applied the day it is paid. On the example loan, the biweekly schedule takes 628 half-payments, about 289.8 months. Total interest falls from $382,633.47 to $294,511.68. The calculator covers fixed-rate loans and shows principal and interest only, so taxes, insurance and escrow are not included. The calculator assumes your servicer applies each half-payment the day it arrives, and a servicer may hold it instead. Regulation Z, at 12 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) 1026.36(c)(1), says a servicer must credit a full periodic payment as of the date it is received. A half-payment is a partial payment, so the servicer may hold it in a suspense or unapplied-funds account and show it on the periodic statement. Once enough builds up to cover a full payment, the servicer must treat the funds as a payment received. If your servicer holds half-payments, the real saving is somewhat smaller than the calculator shows, so ask your servicer whether it applies each half-payment on arrival. You can get almost the same result with no plan by adding 1/12 of the monthly payment to each monthly payment. That path also adds 13 payments' worth a year, and it does not depend on when the servicer applies a half-payment. On the example loan, the extra is $158.02 a month. The loan ends in 290 months, 70 months sooner, with $87,256.29 saved in interest. That is $865.49 less than the biweekly saving. Plan fees come out of the interest you save, and the calculator shows the plan fees over the loan and the interest saved after fees. As an illustration, take a $400 setup fee plus $2.50 per debit. Over 628 debits, those fees cost $1,970. That leaves $86,151.78 saved after fees, which is less than the $87,256.29 the free 1/12-extra path saves. A lower rate also shrinks the saving: take the same $300,000 at 3% for 360 months. The payment is $1,264.81, and biweekly saves $20,637.30 in interest. At 0% interest, biweekly saves $0. Skip a paid biweekly plan if your rate is low, if your servicer holds half-payments, or if the fee is larger than the gap between the plan and the 1/12-extra path. If your servicer applies each half-payment the day it arrives and charges no fee, its biweekly option saves $865.49 more than the 1/12-extra path on the example loan. The 1/12-extra path needs no plan and does not depend on when the servicer applies a payment. Can my servicer hold my half payments? Yes.
