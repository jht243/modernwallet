## CLOSED FACT LIST — anything not on this list, you do not know.
- Wage garnishment may take up to 15 percent of disposable pay per pay period, unless the borrower agrees in writing to more (20 U.S.C. 1095a). Disposable pay is pay left after legally required withholdings.
- Example: $2,000 of disposable pay every two weeks is $300 per paycheck, about $650 a month, $7,800 a year.
- The Treasury can apply part or all of a federal tax refund to certain unpaid debts, and sends the remainder to the filer (IRS).
- A Social Security offset takes the lesser of 15 percent of the monthly benefit or the amount above $750 (31 CFR 285.4). An $850 benefit has a $100 cap; an $800 benefit has a $50 cap.
- Reporting (the Washington Sun, Oct 19, 2026; Forbes reported the same plan the same day): the administration is preparing to penalize roughly 10 million borrowers in default, likely starting next year, using wage garnishment and offsets of tax refunds and Social Security.
