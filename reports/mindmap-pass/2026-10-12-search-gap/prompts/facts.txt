CLOSED FACT LIST (anything not here, you do not know; never invent a number, date, or URL; say to verify with the plan custodian or a tax professional):
1. A "successor beneficiary" is the person who inherits an inherited IRA after the first beneficiary dies. (IRS Publication 590-B)
2. Under the SECURE Act 10-year rule, the account must be emptied by December 31 of the 10th year after the original owner's death.
3. If the first beneficiary was on the 10-year rule, the successor beneficiary does not get a fresh 10 years. The successor generally must finish within the original owner's 10-year window. (IRS Publication 590-B)
4. If annual withdrawals were required for the first beneficiary (original owner died on or after their required beginning date, generally age 73), the successor generally must keep taking annual withdrawals for the remaining years as well.
5. If the first beneficiary was an eligible designated beneficiary taking withdrawals over life expectancy (for example a disabled or chronically ill person, or someone not more than 10 years younger than the owner), the successor generally must empty the account by December 31 of the 10th year after the first beneficiary's death, and keep taking annual withdrawals during that period based on the first beneficiary's remaining life expectancy schedule.
6. If the first beneficiary was a surviving spouse who rolled the IRA into their own IRA, the account is then the spouse's own IRA, and whoever inherits it from the spouse is a new first-generation beneficiary with their own 10-year window counted from the spouse's death.
7. Missing a required withdrawal can trigger a 25% excise tax on the amount not withdrawn, reduced to 10% if corrected in the IRS's correction window (generally two years). (existing page FAQ)
8. Withdrawals from a traditional inherited IRA are ordinary income to the person who takes them; qualified Roth inherited IRA withdrawals are generally tax free.
9. What controls the answer: who the first beneficiary was, the year the original owner died, whether the owner died before or after their required beginning date, and the beneficiary designation form naming a successor. Ask the IRA custodian to confirm the schedule in writing. Rules vary by case; a tax professional can apply them.
10. Name a successor/contingent beneficiary on the inherited IRA so the account does not default to the estate or to the custodian's default rules.
