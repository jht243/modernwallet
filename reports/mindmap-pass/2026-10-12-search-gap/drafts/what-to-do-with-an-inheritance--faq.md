You are usually a successor beneficiary with no fresh 10-year window, and if the first heir was on the 10-year rule, you generally must finish within the original owner's 10 years. If the first heir was an eligible designated beneficiary taking withdrawals over life expectancy, your deadline is December 31 of the 10th year after that heir's death. A surviving spouse who rolled the IRA into their own IRA makes you a new first-generation beneficiary with 10 years from the spouse's death, so ask the IRA custodian to confirm your schedule in writing and read our guide on [how an inherited IRA is taxed](/guides/inherited-ira-taxes-explained/).
