, powerful, profound, remarkable, notable, significant, key (as filler), game-changer, cutting-edge, revolutionary, breakthrough, robust, holistic, "wealth of", myriad, plethora, delve, realm, landscape, testament, "plays a key/vital/pivotal role", "cannot be overstated", unlock, harness, supercharge, boost (as hype). One filler occurrence fails.
- **Punctuation tell** — rhetorical-question section opener, colon-drama ("The result:", "The catch:"), exclamation point, scare-quote overuse, or semicolon-stacked sentence.
- **Faux-insight setup** — "what nobody tells you", "the part everyone misses", "what most people get wrong", "this is the part most people skip".
- **Weasel attribution** — "experts agree", "studies show", "research suggests", "many argue", "widely regarded as", with no named source.
- **Parallel-list padding inside one sentence** — a triple repeating one frame ("different moments, different billing terms, and sometimes different regions") rather than three distinct facts. Any one occurrence FAILS.
- **Trailing judgment appositive** — a comma-attached closing phrase that rates the sentence instead of extending it (", presented as settled fact", ", framed as certainty", ", sold as a feature"). Any one occurrence FAILS.
- **Presupposes the reader's situation** — text that assumes what the reader owns, uses, has already done, has decided, or knows: "leaving" the product as if they already use it, "your current system", "before you switch", "as you know", "you have probably already", "now that you have outgrown". "You" addressing the reader is not this fail. Defaulting to "a firm that…" / "firms that…" so the page never says "you" is encyclopedia voice and FAILS under GATE Register, not a pass on this ban. Check the page's likely audience: a comparison, alternatives, or decision page serves current users, rival-product users, and first-time buyers alike, so an ownership assumption excludes most of them. Any one occurrence FAILS; report as `reader-assumption: "[text]"`. Weight it heavier in H1, subtitle, meta fields, and section headings. The fix scopes the claim ("if you already use this product…", with the H1 name); it never just deletes the sentence, and it does not ban "you".
- **Pseudo-cleft emphasis padding** — "X is what [verb]s Y", "the thing that determines…", "what matters is whether…", "it is X that [verb]s". A cleft frame wrapping a plain SVO sentence. Any one occurrence FAILS; report as `pseudo-cleft: "[text]"`. Fix by unwrapping to the direct sentence.
- **Sentence fails the first-read test** — read once at speed; if who-does-what is not recoverable on that single pass, it FAILS. Triggers: a conditional nested in another conditional; a garden-path or phrasal-verb collision where the first reading is wrong ("a switch off the old plan" → *power off*); three or more light/abstract words chained with no concrete noun ("whether a switch off the old plan pays off"). This is distinct from the length-based overloaded-sentence ban — a SHORT sentence can fail this if it does not parse cleanly. Report as `unparseable: "[text]"` with the misreading it invites. The fix rewrites for a correct first reading; do not merely shorten.
- **Folksy locative metaphor standing in for a fact** — "is where the hours go", "where the money lives", "where deals die", "where the real work happens", "where the magic is", "that's where it falls apart", and the same shape with any noun. A place-metaphor doing the job of a specific quantity, cause, or mechanism. Any one occurrence FAILS; report as `locative-metaphor: "[text]"`. The fix states the fact (the number, the cause, or the step), never a tidier metaphor.
- **Aphoristic fragment as a verdict** — a short declarative standing in for the argument ("Both cannot be current.", "That is the trade."). Any one occurrence FAILS.
- **Superficial `-ing` clause** — a trailing "highlighting/underscoring/reflecting/showcasing" that pretends to explain meaning.
- **Synonym cycling** — the same referent rotated across "agent/assistant/tool/platform" to avoid repetition.
- **Dramatic fragmentation** — "That's it. That's the whole thing.", "X. And Y. And Z."
- **Rhetorical setup** — "What if I told you…", "Think about it:", "Plot twist:", self-answered "Question? Answer."
- **Fake-profound kicker** — a final metaphor/aphorism/mic-drop line instead of ending on a concrete sentence.
- **Summary-recap ending** — "In conclusion", "Ultimately", "Overall", or a closing paragraph restating the piece.
- **Headline tell (scan the headline fields SEPARATELY from body prose — they are the most commonly unchecked text on the page).** Extract the H1, SEO title, meta description, subtitle/deck, every section heading, and every FAQ question, then check each for: presupposed change ("still", "no longer", "now", "these days", "in the age of"); a coy abstraction ("the vendor", "this platform") where the page already names the subject; the two-clause "X, and Why/What/How Y" formula spliced by a comma or colon; a three-item headline tricolon; "actually"/"really"/"truly" standing in for a fact; colon-drama ("The Catch:", "The Result:"); or a curiosity-gap headline that withholds the finding. Any one occurrence FAILS. Report as `headline-tell: [field] "[text]"`. The fix rewrites that field only.
- **Formatting slop** — emoji in a heading, mid-sentence bold for emphasis, a bullet list where prose reads better, three or more parallel steps/checks written as First/Second/Third paragraphs, or a header over a two-sentence section.
- **Inflated word from the extended list** — foster, utilize, facilitate, empower, streamline, tapestry, beacon, multifaceted, meticulous, intricate, embark, elevate, transformative (as filler).

### The meaning bar (POSITIVE tests — check these BEFORE the ban list)

Run this pass first, and read the page as a reader rather than a compliance checker. A page that clears every ban can still fail here. Score each hit as a hard fail.

- **Dead-end problem** — a problem, objection, risk, limitation, or complaint that the passage names without giving the reader a resolution, a workaround, a tradeoff to accept, or a link to where it is handled. Any one occurrence FAILS. Report `dead-end: "[text]"` and state what the reader is left holding. The fix resolves it, routes it to a page that does, or deletes the sentence.
- **Say-it-out-loud** — read each sentence aloud. Any sentence a person would not speak to a colleague FAILS. Report `unsayable: "[text]"`.
- **Placeholder nouns** — a sentence whose nouns are all abstractions (`problem`, `platform`, `solution`, `option`, `approach`, `thing`, `piece`, `area`, `aspect`, `move`, `one`) with nothing concrete a reader can picture FAILS. Report `no-concrete-noun: "[text]"`.
- **Restatement** — a sentence that repeats its predecessor's idea in different words FAILS. The predecessor can be the previous body sentence or the subtitle/deck. Report `restatement: "[both sentences]"`. The fix deletes the repeat (the intro sentence, not the subtitle).
- **Backward pronoun** — a pronoun or "one"/"it"/"that" whose antecedent is in a previous sentence, forcing a re-read, FAILS. Report `backward-pronoun: "[text]"`.
- **Nominalized instruction** — an `-ing` or `-tion` subject hiding an action ("Naming the problem first separates…") FAILS. Report `nominalization: "[text]"`.
- **Wrong collocation** — words that do not naturally go together ("platforms answer problems") FAIL. Report `collocation: "[text]"`.
- **Abstract metaphor for a plain verb** — "separates X from Y", "points at", "drives the cost" where a concrete verb exists. FAILS. Report `abstract-verb: "[text]"`.

For each, quote the sentence and give the plain-English rewrite. If you cannot write a plain rewrite because you cannot tell what the sentence means, say so; that is the strongest possible fail.

### Phrasing tells (two or more = FAIL)

- Throat-clearing section openers: "When it comes to…", "In today's fast-paced world…", "In the ever-evolving landscape of…", "In an age where…".
- Empty tricolons that add no information ("efficient, effective, and scalable", "powerful, flexible, and easy to use").
- Hedged non-conclusions that refuse to commit ("ultimately, the right choice depends on your specific needs and goals", "there's no one-size-fits-all answer").
- LLM-signature filler: "delve into", "leverage" (as a verb), "navigate the complexities of", "unlock the power of", "in the realm of", "a testament to", "it's worth noting that", "that being said".
- Symmetrical listicle padding where every bullet is the same length and shape with no concrete specifics.

A page can carry one isolated phrasing tell and still pass; two or more is the fail line. Any single-instance ban above fails on its own.
