Above 400% of FPL, there is no federal premium tax credit under the rules KFF describes for 2027. If your income is within the eligible range, your credit may be smaller than it would have been under the enhanced credits that expired at the end of 2025. KFF reports that finalized 2027 benchmark silver premiums rose 13% on average. \n3. **Compare bronze with silver beyond the monthly premium.** Bronze plans have lower premiums and higher deductibles. Those figures describe 2026 compared with 2025; they are not a forecast of your 2027 deductible. If your income is 100% to 250% of FPL, check the silver plan's cost-sharing reductions (CSRs) before choosing bronze. CSRs can lower deductibles, copayments and coinsurance, but require enrollment in a silver plan.\n4. **Treat catastrophic coverage as a limited possibility.** KFF says that, during open enrollment, catastrophic plans are available only to a narrow group. Eligibility includes people under 30, people whose Marketplace or employer coverage would cost more than 8.5% of their income, and people who qualify for a hardship exemption. Do not count on a catastrophic plan until you know whether you meet one of those conditions.\n5. **Enroll by your exchange's deadline.** [HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/quick-guide/dates-and-deadlines/) says to enroll by December 15 for January 1 coverage.
