## The tool (describe only what it does — real UI labels)
- Runs in the browser on this page; no signup; marks and notes are saved only in the reader's browser.
- "About the house" panel: Type of home (House, Townhouse (with HOA), Condo (with HOA)); When was it built? (Not sure, Before 1950, 1950 to 1977, 1978 to 1999, 2000 or later, Brand-new / under construction); State; four Local conditions buttons: "Winters reach freezing", "Hurricane-prone coast", "Wildfire area", "Earthquake area". Choosing a state pre-sets the four as a starting point; the reader can change them for the town.
- Two numbered lists, in order of how costly each is to miss: "Ask the Agent or Seller" and "Check With Your Own Eyes". Some items have a one-line note, a source link, or a link to a calculator.
- For each item the reader taps Good (✓), Unsure (?) or Problem (✗); tapping a mark again clears it. Questions also have a "Their answer" box to write down what the agent said. A legend above the lists explains the three marks.
- The screen holds ONE house at a time, with a "House" name field ("Name or address") at the top. A summary card counts the reader's Good, Unsure and Problem marks, shows how many items are not marked yet, and lists the first three Problem items by short label. The tool adds up the reader's own marks; it does not compute a score or rating.
- A notes box for the house.
- "Print or save as PDF" prints a one-page sheet with three house columns: the first carries this house's name, marks and answers, and the other two are blank so the reader can fill them in by hand for the next showings; it has Name and Date lines, an address line per column, and notes lines. To compare houses, readers use the printed sheet's columns or print one sheet per house. "Clear" (below the notes) erases the marks, answers and notes.
- Every tool page also has a "Share" button that opens the phone's share sheet or copies the page link.
- Disclaimer on the tool: a viewing checklist, not a home inspection; a licensed inspector checks the systems in detail after an offer.
- Under the tool, the page lists the complete item library with "Only if" tags.

## TOOL ITEM LIST (facts: the exact items the tool can show)
Ask the Agent or Seller:
- Why are the owners selling, and how long has it been on the market? (A long listing or a rushed sale changes how much room you have to negotiate.)
- Have there been offers, price cuts, or a sale that fell through? (Ask why a deal fell apart; it is often an inspection problem.)
- How old are the roof, heating, air conditioning and water heater? (These are the big replacements you would pay for soonest.) [only if: House or townhouse]
- How old are the unit's heating, air conditioning and water heater, and when was the building's roof replaced? [only if: Condo]
- Has there ever been water in the basement, a roof leak or a burst pipe, and any insurance claims? (Water damage is the costliest problem to find after you buy.) [only if: House or townhouse]
- Have there been leaks from neighboring units or the roof, and any insurance claims? [only if: Condo]
- What repairs or renovations were done, by whom, and were they permitted?
- What do utilities, property taxes and insurance cost per year?
- What are the HOA dues, what do they cover, and are any special assessments planned? (Also ask about rules on rentals and pets.) [only if: Condo or townhouse]
- Can I see the seller's disclosure form before I make an offer? (What a seller must disclose is set by state law.)
- Built before 1978: is there any known lead-based paint? (Sellers of pre-1978 homes must give buyers the federal lead disclosure.) [only if: Built before 1978 (or not sure)]
- Is it on city water and sewer, or a well and septic system? [only if: House]
- Which appliances, fixtures and window coverings are included in the sale?
- Any flooding, noise or planned construction nearby? (Look up the address on the FEMA flood map too.)
- Has the house had storm damage, and does it have shutters or impact windows? [only if: Hurricane-prone coast, not a condo]
- Can a new owner get homeowners insurance here, and at what cost? [only if: Hurricane-prone coast or wildfire area]
Check With Your Own Eyes:
- Water stains on ceilings and walls, around windows and under sinks (A fresh coat of paint on just one patch of ceiling can be covering a leak.)
- Musty, damp, smoke or pet smells (Smells point to moisture, mold or odors that are hard to remove.)
- Basement or crawlspace: damp walls, water lines, white residue, a sump pump [only if: House or townhouse]
- Cracks in walls or the foundation, sloping floors, doors that stick (Wide, stair-step or horizontal cracks are worth an inspector's opinion.) [only if: House or townhouse]
- Roof from the street: missing or curling shingles, sagging, overflowing gutters [only if: House or townhouse]
- Hallways, stairs and other common areas: signs of upkeep or neglect (Neglected common areas can mean low HOA reserves.) [only if: Condo]
- Run the shower and kitchen tap: water pressure, how long hot water takes, leaks below
- Electrical panel and outlets: scorch marks, two-prong outlets, flickering lights
- Turn on the heat and the air conditioning; look for the age sticker
- Open a few windows and doors: do they move, latch and seal, with no fogged glass?
- Outside: ground sloping away from the house, drainage, trees over the roof [only if: House or townhouse]
- Old wiring or pipes: two-prong outlets, knob-and-tube, galvanized or lead pipes [only if: Built before 1978]
- Peeling or chipping paint (built before 1978, it may contain lead) [only if: Built before 1978 (or not sure)]
- Gray plastic supply pipes under sinks (polybutylene, common from about 1978 to 1995) [only if: Built 1978 to 1999]
- Pipes in exterior walls, the garage or crawlspace, and how they are insulated [only if: Freezing winters, not a condo]
- Dry brush or wood piles against the house; vents and roofing that resist embers [only if: Wildfire area, not a condo]
- Water heater strapped to the wall [only if: Earthquake area]
