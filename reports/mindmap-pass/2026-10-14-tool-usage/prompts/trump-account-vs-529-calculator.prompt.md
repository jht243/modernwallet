## The calculator (describe only what it does)
Inputs: child's age now; expected annual return; yearly family contribution; tax rate on the withdrawal (usually the child's rate at 18); what the money pays for (college, or something else cashed out at 18); whether the child gets the $1,000 federal seed.
Outputs: which account leaves more after tax and by how much; each account's after-tax amount; each account's value at 18; each account's tax; a line explaining how the tax was figured; a warning when the yearly amount is above the Trump Account's $5,000 cap.
Method the page may state: both accounts get the same yearly money, spread evenly by month, at the same return, compounded monthly, until age 18. Trump Account: family contributions come out untaxed (they are after-tax money); the seed and the growth are taxed at the reader's rate; a 10% additional tax applies before age 59½ unless an exception applies, and qualified higher-education costs are an exception. 529: no federal tax when spent on qualified education; for other uses the earnings are taxed at the reader's rate plus a 10% additional tax on the earnings.
Assumptions the page must state: the return is a placeholder, not a forecast; state tax breaks on 529 contributions are not included; the tool cashes the money out at 18, while a Trump Account can stay invested after 18 under traditional-IRA rules.

Rules (IRS Trump Accounts page; IRS Notice 2025-68; IRS Publication 970; IRS Publication 590-B):
- The $1,000 federal seed is for U.S.-citizen children with a Social Security number born January 1, 2025 through December 31, 2028.
- A Trump Account takes at most $5,000 a year from all private sources combined; an employer may give up to $2,500 of that, inside the $5,000.
- Trump Account money is generally locked until January 1 of the year the child turns 18; after that it follows traditional IRA rules.
- Trump Account family contributions are after-tax and not deductible, so they come out untaxed; the seed, employer money and growth are taxed as ordinary income when withdrawn.
- A 10% additional tax applies to IRA-style withdrawals before age 59½ unless an exception applies; qualified higher-education expenses are an exception.
- 529 withdrawals spent on qualified education costs are free of federal income tax; a non-qualified withdrawal taxes the earnings as income plus a 10% additional tax on the earnings.
- A 529 has no federal yearly contribution limit.
- A child can have both a Trump Account and a 529.
Worked examples (computed by the calculator, 7% return, 12% tax rate):
- Newborn, $2,400 a year, seed, college use: Trump Account $89,656.74 at 18, $5,574.81 tax, $84,081.94 after tax. 529: $86,144.21 at 18, no tax. The 529 leaves $2,062.27 more.
- Same, cashed out at 18 for something else: Trump Account $79,436.26 after tax ($5,574.81 tax plus $4,645.67 additional tax). 529: $76,696.48 after tax ($5,153.30 tax plus $4,294.42 additional tax). The Trump Account leaves $2,739.78 more.
- Newborn, $6,000 a year, seed, college use: the Trump Account is capped at $5,000 a year and reaches $182,979.63; the 529 takes the full $6,000 and reaches $215,360.51.
- $100 a month ($1,200 a year) in a 529 from birth to 18 at 7%: $43,072.10.
- Without the seed, at $5,000 a year or less, both accounts leave the same after-tax amount for a non-college use (both return contributions untaxed and tax growth plus 10%); the Trump Account's non-college edge in the example comes entirely from the $1,000 seed and its growth.
