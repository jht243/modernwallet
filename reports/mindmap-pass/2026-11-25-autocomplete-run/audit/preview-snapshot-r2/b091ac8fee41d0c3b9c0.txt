How the Estimate Works To calculate the monthly tax portion of a mortgage payment, multiply your home purchase price by the state effective rate and divide the total by 12. In our internal modeling, we converted each state's effective rate to a monthly dollar figure on a $400,000 home using baseline data from the Tax Foundation . The underlying data comes from the 2024 American Community Survey (ACS) of the U.S. Census Bureau . An effective property tax rate represents the percentage of total owner-occupied market value paid in taxes. State averages often conceal dramatic county-level variations. In New Jersey, for example, county-level effective rates span from 0.93% in Cape May County to 2.47% in Camden County. That means an identical $400,000 purchase incurs about $310 a month in property taxes in Cape May County, but about $823 a month in Camden County. A buyer relying solely on the New Jersey state average of $627 a month faces a substantial discrepancy when closing on a home in either jurisdiction. Highest and Lowest Property Tax States The five states with the highest monthly property taxes on a $400,000 home are New Jersey ($627), Illinois ($627), Connecticut ($513), Vermont ($503), and New Hampshire ($500). Conversely, the five lowest property tax states for owner-occupied housing are Hawaii ($97 at 0.29%), Alabama ($123 at 0.37%), Utah ($160 at 0.48%), Arizona ($160 at 0.48%), and South Carolina ($163 at 0.49%). An effective property tax rate measures the annual property tax paid as a percentage of the total market value of owner-occupied housing. It lets you compare states on one scale.
