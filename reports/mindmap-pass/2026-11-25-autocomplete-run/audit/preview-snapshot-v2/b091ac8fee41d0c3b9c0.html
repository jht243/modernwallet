Property Tax by State: What It Adds to Your Mortgage Payment

By ModernWallet Research Desk · Reviewed by Jonathan Teplitsky, MBA · Updated November 25, 2026 · How we review

Publisher's Take — Jonathan Teplitsky, MBA · November 25, 2026

State-level effective tax rates offer a helpful baseline, but regional millage variances and assessment formulas ultimately dictate your closing escrow requirements. When sizing residential debt, always verify your specific county tax rate to avoid sudden shifts in debt-to-income underwriting.

Property tax adds roughly (effective rate x home price) / 12 to your monthly principal and interest payment, ranging from about $97 a month in Hawaii to about $627 a month in New Jersey and Illinois on a $400,000 home.

Evaluating the tax baseline in your target region helps protect against unexpected escrow shortages after closing. The breakdown below details the monthly property tax impact across all 50 states using standardized baseline pricing.

Property Tax Added to a $400,000 Mortgage by State

Property tax adds about (effective rate x home price) / 12 a month to a borrower's housing expense, which translates to about $97 in Hawaii up to about $627 in New Jersey and Illinois on a $400,000 home.

The District of Columbia carries an effective property tax rate of 0.60%, which adds $200 a month on a $400,000 residence and sits outside the 50-state ranking. Across the entire country, the median state effective rate results in a monthly property tax charge of about $263.

How the Estimate Works

To calculate the monthly tax portion of a mortgage payment, multiply your home purchase price by the state effective rate and divide the total by 12.

In our internal modeling, we converted each state's effective rate to a monthly dollar figure on a $400,000 home using baseline data from the Tax Foundation. The underlying data comes from the 2024 American Community Survey (ACS) of the U.S. Census Bureau.

An effective property tax rate represents the percentage of total owner-occupied market value paid in taxes.

State averages often conceal dramatic county-level variations. In New Jersey, for example, county-level effective rates span from 0.93% in Cape May County to 2.47% in Camden County.

That means an identical $400,000 purchase incurs about $310 a month in property taxes in Cape May County, but about $823 a month in Camden County. A buyer relying solely on the New Jersey state average of $627 a month faces a substantial discrepancy when closing on a home in either jurisdiction.

Highest and Lowest Property Tax States

The five states with the highest monthly property taxes on a $400,000 home are New Jersey ($627), Illinois ($627), Connecticut ($513), Vermont ($503), and New Hampshire ($500).

A higher monthly tax leaves less of your budget for the loan payment. You can test how a higher or lower tax changes what you can afford with our mortgage affordability calculator.

Conversely, the five lowest property tax states for owner-occupied housing are Hawaii ($97 at 0.29%), Alabama ($123 at 0.37%), Utah ($160 at 0.48%), Arizona ($160 at 0.48%), and South Carolina ($163 at 0.49%).

What Else Goes Into Escrow

Property tax is only one piece of a mortgage payment.

Our state comparison table models property taxes alone, meaning homeowners insurance premiums and private mortgage insurance remain excluded from these specific figures. Homeowners association (HOA) dues are also not included.

To calculate your comprehensive monthly housing obligation including all loan and insurance components, utilize our PITI mortgage calculator, where PITI stands for principal, interest, taxes, and insurance.

If you know your county's effective rate, use it in the same formula instead of the state figure. Our guide to mortgage mistakes before applying covers other costs buyers overlook.

Our Takeaway

Property tax can add from about $97 to about $627 a month on a $400,000 home depending on state, and your county rate decides the real number.

This benchmark modeling applies to owner-occupied residential properties assessed at the exact $400,000 baseline price. This analysis is not applicable to residential tenants, who pay shelter expenses via lease agreements rather than direct tax bills, nor does it capture multi-million-dollar purchases without linear adjustment.

If your home purchase differs from $400,000, scale the monthly dollar figures proportionally by multiplying the state rate against your target valuation before dividing by 12.

Our conclusions would change if local taxing jurisdictions revise their assessment ratios, if state legislatures expand homestead exemption limits, or if the Tax Foundation publishes updated effective rates. Multiply your own price by your county's effective rate and divide by 12 before you make an offer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a mortgage calculator include property tax and insurance?

Some mortgage calculators cover only principal and interest, while others add property tax and insurance. To compute your complete monthly housing expense including tax and insurance escrows, enter your loan and property details into our PITI mortgage calculator.

How much is property tax on a $400,000 house in the highest and lowest states?

On a $400,000 home, property taxes add about $627 a month in New Jersey and Illinois at the top of the state rankings, compared to about $97 a month in Hawaii at the bottom. The median state level sits at roughly $263 a month.

What is an effective property tax rate?

An effective property tax rate measures the annual property tax paid as a percentage of the total market value of owner-occupied housing. It lets you compare states on one scale.

Why do property taxes differ inside the same state?

Rates differ by county inside a state. For instance, New Jersey county rates span from 0.93% in Cape May County to 2.47% in Camden County, creating very different monthly payments on identical home prices.

Is homeowners insurance included in the monthly property tax figures?

Homeowners insurance is not included in the monthly property tax figures on this page.

How do I calculate monthly property taxes for my specific home price?

To calculate monthly property taxes for your specific home price, multiply your target purchase price by your local effective tax rate percentage and divide the resulting figure by 12.
