## The calculator (describe only what it does)
Inputs: dwelling coverage limit in dollars; the hurricane deductible option (2%, 5%, 10% of the dwelling limit, or a flat dollar amount such as $500); the amount of covered hurricane damage; the part of this calendar year's hurricane deductible already used up by an earlier hurricane claim with the same insurer (default $0); the policy's separate "all other perils" deductible (default $1,000).
Outputs: the hurricane deductible in dollars; the deductible that applies to this claim; what the policyholder pays; what the insurer pays; the hurricane deductible balance left for later hurricanes this calendar year. A note appears when the damage is smaller than the deductible (the policyholder pays all of it and the insurer pays nothing).
Method the page may state: deductible = percentage × dwelling limit. If part was used earlier in the calendar year, the deductible for this claim is the greater of the balance left and the all-other-perils deductible; if the full amount was already used, the all-other-perils deductible applies. Insurer pays damage minus the deductible that applies, never below zero.
Assumptions the page must state: this follows Florida's rules for personal residential policies; other states' wind and hurricane deductibles work differently and are not modeled; the figures come from the policy declarations page, which lists the hurricane deductible as a dollar amount; this is an estimate and the insurer's adjuster decides how a claim is applied; it does not model deductibles applied per structure, exclusions, or coinsurance.

## CLOSED FACT LIST — every number the page may state. Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never state a premium, a discount, a claim-payout statistic, or a rule from any state other than Florida.
Florida CFO (Department of Financial Services) facts:
- Insurers must offer hurricane deductible options of $500, 2 percent, 5 percent, or 10 percent of the policy dwelling limits, unless the specific percentage deductible is less than $500. The hurricane deductible must be listed as a dollar amount even if it is a percentage. Most policies have a hurricane deductible of 2 to 5 percent of insured value; some are a set amount such as $2,000.
- When a hurricane deductible is applied, no other deductible under the policy may be applied.
- The hurricane deductible applies from the time a hurricane watch or warning is issued for any part of Florida by the National Hurricane Center until 72 hours after the last hurricane watch or warning ends. It must be a "hurricane" declared by the National Hurricane Center.
- Exceptions to what must be offered: dwellings insured for $100,000 to $249,999 may be offered a non-renewal guarantee for one 12-month renewal period instead of the $500 option; dwellings insured for $250,000 or more need not be offered the $500 option but must be offered 2, 5 and 10 percent; for dwellings insured from $1 million to $3 million the insurer may offer 3, 5 and 10 percent in place of 2, 5 and 10; above $3 million only 5 and 10 percent need be offered. A dwelling insured for less than $500,000 may not have a hurricane deductible above 10 percent unless the policyholder personally writes and signs a statement (section 627.701, Florida Statutes).
- The hurricane deductible applies on an annual basis to all covered hurricane losses in the calendar year, for policies from the same insurer or insurer group. If the deductible was not fully met by the first hurricane, the deductible for the second is the greater of the balance left or the all-other-perils deductible; if it was met in full, the all-other-perils deductible applies to later hurricane claims that year.
- CFO example: a $200,000 policy with a 2 percent ($4,000) hurricane deductible and a $1,000 all-other-perils deductible. Hurricane 1 causes $2,000 of damage: the policyholder pays it all and $2,000 of the deductible is left. Hurricane 2 causes $5,000 of damage: the $2,000 balance is greater than the $1,000 deductible, so the insurer pays $3,000; later hurricane claims that year carry the $1,000 deductible.
- Notice of a claim (section 627.70132, Florida Statutes): a claim or reopened claim is barred unless notice is given to the insurer within 1 year after the date of loss; a supplemental claim within 18 months after the date of loss. For hurricanes the date of loss is the date of landfall.
Worked example (computed by the calculator): $300,000 dwelling limit. 2% = $6,000; 5% = $15,000; 10% = $30,000. With $40,000 of covered damage and a 5% deductible, the policyholder pays $15,000 and the insurer pays $25,000. With only $9,000 of damage and a 10% deductible ($30,000), the policyholder pays all $9,000 and the insurer pays $0.
No other figures.
