Published guidance the rules use:
- FTC rental scam warning signs: pressure to pay before seeing the unit, payment demanded by wire transfer, gift card or cryptocurrency, rent well below the local market, and excuses for not showing the unit. Protective steps: see the unit in person, search the address for duplicate listings, confirm the listing on the company's own site, and hold off on sharing a Social Security number until you have agreed to rent.
- Homes built before 1978: federal rules require sellers and landlords to disclose known lead-based paint and give the EPA pamphlet "Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home"; buyers get a 10-day opportunity to test for lead (they can waive it). A few exemptions apply. Source: EPA real estate disclosure page.
- Security-deposit limits, deposit return deadlines and landlord entry-notice rules are set by state (and sometimes city) law and differ by state. HUD keeps a page of tenant rights, laws and protections by state. Some states require landlords to give tenants a written move-in condition checklist.
- FTC: when a tenant-screening report plays any part in denying an applicant, requiring a co-signer, or charging a higher deposit or rent, the landlord must give an adverse action notice naming the screening company, and the applicant can get a free copy of the report and dispute errors. Landlords must securely dispose of reports when done.
- FEMA's Flood Map Service Center shows an address's flood zone. Flood insurance is required on most mortgages for homes in high-risk flood zones. Standard homeowners and renters policies generally do not cover flood damage.
- NFPA / USFA guidance: smoke alarms inside every bedroom, outside each sleeping area and on every level of the home; test them monthly; replace each alarm every 10 years.
- Climate items the switches add: freezing winters → pipes in unheated spaces, the main water shutoff, heating service (Red Cross winter guidance); hurricane coast → roof age and attachment, impact windows or shutters, the separate hurricane or wind deductible many coastal policies carry; wildfire area → defensible space, ember-resistant vents and roofing, insurance availability; earthquake area → water heater strapping, frame-to-foundation bolting on older houses, earthquake coverage is generally excluded from standard homeowners policies (ready.gov).

## TOOL ITEM LIST (facts: the exact items the tool can show; the page may describe any of them)
1. Before You Apply:
- Set your rent limit including utilities, parking, pet rent and renters insurance
- Confirm the listing is real: see the unit in person, search the address for duplicate listings, and never pay by wire, gift card or crypto
- Confirm the person renting it owns or manages it (county property records or the management company's own site) [only if: Small landlord, condo, house or room]
- Read recent reviews of the management company, especially about repairs and deposits [only if: Large building or complex]
- Test the commute at the hours you'd actually travel
- Look up the address on the FEMA flood map
2. On the Tour:
- Run the shower and kitchen tap: pressure, how long hot water takes, any leaks under sinks
- Look in cabinets, under sinks and along baseboards for droppings, roaches or bedbug signs
- Look for stains, peeling paint or musty smells on ceilings, around windows and in bathrooms
- Test every door and window lock, including sliding doors
- Find the smoke and carbon monoxide alarms
- Count outlets in each room and check cell signal
- Turn on the heat and AC, and ask the system's age and who services it
- Open the fridge, oven and dishwasher; ask which appliances the landlord repairs
- Listen for street, neighbor and building noise; come back at night if you can [only if: Apartment, condo or room]
- Check where laundry is and what it costs
- Confirm parking: assigned spot, permit, fee and guest rules [only if: Need parking]
- Ask how trash, recycling and package delivery work [only if: Apartment, condo or room]
- Walk the yard, gutters, driveway and exterior; ask who handles lawn care [only if: Whole house]
- Check the garage, basement or attic for water, pests and storage [only if: Whole house]
- Ask which spaces are shared, how bills are split and what the house rules are [only if: Room in a shared home]
- Ask whether you'll sign a lease with the owner or sublet from another tenant [only if: Room in a shared home]
- Built before 1978: expect the federal lead-paint disclosure and pamphlet before you sign [only if: Built before 1978 (or not sure)]
3. Questions to Ask the Landlord:
- What is the total due at move-in (first month, deposit, fees)?
- Which utilities are included, and what have past bills averaged?
- How do I request repairs, and what counts as an emergency?
- How much has rent gone up at renewal in the past?
- What happens if I need to break the lease?
- How much notice do you give before entering?
- What are the pet deposit, monthly pet rent and breed or weight limits? [only if: Bringing a pet]
- Who pays for lawn care, snow removal, pest control and HVAC filters? [only if: Whole house]
- What heat setting do you require in winter, and who pays if a pipe freezes? [only if: Freezing winters, not a large complex]
- Are there storm shutters, and who puts them up before a hurricane? [only if: Hurricane-prone coast, not a large complex]
4. Reading the Lease:
- Rent, due date, grace period and late fee
- Deposit amount, where it is held and the conditions and deadline for its return (Limits and return deadlines are set by state law.)
- Lease length, renewal terms and how much notice you must give to leave
- Every fee: application, admin, amenity, parking, trash, early termination
- Guest, roommate and subletting rules
- Get any verbal promise (repairs, paint, new appliances) written into the lease
- Pet addendum terms [only if: Bringing a pet]
- Exactly which maintenance jobs are yours (yard, snow, gutters, filters, pest control) [only if: Whole house]
5. Applying:
- Have photo ID, recent pay stubs or offer letter, and landlord references ready
- Check your own credit report first so nothing surprises you
- Ask what the application fee covers and whether it is refundable
- If you're denied because of a screening report, you're entitled to the name of the screening company and a free copy
6. Move-In:
- Fill out a room-by-room move-in condition report with photos and have the landlord sign it
- Buy renters insurance (Renters policies generally don't cover flood; that's a separate policy.)
- Set up the utilities in your name and forward your mail [only if: Not a large complex]
- Forward your mail [only if: Large building or complex]
- Find the water shutoff and breaker panel [only if: Not a large complex, or freezing winters]
- Learn two ways out of the neighborhood and sign up for local emergency alerts [only if: Wildfire area]
- Secure tall furniture to the wall and ask whether the landlord allows it [only if: Earthquake area]
- Know your evacuation zone and where you'd go [only if: Hurricane-prone coast]
