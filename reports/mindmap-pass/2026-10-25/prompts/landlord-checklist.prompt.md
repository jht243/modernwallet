- Homes built before 1978: federal rules require sellers and landlords to disclose known lead-based paint and give the EPA pamphlet "Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home"; buyers get a 10-day opportunity to test for lead (they can waive it). A few exemptions apply. Source: EPA real estate disclosure page.

- FTC: when a tenant-screening report plays any part in denying an applicant, requiring a co-signer, or charging a higher deposit or rent, the landlord must give an adverse action notice naming the screening company, and the applicant can get a free copy of the report and dispute errors. Landlords must securely dispose of reports when done.

- CPSC: carbon monoxide alarms on each level of the home and outside sleeping areas.

## TOOL ITEM LIST (facts: the exact items the tool can show; the page may describe any of them)
1. Set Up the Rental:
- Switch to a landlord (dwelling) policy and require tenants to carry renters insurance (A standard homeowners policy may not cover a home you rent out; ask your insurer.)
- Read your mortgage for owner-occupancy terms before renting it out [only if: Mortgage on the property]
- Read the HOA rules on rentals: minimum lease length, caps and registration [only if: Condo or townhouse]
- Check whether your city or county requires a rental license, registration or inspection
- Check local rules and taxes for furnished or short stays; many cities regulate short-term rentals [only if: Furnished / short or mid-term]
- Decide whether to hold the property in your name or an LLC (ask an attorney and your lender)
- Open a separate bank account for rent and deposits
- Read the management agreement: fees, leasing fee, repair approval limit and how to end it [only if: Property manager]
- Set up records for rental income, expenses and depreciation
2. Make It Rent-Ready and Safe:
- Install and test smoke alarms in every bedroom, outside sleeping areas and on every level
- Install carbon monoxide alarms outside sleeping areas and on each level if there is any fuel-burning appliance or attached garage
- Built before 1978: prepare the federal lead-paint disclosure and the EPA pamphlet for every lease [only if: Built before 1978 (or not sure)]
- Rekey the locks between tenants
- Service HVAC, water heater and appliances; fix leaks and broken fixtures
- Confirm pool fencing, gates and alarms meet local code [only if: Pool]
- Inventory every furnished item with photos [only if: Furnished / short or mid-term]
- Check common-area lighting, stair railings and exits [only if: Duplex / small multifamily]
- Look for pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, attics or garages, and ask how they are insulated or heat-taped (Unheated spaces are where pipes freeze and burst.) [only if: Freezing winters]
- Find the main water shutoff and confirm it turns (You need it in seconds if a pipe bursts.) [only if: Freezing winters]
- Plan who installs storm protection and checks the property after a storm [only if: Hurricane-prone coast]
- Clear defensible space around the building [only if: Wildfire area]
- Strap the water heater [only if: Earthquake area]
3. Price and List:
- Price from comparable rentals listed nearby
- Run the numbers: rent vs. mortgage, taxes, insurance, repairs and vacancy
- Write a listing with the rent, deposit, move-in date, pet policy and the screening criteria
4. Screen Tenants:
- Write your screening criteria (income, credit, rental history) and apply them to every applicant the same way
- Follow the Fair Housing Act: no decisions based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status or disability
- Treat assistance animals as a disability accommodation, not as pets [only if: Not a room in your own home]
- Verify income and call previous landlords
- If a screening report plays any part in denying or adding conditions, send an adverse action notice
- Store and later securely destroy applicants' reports and ID copies
5. Lease and Deposit:
- Use a lease written for your state, covering rent, late fees, repairs, entry notice, pets and termination
- Set the deposit within your state's limit and note your state's return deadline (Both are set by state law.)
- Spell out which utilities and maintenance jobs are the tenant's [only if: Single-family house]
- For a room or ADU, set house rules and shared-space terms in writing [only if: Room or ADU]
- Collect the first month and deposit by a traceable method before handing over keys
6. Move-In Day:
- Walk the unit with the tenant and both sign a move-in condition report with photos
- Hand over keys and record how many
- Give the tenant your repair-request process and emergency contact
7. Ongoing:
- Test smoke and CO alarms at each turnover and replace smoke alarms at 10 years
- Service HVAC once a year
- Clean gutters and check the roof each fall [only if: Not a condo]
- Before the first freeze: disconnect hoses, shut exterior faucets, insulate exposed pipes [only if: Freezing winters]
- Give the notice your lease and state require before entering
- At move-out, compare against the move-in report and return the deposit with an itemized list by your state's deadline
