## A. Tesla Cybercab — the vehicle (source: Wikipedia, Tesla Cybercab)
- Unveiled October 10, 2024, at Warner Bros. Studios in Burbank, California.
- Elon Musk stated a target price of "sub-$30,000". This is a stated target, NOT a confirmed price. Tesla has not published a consumer price.
- Musk stated a production target of "before 2027" and a volume goal of at least 2 million units a year. Both are stated goals.
- First production unit built February 2026 at Gigafactory Texas. Formal production began April 2026.
- As of August 2026, roughly 45 Cybercabs were registered to Tesla's own fleet.
- Two passenger seats. No steering wheel, no pedals, no side mirrors, no rear window. Scissor doors that open automatically.
- DC fast charging only through a NACS port. No AC charging.
- Single AC permanent-magnet motor, 219 hp (163 kW). 48 kWh lithium-ion battery. Range about 293 miles.
- Paid public rides in a Cybercab began September 4, 2026, in Austin, Texas. Tesla's earlier robotaxi service, launched June 2025, used Model Y vehicles running FSD.
- In September 2026 NHTSA opened an investigation into Tesla's self-certification, because the vehicle lacks equipment typically required under federal motor vehicle safety standards.

## B. The fleet-purchase interest form (sources: TechCrunch 2026-09-03, Electrek 2026-09-03)
- On September 3, 2026, Tesla published an interest form headed "help us build our robotaxi network", at tesla.com/robotaxi/interest.
- It went live the same day as an invite-only Cybercab event in Austin.
- The form's options are: Cybercab fleet purchasing; mobility hubs and infrastructure; event collaboration; and "other". It also has a deployment-region field.
- It is addressed to businesses interested in buying Cybercab fleets or providing network infrastructure. It is not an individual consumer order page.
- TechCrunch states the form "is not definitive proof that Tesla will sell its autonomous vehicles to third-party operators", and calls it an indicator of longer-term plans.
- No price, no timeline, no production date, no revenue-share terms and no fleet economics were disclosed with the form.
- No direct quote from Tesla or Musk accompanied the form.
- Electrek reports that individual owners who bought Full Self-Driving are not included: FSD was sold in part on a promise of future taxi income, and no private owner can run their car on the robotaxi network. Electrek characterises the form as a step toward restricting robotaxi car ownership to fleet buyers or to Tesla itself.
- Electrek published a follow-up on 2026-09-07 arguing that if a Cybercab fleet were reliably profitable, Tesla would keep the cars rather than sell them, because selling transfers the capital cost and depreciation to the buyer while Tesla keeps the software margin and its cut of each fare. Attribute this as Electrek's argument, not as fact.

## F. IRS figures — VERIFIED AGAINST THIS SITE'S OWN CALCULATOR ENGINES (authoritative here)
- 2026 has TWO business standard mileage rates: **72.5 cents per mile for January 1 to June 30, 2026**, and **76 cents per mile for July 1 to December 31, 2026**. Most published guides quote only 72.5 cents and get the second half of the year wrong.
- Self-employment tax is 15.3% charged on 92.35% of net profit, so the effective rate on Schedule C net profit is **14.13%**, not 15.3%. Social Security portion 12.4%, Medicare portion 2.9%.
- 2026 Social Security wage base: $184,500.
- The standard mileage rate bundles gas, depreciation, maintenance and insurance into one per-mile figure. It is a tax deduction, and it is also the closest published proxy for what a mile of driving actually costs a vehicle owner. Say which of the two you mean.

## G. GROUND-TRUTH WORKED EXAMPLE — computed with this site's own engines. Use these exact numbers; do not recompute or round differently.
Stated assumptions: a driver earning the Gridwise average of $23.88 per active hour, 21.2 active hours a week, 50 weeks a year, driving 20,000 business miles split evenly between the first and second halves of 2026, filing single with no other income.
- Gross for the year: **$25,312.80**
- Standard mileage deduction: 10,000 miles x 72.5 cents = **$7,250**, plus 10,000 miles x 76 cents = **$7,600**, total **$14,850**
- Blended rate actually achieved: **74.25 cents per mile**
- Schedule C net profit after the mileage deduction: **$10,462.80**
- Self-employment tax on that profit: **$1,478.35** (14.13% effective; the engine computes 92.35% of the UNROUNDED $10,462.80, so do not round the profit first)
- Federal income tax at this profit level, filing single: **$0**, because the standard deduction covers it
- Left after self-employment tax: **$8,984.45**
- Across 1,060 active hours, that is **about $8.48 per active hour** ($8.4759 — say "about", never "exactly")
- Note the reader must understand: the mileage deduction is a tax deduction, not a cash bill. It is used here as the best published stand-in for what those 20,000 miles cost the vehicle in fuel, maintenance and lost resale value. A driver whose real vehicle cost is lower keeps more; one driving an expensive car keeps less.
