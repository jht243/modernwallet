HSA
- 2026 HSA contribution limit: $4,400 self-only HDHP coverage; $8,750 family HDHP coverage.
- Age 55 or older: an additional $1,000 catch-up contribution (Pub 969 gives it as a flat $1,000).
- 2026 HDHP: minimum annual deductible $1,700 self-only, $3,400 family; maximum annual deductible plus other out-of-pocket expenses $8,500 self-only, $17,000 family.
- Eligibility: covered under an HDHP on the first day of the month; no other disqualifying health coverage (except what the publication permits); not enrolled in Medicare; cannot be claimed as a dependent on someone else's return.
- Portability: the HSA "stays with you if you change employers or leave the work force."
- Tax: contributions you or someone other than your employer make are deductible even if you do not itemize; employer contributions are excludable from income; earnings grow tax free; distributions for qualified medical expenses are not taxed.
- Non-qualified distributions are taxable and generally carry an additional 20% tax; that additional tax does not apply to distributions after you become disabled, reach age 65, or die.
HRA
- An HRA must receive contributions from the employer only; employees may not contribute.
- Self-employed persons are not eligible for HRAs.
- Employer contributions are not included in the employee's income and are free of federal income and employment tax; reimbursements must be for qualified medical expenses and are tax free.
- Carryover depends on the plan: "Some, but not all, HRAs permit amounts that remain at the end of the year to be carried to the next year."
- Qualified expenses for an HRA can include health insurance premiums and long-term care coverage premiums.
- Reimbursements can go to current and former employees, their spouses and dependents, and spouses and dependents of deceased employees.
- If an unused HRA amount is payable to you in cash at year end or on termination of employment, the distributions are included in your income.
HSA + HRA together
- Generally you cannot contribute to an HSA while covered by an HDHP and also by an HRA that pays qualified medical expenses. Exceptions include limited-purpose, suspended, post-deductible and retiree-only HRAs. With a retiree-only HRA, after retirement you are no longer eligible to contribute to an HSA.
