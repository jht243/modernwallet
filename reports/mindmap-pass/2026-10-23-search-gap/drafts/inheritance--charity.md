## Optional: Give Part of It Through a Donor-Advised Fund

Yes, you can donate inherited cash or stock to a donor-advised fund (DAF), but the gift lowers your tax only if you itemize deductions. A DAF is a charitable account at a public-charity sponsor: you take the deduction in the year you give, then recommend grants to charities later. The gift is irrevocable, so the money cannot come back to you.

Itemizing lowers your tax only when your itemized deductions exceed the standard deduction, which is $32,200 for married couples filing jointly in 2026. A $20,000 gift on its own falls $12,200 short of that line. That gift would cut your tax only if your other itemized deductions add up to more than $12,200.

Per [IRS Publication 526](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526), cash to a public charity is generally deductible up to 60% of adjusted gross income (AGI), and long-term appreciated stock is deductible up to 30%. Any excess carries over to later years. Most inherited stock gets a step-up in basis, so giving shares avoids little capital gains tax, and the deduction is the main benefit.

Keep the 30-to-90-day pause this page recommends before you move any inherited money. Then read [donor-advised funds explained](/guides/donor-advised-funds-explained/) to see how the account works before you pick a sponsor. For a large gift, ask a certified public accountant (CPA) how the AGI limits apply to your return.
