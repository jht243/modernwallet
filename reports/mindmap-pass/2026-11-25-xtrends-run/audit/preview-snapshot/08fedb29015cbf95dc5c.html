2027 Conforming Loan Limit: $855,400 and the Jumbo Line

By ModernWallet Research Desk · Reviewed by Jonathan Teplitsky, MBA · Updated November 25, 2026 · How we review

You can borrow up to $855,400 for a one-unit property in most counties in 2027 before your mortgage crosses into a jumbo loan. The Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) raised the baseline conforming loan limit (CLL) by $22,650 from the 2026 level of $832,750, tracking a 2.72 percent rise in third-quarter average national home prices.

In designated high-cost housing markets, the ceiling reaches $1,283,100, while special statutory provisions set higher baseline and ceiling levels in Alaska, Hawaii, Guam, and the U.S. Virgin Islands.

Because the conforming limit caps the total amount borrowed rather than the property purchase price, your down payment determines exactly how expensive a home you can buy before leaving the conforming mortgage market.

The 2027 Conforming Loan Limit

The 2027 baseline conforming loan limit for one-unit properties is $855,400 across the majority of United States counties. This represents an increase of $22,650 over the 2026 baseline limit of $832,750. Under federal statutory formulas, the baseline limit adjusts annually based on changes in the national average home price between the third quarter of the previous year and the third quarter of the current year. Between the third quarter of 2025 and the third quarter of 2026, average home prices increased by 2.72 percent, and FHFA applied that identical 2.72 percent rate of increase to the baseline loan threshold.

In high-cost areas where local median home values exceed the national baseline, federal law establishes a local ceiling calculated at 150 percent of the baseline conforming limit. For one-unit properties in these high-cost counties, the 2027 ceiling is $1,283,100. Specific statutory exceptions apply to Alaska, Hawaii, Guam, and the U.S. Virgin Islands. In those four designated regions, the local baseline limit is set at $1,283,100 and the maximum high-cost ceiling reaches $1,924,650 for a single-family one-unit residence.

Conforming loan limits define the maximum dollar size of a mortgage that government-sponsored enterprises Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac can acquire. These limits apply specifically to the total principal balance of the mortgage note, not to the contract purchase price of the real estate. When a buyer submits a down payment, the loan amount represents only the remaining unpaid balance. A homebuyer purchasing a property priced above $855,400 can still obtain a conforming loan as long as their cash down payment reduces the total borrowed principal to $855,400 or less.

Purchase Price Before a Loan Becomes Jumbo

In our internal calculation, we modeled the relationship between down payment percentages and maximum purchase prices to show how much home you can buy before crossing into jumbo financing. Using the mathematical formula of purchase price equal to loan limit divided by one minus the down payment share, we calculated the maximum transaction value supported by conforming loans at baseline and high-cost limits. Understanding Down Payment Math and Equity Impact

Every extra dollar added to a down payment lowers the amount you borrow by one dollar. If your loan lands slightly above the limit, a larger down payment can bring it back under.

The table assumes closing costs and prepaid items are paid in cash rather than added to the loan. If fees or financed insurance premiums are added to the loan principal, the purchase price a conforming loan supports is lower. A lender may also set its own thresholds.

Find Your County Limit

While $855,400 is the nationwide baseline, the conforming loan limit is higher than baseline in all but 24 counties or county equivalents across the United States. In the remaining counties where local median home values exceed baseline calculations, the limit scales upward between $855,400 and the high-cost ceiling of $1,283,100. Homebuyers must check the specific limit assigned to the county where the subject property is located rather than relying on national figures alone.

To identify your local lending threshold, consult the official county datasets provided on the FHFA conforming loan limit page. The agency publishes comprehensive downloadable county lists, interactive mapping resources, a technical methodology addendum, and detailed administrative guidelines. These documents list every county and county equivalent by state.

In high-cost areas, county limits reach the maximum ceiling of $1,283,100. Many suburban or intermediate housing markets feature custom limits established between the baseline and the ceiling. The baseline figures modeled in our table serve as a legal floor. For any county with an elevated limit, the maximum purchase price supported by a conforming loan will be higher than the baseline figures at every down payment level.

Does Waiting until January Help?

Waiting until January 2027 to finalize a mortgage transaction only benefits borrowers borrowing within a specific dollar window. Because the baseline limit increases from $832,750 in 2026 to $855,400 in 2027, the potential advantage applies exclusively to loan amounts between $832,750 and $855,400 in baseline counties. If your anticipated loan balance is below $832,750, the loan already meets conforming standards under 2026 rules. If your loan balance exceeds $855,400, the mortgage remains non-conforming regardless of the annual calendar change unless your county features a higher localized limit.

Even for loans falling inside that $22,650 band, waiting until January does not guarantee conforming treatment without direct lender confirmation. FHFA states in its official documentation that conforming loan limits apply to mortgages acquired by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac in the 2027 calendar year. The agency's public data pages tie the limits to the calendar year in which the loan is acquired and do not state a specific loan delivery rule or origination date rule.

Mortgage lenders establish their own pipeline policies regarding when they begin applying upcoming loan limits to newly originated applications. You must ask your lender directly which annual limit applies to your specific closing date.

What Changes If You Go Jumbo

A loan above the conforming limit in your county is a jumbo loan. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac cannot acquire it under the 2027 limit, so each lender sets its own underwriting rules and terms for it.

Ask each lender for its jumbo requirements and rates before you commit. To compare jumbo financing with conventional, government-insured and other options, read our breakdown of types of mortgage loans.

Our Takeaway

If your total borrowed mortgage balance is $855,400 or less, your loan qualifies as conforming across all United States counties in 2027. In the vast majority of counties where median property values are elevated, the conforming threshold scales higher, reaching a maximum high-cost ceiling of $1,283,100. If your proposed loan balance exceeds your county's designated limit, you will need to either secure a non-conforming jumbo mortgage or increase your down payment cash to bring the loan amount below the limit.

Waiting until January to close your transaction is only meaningful if your intended loan balance sits inside the specific $22,650 window between $832,750 and $855,400 in a baseline county, or the corresponding increment in a high-cost area. Because FHFA rules govern the calendar year the loan is acquired and individual lenders establish their own application schedules, you must verify your lender's intake rules before shifting transaction dates. For any purchase near the boundary, remember that each additional dollar contributed toward your down payment reduces the required loan principal dollar for dollar, providing an actionable way to maintain conforming financing.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 2027 conforming loan limit for a one-unit home?

The 2027 baseline conforming loan limit for a one-unit property is $855,400 in most counties across the United States. In designated high-cost housing markets, the ceiling reaches $1,283,100. In Alaska, Hawaii, Guam, and the U.S. Virgin Islands, the baseline is $1,283,100 and the maximum ceiling is $1,924,650.

Does the conforming loan limit cap the purchase price or the loan amount?

The conforming loan limit caps the loan amount, not the purchase price. A home priced above $855,400 can still carry a conforming loan if your down payment brings the loan to $855,400 or less in a baseline county.

How did FHFA calculate the $855,400 limit for 2027?

The Federal Housing Finance Agency calculated the 2027 limit by applying the percentage increase in national average home prices between the third quarter of 2025 and the third quarter of 2026. National home prices rose by 2.72 percent during that period, increasing the baseline limit by $22,650 over the 2026 figure of $832,750.

How many counties have loan limits above the $855,400 baseline?

According to FHFA, the conforming loan limit is higher than the baseline amount in all but 24 counties or county equivalents across the country. In those higher-cost jurisdictions, median home values justify limits between $855,400 and the high-cost ceiling of $1,283,100.

Will closing in December versus January change whether my loan is jumbo?

Closing in January matters only if your loan sits between $832,750 and $855,400 in a baseline county, and only if your lender confirms which year's limit applies to your loan. FHFA states the limits for mortgages Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac can acquire in 2027, and it states no delivery-date rule.

Who sets jumbo mortgage rates and terms?

Each lender sets its own rates and terms for loans above the limit, so ask each lender for its jumbo requirements.
