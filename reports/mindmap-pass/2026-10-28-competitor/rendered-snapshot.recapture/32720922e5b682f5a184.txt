That is the most an administrative wage garnishment may take under Title 20 of the United States Code (U.S.C.), section 1095a , unless you agree in writing to more. Disposable pay is the part of your pay left after legally required withholdings are subtracted, so leave voluntary deductions such as 401(k) contributions or health premiums in the number you enter. The monthly amount is 15% of disposable pay times paychecks per year, divided by 12. A tax refund can be taken in part or in full, because the Treasury can apply a federal refund to certain unpaid debts and send the rest to the filer, as the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) explains. The calculator counts the refund you enter, up to your balance. For Social Security, it uses the cap in Title 31 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), section 285.4 , which is the lesser of 15% of the monthly benefit or the amount above $750. According to Federal Student Aid , a repayment agreement with the first payment made within 65 days of the notice avoids the offset, and a hearing request must be postmarked within 65 days of receiving it. Frequently asked questions How much can the government garnish from my paycheck for defaulted student loans? The government can take up to 15% of your disposable pay per paycheck, unless you agree in writing to more. On $2,000 of disposable pay every two weeks, that is $300 per paycheck. Across 26 paychecks it comes to $7,800 a year. What counts as disposable pay? Disposable pay is the part of your pay that remains after legally required withholdings are subtracted. Voluntary deductions such as 401(k) contributions or health premiums are not subtracted. Leave them in the disposable pay figure you enter in the calculator above. Can my tax refund be taken for a defaulted student loan? Yes. The Treasury can use part or all of a federal tax refund to pay certain unpaid debts and sends the rest to the filer, according to the Internal Revenue Service . The calculator counts the full refund you enter, up to your balance. A $3,000 refund on a $30,000 balance is taken in full. Can Social Security be garnished for student loans? Yes, a Social Security offset can take the lesser of 15% of the monthly benefit or the amount above $750. Take an $800 monthly benefit, where 15% would be $120. Only $50 of that benefit sits above $750, so the offset is $50. Under this rule, a benefit of $750 or less leaves nothing to offset. Do I get notice before wage garnishment starts? Yes. You must get written notice at least 30 days before wage garnishment proceedings start, under 20 U.S.C. 1095a . If you file a petition within 15 days of the notice's mailing, you get a hearing. A tax refund offset comes with its own Treasury notice and a 65-day window. Are student loan collections really coming back after the midterms? The Education Department has not announced when expanded collections will start. The Washington Sun reported on Oct 19, 2026, that the administration is preparing wage garnishment and offsets for roughly 10 million borrowers in default, likely starting next year. The report said an announcement is unlikely before the midterm elections and that some officials want garnishment to begin for the 2027 tax year. It also said Treasury is moving to hire private debt collectors, possibly as soon as November. Does this apply to private student loans? No. Private lenders cannot use these wage garnishment and offset tools, which apply to defaulted federal student loans. If you hold only private loans, the calculator's figures do not apply to you. How do I stop garnishment or offset? You can avoid a refund offset with a repayment agreement if the first payment is made within 65 days of the notice, and you can leave default through rehabilitation or Direct Consolidation. Rehabilitation takes nine on-time agreed payments over roughly 10 months and removes the default record. Direct Consolidation can resolve the default in weeks but leaves the default record.
