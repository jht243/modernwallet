### A. Reported (U.S. Department of Education regulation 34 CFR 685.204, "Deferment", current eCFR text, read 2026-10-28; Direct Loan Program)
- (a)(1): a Direct Subsidized Loan or Direct Subsidized Consolidation Loan borrower who meets the requirements is eligible for a deferment during which periodic installments of principal and interest need not be paid.
- (a)(2): for Direct Unsubsidized, Unsubsidized Consolidation, PLUS and PLUS Consolidation loans, principal need not be paid but interest does accrue and is capitalized or paid by the borrower. The Secretary provides information at or before the time a deferment is granted to explain the impact of capitalization.
- (a)(3): a borrower whose loan is in default is not eligible for a deferment unless the borrower has made payment arrangements satisfactory to the Secretary.
- (a)(4): except for in-school deferments processed automatically, the borrower must request the deferment and provide the information and documents required to establish eligibility.
- (b) In-school deferment: available while carrying at least one-half the normal full-time workload for the course of study, excluding most medical internships and residencies.
- (f) Unemployment deferment: for loans disbursed before July 1, 2027, periods that collectively do not exceed three years in which the borrower is seeking and unable to find full-time employment. Qualify by providing evidence of eligibility for unemployment benefits, or a written certification that the borrower has registered with a public or private employment agency (if one is available within 50 miles) and, for all requests beyond the initial one, has made at least six diligent attempts during the preceding six-month period to secure full-time employment. Full-time employment means at least 30 hours a week and expected to last at least three months. The initial period may begin up to six months before the request is received and may be granted for up to six months after that date. A borrower may qualify whether or not previously employed; a deferment is not justified if the borrower refuses to seek or accept employment for which the borrower feels overqualified.
- (g) Economic hardship deferment: for loans disbursed before July 1, 2027, periods that collectively do not exceed three years, granted up to one year at a time. Qualify by (i) receiving a federal or state public assistance payment such as Supplemental Security Income, Food Stamps or state general public assistance; (ii) working full-time with monthly income that does not exceed the greater of the federal minimum wage rate or 150 percent of the poverty guideline for the borrower's family size; or (iii) serving as a Peace Corps volunteer.
- Other deferments named in section (a)(5) and (d) to (i): graduate fellowship, rehabilitation training, military service and post-active duty student deferments exist; do not describe their details.
- For loans disbursed on or after July 1, 2027, the regulation says a borrower may not receive an unemployment deferment (f)(1)(ii) or an economic hardship deferment (g)(1)(ii).
- (g)(2)(i) Economic hardship also qualifies a borrower who was granted an economic hardship deferment under the FFEL or Federal Perkins Loan programs for the period requested. So the economic hardship deferment has FOUR ways to qualify: that prior FFEL/Perkins deferment, public assistance, the full-time low-income test, or Peace Corps service.
- (f)(4) The Secretary does not grant an unemployment deferment beyond the date that is six months after the date the borrower provides evidence of eligibility for unemployment benefits or the written certification. Each request therefore covers at most six months forward, and reaching the three-year total takes repeated requests.
- WORDING RULE: say a subsidized-loan borrower "does not have to pay principal or interest installments" or "does not pay interest" during deferment. Never say a subsidized loan "owes no principal", "charges no interest" or "none owed".
### B. Reported (34 CFR 685.205, "Forbearance", current eCFR text, read 2026-10-28)
- Forbearance means permitting the temporary cessation of payments, an extension of time to pay, or temporarily accepting smaller payments. If interest payments are forborne, they are capitalized (with one stated exception that you must not describe).
- The Secretary grants forbearance when the borrower intends to repay and requests it with sufficient documentation and, for example, the Secretary determines that due to poor health or other acceptable reasons the borrower is currently unable to make scheduled payments (a)(1). Another stated ground: for not more than three years, loan payments each month are 20 percent or more of total monthly gross income (a)(6).
- 685.205(b)(9) Administrative forbearance: the Secretary may grant forbearance without borrower documentation in certain circumstances, including a period of up to 60 days necessary for the Secretary to collect and process documentation supporting the borrower's request for a deferment or forbearance; interest that accrues during this period is not capitalized. It is a circumstance the Secretary may grant, so tell the reader to ask the servicer whether it applies.
- Period: the Secretary grants forbearance for up to one year (c)(1)(i). For loans disbursed on or after July 1, 2027, forbearances under paragraph (a)(1) cannot exceed nine months within a 24-month period (c)(1)(ii).
