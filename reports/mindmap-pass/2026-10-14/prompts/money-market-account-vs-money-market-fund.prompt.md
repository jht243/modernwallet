answer: A money market account is a deposit account at a bank, covered by FDIC insurance within limits; a money market fund is a mutual fund that holds short-term debt and cash, and mutual funds are not covered by FDIC insurance. The same two words in the name hide a different kind of product.

## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — every number/date/limit this page may state

- FDIC deposit insurance covers $250,000 per depositor, per FDIC-insured bank, for each account ownership category (FDIC). Ownership categories include single accounts, joint accounts, certain retirement accounts such as IRAs, and trust accounts (FDIC).
- FDIC insurance covers deposits at FDIC-insured banks: checking accounts, savings accounts, money market deposit accounts (MMDAs), certificates of deposit, cashier's checks and money orders (FDIC). A "money market account" at a bank is a money market deposit account.
- FDIC insurance does not cover stock investments, bond investments, mutual funds, annuities, life insurance policies, U.S. Treasury bills, bonds or notes, municipal securities, or crypto assets (FDIC). FDIC deposit insurance only covers deposits, and only if the bank is FDIC-insured; the FDIC points readers to its BankFind Suite search tool to confirm a bank is insured (FDIC).
- FDIC insurance covers deposits, and does not cover non-deposit investment products, even those offered by an FDIC-insured bank (FDIC).
- A money market fund is a type of mutual fund that invests in liquid, short-term debt securities, cash and cash equivalents (SEC Investor.gov).
- Money market funds have relatively low risks compared to other mutual funds and most other investments, but historically have had lower returns (SEC Investor.gov).
- Money market funds pay dividends that generally reflect short-term interest rates (SEC Investor.gov).
- Many investors use money market funds to store cash or as an alternative to bank savings vehicles (SEC Investor.gov).
- Before investing in a money market fund, read the fund's available information, including its prospectus and its most recent shareholder report (SEC Investor.gov).
- A mutual fund is an SEC-registered open-end investment company that pools money from many investors and invests in stocks, bonds, short-term money-market instruments, other securities or assets, or a combination (SEC Investor.gov). Each share represents part ownership of the fund's portfolio and the income it generates. Investors buy through the fund or through a financial intermediary such as a broker or investment adviser. The price paid is the fund's per share net asset value (NAV) plus any fees charged at purchase; shares are redeemable, meaning an investor can sell them back to the fund at NAV minus any fees charged at redemption (SEC Investor.gov).
