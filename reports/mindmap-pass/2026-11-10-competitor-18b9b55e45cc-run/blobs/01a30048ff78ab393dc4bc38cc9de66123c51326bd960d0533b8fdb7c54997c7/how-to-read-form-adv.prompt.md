## Planned takeaway (inside an "Our Takeaway" section; do not repeat the opening verbatim)
Read Form ADV in order: find the firm on IAPD via Investor.gov, start with the Part 2A brochure (the SEC calls it the primary disclosure document) for fees, conflicts and discipline, then Part 2B for the individual, Part 3 for a quick firm comparison and Part 1 for ownership and disciplinary data; the order is our editorial judgment, and anything unclear becomes a question for the adviser. Applies to anyone hiring or reviewing a registered investment adviser in the US. Evidence basis: reported (SEC OIEA bulletin, Investor.gov). Changes if the person is a broker only (use FINRA BrokerCheck and Form CRS) or the SEC revises the form. Next step: search your adviser on IAPD and read Part 2A items on fees and disciplinary information before the next meeting.

## CLOSED FACT LIST (everything the page may state; anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a figure, fee, date, rule, name or URL; if it is not on this list, say it is not covered here)
FB1. [SEC Investor Bulletin: Form ADV, Investment Adviser Brochure and Brochure Supplement, checked 2026-11-10] SEC OIEA Investor Bulletin "Form ADV - Investment Adviser Brochure and Brochure Supplement" (June 24, 2016, updated Aug. 27, 2020). Form ADV is the uniform registration form for SEC and state authorities; three parts. Part 1: business, ownership, clients, employees, business practices, affiliations, disciplinary events of adviser or employees; check-the-box format; Part 1 filings are public on IAPD. Part 2 (plain-English narrative): Part 2A brochure has 18 items answered in the order listed with the form headings, to ease comparison; examples named: advisory business (services and amount of client assets managed), fees and compensation (how compensated, fee schedule, negotiability, billing vs deduction, other fees such as brokerage, custody and fund expenses), performance-based fees and side-by-side management, methods of analysis/strategies/risk of loss, disciplinary information (certain events presumed material within last 10 years), code of ethics/participation in client transactions/personal trading, brokerage practices; annual delivery of a summary of material changes with a revised brochure or offer to deliver it. Part 2B brochure supplement: information about individuals who give advice (education, business experience for the past five years, disciplinary information, other business activities; six items); delivered for each supervised individual who formulates advice with direct client contact or makes discretionary decisions. Part 3 relationship summary (SEC-registered advisers serving retail investors): services, fees and costs, conflicts, standard of conduct, legal and disciplinary history, key questions to ask. Brochures and relationship summaries are public on IAPD; IAPD is reachable via the Check Out Your Investment Professional search tool on Investor.gov. The brochure is the primary disclosure document for investment advisers.
FB2. [Investor.gov Glossary, Form ADV, checked 2026-11-10] Investor.gov glossary: Form ADV is the uniform form to register with the SEC and state authorities; Part 1 business information (check-the-box), Part 2 narrative brochures on practices, fees, conflicts and discipline (primary disclosure document, delivered to clients), Part 3 relationship summary; parts available on IAPD.
FB3. [Investor.gov, Investment Advisers, checked 2026-11-10] Investor.gov: investment advisers are required to act in your best interest and not put their interest ahead of yours; the way advisers make money creates conflicts to ask about. An adviser is a firm or person that, for compensation, provides investment advice about securities. Advisers typically provide ongoing advice about buying, selling or holding investments and monitor performance; the fee is typically based on the value of all of the assets held in your account with the adviser; they may also give planning services. Before selecting: services you need, limits, cost, how the adviser is paid, conflicts, legal or disciplinary history. Read the contract, the relationship summary and the Form ADV Part 2 brochure. The services and fees depend on the contract you negotiate. Advisers generally must register with the SEC or state authorities; check both the person and the firm.
FB4. [Investor.gov, Relationship Summary (Form CRS), checked 2026-11-09] Investor.gov/CRS (captured 2026-11-09): registered broker-dealers and registered investment advisers must provide a relationship summary (Form CRS) to retail investors; it covers services, fees and costs, conflicts, required standard of conduct, legal/disciplinary history and conversation starters; many firms are dual registrants and you should understand when they act as a broker and when as an adviser. Brokers are generally transactional and typically paid commission or markup per transaction; advisers are generally focused on your portfolio over time and typically paid an ongoing asset-based fee on total account value. Asset-based fees may cost more than transaction-based but suit ongoing advice or delegated decisions.
FB5. [Investor.gov, Working with an Investment Professional, checked 2026-11-10] Investor.gov: working with an investment professional (fetched 2026-11-10); used for the registration-check and Check Out Your Investment Professional tool references.
