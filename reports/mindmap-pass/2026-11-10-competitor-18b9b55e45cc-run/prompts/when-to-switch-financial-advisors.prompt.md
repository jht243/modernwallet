## Planned takeaway (inside an "Our Takeaway" section; do not repeat the opening verbatim)
Switch when your adviser's services, all-in cost or conflicts no longer fit what you need, and move in order: read your contract, check the new adviser on IAPD, open the new account and let the receiving firm start the transfer rather than selling or taking a check yourself. Applies to clients of SEC- or state-registered advisers and broker-dealers in the US. Evidence basis: reported (investor.gov, FINRA, IRS). Changes if your contract has termination charges, some assets cannot transfer, or the account is a retirement account where a payment to you triggers withholding and rollover limits. Next step: read your current contract's termination and fee terms before asking the new firm for its Transfer Initiation Form.

## CLOSED FACT LIST (everything the page may state; anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a figure, fee, date, rule, name or URL; if it is not on this list, say it is not covered here)
FB1. [Investor.gov, Investment Advisers, checked 2026-11-10] Investor.gov: investment advisers are required to act in your best interest and not put their interest ahead of yours; the way advisers make money creates conflicts to ask about. An adviser is a firm or person that, for compensation, provides investment advice about securities. Advisers typically provide ongoing advice about buying, selling or holding investments and monitor performance; the fee is typically based on the value of all of the assets held in your account with the adviser; they may also give planning services. Before selecting: services you need, limits, cost, how the adviser is paid, conflicts, legal or disciplinary history. Read the contract, the relationship summary and the Form ADV Part 2 brochure. The services and fees depend on the contract you negotiate. Advisers generally must register with the SEC or state authorities; check both the person and the firm.
FB2. [SEC Investor Bulletin: Form ADV, Investment Adviser Brochure and Brochure Supplement, checked 2026-11-10] SEC OIEA Investor Bulletin "Form ADV - Investment Adviser Brochure and Brochure Supplement" (June 24, 2016, updated Aug. 27, 2020). Form ADV is the uniform registration form for SEC and state authorities; three parts. Part 1: business, ownership, clients, employees, business practices, affiliations, disciplinary events of adviser or employees; check-the-box format; Part 1 filings are public on IAPD. Part 2 (plain-English narrative): Part 2A brochure has 18 items answered in the order listed with the form headings, to ease comparison; examples named: advisory business (services and amount of client assets managed), fees and compensation (how compensated, fee schedule, negotiability, billing vs deduction, other fees such as brokerage, custody and fund expenses), performance-based fees and side-by-side management, methods of analysis/strategies/risk of loss, disciplinary information (certain events presumed material within last 10 years), code of ethics/participation in client transactions/personal trading, brokerage practices; annual delivery of a summary of material changes with a revised brochure or offer to deliver it. Part 2B brochure supplement: information about individuals who give advice (education, business experience for the past five years, disciplinary information, other business activities; six items); delivered for each supervised individual who formulates advice with direct client contact or makes discretionary decisions. Part 3 relationship summary (SEC-registered advisers serving retail investors): services, fees and costs, conflicts, standard of conduct, legal and disciplinary history, key questions to ask. Brochures and relationship summaries are public on IAPD; IAPD is reachable via the Check Out Your Investment Professional search tool on Investor.gov. The brochure is the primary disclosure document for investment advisers.
FB3. [Investor.gov, Relationship Summary (Form CRS), checked 2026-11-09] Investor.gov/CRS (captured 2026-11-09): registered broker-dealers and registered investment advisers must provide a relationship summary (Form CRS) to retail investors; it covers services, fees and costs, conflicts, required standard of conduct, legal/disciplinary history and conversation starters; many firms are dual registrants and you should understand when they act as a broker and when as an adviser. Brokers are generally transactional and typically paid commission or markup per transaction; advisers are generally focused on your portfolio over time and typically paid an ongoing asset-based fee on total account value. Asset-based fees may cost more than transaction-based but suit ongoing advice or delegated decisions.
FB4. [FINRA, Brokerage Accounts: Account Transfers, checked 2026-11-10] FINRA, Brokerage Accounts - Account Transfers: most customer accounts move between broker-dealers through ACATS; start by completing a Transfer Initiation Form sent to the receiving firm; the receiving firm need not accept the account (nontransferable assets such as third-party funds without a selling agreement or proprietary products; most common reason is the new firm's credit policies); firms must notify customers of nontransferable assets and provide alternatives; validation plus delivery generally about three to four business days; transfers from non-broker-dealers (bank, credit union, mutual fund) generally take more time; accounts requiring a custodian, like an IRA, may take additional time; buying and selling during a transfer often complicates and delays it, and some firms freeze trading.
FB5. [IRS, Rollovers of Retirement Plan and IRA Distributions, checked 2026-11-10] IRS rollover page: most pre-retirement payments from a retirement plan or IRA can be rolled over by depositing within 60 days; you can also have your financial institution or plan directly transfer the payment to another plan or IRA. "The one-per year limit does not apply to: rollovers from traditional IRAs to Roth IRAs (conversions), trustee-to-trustee transfers to another IRA, IRA-to-plan rollovers, plan-to-IRA rollovers, plan-to-plan rollovers." If taxes were withheld on a distribution paid to you and you roll it over within 60 days you must use other funds to make up the withheld amount.
FB6. [Investor.gov, Working with an Investment Professional, checked 2026-11-10] Investor.gov: working with an investment professional (fetched 2026-11-10); used for the registration-check and Check Out Your Investment Professional tool references.
