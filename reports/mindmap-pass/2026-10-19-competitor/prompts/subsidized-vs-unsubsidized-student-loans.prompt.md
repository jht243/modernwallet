CLOSED FACT LIST:
- Direct Subsidized Loans are need-based and limited to undergraduates; you must have financial need.
- Direct Unsubsidized Loans are open to undergraduate and graduate students and do not require financial need.
- On Direct Subsidized Loans, the government does not charge interest while you are enrolled at least half-time, during your grace period, and during deferment periods.
- On unsubsidized loans you are responsible for all interest during all periods; interest starts accumulating from the date of your first loan disbursement. You can pay it as it accrues or let it capitalize (be added to principal).
- Dependent undergraduate annual limits: $5,500 in year one, $6,500 in year two, $7,500 in year three and beyond, of which no more than $3,500, $4,500 and $5,500 respectively may be subsidized. Aggregate cap for dependent undergraduates: $31,000, of which up to $23,000 may be subsidized. Independent students have higher limits, with a $57,500 aggregate (undergraduate).
- First-time borrowers on or after July 1, 2013 generally cannot receive subsidized loans for more than 150% of their program length (for example, six years for a four-year bachelor's degree).
- Interest rates are set for each award year and the rate on a loan depends on when it was first disbursed; the 4.99% rate in one FSA PDF applied only to loans first disbursed from July 2022 to July 2023, so do not present it as current; tell the reader to check StudentAid.gov for current rates.
