CLOSED FACT LIST:
- VA purchase loans often require no down payment as long as the sales price is not higher than the home's appraised value.
- VA eligibility starts with a Certificate of Eligibility (COE); lenders can often get a COE in one day.
- VA funding fee: a one-time payment, a percentage of the loan amount (not the purchase price), can be financed or paid at closing; it ranges from 0.5% to 3.3% depending on loan type, loan amount, down payment amount, and whether the veteran is a first-time user; a down payment of at least 5% can lower it; Veterans with a service-connected disability rating and certain surviving spouses are exempt.
- VA loans carry no private mortgage insurance or mortgage insurance premiums.
- USDA loans: for eligible rural and low-to-moderate-income borrowers; no down payment is typically required; income must be at or below the limit for the area; the property must be in an eligible area and used as a primary residence.
- USDA borrowers pay an upfront guarantee fee and ongoing mortgage insurance premiums to USDA. Current percentages are not on this list: tell the reader to check USDA's current rate.
- FHA loans require a minimum down payment of 3.5% with a credit score of at least 580 (HUD rule; lenders may set stricter overlays) .
- Many conventional programs allow as little as 3% down; state and local housing agencies run down payment assistance programs with their own eligibility rules (tell the reader to check their state housing finance agency).
- Closing costs are separate from the down payment.
