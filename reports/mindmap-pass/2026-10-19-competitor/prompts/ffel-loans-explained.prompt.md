CLOSED FACT LIST:
- The FFEL Program ended for new loans on July 1, 2010; you may still have an FFEL loan if you were in school before then.
- To check: log in to StudentAid.gov, under Loan Breakdown select View Loans; a loan with "FFEL" at the front of its listing is an FFEL Program loan. My Aid shows whether loans are Direct or commercial FFELP. NSLDS is another way to find out.
- In My Loan Servicers on the dashboard, if the servicer name starts with "ED", the loan is held by ED.
- PSLF: only Direct Loans count. FFEL, Perkins and private loans do not qualify, but FFEL and Perkins loans may become eligible if consolidated into a Direct Consolidation Loan.
- Consolidating generally means losing credit for earlier payments. Borrowers had to consolidate by June 30, 2024 to benefit from the one-time IDR account adjustment; that deadline has passed.
- Many FFEL borrowers get a reduced interest rate for paying on time; adding an FFEL loan to a Direct Consolidation Loan can cost you that rate reduction. Consolidation can also lengthen repayment.
- Steps 1 and 2 of the Direct Consolidation Loan Application show how consolidation will change your monthly payment and total repayment period.
- PSLF rules are changing: the Education Department published final PSLF regulations on Oct. 30, 2025, effective July 1, 2026; check StudentAid.gov/publicservice for current requirements.
