## CLOSED FACT LIST — anything not here, you do not know. Never invent a number, date, or URL.

1. Net worth is a snapshot at a point in time: total assets minus total liabilities. This is
   already the page's own definition (see the page's current intro and its first FAQ).
2. Net income (personal-finance sense) is a flow over a period — what you actually earned after
   taxes and deductions in that period (a paycheck, a year). It is not a stock/snapshot figure
   the way net worth is.
3. A high income does not automatically produce a high net worth, and a low or zero income does
   not automatically mean a low net worth. Someone can earn a large salary while spending all of
   it (or more) and have little or negative net worth; someone can have modest income but a
   large net worth built from past saving, investment growth, or inherited/accumulated assets.
   This is a logical/definitional point, not a statistic — do not attach a specific percentage
   or study to it.
4. "Equity" in a personal-finance context most commonly refers to the value of a specific
   ownership stake in one asset (most often home equity: a home's value minus what is still
   owed on its mortgage, or equity in a business or a specific investment holding).
5. Net worth is the sum across EVERY asset and liability a person or household has; equity (in
   the sense of fact 4) is a value confined to ONE asset. Home equity is one input that adds
   into the broader net worth total; it is not a synonym for it. This is the same relationship
   already implied by the page's own calculator, which takes home equity as one input field
   among several feeding the total net worth output.
6. Do not invent any numeric example, dollar figure, statistic, or named study for this FAQ —
   this is a definitional clarification, not a data claim. Do not cite a source that is not
