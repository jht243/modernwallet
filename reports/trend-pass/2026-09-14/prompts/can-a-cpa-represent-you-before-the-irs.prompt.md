## THE CLOSED FACT LIST — the ONLY facts, numbers, and specifics this page may state

Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a number, date, fee, form field, phone number, or URL not given here; say a detail is unpublished/not stated by the IRS and point the reader to the primary source instead.

1. Under Circular 230, a CPA who is not currently under suspension or disbarment from practice before the IRS, and who is duly qualified to practice as a CPA in any U.S. state, possession, territory, commonwealth, or the District of Columbia, may practice before the IRS. (Source: IRS, "Understanding Tax Return Preparer Credentials and Qualifications" / IRS Publication 947.)
2. CPAs, attorneys, and Enrolled Agents are the three credential types with **unlimited representation rights** before the IRS — they may represent a client on any matter, including audits, payment/collection issues, and appeals, regardless of who prepared the return. (Source: IRS, "Understanding Tax Return Preparer Credentials and Qualifications.")
3. A CPA's unlimited rights are the same scope of practice rights an attorney or an Enrolled Agent holds — the credential differs, the practice rights before the IRS do not. (Source: IRS Publication 947 / Circular 230.)
4. By contrast, preparers without one of those three credentials have only limited representation rights: an Annual Filing Season Program participant may represent only clients whose returns they personally prepared and signed, and only before IRS revenue agents, customer service representatives, and similar employees — never in appeals or collection matters. A PTIN holder with no credential and no AFSP completion has, since January 1, 2016, no authority to represent a client before the IRS at all. (Source: IRS, "Understanding Tax Return Preparer Credentials and Qualifications.")
5. IRS Form 2848, "Power of Attorney and Declaration of Representative," is the form that authorizes a named individual to represent a taxpayer before the IRS. (Source: IRS, "About Form 2848.")
6. Form 2848 grants the named representative authority to inspect and/or receive the taxpayer's confidential tax information AND to perform acts the taxpayer could perform — for example, sign agreements, consents, and waivers on tax matters — for the tax matters and periods listed on the form. (Source: IRS Instructions for Form 2848.)
7. Form 2848 covers Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FBAR) examinations in addition to standard IRS tax matters. (Source: IRS, "About Form 2848.")
8. Form 8821, "Tax Information Authorization," is a separate, narrower form: it lets a named individual or organization inspect and/or receive the taxpayer's confidential tax return information, but it does NOT authorize that person to represent the taxpayer or to speak/act on the taxpayer's behalf. (Source: IRS, "About Form 2848" comparison language; IRS "About Form 8821.")
9. Eligible representatives a taxpayer can name on Form 2848 include: attorneys, Certified Public Accountants (CPAs), Enrolled Agents, Enrolled Actuaries, Enrolled Retirement Plan Agents, officers of an organization, full-time employees, certain family members (spouse, parent, child, sibling, grandparent, or grandchild, including step-relations), unenrolled return preparers (limited to returns they prepared), and qualifying students or law graduates working in a Low Income Taxpayer Clinic (LITC) or Student Tax Clinic Program (STCP). (Source: IRS Instructions for Form 2848.)
10. A representative's authorization for a qualifying student or law graduate under an LITC/STCP automatically expires (the IRS "purges" them as the taxpayer's representative) 130 days after the taxpayer signs the form. (Source: IRS Instructions for Form 2848.) This 130-day auto-expiration applies ONLY to that student/law-graduate category — do not extend it to CPAs, attorneys, or Enrolled Agents.
11. The Centralized Authorization File (CAF) number is a unique nine-digit identification number the IRS assigns to a representative — it is not the representative's Social Security number, Employer Identification Number, PTIN, or enrollment card number. (Source: IRS Instructions for Form 2848.)
12. Form 2848 can be submitted online at IRS.gov/Submit2848 (the online tool supports electronic signatures), by fax to an IRS number that depends on the taxpayer's location, or by mail to an IRS address that depends on the taxpayer's location. (Source: IRS Instructions for Form 2848 / IRS "About Form 2848.")
13. The IRS instructions for Form 2848 do not state a filing fee for the form. Do not state a specific dollar fee; say there is no indicated filing fee.
14. Form 2848 must specify the tax matters (for example, income tax), the tax form number, and the specific years or periods it covers — it is not a blanket, all-matters authorization. (Source: IRS Instructions for Form 2848 — Part I of the form requires these fields.)
15. A CPA representative must complete Part II of Form 2848, the Declaration of Representative, which requires the representative to state their designation (CPA) and attest they are currently authorized to practice, are aware of the Circular 230 responsibilities that apply, and are authorized to represent the taxpayer for the matters listed on the form. (Source: IRS Instructions for Form 2848 — this is what Part II requires structurally; do not invent the exact attestation wording, describe its function only.)
