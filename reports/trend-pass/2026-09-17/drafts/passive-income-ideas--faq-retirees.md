For retirees who need capital preservation and steady cash flow, high-yield savings accounts, certificates of deposit, and dividend-paying index funds are usually the most practical choices. These options generate regular distributions without the landlord responsibilities of rental properties, while qualifying deposit accounts carry Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) coverage up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank. Additional investment income can also cause a larger portion of your monthly benefit to become taxable, as explained in our guide on [taxable Social Security benefits](/guides/is-social-security-taxable/). You can estimate your baseline payments with our [Social Security retirement calculator](/retirement/social-security-retirement-calculator/) before adding new cash streams to your plan.
