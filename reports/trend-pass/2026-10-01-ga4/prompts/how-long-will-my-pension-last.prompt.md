reader question: "How long will my pension money last if I take a fixed amount out each month?"
answer: It depends on three numbers: the balance, the monthly withdrawal and the return. A $500,000 balance paying $3,000 a month at a 5% return lasts about 23.8 years; paying $2,000 a month it never runs out at 5%, because monthly interest ($2,083) exceeds the withdrawal.

### The method (state it in plain words, no code)
- Money lasts n months, where the balance is drawn down by a fixed withdrawal at the end of each month and the balance earns the annual return divided by 12 each month. If the monthly interest on the starting balance is at least the withdrawal, the balance never runs out.
- Assumptions that MUST appear immediately above Table 1: 5% annual return, credited monthly, fixed withdrawal at the end of each month, no inflation adjustment to the withdrawal, no taxes, no fees, the return is constant (real markets are not).
- Interest-only check: $500,000 at 5% earns about $2,083 a month ($500,000 x 0.05 / 12). That is why $2,000 a month never depletes it and $3,000 a month does.

### TABLE 1: years of withdrawals by starting balance and monthly withdrawal. Reproduce EXACTLY.
| Starting balance | $2,000/mo | $3,000/mo | $4,000/mo | $5,000/mo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $250,000 | 14.7 years | 8.5 years | 6.0 years | 4.7 years |
| $500,000 | never runs out | 23.8 years | 14.7 years | 10.8 years |
| $750,000 | never runs out | never runs out | 30.5 years | 19.7 years |
| $1,000,000 | never runs out | never runs out | never runs out | 35.9 years |

### Derived facts you MAY state (arithmetic on the tables only)
- At $500,000, raising the withdrawal from $3,000 to $4,000 a month cuts the runway from 23.8 years to 14.7 years, about nine years, for a one-third bigger withdrawal.
- On Table 2, moving the return from 3% to 7% almost doubles the runway, from 14.7 to 25.7 years.
- The same $4,000 a month lasts 6.0 years on $250,000 and 30.5 years on $750,000, so the starting balance matters, but the withdrawal-to-balance ratio matters more.

### Context facts (all already published on this site, may be stated)
- The 4% figure comes from the 1998 Trinity Study of historical 30-year periods. A 30-year-or-longer retirement is at the edge of what it modelled; this site's early-retirement calculator page suggests 3% to 3.5% for 35-year-plus horizons.
- A pension that pays a monthly benefit for life (a defined benefit pension) does not "run out" the way a lump sum does; this page is about a lump sum or a savings pot you draw down yourself. The monthly-benefit estimate lives on /retirement/pension-calculator/.
- If a pension lump sum is paid to you directly rather than rolled over, eligible rollover distributions from employer plans generally have 20% federal income tax withheld, and the whole amount is generally taxable income in that year. A direct rollover to an IRA generally avoids the immediate tax. Tell the reader to check with the plan administrator and a tax professional. (Source: IRS, closed URL list.)
- Taking money out before age 59 and a half can add a 10% additional tax unless an exception applies. (Source: IRS.)
