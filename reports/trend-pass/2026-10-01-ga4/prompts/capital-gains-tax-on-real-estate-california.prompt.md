reader question: "How is capital gains tax on a California real estate sale calculated?"
answer: You pay federal tax on the gain (the sale price minus selling costs minus your adjusted basis), at 0%, 15% or 20% for property held over a year, plus California income tax, which taxes the same gain as ordinary income up to 13.3%, because California has no lower capital gains rate. A primary residence can exclude up to $250,000 (single) or $500,000 (married filing jointly) of gain.

### Federal facts
- Gain = amount realized (sale price minus selling costs) minus adjusted basis. Adjusted basis = purchase price plus capital improvements (and closing costs added to basis) minus depreciation claimed or allowed.
- Property held more than one year is long-term: federal rates of 0%, 15% or 20% depending on taxable income. Held one year or less is short-term and taxed as ordinary income. Do NOT state the income thresholds for the 0/15/20 bands. You do not have them; tell the reader to check IRS Topic 409.
- The net investment income tax adds 3.8% for taxpayers above modified adjusted gross income thresholds of $200,000 (single) and $250,000 (married filing jointly). These thresholds are set by statute and not indexed for inflation.
- Home sale exclusion (Section 121): up to $250,000 of gain for single filers, $500,000 married filing jointly, if you owned the home and used it as your main home for at least 2 of the 5 years before the sale. Generally usable once every two years. (IRS Topic 701.)
- Depreciation on rental property: the gain attributable to depreciation previously taken is "unrecaptured Section 1250 gain", taxed at a federal maximum of 25%. It does not qualify for the home-sale exclusion on the rented portion. (IRS Publication 523 covers sale of a home; Topic 409 covers capital gains.)
- A Section 1031 like-kind exchange can defer federal gain on investment or business real property.

### California facts
- California has no separate or reduced capital gains rate. It taxes capital gains as ordinary income at its graduated rates, and the top marginal rate is 12.3% plus a 1% Mental Health Services Tax on taxable income over $1 million, for 13.3% at the top. Do NOT list California's brackets or income cutoffs. You do not have them; tell the reader to check the Franchise Tax Board.
- California generally conforms to the federal home sale exclusion.
- California requires a report (Form FTB 3840) each year when California property is exchanged for property outside California in a 1031 exchange, so deferral is tracked.
- Real estate withholding at closing (Form 593): when a seller is not a California resident, or the property was not the seller's principal residence, the escrow company generally withholds 3 1/3% of the sale price toward the seller's tax, unless an exemption applies. It is a prepayment, not an extra tax. Tell the reader to check with the escrow officer and the FTB.

### Worked example (arithmetic, state it exactly; it is NOT a tax computation of dollars owed)
Bought for $400,000, spent $50,000 on a new roof and kitchen (capital improvements), sold for $900,000 with $54,000 in selling costs. Adjusted basis = $400,000 + $50,000 = $450,000. Amount realized = $900,000 - $54,000 = $846,000. Gain = $846,000 - $450,000 = $396,000.
- As a primary residence owned and lived in 2 of 5 years: married filing jointly excludes up to $500,000, so none of the $396,000 is taxed. Single excludes up to $250,000, leaving $146,000 taxable ($396,000 - $250,000).
- As a rental or investment property (no exclusion): all $396,000 is taxable, before any depreciation recapture adjustment, which would change the split but not the total gain unless depreciation was taken, in which case the basis is lower and the gain bigger.
