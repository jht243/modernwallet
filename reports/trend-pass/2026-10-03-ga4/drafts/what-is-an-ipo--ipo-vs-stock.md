An IPO is an event where a private company sells shares to the public for the first time, while a stock is the actual share of ownership you hold. Once that offering concludes, those same shares trade on the open market as ordinary public stock. The practical difference for buyers comes down to access and pricing. Underwriters set the IPO price the night before trading begins and distribute most of those initial shares to institutional and high-net-worth clients first. Most retail investors must wait until trading opens on the exchange, where they often pay a price already above that initial offering figure.
