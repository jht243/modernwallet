An initial public offering (IPO) is the transaction event where a private company sells its shares to the public for the first time, while a stock is the actual share of company ownership you hold. Once that listing event finishes, those same shares become ordinary public stock trading on an exchange. The practical difference is when and how you buy them. Underwriters allocate the initial offering price mostly to institutional and high-net-worth investors the night before, meaning most retail investors can only buy after open-market trading begins at a potentially higher price. As the [Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/files/ipo-investorbulletin.pdf) outlines in its investor bulletin, newly public shares lack a public trading history, so retail buyers rely on the prospectus to assess the business and its risks.
