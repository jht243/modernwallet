## Closed fact list — anything not here, you do not know
All facts below are already published on this same site, in the Danelfin listing inside
/roundup/best-ai-stock-pickers/. Restate them faithfully; do not add any number, date, or claim
that is not on this list.
- Danelfin evaluates thousands of daily data points across United States and European equities to
  assign an AI Score from 1 to 10.
- A score of 10 indicates the highest statistical probability of outperforming the broader market
  over a three-month horizon; a score of 1 indicates expected underperformance.
- Danelfin's core strength is its explainable architecture: it breaks every overall AI Score down
  into separate sub-scores for technical indicators, fundamental valuation metrics, market
  sentiment data, and low-risk signals.
- Danelfin's own backtest shows that stocks it rated 10 out of 10 outperformed the market since
  2017, while stocks it rated 1 out of 10 underperformed.
- Danelfin offers a free tier with limited stock reports, so a reader can test it before paying.
- Paid tiers: Plus costs approximately $22 per month; Pro costs approximately $59 per month.
  (These are approximate published figures — tell the reader to confirm the current price
  directly on Danelfin's own pricing page before subscribing.)
- Danelfin is built for three-month swing-trading horizons, not intraday day-trading scalping.
- Danelfin is a stock research and scoring platform, not an automated trade-execution program —
  investors place trades manually through their own brokerage after using Danelfin's score.
- This site's own roundup verdict: Danelfin is the site's pick for best AI stock picker for most
  retail investors, because it pairs a predictive 1-to-10 rating model with full factor
  transparency, avoiding the black-box opacity of other automated trading software.
- That same roundup verdict says who should NOT use Danelfin: day traders who need split-second
  intraday execution signals should use Trade Ideas instead, because Danelfin's three-month
  predictive window does not serve high-frequency scalping; and passive long-term index investors
  should avoid all AI stock-picking tools, because systematically buying low-cost total-market
  index funds is mathematically superior to chasing individual equity momentum.
- Regulators have warned generally about AI investment tools: the SEC and FINRA have both warned
  that no algorithmic engine guarantees market-beating returns and that automated outputs should
  never be treated as guaranteed profit signals (already cited elsewhere on this site).
- Do NOT state a specific overall win-rate percentage, a specific number of Danelfin subscribers,
