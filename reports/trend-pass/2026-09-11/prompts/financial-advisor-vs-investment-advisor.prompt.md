## CLOSED FACT LIST — anything not here, you do not know. Never invent a number, date, or URL.

1. "Investment adviser" is a legal term defined in Section 202(a)(11) of the Investment Advisers
   Act of 1940: any person who, for compensation, engages in the business of advising others,
   directly or through publications, as to the value of securities or the advisability of
   investing in, purchasing, or selling securities. (Source: Investment Advisers Act of 1940 /
   15 U.S.C. § 80b-2, via Cornell Legal Information Institute.)
2. Registered investment advisers (RIAs) owe their clients a fiduciary duty under the Investment
   Advisers Act of 1940. (Source: SEC.gov — Investment Advisers overview.)
3. Investment advisers with $100 million or more in regulatory assets under management (RAUM)
   generally must register with the SEC; advisers under $100 million generally register with
   state securities regulators instead. Advisers between $100 million and $110 million may
   choose SEC or state registration (a buffer meant to avoid forcing firms to flip back and forth
   as asset values move). This $100 million threshold was set by the Dodd-Frank Act, which raised
   it from the prior $25 million level. (Source: SEC.gov — Investment Advisers overview.)
4. The Series 65 (Uniform Investment Adviser Law Examination) is the exam commonly required to
   become a licensed investment adviser representative in most states. It is administered by
   FINRA on behalf of NASAA, has 130 scored questions (plus 10 unscored) with a 180-minute time
   limit, and requires correctly answering at least 92 of the 130 scored questions to pass.
   Unlike many other securities exams, no sponsoring firm is required to sit for it. (Source:
   FINRA.org — Series 65 exam page.)
5. "Financial advisor" (also spelled "financial adviser") is not itself a licensed or legally
   defined title — nothing stops anyone from using it regardless of registration status. This is
   the same point the site's own financial-advisor-vs-cpa page makes about the "financial
   advisor" title having "no universal license."
6. Broker-dealers and their registered representatives — who may also market themselves as
   "financial advisors" — operate under Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI), a rule under the
   Securities Exchange Act of 1934 that took effect June 30, 2020. Reg BI requires that a
   recommendation to a retail customer be in the customer's best interest, built on four
   obligations (disclosure, care, conflict of interest, and compliance). This is a different,
   narrower standard than the fiduciary duty an RIA owes on an ongoing advisory relationship — Reg
   BI applies per-recommendation to broker-dealer conduct, not as a continuous fiduciary
   relationship. (Source: FINRA.org — Regulation Best Interest page.)
7. Investors can look up whether a specific person or firm is a registered investment adviser,
   and read their Form ADV (including the Part 3 relationship summary), for free through the
   SEC's Investment Adviser Public Disclosure (IAPD) database, accessible via the "Check Out Your
   Investment Professional" tool on Investor.gov. (Source: Investor.gov — Check Out Your
   Investment Professional.)
8. Do NOT restate the specific numeric details (AUM figures, minimums) from the site's existing
   financial-advisor-vs-wealth-manager or financial-advisor-vs-cpa pages as if they apply here —
   those are about a different distinction (client segment / tax licensing) and are out of scope
   for this page.
