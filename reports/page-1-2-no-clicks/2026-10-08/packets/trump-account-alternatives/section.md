## Alternative Savings Vehicles and Funding Rules

A 529 plan serves families focused strictly on tax-free college savings, while a custodial brokerage account under the Uniform Transfers to Minors Act (UTMA) or Uniform Gifts to Minors Act (UGMA) fits parents who want flexible spending without contribution caps. A custodial Roth IRA works for a teenager with earned income, a Coverdell Education Savings Account (ESA) covers smaller education goals up to $2,000 annually, and high-yield savings accounts or Treasury inflation-protected I bonds protect cash needed on a shorter timeline. If you weigh a [Trump Account vs a 529](/compare/trump-account-vs-529/), the broader utility of these established vehicles often outweighs the restrictions tied to the newer model.

Outside of family deposits, other authorized entities can contribute to a child's account under [IRS guidance on Trump Accounts](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-68.pdf). Under Internal Revenue Service (IRS) Notice 2025-68, governments at the federal, state, local, or tribal level, alongside section 501(c)(3) charities, can provide qualified general contributions for specific classes of children. Employers may also add up to $2,500 per year under section 128 as a tax-free benefit to the worker. Our guide on [how a Trump Account works](/guides/trump-accounts/) breaks down how these outside contributions share an annual $5,000 cap with family deposits for 2026 and 2027.
