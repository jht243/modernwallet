## What this page must establish, up top

Sentence 1 of the intro is the direct answer: Dave Ramsey's 12% figure is a real, sourced number
(the historical average annual S&P 500 return over his stated 1928-2025 window), but it is a
NOMINAL figure that is higher than most other commonly cited long-run averages and does not
subtract inflation — which is the core of the disagreement, not a disagreement about whether
Ramsey invented the number.

## CLOSED FACT LIST — anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a percentage,

1. Ramsey Solutions' own site states: "The historical average annual return from 1928 through
   2025 is 11.86%." It also cites specific 30-year rolling windows: 1981-2010 averaged 12.08% a
   year; 1986-2015 averaged 11.73% a year; 1996-2025 averaged 11.80% a year. Source: Ramsey
   Solutions, ramseysolutions.com/retirement/the-12-reality.
2. That same Ramsey Solutions page acknowledges year-to-year volatility explicitly, citing 2022's
   return of -18.04% against 2023's return of 26.06%, and it addresses the 2000-2009 "Lost
   Decade" (which it says averaged around 1% a year) by noting that decade combined with the
   previous decade's roughly 19% average produces "a respectable 10% average annual return."
   Source: Ramsey Solutions, as above.
3. Ramsey Solutions' stated defense of the figure is twofold: focus on performance across many
   years rather than any single year, and treat your own savings rate as more important to
   retirement success than the exact return percentage used in a projection. Source: Ramsey
   Solutions, as above.
4. Ramsey Solutions' cited figures (11.86%, and the 12.08%/11.73%/11.80% rolling windows) are
   NOMINAL returns — Ramsey Solutions' page does not present them as inflation-adjusted, and this
   page must say so plainly rather than let the reader assume otherwise.
5. Separately, officialdata.org's own long-run S&P 500 dataset (built from Robert Shiller's
   historical price/dividend data plus U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI records) states the
   S&P 500's average annual return since 1926, with dividends reinvested, is 10.47% a year in
   nominal terms and 7.29% a year in real (inflation-adjusted) terms. Source: officialdata.org,
   officialdata.org/us/stocks/s-p-500/1926.
6. David Blanchett, head of retirement research at PGIM DC Solutions (part of PGIM, the asset
   management arm of Prudential Financial), publicly criticized the 12% figure used by personal-
   finance pundits including Dave Ramsey, calling it "absolutely nuts" because it does not account
   for volatility or for average annual inflation, which ran around 3% from 1926 to 2023.
   Blanchett said 7% is a more accurate historical estimate for an aggressive, stock-heavy
   investor, and 5% is more appropriate for someone in a balanced stock-and-bond portfolio.
   Source: reported by Yahoo Finance (citing CNBC's original reporting of Blanchett's comments),
   finance.yahoo.com/news/absolutely-nuts-expert-slams-suze-210000688.html.
