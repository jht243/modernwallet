## FAQ question (use verbatim)
"Is there a maximum mileage deduction?"

## CLOSED FACT LIST — state ONLY these facts. Anything else, you do not know.
1. The IRS standard mileage rate itself has no dollar cap and no mileage cap — you deduct the rate times however many business miles you actually drove and can substantiate; there is no published ceiling on the total dollar amount or number of miles. Source already cited elsewhere on this hub: IRS Publication 463 and the IRS standard mileage rates page (do not add a new external link here; these are already in this page's Sources list, so state the fact without adding a duplicate citation link).
2. The real constraint is NOT a cap — it is substantiation. The IRS requires a contemporaneous mileage log (date, business miles, destination, and business purpose for each trip) to support the deduction. A deduction with no supporting log is the kind of thing that gets disallowed on audit, however large or small the dollar amount.
3. The second real constraint is the standard-mileage-vs-actual-expenses election rule already covered elsewhere on this hub: you must choose the standard mileage rate in the first year a vehicle is used for business if you want it available for that vehicle at all; taking actual expenses first permanently forfeits the standard rate for that car.
4. Do not invent a specific IRS audit-flag mileage threshold (e.g., "the IRS flags returns over X miles") — not verified, not stated anywhere in the facts above.
