## The one thing this page must get right

This hub's calculator prices the US IRS federal standard mileage rate for the US self-employed tax deduction. The UK, Canada, Ireland, and New Zealand each run their OWN, completely separate government mileage-rate system, with different names, different structures (some tiered by distance, some by engine size, some by fuel type), and different units (pence/km vs cents/mile vs dollars/km). Say this distinction plainly near the top so a non-US reader is not left trying to map their own country's rate onto the US figure.

### United Kingdom — HMRC Approved Mileage Allowance Payments (AMAP)
1. Current AMAP rate for cars and vans, effective from 6 April 2026 (the 2026-27 UK tax year): 55 pence per mile for the first 10,000 business miles in the tax year, then 25 pence per mile for every business mile after that. Source: GOV.UK, Travel — mileage and fuel rates and allowances (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rates-and-allowances-travel-mileage-and-fuel-allowances/travel-mileage-and-fuel-rates-and-allowances).
2. This 55p/25p rate is a rise from the prior 45p/25p rate, which had applied since 2011 — the first change to the car/van AMAP rate in roughly 15 years. Source: same GOV.UK page.
3. Motorcycles: 24 pence per mile flat (no first-10,000 tier). Bicycles: 20 pence per mile flat. Source: same GOV.UK page.
4. AMAP rates already bundle fuel, insurance, servicing, tyres, road tax, and general wear — an employer paying at or below the approved rate makes a tax-free payment; nothing extra is added for VAT or fuel separately at that level.
5. Mileage Allowance Relief (MAR): if an employer pays a mileage allowance below the approved AMAP rate (including paying nothing at all), the employee can claim tax relief on the shortfall — the difference between what the approved rate would have paid and what the employer actually paid. Claimed either through Self Assessment or through HMRC's dedicated online service, depending on the employee's situation. Source: GOV.UK, Tax relief for employees — vehicles you use for work (https://www.gov.uk/tax-relief-for-employees/vehicles-you-use-for-work).

### Canada — CRA automobile allowance rate
6. 2026 CRA tax-exempt automobile allowance rate for an employee using a personal vehicle for business, in the provinces: 73 cents per kilometre for the first 5,000 km driven in the year, then 67 cents per kilometre after that. In the territories (Yukon, Northwest Territories, Nunavut): 77 cents per kilometre for the first 5,000 km, then 71 cents per kilometre after — 4 cents per km higher than the provincial rate. Effective January 1, 2026, a 1-cent increase from the 2025 rate. Source: Department of Finance Canada (https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/01/government-announces-the-2026-automobile-deduction-limits-and-expense-benefit-rates-for-businesses.html).
7. This is the ceiling for a tax-free employer allowance; an employer may pay above it, but the excess becomes a taxable benefit to the employee.

### Ireland — Civil Service motor travel rates
8. Ireland's Civil Service motor travel rates (unchanged since 1 September 2022) apply in 4 distance bands, each split by 3 engine-size categories. Use ONLY the "1501cc and over" column, consistently, for any worked example on this page: Band 1 (up to 1,500 km) = 51.82 cent/km; Band 2 (1,501-5,500 km) = 90.63 cent/km; Band 3 (5,501-25,000 km) = 39.22 cent/km; Band 4 (25,001 km and over) = 25.87 cent/km. (The lower engine-size columns exist too — up to 1200cc and 1201-1500cc — but do not quote their figures; naming one clean column avoids mixing numbers across categories.) Source: Revenue.ie, Civil Service rates (https://www.revenue.ie/en/employing-people/employee-expenses/travel-and-subsistence/civil-service-rates.aspx).
9. Electric vehicles are reimbursed using the 1201cc-1500cc rate band.
10. Private-sector employers in Ireland are not legally bound to the Civil Service rates, but Revenue allows them to make tax-free mileage reimbursements using the current Civil Service schedule, or any other rate that is not higher than it — so in practice the Civil Service scale is the tax-free benchmark private employers use too. Source: same Revenue.ie page (civil-service-rates.aspx) — the "private sector employers" tax-free reimbursement point comes from Revenue's own published guidance on this same rate schedule.
11. Band 2 pays MORE per km than Band 1 — this is a genuinely unusual, non-obvious structure worth flagging (most mileage systems pay a flat or declining rate; Ireland's middle band briefly pays the most).

### New Zealand — Inland Revenue kilometre rates
12. IRD kilometre rates for the 2025-26 income year, a two-tier system. Tier 1 (covers both fixed and running costs, for the first 14,000 km of combined business-and-private use in the year): petrol $1.20/km, diesel $1.30/km, petrol hybrid $0.90/km, electric $1.22/km. Tier 2 (running costs only, for km beyond the 14,000 km threshold): petrol $0.37/km, diesel $0.38/km, petrol hybrid $0.24/km, electric $0.23/km. Source: Inland Revenue, Operational Statement OS 19.04 (https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/operational-statements/2026/os-19-04-km-2026).
13. The 14,000 km threshold counts ALL driving in the vehicle for the year (business plus private), not just business kilometres — a common point of confusion worth stating explicitly.
14. Using IRD's kilometre rates is optional, not compulsory; a business or self-employed person may instead deduct actual vehicle costs. Employers are not restricted to reimbursing at the IRD rate either — they may pay more or less. Source: same IRD operational statement.

### Cross-country comparison point (for the US reader who lands here by mistake)
