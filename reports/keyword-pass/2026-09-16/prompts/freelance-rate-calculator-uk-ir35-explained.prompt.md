## CLOSED FACT LIST — anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a number, rate, date,
threshold, or URL. Where a fact below is attributed to a secondary source rather than gov.uk directly,
say so in the text (e.g., "contractor-compliance specialists report...") rather than presenting it as
an HMRC position.

1. IR35 (the off-payroll working rules) tests whether a worker who provides services through their
   own intermediary — typically a personal service company (PSC) — would, ignoring that intermediary,
   look like an employee of the client doing that work directly. Source: gov.uk.
2. "Inside IR35" means the engagement is treated as deemed employment for tax purposes. The party
   responsible for paying the worker (the "fee-payer") must deduct Income Tax and employee National
   Insurance contributions from what it pays the intermediary, and separately account for employer
   National Insurance and the Apprenticeship Levy. Source: gov.uk.
3. "Outside IR35" means the worker is treated as genuinely self-employed for that engagement and
   handles their own tax — via Self Assessment, or via their own limited company's corporation tax and
   dividends — rather than having tax deducted at source like an employee. Source: gov.uk.
4. Who determines IR35 status depends on the client: public-sector clients and medium/large
   private-sector clients are responsible for making the determination; small private-sector clients
   leave the determination with the contractor's own intermediary instead. Source: gov.uk.
5. Whoever is responsible for the determination should produce a Status Determination Statement (SDS)
   setting out the decision and the reasons for it. Source: gov.uk.
6. HMRC provides a free online tool called CEST (Check Employment Status for Tax) that anyone —
   a hirer, a worker, an agency, or a third party — can use to get an indication of employment status
   for tax, including whether the off-payroll working (IR35) rules apply to a specific engagement.
   Source: gov.uk (CEST guidance page).
7. HMRC states it will stand by a CEST result as long as the information entered is accurate and not
   contrived, but HMRC's own guidance also says the result should not be relied on alone. Source:
   gov.uk (CEST guidance page).
8. The off-payroll working rules were reformed for the private sector on 6 April 2021, shifting
   responsibility for making the status determination from the contractor/intermediary to the client
   in most medium and large private-sector engagements. Source: gov.uk.
9. From 6 April 2026, the statutory thresholds that decide whether an end-user client counts as
   "small" (and therefore exempt from having to issue the determination itself) are increasing:
   the turnover threshold rises from GBP 10.2 million to GBP 15 million, and the balance sheet total
   threshold rises from GBP 5.1 million to GBP 7.5 million; the employee headcount threshold stays at
   50 employees. Report this as reported by contractor-compliance specialists (Kingsbridge), citing
   the underlying statutory change, not as a verbatim HMRC quote.
10. Because the small-company test looks at a client's previous financial year(s), contractor-
    compliance specialists report the practical effect of that 2026 threshold change will not land for
    most affected engagements until the 2026/27 tax year at the earliest. Source: Kingsbridge (as
    reported).
11. From 6 April 2026, new rules mean agencies and end clients can become liable for PAYE
    underpayments on workers supplied through umbrella companies, even where the umbrella company
    itself is the one running payroll. Source: Kingsbridge (as reported).
12. This site's own freelance rate calculator (the /freelance-rate/ hub and its spokes) works
    backwards from a target take-home through 2026 US federal self-employment tax (15.3%) and the
    federal Qualified Business Income (QBI) deduction — a US-specific model with no IR35 equivalent.
    This is our own already-published page content, not an external source.
13. Do not state any specific UK Income Tax band, National Insurance percentage, dividend tax rate, or
    corporation tax rate anywhere on this page — none of those figures are on this fact list. Where the
    page needs the reader to know their own rate, tell them to get the current figure from HMRC/gov.uk
    directly rather than stating a number here.
