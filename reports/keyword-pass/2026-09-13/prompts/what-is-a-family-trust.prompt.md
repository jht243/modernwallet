## CLOSED FACT LIST — the ONLY facts this page may state. Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, benchmark, or URL; say it is unpublished and tell the reader to verify at the source.

1. "Family trust" is not a distinct legal category — it is the common name for a trust set up to hold and pass on assets for the benefit of family members, and it is nearly always structured as one of the two legal forms below (revocable or irrevocable). [American Bar Association — Revocable Living Trusts](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba-cms-dotorg/products/inv/brochure/339471413/rev-living-trusts_wtrmk-2-5431040.pdf)
2. A revocable trust (also called a living trust) can be revoked, amended, or dissolved by the person who created it (the grantor) at any time while they are alive and competent; state law and the trust document itself govern exactly how. The IRS treats every revocable trust as a "grantor trust," meaning its income is taxed to the grantor personally, not to the trust. [IRS — Abusive Trust Tax Evasion Schemes, Questions and Answers](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/abusive-trust-tax-evasion-schemes-questions-and-answers)
3. An irrevocable trust, by its terms, cannot be modified, amended, or revoked once it is created — control over the assets placed inside it passes out of the grantor's hands. An irrevocable trust can still be taxed as a grantor trust for income-tax purposes if it meets specific Internal Revenue Code tests (sections 671, 673, 674, 675, 676, or 677), but that is a tax-classification question separate from whether the trust itself can be changed. [IRS — Abusive Trust Tax Evasion Schemes, Questions and Answers](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/abusive-trust-tax-evasion-schemes-questions-and-answers)
4. A revocable living trust takes effect while its creator is alive and generally avoids probate — the court process used to validate a will and supervise the distribution of an estate — because the trust, not the deceased person, legally owns the assets titled into it. A will, by contrast, only takes effect at death and must go through probate. [American Bar Association — Revocable Living Trusts](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba-cms-dotorg/products/inv/brochure/339471413/rev-living-trusts_wtrmk-2-5431040.pdf)
5. A will is also the only one of the two documents that can name a guardian for minor children; a living trust does not do this. [American Bar Association — Revocable Living Trusts](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba-cms-dotorg/products/inv/brochure/339471413/rev-living-trusts_wtrmk-2-5431040.pdf)
6. A will becomes a public court record once filed for probate; a living trust's terms are generally not filed with a court and stay private. [American Bar Association — Revocable Living Trusts](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba-cms-dotorg/products/inv/brochure/339471413/rev-living-trusts_wtrmk-2-5431040.pdf)
7. For 2026, the federal estate and gift tax basic exclusion amount is $15,000,000 per person, up from $13,990,000 in 2025 — the threshold below which an estate owes no federal estate tax regardless of how it is structured. [IRS — IRS releases tax inflation adjustments for tax year 2026](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)
8. Because that federal exclusion is $15,000,000 per person in 2026, most families' estates fall well under it, and an irrevocable trust's main historical selling point — removing assets from a taxable estate — has no federal estate-tax benefit for an estate under that amount. State-level estate or inheritance taxes can apply at much lower thresholds than the federal amount and vary by state; a reader should check their own state's rules rather than assume only the federal number applies. [IRS — IRS releases tax inflation adjustments for tax year 2026](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)
9. Living trusts and wills are commonly used together: a "pour-over will" catches any asset never retitled into the trust and directs it into the trust at death, while the trust still avoids probate for everything already titled in its name. [American Bar Association — Revocable Living Trusts](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba-cms-dotorg/products/inv/brochure/339471413/rev-living-trusts_wtrmk-2-5431040.pdf)
