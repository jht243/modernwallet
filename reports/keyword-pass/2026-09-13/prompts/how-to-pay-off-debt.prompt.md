## CLOSED FACT LIST — the ONLY facts this page may state. Anything not on this list, you do not know. Never invent a price, limit, benchmark, or URL; say it is unpublished and tell the reader to verify at the source.

1. The Federal Reserve's G.19 Consumer Credit release reported the average APR on credit card accounts assessed interest at 22.15% in Q2 2026, up from 21.52% in Q1 2026; the average APR across all credit card accounts was 20.94% in Q2 2026; and the average APR on new credit card offers was 23.82%. [Federal Reserve — Consumer Credit (G.19)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/)
2. Paying only the minimum due on a credit card is called negative or "no" amortization when the payment is small enough that it barely covers, or does not cover, the interest charged that month — the balance then stops shrinking or keeps growing even though payments are being made. [Federal Reserve — Regulation Z, Minimum Payment Warning requirement (12 CFR 1026.7(b)(12)(ii))](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/7/)
3. Under Regulation Z, when a card's minimum payment would result in negative or no amortization, the issuer must print this exact disclosure on the statement: "Minimum Payment Warning: Even if you make no more charges using this card, if you make only the minimum payment each month we estimate you will never pay off the balance shown on this statement because your payment will be less than the interest charged each month." [Federal Reserve — Regulation Z, Minimum Payment Warning requirement (12 CFR 1026.7(b)(12)(ii))](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/7/)
4. The National Foundation for Credit Counseling (NFCC), founded in 1951, is the largest and longest-serving nonprofit credit-counseling organization in the U.S., with more than 1,500 NFCC-certified counselors serving all 50 states. [NFCC — Debt Management Plan](https://www.nfcc.org/debt-management-plan/)
5. A debt management plan (DMP) run through an NFCC-member agency consolidates unsecured debts (mainly credit cards) into one monthly payment the agency distributes to creditors on the borrower's behalf, typically over 36 to 60 months; the counselor negotiates with creditors and may secure reduced or waived interest and fees. [NFCC — Debt Management Plan](https://www.nfcc.org/debt-management-plan/)
6. An initial NFCC credit-counseling session typically runs 30 minutes to an hour and results in an action plan for the borrower's situation. [NFCC — Debt Management Plan](https://www.nfcc.org/debt-management-plan/)
7. Chapter 7 bankruptcy is a liquidation proceeding: the debtor's non-exempt property is sold and the proceeds go to creditors, and it does not involve a repayment plan. [U.S. Courts — Chapter 7 Bankruptcy Basics](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics)
8. Chapter 13 bankruptcy instead uses a court-approved repayment plan, usually lasting three to five years — three years if the debtor's income is below their state's median, five years if above, and a plan can never run longer than five years in any case. Chapter 13 lets a debtor keep property (including a home facing foreclosure) and catch up on secured debts over the life of the plan, and its discharge covers some debts, such as certain property-settlement obligations from divorce, that Chapter 7 does not discharge. As of the date of filing, Chapter 13 eligibility requires unsecured debts under $526,700 and secured debts under $1,580,125. [U.S. Courts — Chapter 13 Bankruptcy Basics](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics)
9. A Chapter 13 filing protects the debtor from creditor lawsuits, wage garnishment, and other collection actions while the repayment plan is in effect. [U.S. Courts — Chapter 13 Bankruptcy Basics](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics)
