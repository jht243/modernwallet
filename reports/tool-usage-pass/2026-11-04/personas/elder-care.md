## Friction
- "I scrolled two screens of paragraphs on my phone and still hadn't seen a box to type in. I nearly closed it." The tool sits at 1685px on phone and 839px on desktop, and nothing above it points to it. That is the most likely reason 81% of views never touch the tool. → Additive fix in `src/pages/[category]/[slug].astro`: render the existing `.skip-tool` link for calculator pages too ("Skip to the calculator"). Today it is only rendered for checklist/planner pages (line 138). Give the tool `<section>` an `id` (e.g. `calculator-tool`) for the link to target. The `data-tool` attribute and every intro paragraph stay exactly as they are.
- "It says my dad has **$$180,000**. That looks broken. Can I trust the rest?" The template literal puts a stray `$` before `fmtUSD()`, which already adds one. → Fix in `src/components/LongTermCareCostCalculator.tsx` line 113: change `Your ${fmtUSD(v.otherLiquidAssets)}` to `Your {fmtUSD(v.otherLiquidAssets)}`. This is a display bug only and changes no math.
- "The total says $339k but the shortfall only adds up to $256k. Which number do I tell my brother?" The total inflates each care year, but the shortfall multiplies the start-year monthly gap by the number of months (`src/lib/long-term-care-cost.ts`, `totalShortfall = annualShortfall × yrsOfCare`). → Additive, in `LongTermCareCostCalculator.tsx`. Keep the existing row and relabel its context "(at start-year prices)". Then add one row, "Total shortfall if costs keep rising 4.5%/yr during care", = `totalLifetimeCost − monthlyIncomeAvailable × 12 × years − monthlyLTCInsuranceBenefit × 12 × years` (floored at 0), with a one-line note that income is held flat (Social Security COLAs would narrow it slightly). Reason: the cost side already inflates, so the gap should be on the same basis. Worked example (Ohio, semi-private, 1 year out, 3 years of care, $1,900/mo): 339,291 − 68,400 = **$270,891** vs $256,068 shown. Source for the 4.5% rate is the tool's own cited Genworth trend data (https://www.genworth.com/aging-and-you/finances/cost-of-care.html). If the reviewer judges the flat-income assumption uncertain, ship only the "(at start-year prices)" label plus a sentence explaining the difference.
- "Medicare's copay is $209.50 in 2026? The hospital social worker said $217." The 2025 figure is labelled as 2026. → Text fix in `src/lib/long-term-care-cost.ts` (`medicareCoverageNote` string and header comment): change it to $217.00/day (2026), source the CMS 2026 Parts A & B fact sheet above. This matches the site's own elder-care hub. It changes no calculation.
- "Is $103,500 really Ohio's nursing-home price, or a guess?" The state number is the national median × one state factor applied to every care type, and the page never says so. → Additive explanation in the tool's source note (`LongTermCareCostCalculator.tsx`, the `S.note` block): "State figures apply one Genworth-derived state cost factor to the national median for each care type; check your county in the CareScout/Genworth Cost of Care Survey." Add the link to the survey page. No math change.
- "It tells me my LTC insurance will cover it, but I said Dad doesn't have any." The LOW-likelihood text always mentions LTC insurance. → In `src/lib/long-term-care-cost.ts`, branch the LOW `reasoning` string so the "and LTC insurance … §7702B" clause only appears when `hasLTCInsurance` is true. This is wording only.
- "I'm 54, he's 78. Whose age is 'Current age'?" The person filling this in is often the adult child. → Add a hint under the label in `LongTermCareCostCalculator.tsx`: "Age of the person who may need care". The label itself stays.
- "The insurance checkbox is tiny on my phone." It is a 13×13px tap target. → Additive style in `LongTermCareCostCalculator.tsx` `S.checkboxRow`: give the input `width/height: 20px` and the row `minHeight: 44px`.
