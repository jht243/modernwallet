## FR-2026-22306
URL: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/02/2026-22306/improving-transparency-into-pharmacy-benefit-manager-fee-disclosure
Fetched text: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-11-02/html/2026-22306.htm (the Federal Register site served a bot-check shell for its own text endpoints; the govinfo copy is the same document, 91 FR 69092, Vol. 91 No. 210, Nov. 2, 2026)

Captures (project-relative): reports/mindmap-pass/2026-11-12-xtrends-run/fr-2026-22306-text.txt (full document text), reports/mindmap-pass/2026-11-12-xtrends-run/fr-2026-22306-operative-text.txt (regulatory text, new 29 CFR 2550.408b-22), reports/mindmap-pass/2026-11-12-xtrends-run/fr-meta.json
Signed Oct. 28, 2026 by Daniel Aronowitz, Assistant Secretary, EBSA; filed Oct. 30, 2026.

Facts used (each quoted or paraphrased from the text):
1. Who must disclose: "providers of pharmacy benefit management services and affiliated providers of brokerage and consulting services" (SUMMARY). Covered service provider = a provider that enters a contract or arrangement with the covered plan and reasonably expects $1,000 or more in direct or indirect compensation (para (c)(1)). PBM services are defined by function "regardless of whether the person ... identifies itself as a 'pharmacy benefit manager'" (para (d)(1)).
2. Who receives the disclosures: "fiduciaries of large, self-insured group health plans" (SUMMARY); the disclosure goes "to a responsible plan fiduciary, in writing" (paras (e), (g)). Plan fiduciaries include plan sponsors who are responsible for selecting and monitoring service providers (preamble, section A). The operative text contains no participant-facing disclosure duty; the words participant/participants appear only in the per-participant fee example, the copay claw-back definition, the formulary-incentive explanation and the plan-size definitions (grep of the operative text).
3. Which plans: "specified large employers" (average of at least 100 employees on business days in the preceding calendar year, counted under Code section 4980H) and "specified large plans" (average of at least 100 participants in the preceding plan year); self-insured only. Excluded from "covered plan": a plan whose benefits are provided exclusively through an insurance policy issued by a health insurance issuer, and a plan whose sponsor is or is affiliated with a drug manufacturer, distributor, wholesaler, PBM or rebate aggregator (para (b); preamble 1.2.1 and 1.2.2). The Department "is reserving" action on fully insured plans.
4. Smaller self-insured plans: not covered by this rule's detailed disclosures but "they are covered plans for purposes of the application of the statutory exemption in ERISA section 408(b)(2)(B)", so their service providers "must provide the generalized compensation disclosures specified in the statute" (preamble 1.2.1).
5. Timing of disclosures: initial disclosure "not later than the date that is reasonably in advance of the date on which the service contract or arrangement is entered, and extended or renewed (for extensions and renewals, 90 calendar days in advance is deemed to be a reasonable period of time)" (para (e)); then "on a semiannual basis (or at the request of the covered plan, quarterly) no later than 30 calendar days after the end of each six-month period or quarter" (para (g)); errors corrected within 30 calendar days of knowing (para (l)).
6. What is disclosed (para (e), (g)): description of services; direct compensation in aggregate and by service; indirect compensation including manufacturer payments (rebates, fees, alternative discounts, other remuneration) in aggregate and for each formulary drug with the amount passed to the plan and the amount retained; spread compensation by drug (National Drug Code) and by pharmacy channel (retail, mail order, specialty) plus arrangements that steer patients to affiliated pharmacies; pharmacy recoupment including "copay claw-back compensation" (defined as the difference between a copayment or coinsurance amount paid to the pharmacy by a plan participant and the reimbursement to the pharmacy); price-protection agreements; termination compensation; formulary placement incentives and equivalent-alternative explanations; net price per fill by drug and channel (or the methodology with an objective way to verify); fiduciary status; audit right.
7. Format and use: amounts expressed in dollars, estimates allowed in good faith; machine-readable file on request (para (k)(3)); the provider may not restrict the plan's use of the disclosures except for reasonable confidentiality agreements for third parties the fiduciary rediscloses to, and plans may share with regulators and legal counsel (para (k)(4)).
8. Dates: "This regulation is effective January 4, 2027. This regulation is applicable to plan years beginning on or after July 1, 2027." (DATES; para (p)). The preamble explains the applicability date is meant to give time to revise current and future contracts. Arithmetic (ours): a calendar-year plan's first plan year beginning on or after July 1, 2027 starts January 1, 2028, so the rule does not apply to a calendar-year plan's 2027 plan year; a plan whose year begins July 1, 2027 would be the earliest.
9. The rule sets no cost-sharing amount: the regulatory text (paras (a) to (p)) imposes disclosure and audit obligations and contains no copay, coinsurance, deductible or premium limit. The preamble says rebates "can offset premiums and other healthcare costs" and that without disclosure "responsible plan fiduciaries are unable to assess whether the underlying fees for PBM services are reasonable".
10. Why copays can still be affected indirectly (Department's own preamble): "In many self-insured group health plans, coinsurance is often based on list price, resulting in higher out-of-pocket costs for participants"; participants "often pay the full cost of the drug up to the amount of their annual deductible and a portion of prescription drug costs after the deductible is satisfied, typically in the form of coinsurance" (section B.1); the cost controls PBMs market to employers "can result in higher out-of-pockets costs for employees due to less favorable copayments or coinsurance" (regulatory impact analysis).
11. Evidence on whether disclosure lowers costs (the Department's own regulatory-impact discussion, 91 FR 69159-69160): for S. 1339, the Pharmacy Benefit Manager Reform Act, a different bill, the Congressional Budget Office estimated it "could reduce net retail prescription drug costs by more than 0.5 percent in the first full year of implementation, which could lower average premiums for employment-based health insurance by less than 0.1 percent in the first year", with the effect diminishing to "less than 0.01 percent by 2034", adding "this does not imply that premiums would decline; rather, premiums are still expected to increase, but at a slower rate". A 2025 study of Medicare Part D data from 2016 to 2022 found state PBM rebate-disclosure laws "did not reduce prescription drug costs, including premiums, deductibles, or out-of-pocket spending"; the preamble concludes that "their direct effects on consumer drug spending remain uncertain". These are third-party findings the Department cites, not our tests, and neither concerns this final rule.
12. Related statute: ERISA section 726 (added by the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026) requires PBM reports on drug spending to large self-insured plans every six months, applicable to plan years beginning on or after August 3, 2028, and requires all group health plans to receive a summary document "that plans can provide to participants and beneficiaries upon request by the participant or beneficiary" (preamble 1.2). This is separate from, and later than, this rule.

Counterexample test (strongest plausible primary-source counterexample to "this rule does not change your copays in open enrollment 2026"): the preamble itself discusses copay claw-backs and list-price coinsurance, so a reader could expect direct copay effects. Result: those items are disclosed to the plan fiduciary only; the text imposes no copay change and no participant disclosure; the preamble's research citations describe direct consumer-spending effects as uncertain. Source silence on a future employer decision is not proof that no plan will change its design, so the page says "the rule does not itself change" and "an employer could use the information", not "no plan will change".
