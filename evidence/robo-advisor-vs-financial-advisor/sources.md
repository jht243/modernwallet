## INV-robo-bulletin

Key facts (quoted or tight paraphrase of the saved capture; no figure beyond these may be written):
SEC OIEA Investor Bulletin: Robo-Advisers (Feb. 23, 2017). Robo-adviser = automated digital investment advisory program; collects goals, horizon, income, other assets and risk tolerance via an online questionnaire, then builds and manages a portfolio. Often lower costs and fees and lower account minimums than traditional programs; services vary widely and are described in Form ADV Part 1 and the Part 2 brochure. Human interaction varies: some offer an investment professional (hybrid, sometimes called bionic), others only technical support; access may depend on a minimum account size; unlike a traditional adviser there may be no conversation with a person to gather your needs. Recommendations are limited by information requested and the burden to update it often falls on the client. Some robo-advisers may not have been tested under stressed market conditions. Many use ETFs.

## INV-advisers
URL: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/working-investment-professional/investment-advisers
saved capture: reports/mindmap-pass/2026-11-10-competitor-18b9b55e45cc-run/research/sources/www.investor.gov_introduction-investing_getting-started_working-investment-professional_investment-advisers (sha256 ffda7445ecd73d962912bb3ead41eca63f60c2bd345b4af5908368d646816d38), checked 2026-11-10
Key facts (quoted or tight paraphrase of the saved capture; no figure beyond these may be written):
Investor.gov: investment advisers are required to act in your best interest and not put their interest ahead of yours; the way advisers make money creates conflicts to ask about. An adviser is a firm or person that, for compensation, provides investment advice about securities. Advisers typically provide ongoing advice about buying, selling or holding investments and monitor performance; the fee is typically based on the value of all of the assets held in your account with the adviser; they may also give planning services. Before selecting: services you need, limits, cost, how the adviser is paid, conflicts, legal or disciplinary history. Read the contract, the relationship summary and the Form ADV Part 2 brochure. The services and fees depend on the contract you negotiate. Advisers generally must register with the SEC or state authorities; check both the person and the firm.

## INV-CRS

Key facts (quoted or tight paraphrase of the saved capture; no figure beyond these may be written):
Investor.gov/CRS (captured 2026-11-09): registered broker-dealers and registered investment advisers must provide a relationship summary (Form CRS) to retail investors; it covers services, fees and costs, conflicts, required standard of conduct, legal/disciplinary history and conversation starters; many firms are dual registrants and you should understand when they act as a broker and when as an adviser. Brokers are generally transactional and typically paid commission or markup per transaction; advisers are generally focused on your portfolio over time and typically paid an ongoing asset-based fee on total account value. Asset-based fees may cost more than transaction-based but suit ongoing advice or delegated decisions.

## INV-bulletin-formadv

Key facts (quoted or tight paraphrase of the saved capture; no figure beyond these may be written):
SEC OIEA Investor Bulletin "Form ADV - Investment Adviser Brochure and Brochure Supplement" (June 24, 2016, updated Aug. 27, 2020). Form ADV is the uniform registration form for SEC and state authorities; three parts. Part 1: business, ownership, clients, employees, business practices, affiliations, disciplinary events of adviser or employees; check-the-box format; Part 1 filings are public on IAPD. Part 2 (plain-English narrative): Part 2A brochure has 18 items answered in the order listed with the form headings, to ease comparison; examples named: advisory business (services and amount of client assets managed), fees and compensation (how compensated, fee schedule, negotiability, billing vs deduction, other fees such as brokerage, custody and fund expenses), performance-based fees and side-by-side management, methods of analysis/strategies/risk of loss, disciplinary information (certain events presumed material within last 10 years), code of ethics/participation in client transactions/personal trading, brokerage practices; annual delivery of a summary of material changes with a revised brochure or offer to deliver it. Part 2B brochure supplement: information about individuals who give advice (education, business experience for the past five years, disciplinary information, other business activities; six items); delivered for each supervised individual who formulates advice with direct client contact or makes discretionary decisions. Part 3 relationship summary (SEC-registered advisers serving retail investors): services, fees and costs, conflicts, standard of conduct, legal and disciplinary history, key questions to ask. Brochures and relationship summaries are public on IAPD; IAPD is reachable via the Check Out Your Investment Professional search tool on Investor.gov. The brochure is the primary disclosure document for investment advisers.
